# SECTION 4: Units, times and polarity (owned by Associate m01 l04)

DEPTH. Feet throughout: 1 ft = 0.3048 m. The top of the Ekene Sand at Ekene-1 is 5078.740157 ft (1548.000000 m in the kit).

VELOCITY AND DENSITY. Velocities in metres per second, density in kilograms per cubic metre (a density log in g/cc times 1000). The engines work in SI throughout.

IMPEDANCE. Acoustic impedance AI = Vp x density, in kg/(m2 s). The same number appears in three ways, and a study states which:

| quantity | Ekene-1 oil-leg mean |
| --- | --- |

| the same in units of 10^6 kg/(m2 s) | 6.639019 |
| the same as (m/s)(g/cc), that is AI / 1000 | 6639.018603 |

TIME. Seismic time is two-way time (TWT) in milliseconds; a checkshot records one-way time (OWT) from the reference. At Ekene-1 the checkshots bracketing the top of the Ekene Sand are at 4687.500000 ft (OWT 585.055000 ms) and 5079.002625 ft (OWT 644.438000 ms); linear between them the top is at OWT 644.398189 ms, TWT 1288.796378 ms.

POLARITY. The kit's seismic is SEG normal polarity: an increase in acoustic impedance downwards is a peak, a positive number. The Ekene Sand is harder than the Ogbia Shale above it at Ekene-1 (section 15), so its top is a peak.

# SECTION 9: The amplitude spectrum (owned by Associate m03 l01)

HOW THE ENGINE MEASURES IT (seismicQc.js). Each trace is tapered at both ends by a Tukey window (the stated taper fraction), padded with zeros to twice the next power of two samples, and transformed; the one-sided amplitude is the modulus at each frequency. `averageSpectrum` averages the POWER of many traces and takes the root. The frequency step is 1000 / (padded length x sample interval in ms).

# SECTION 10: Band edges and the centroid (owned by Associate m03 l02 and Associate m06 l03)

DEFINITIONS (spectrumStats). The -6 dB edges are where the amplitude falls to 10^(-6/20) of the peak amplitude, below and above the peak, interpolated linearly between frequency steps; the -20 dB edges the same at 10^(-20/20). The -6 dB bandwidth is the high edge less the low edge. The centroid is the power-weighted mean frequency, the sum of f A(f)^2 over the sum of A(f)^2.

# SECTION 11: Signal to noise from trace coherency (owned by Associate m03 l03 and Associate m06 l03)

THE METHOD (Hatton, Worthington and Makin, section 4.4). Two neighbouring traces carrying the same signal and independent noise of equal power have a normalised cross-correlation c at their best alignment, and the signal to noise POWER ratio is c / (1 - c). The engine searches the stated number of samples either side for the best alignment (for dip), clamps c at 0.999999 at most, takes every neighbouring pair, and reports the median ratio and the same in decibels (10 log10 of the ratio).

THE EKENE NEIGHBOURS. 21 neighbouring traces along inline 1027 through Ekene-1, maximum lag 3 samples:

| window ms | pairs | median signal to noise (power) | in dB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 800 to 1600 | 20 | 361.155122 | 25.576938 |
| 1600 to 2400 | 20 | 77.008444 | 18.865384 |

The first five pairs of the shallow window, in order: 388.889121, 471.612452, 367.582682, 350.585441, 390.813495.

THE LAG, A STATED CHOICE, ON FLAT EVENTS. The shallow window at four maximum lags returns the same median at every lag, because the best alignment of every neighbouring pair here is at zero lag:

| --- | --- | --- |

A RATIO NEAR THE CLAMP IS A CEILING. When neighbouring traces are almost identical, c is close to 1 and c / (1 - c) grows without bound; at the clamp 0.999999 the ratio is 999998.999971. QI Studio prints any ratio of 10,000 or more as "over 10,000 (over 40 dB)" for that reason (src/pages/apps/QIStudio/services/qcRun.js).

# SECTION 12: Acquisition footprint and where to measure it (owned by Associate m03 l04)

THE METHOD (footprint). On a time slice (inlines by crosslines) of RMS or absolute amplitudes, the engine averages the slice along one axis to a profile, removes its straight-line trend, and finds the strongest periodic stripe in the stated band of periods: the period in lines, its share of the profile's variance (with its harmonics), and its prominence over the median power of the band. Measure it on RMS amplitude: on signed seismic a stripe of gain averages away.

QI STUDIO'S RULE for calling a stripe a footprint (`isStripe`, src/pages/apps/QIStudio/services/qcRun.js): a share above 0.3, a prominence of 10 or more, and at least five repeats of the period across the slice (cycles = lines in the slice / period).

# SECTION 23: The synthetic in one lesson (owned by Professional m01 l02)

THE SEISMIC INTERPRETATION COURSE TEACHES THE SYNTHETIC IN FULL. The recap: impedance from the sonic and the density, placed in two-way time by the checkshots, resampled to the seismic sample interval, turned into reflection coefficients rc = (Z2 - Z1) / (Z2 + Z1) between neighbouring samples (SEG normal polarity: an increase in impedance is positive), and convolved with a wavelet.

THE ENGINE'S CONVENTIONS (synthetics.js): impedance in (m/s)(g/cc); a sample is a gap when it is not finite or is a seismic or LAS null; the convolution zero-fills gaps and a convolved value whose own coefficient is a gap is not used.

# SECTION 24: Statistical and well wavelets (owned by Professional m01 l03 and Professional m02 l03)

- STATISTICAL (extractStatisticalWavelet): the amplitude spectrum of the traces' autocorrelation, taken to zero phase. # SECTION 40: The background trend, the off-trend distance, the fluid factor, chi, and conditioning before AVO (owned by Professional m05 l03 and Professional m05 l04 and Professional m05 l05)

# SECTION 43: The convolutional model and its adjoint (owned by Expert m01 l02)

THE MODEL (forwardPoststack, the operator of pylops' PoststackLinearModelling, kind centred). The unknown is m = ln(AI) on the trace clock; D is the centred first difference, (D m)_i = (m_(i+1) - m_(i-1)) / 2, zero at the first and last sample; C is the centred convolution with the wavelet (offset the floor of half its length). The seismic trace is d = C D m. Half the difference of ln(AI) is close to the reflection coefficient for small contrasts, which is why the model is written in ln(AI).

THE ADJOINT. Every solver here needs the transpose of the operator. The test of an adjoint is the dot product: for any x and y, (C x) . y equals x . (C^T y). On two vectors of 101 samples from the canonical seeded generator (mulberry32 on seed 42, each value uniform less one half, x drawn first and then y), the test the inversion explorer's forward view runs on the seed it states:

WHY IT IS NEEDED. The seismic band holds no trend: a band-limited trace says how impedance changes and says nothing of its level. The low-frequency model (LFM) supplies what the seismic lacks below about 8 Hz, from the wells.

HOW IT IS BUILT (lfm.js). Each well's ln(AI) in time is low-passed by a moving average whose half window is round(1000 / (cut x dt) / 2) samples (16 samples here, lowFrequencyModel), then carried to a trace by inverse distance weighting with power 2. Between two horizons a sample keeps its proportional position (mapToWellTime), so a layer that thickens or dips between wells keeps its impedance; above the first horizon and below the last the time offset from that horizon is kept. Samples no well covers hold the nearest covered value.

# SECTION 45: The wavelet scale (owned by Expert m01 l04)

THE PROBLEM. A wavelet extracted or chosen for its shape carries an arbitrary amplitude, and the inversion reads impedance contrast from the ratio of the trace to the synthetic. The scale is set at the wells: the one number s that makes s times the forward model of the logs match the seismic best, by least squares pooled over every well sample whose wavelet span the log covers.

THE EKENE SCALE. The 25 Hz Ricker (peak amplitude 1.000000) against the full stack at the four pack wells: scale 769.984107 over 19 samples; the scaled wavelet's peak is 769.984107.

THE ENGINE. `waveletScale` in engines/qi/inversionQc.js, the rule QI Studio runs, computes the scale; the course's lab calls it on the four pack wells.

A REFUSAL BY THE SAME RULE. The engine refuses to scale when the wells overlap fewer than ten usable samples of seismic: "The wells overlap too little seismic to scale the wavelet."

# SECTION 46: Model-based least squares (owned by Expert m02 l01)

THE PROBLEM (modelBased). Minimise ||C D m - d||^2 + eps^2 ||m - m0||^2 by conjugate gradients on the normal equations (CGLS), starting from the LFM m0, for the stated number of iterations or until the gradient falls to 1e-10 of its first value. eps pulls the result towards the LFM. The output is ln(AI) and AI = exp(ln(AI)).

# SECTION 47: Blocky and sparse-spike inversion (owned by Expert m02 l02)

BLOCKY (modelBased with blocky). Adds an iteratively reweighted total variation term, epsTV^2 sum of w_i (m_(i+1) - m_i)^2 with w_i = 1 / sqrt((m_(i+1) - m_i)^2 + beta^2), re-solved over the stated outer loops (beta 0.001, an engine argument the course leaves at that value), so few large steps cost less than many small ones.

SPARSE-SPIKE (sparseSpike). FISTA (Beck and Teboulle 2009) solves min 1/2 ||C r - d||^2 + lambda ||r||_1 for a sparse reflectivity r (fistaReflectivity); r is integrated to a relative ln(AI) (m_(i+1) = m_(i-1) + 2 r_i, each of the two interleaved chains levelled to its mean), and the low frequencies come from the LFM through a moving-average crossover at 8 Hz.

THE SPARSITY IS SET BY LAMBDA, ON THE TRACE'S OWN AMPLITUDE SCALE. Non-zero reflectivity samples in the 101-sample Ekene-1 trace (largest absolute amplitude 399.814697), with the scaled wavelet:

# SECTION 51: Fatti's three parameters (owned by Expert m03 l01)

THE LINEARISATION (Fatti and others 1994; fattiCoefficients). R(theta) = G1 dln(AI) + G2 dln(SI) + G3 dln(rho), with G1 = (1 + tan^2 theta) / 2, G2 = -4 K^2 sin^2 theta, G3 = (4 K^2 sin^2 theta - tan^2 theta) / 2 and K the background Vs/Vp. The simultaneous inversion (simultaneousInversion, pylops' PrestackLinearModelling with the Fatti linearisation, kind centred) solves for ln(AI), ln(SI) and ln(rho) together by CGLS, each pulled to its own LFM by its own weight.

The engine reads the anomaly's downdip edge (an anomaly node beside a lower node outside it, at their midpoint), its scatter against the closure's relief, conformance = 1 - scatter / relief clipped to 0 to 1, the share of the anomaly inside the closure, and the contact the edge implies. EVIDENCE (evidenceIndependence). Attributes computed from one seismic response are one piece of evidence. A full-stack RMS amplitude, an impedance from the same full stack and a far-stack brightening make 3 supporting items and 2 independent sources; the shared source is full_stack (full-stack RMS amplitude, impedance from the full stack).

| case | recommendation | seismic support |

| anomaly present, conformance 0.83, inside 0.9, two independent sources, feasible, nothing open | mature | supports |

The absence of an anomaly counts against a prospect only where the feasibility study says the case would be visible. A COMPETING EXPLANATION (tuning, a lithology change, a processing artefact) is an input with a status of open, ruled out or likely; one judged likely holds the prospect at investigate, as the last row shows. The prospect explorer's assessment states it as a control.

THE CLIMB TO A CREST (climbToCrest, engines/qi/prospectTrap.js). A prospect picked at a point starts from the nearest mapped node and steps to the highest of its eight neighbours until none is higher. On the Ekene grid, from the mapped node nearest the grid's centre (column 12, row 9, -5057.417979 ft), the climb ends at node 310, at -5051.568570 ft; the highest mapped node is -5051.568570 ft, so the climb reaches the same crest.

# SECTION 59: The report and the limits, and the Expert capstone (owned by Expert m06 l01 and Expert m06 l02 and Expert m06 l03 and Expert m06 l04)

THE QI REPORT states, for every product, the method, the stated settings (window, wavelet and its scale, LFM cut, weights, iterations, seeds), the blind-well figures per parameter, the calibration and its left-out check, the prospect figures with their limits, and every issue raised. The handover files are the products and that record; a result without its settings cannot be checked.

WHAT QI NEVER SETS: the geological chance of success. QI reports what the seismic says (supports, neutral, against) for the risk team to weigh; prospectAssessment.js returns a recommendation and a seismic support and no probability of success.

WHAT THE ENGINES DO NOT COMPUTE, checked against the functions this tier calls: no wavelet is estimated inside an inversion (it is an input); no LFM is built inside modelBased or simultaneousInversion (m0 is an input); no horizon is picked; no grid is gridded (closure reads a stated grid); no fluid is inferred from a class name; no geological chance; no volume in barrels (closure returns gross rock volume only).

THE EXPERT CAPSTONE runs on a held-out synthetic case (the Ekene field is never used), supplied as a case file the learner pastes into the inversion and prospect explorers. It asks for six figures, each read from the panels: the LFM ln(AI) at a stated sample of a second trace position, built from the case's wells by inverse distance and their horizons; the model-based AI at a stated sample of the target trace, with the case's supplied low-frequency model, wavelet, weight and iteration count; the Vp/Vs from the simultaneous inversion at that sample, with the case's angle wavelets; the high end of the 80 percent porosity interval at a stated AI; the posterior probability of the oil-sand facies at a stated AI; and the column from crest to spill on the case's depth grid, in feet. Every input those figures depend on is stated in the case file.

# SECTION 60: Vocabulary this course legislates before a word is written (owned by Associate m01 and Professional m01 and Expert m01)

THE RULE. Each term below means one thing everywhere a learner reads, and a lesson that uses it in another sense is a defect.

- **AI**: acoustic impedance, Vp x density, in kg/(m2 s); the course writes the word impedance beside it the first time a lesson uses it, and uses AI for nothing else.
- **SI**: shear impedance, Vs x density.
- **net sand**: a sample of the reservoir with Vsh at or below the stated cut-off.
- **oil leg, water leg**: net sand above, and at or below, the stated oil-water contact.
- **cap rock**: the stated thickness of log above the top of the reservoir, read as one layer by its means.
- **the -6 dB band**: the frequencies between the two -6 dB edges of the mean spectrum of stated traces with a stated taper; its width is the -6 dB bandwidth.
- **centroid**: the power-weighted mean frequency of a spectrum.
- **signal to noise**: a POWER ratio, from trace coherency, with its decibel form 10 log10 of the ratio.
- **footprint**: a periodic stripe on a slice of RMS amplitude, called one only by a stated rule.
- **the local shear trend**: a least squares fit of measured Vs on Vp on stated samples, with its prediction interval at a stated level.
- **substitution**: Gassmann through the dry rock from one pore fluid to another, at stated conditions, mineral and porosity.
- **the wet trend**: the fluid line through the origin fitted to the intercept and gradient of the brine-substituted block boundaries; the published line is the Castagna, Swan and Foster slope, and a lesson names which line it means.
- **distance**: the signed perpendicular distance of an (A, B) point from a stated line; negative is the hydrocarbon side.
- **class**: the Rutherford and Williams class from A and B, with class IV, at the stated 0.02 band; a class is never graded.
- **feasibility**: the Associate question: can this seismic, on this data, see this fluid, written as a verdict that states its numbers and its choices.
