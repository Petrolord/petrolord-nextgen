TIME. Seismic time is two-way time (TWT) in milliseconds; a checkshot records one-way time (OWT) from the reference. POLARITY. The kit's seismic is SEG normal polarity: an increase in acoustic impedance downwards is a peak, a positive number. The Ekene Sand is harder than the Ogbia Shale above it at Ekene-1 (section 15), so its top is a peak.

# SECTION 9: The amplitude spectrum (owned by Associate m03 l01)

HOW THE ENGINE MEASURES IT (seismicQc.js). Each trace is tapered at both ends by a Tukey window (the stated taper fraction), padded with zeros to twice the next power of two samples, and transformed; the one-sided amplitude is the modulus at each frequency. `averageSpectrum` averages the POWER of many traces and takes the root. The frequency step is 1000 / (padded length x sample interval in ms).

# SECTION 10: Band edges and the centroid (owned by Associate m03 l02 and Associate m06 l03)

DEFINITIONS (spectrumStats). The -6 dB edges are where the amplitude falls to 10^(-6/20) of the peak amplitude, either side of the peak, interpolated linearly between frequency steps; the -20 dB edges the same at 10^(-20/20). The -6 dB bandwidth is the high edge less the low edge. The centroid is the power-weighted mean frequency, the sum of f A(f)^2 over the sum of A(f)^2.

# SECTION 11: Signal to noise from trace coherency (owned by Associate m03 l03 and Associate m06 l03)

THE METHOD (Hatton, Worthington and Makin, section 4.4). Two neighbouring traces carrying the same signal and independent noise of equal power have a normalised cross-correlation c at their best alignment, and the signal to noise POWER ratio is c / (1 - c). The engine searches the stated number of samples either side for the best alignment (for dip), clamps c at 0.999999 at most, takes every neighbouring pair, and reports the median ratio and the same in decibels (10 log10 of the ratio).

THE EKENE NEIGHBOURS. 21 neighbouring traces along inline 1027 through Ekene-1, maximum lag 3 samples:

| window ms | pairs | median signal to noise (power) | in dB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 800 to 1600 | 20 | 361.155122 | 25.576938 |
| 1600 to 2400 | 20 | 77.008444 | 18.865384 |

The first five pairs of the shallow window, in order: 388.889121, 471.612452, 367.582682, 350.585441, 390.813495.

THE LAG, A STATED CHOICE, ON FLAT EVENTS. The shallow window at four maximum lags returns the same median at every lag, because the best alignment of every neighbouring pair here is at zero lag:

| maximum lag, samples | median signal to noise | in dB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 361.155122 | 25.576938 |
| 1 | 361.155122 | 25.576938 |

| 6 | 361.155122 | 25.576938 |

A RATIO NEAR THE CLAMP IS A CEILING. When neighbouring traces are almost identical, c is close to 1 and c / (1 - c) grows without bound; at the clamp 0.999999 the ratio is 999998.999971. QI Studio prints any ratio of 10,000 or more as "over 10,000 (over 40 dB)" for that reason (src/pages/apps/QIStudio/services/qcRun.js).

# SECTION 12: Acquisition footprint and where to measure it (owned by Associate m03 l04)

THE METHOD (footprint). On a time slice (inlines by crosslines) of RMS or absolute amplitudes, the engine averages the slice along one axis to a profile, removes its straight-line trend, and finds the strongest periodic stripe in the stated band of periods: the period in lines, its share of the profile's variance (with its harmonics), and its prominence over the median power of the band. Measure it on RMS amplitude: on signed seismic a stripe of gain averages away.

QI STUDIO'S RULE for calling a stripe a footprint (`isStripe`, src/pages/apps/QIStudio/services/qcRun.js): a share above 0.3, a prominence of 10 or more, and at least five repeats of the period across the slice (cycles = lines in the slice / period).

THE EKENE SLICE: RMS amplitude of the full stack, 1250 to 1350 ms, 128 inlines by 128 crosslines, periods searched 2 to 16 lines. And a DESIGNED TEACHING CASE: the same slice with every sixth crossline multiplied by 1.2 (a stated stripe added by the course, labelled as such):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ekene, along the crosslines | 15.058824 | 0.015148 | 27.724874 | 8.500000 | no |
| Ekene, along the inlines | 13.473684 | 0.014838 | 27.074395 | 9.500000 | no |

# SECTION 14: The local shear trend and its prediction interval (owned by Associate m04 l03)

THE FIT (fitVsRegression). Ordinary least squares of measured Vs (m/s) on Vp in km/s: linear Vs = c0 + c1 Vp, or quadratic with a c2 Vp^2 term. The slope c1 is in m/s of Vs per km/s of Vp. It returns the coefficients, the sample count n, the degrees of freedom (n less the coefficients), the residual standard error s, R squared and the Vp range of the calibration.

THE PREDICTION (predictVs). At a new Vp the predicted Vs carries a prediction interval Vs +/- t s sqrt(1 + leverage), with t Student's quantile at (1 + level) / 2 on the degrees of freedom; sigma is s sqrt(1 + leverage). A Vp outside the calibration range is flagged as extrapolated.

# SECTION 17: Oil-to-brine substitution on a log, and how far impedance and Vp/Vs move (owned by Associate m05 l02 and Associate m05 l03 and Associate m06 l02)

THE METHOD, sample by sample (lab `substituteSample`): the mineral modulus from quartz and clay by Voigt-Reuss-Hill on the sample's Vsh (mixMinerals; quartz K 36.600000 GPa, clay K 20.900000 GPa); the in situ pore fluid from brine and oil by Wood at the sample's Sw; the new fluid brine (Sw 1); then Gassmann through the dry rock (substituteVels) with the sample's PHIT. # SECTION 18: The wet trend on a crossplot (owned by Associate m05 l04)

THE METHOD, as QI Studio and Rock Physics Studio draw it (lab `wetTrend`). Every sample of the window with oil in it (Sw below 1) is taken to brine by the substitution of section 17; the logs are blocked into blocks of the stated length (blockLogs, a block's mean Vp, Vs and density); every boundary between two blocks gives an intercept A and a gradient B by Shuey (interfacePoints); and the fluid line through the origin is fitted to the points by total least squares, the principal axis of the points about the origin (fitFluidLine), so neither A nor B is treated as free of error.

# SECTION 23: The synthetic in one lesson (owned by Professional m01 l02)

THE SEISMIC INTERPRETATION COURSE TEACHES THE SYNTHETIC IN FULL. The recap: impedance from the sonic and the density, placed in two-way time by the checkshots, resampled to the seismic sample interval, turned into reflection coefficients rc = (Z2 - Z1) / (Z2 + Z1) between neighbouring samples (SEG normal polarity: an increase in impedance is positive), and convolved with a wavelet.

THE ENGINE'S CONVENTIONS (synthetics.js): impedance in (m/s)(g/cc); a sample is a gap when it is not finite or is a seismic or LAS null; the convolution zero-fills gaps and a convolved value whose own coefficient is a gap is not used.

EKENE-1 on its full-stack trace clock (4.000000 ms, first sample 900 ms), with the statistical wavelet of section 24: the pack's logs give 26 live synthetic samples (the logs span 4931.500000 to 5331.000000 ft). The largest reflection coefficient in that span is -0.190340, at 1312.000000 ms. The normalised correlation of the synthetic with the trace at zero shift is 0.983438.

# SECTION 24: Statistical and well wavelets (owned by Professional m01 l03 and Professional m02 l03)

TWO WAVELETS COME FROM THE SEISMIC AT A WELL.
- STATISTICAL (extractStatisticalWavelet): the amplitude spectrum of the traces' autocorrelation, taken to zero phase. Its spectrum is the source wavelet's when the reflectivity is white; its phase is ASSUMED zero. The engine reads the stated window length (default 120 ms) and smoothing (default 5 Hz), Hann-tapers the lags and normalises the peak to 1.

This one is Seismolord app code (src/pages/apps/Seismolord/lib/wellWavelet.js, petrolord-suite 5df9d9410), and it needs a log long enough for the wavelet it is asked for.

# SECTION 40: The background trend, the off-trend distance, the fluid factor, chi, and conditioning before AVO (owned by Professional m05 l03 and Professional m05 l04 and Professional m05 l05)

# SECTION 60: Vocabulary this course legislates before a word is written (owned by Associate m01 and Professional m01 and Expert m01)

THE RULE. Each term below means one thing everywhere a learner reads, and a lesson that uses it in another sense is a defect.

- **AI**: acoustic impedance, Vp x density, in kg/(m2 s); the course writes the word impedance beside it the first time a lesson uses it, and never uses AI for anything else.
- **SI**: shear impedance, Vs x density.
- **net sand**: a sample of the reservoir with Vsh at or below the stated cut-off.
- **oil leg, water leg**: net sand above, and at or below, the stated oil-water contact.
- **cap rock**: the stated thickness of log above the top of the reservoir, read as one layer by its means.
- **the -6 dB band**: the frequencies between the two -6 dB edges of the mean spectrum of stated traces with a stated taper; its width is the -6 dB bandwidth.
- **centroid**: the power-weighted mean frequency of a spectrum.
- **signal to noise**: a POWER ratio, from trace coherency, with its decibel form 10 log10 of the ratio.
- **footprint**: a periodic stripe on a slice of RMS amplitude, called one only by a stated rule.
- **the local shear trend**: a least squares fit of measured Vs on Vp on stated samples, with its prediction interval at a stated level.
- **substitution**: Gassmann through the dry rock from one pore fluid to another, at stated conditions, mineral and porosity.
- **the wet trend**: the fluid line through the origin fitted to the intercept and gradient of the brine-substituted block boundaries; the published line is the Castagna, Swan and Foster slope, and a lesson names which line it means.
- **distance**: the signed perpendicular distance of an (A, B) point from a stated line; negative is the hydrocarbon side.
- **class**: the Rutherford and Williams class from A and B, with class IV, at the stated 0.02 band; a class is never graded.
- **feasibility**: the Associate question: can this seismic, on this data, see this fluid, written as a verdict that states its numbers and its choices.
