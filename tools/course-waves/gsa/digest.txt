THE PETROLEUM INDUSTRY ACT 2021:

PIA s.110(1)(a): The Commission prescribes the domestic gas delivery obligation and allocates it among all lessees before 1 March of each year, from the domestic gas demand requirements.
> "prescribe and allocate the domestic gas delivery obligation among all lessees before 1st March of each year based on the domestic gas demand requirements" (PIA s.110(1)(a))

PIA s.110(2): A lessee whose voluntary contracts with the strategic sectors reach its obligation is deemed to have fulfilled it.
> "where the volume of the contracts is equal to or higher than the domestic gas delivery obligation for the lessee, the lessee shall" (PIA s.110(2))

PIA s.110(2)(a): The first consequence of voluntary contracts that reach the obligation: the obligation is deemed fulfilled.
> "(a) be deemed to have fulfilled its domestic gas delivery obligation ;" (PIA s.110(2)(a))

PIA s.110(8): A lessee that fails its obligation pays US$3.50 for each MMBtu not delivered.
> "a lessee who fails to comply with the domestic gas delivery obligation shall incur a penalty of US $3.50 per MMBtu not delivered," (PIA s.110(8))

PIA s.110(8): Where the lessee has signed a gas purchase and sale agreement with a wholesale supplier of the strategic sectors, the agreement states the penalty.
> "where the lessee has signed a gas purchase and sale agreement with a wholesale supplier of the strategic sectors, the penalty for failure to deliver shall be as stated in that agreement." (PIA s.110(8))

PIA s.110(9): The Commission may adjust the US$3.50 rate by regulation.
> "The penalty amount of US $3.50 per MMBtu referred to under subsection (8) may be adjusted as the Commission may prescribe in a regulation made under this Act." (PIA s.110(9))

PIA s.110(10): No penalty is due where the lessee establishes that its failure came from one of four listed causes.
> "A lessee shall not incur a penalty prescribed under subsection (8), where it can establish that its failure to comply is as a result of" (PIA s.110(10))

PIA s.110(10)(b) and (c): Two of the causes: a purchaser that cannot accept the allocated volumes, and gas that cannot be transported for reasons beyond the lessee's control.
> "(b) the inability of a purchaser to accept allocated natural gas volumes ; (c) the inability to transport the allocated natural gas for reasons beyond the control of the lessee ; or" (PIA s.110(10)(b) and (c))

PIA s.110(10)(d): The fourth cause: a purchaser that fails to pay for the allocated volumes. The first, (a), is force majeure.
> "(d) the failure of a purchaser to pay for allocated natural gas volumes." (PIA s.110(10)(d))

PIA s.110(13): A producer that defaults on supply through the domestic gas aggregator compensates the customer for its loss.
> "A producer-customer of the domestic gas aggregator shall pay compensation to customer-client for any loss suffered as a result of default to supply marketable natural gas" (PIA s.110(13))

PIA s.110(14)(a): A lessee that does not comply may not supply gas to any new midstream gas export operation, in addition to the penalty.
> "in addition to the penalties provided under subsection (8), not be entitled to supply natural gas to any new midstream gas export operations," (PIA s.110(14)(a))

PIA s.110(15): Approval to supply gas for export requires the lessee to have complied with its domestic obligation first.
> "An approval for the supply of natural gas for export projects shall, from the effective date, be subject to prior compliance by the lessee with its domestic gas delivery obligation." (PIA s.110(15))

PIA s.110(16): Domestic supply contracts signed before the Act took effect and still running count towards the obligation.
> "Domestic gas delivery contracts entered into by lessees or licensees prior to the effective date and continuing after the effective date, shall be counted towards their domestic gas delivery obligation" (PIA s.110(16))

PIA s.167(1): The Authority determines the domestic base price each year under the Third Schedule.
> "determine the domestic base price under the Third Schedule to this" (PIA s.167(1))

PIA s.167(3): Price control, and the aggregator's role with it, is not required where either of two conditions is met.
> "The price control and the corresponding role of the domestic gas aggregator shall not be required, where the" (PIA s.167(3))

PIA s.167(3)(b): The second condition: the domestic market is largely free-market contracting between willing buyers and willing sellers, on criteria the Authority sets.
> "(b) domestic market for natural gas is largely characterised by free market based contracting for natural gas between willing buyers and willing sellers," (PIA s.167(3)(b))

PIA s.167(3)(b): Once that condition is met, the sector prices of s.167(4) to (7) and the gas based industries price of s.168 cease to apply.
> "and at such time the provisions of subsections (4), (5), (6) and (7) and section 168 shall no longer be applicable" (PIA s.167(3)(b))

PIA s.167(5): The power sector price is the domestic base price at the delivery point.
> "The price of marketable natural gas applicable to the power sector shall be the domestic base price at the marketable natural gas delivery point." (PIA s.167(5))

PIA s.167(6): The commercial sector price is the domestic base price plus US$0.50 per MMBtu.
> "The price of marketable natural gas applicable to the commercial sector shall be the domestic base price at the marketable natural gas delivery point plus US $0.50 per MMBtu." (PIA s.167(6))

PIA s.167(7): Gas distributors negotiate their price, which may not exceed the commercial sector price.
> "the applicable price for gas distributors for the marketable natural gas at the marketable gas delivery point shall not exceed that of the commercial sector under subsection (6)." (PIA s.167(7))

PIA s.167(8): The buyers pay the transport cost from the delivery point to their own facilities.
> "shall pay for the transportation cost of the marketable natural gas from the marketable natural gas delivery point to the facilities of the wholesale customers." (PIA s.167(8))

PIA s.168(2): The gas based industries price has a floor of US$0.90 per MMBtu.
> "(2) The floor price for the gas based industries shall be US $0.90 per MMBtu." (PIA s.168(2))

PIA s.168(3): Its ceiling is the domestic base price of the year.
> "(3) The ceiling price shall be the domestic base price applicable for any particular year." (PIA s.168(3))

PIA s.168(4) and (5): The Fourth Schedule prices are prices at the delivery point, and transport costs are added for delivery (s.168(4)); the Authority may add other gas based industries by regulation (s.168(5)).
> "be added for delivery to the respective gas based industries. (5) The Authority may by regulation adjust the price mechanism to add other gas based industries in line with market realities." (PIA s.168(4) and (5))

PIA Third Schedule para 1(a): The first principle for the domestic base price: high enough to bring forward enough gas for the domestic market voluntarily.
> "(a) the price must be of a level to bring forward sufficient natural gas supplies for the domestic market on a voluntary basis by the upstream petroleum industry ;" (PIA Third Schedule para 1(a))

PIA Fourth Schedule: The gas based industries formula: CP = NRP x (1 + EPF), no higher than EPP, the domestic base price; NRP is US$1 per MMBtu.
> "CP = NRP * (1 + EPF) <=EPP Where - CP is the applicable price in US $/MMBtu, EPP is the domestic base price under section 168 (3), NRP is the National Reference Price which is US $1/MMBtu" (PIA Fourth Schedule)

PIA Fourth Schedule: EPF = (CMPP - PRP) / PRP, where CMPP is the average current month end product price and PRP the product reference price, both in US$ per tonne.
> "CMPP is the Average Current Month End Product Price in US $/MT PRP = Product Reference Price in US $/MT i.e. dollar per metric tonne which varies depending on the industry" (PIA Fourth Schedule)

PIA Fourth Schedule: The Authority may change the formula or the NRP, CMPP and PRP values by regulation.
> "The Authority may by regulation change the formulas or the values for NRP, CMPP and PRP and introduce other values for one or more gas based industries" (PIA Fourth Schedule)

PIA s.104(1): Flaring or venting outside the listed exceptions is an offence with a fine the Commission prescribes by regulation; the Act prints no rate.
> "commits an offence under this Act and is liable to a fine as prescribed by the Commission in regulations under this Act." (PIA s.104(1))

PIA s.105(2): The Commission may take, free of charge, gas that is destined for flaring.
> "(2) The Commission shall have the right to take free of charge natural gas that is destined for flaring at the flare stack." (PIA s.105(2))

THE DOMESTIC GAS DELIVERY OBLIGATION REGULATIONS 2022:

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(1): Regulation 6(1) opens by preserving the four excuses of the Act.
> "Except as provided under section 110(10) of the Act, a lessee" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(1))

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(1): The Regulations repeat the Act's rate: US$3.50 per MMBtu of gas not delivered.
> "shall incur a penalty of US$3.50 per MMBtu of gas not" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(1))

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(2): Under a signed agreement the penalty is not less than the US$3.50 of r.6(1).
> "the penalty payable to the Commission for failure to deliver such gas shall not be less than the amount specified in subregulation (1) of this regulation." (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(2))

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(3)(b): If the Commission does not investigate a claimed excuse within 90 days, the lessee is deemed to have met its obligation.
> "(b) Commission fails to investigate within 90 days, the lessee shall be deemed to have met the domestic gas delivery obligation" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(3)(b))

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.5(2): The Commission finds on the national supply curve the lowest supply price that covers the strategic sectors' demand.
> "The Commission shall identify on the supply curve the lowest level for the domestic gas supply price" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.5(2))

DGDO Regulations 2022 r.9: The Regulations define the domestic base price by reference to the Act's Third Schedule.
> "“domestic base price” is the price for marketable natural gas determined pursuant to the Third Schedule to the Act ;" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.9)

THE COMMONWEALTH MODEL GAS SALES AGREEMENT (2025, CC BY 4.0):

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), front matter: The model agreement is published under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, so it may be quoted with attribution.
> "This work is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International." (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), front matter)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Adjusted ACQ: The Adjusted ACQ is the ACQ less scheduled maintenance, force majeure and seller shortfall quantities, and any operational flexibility credit.
> "the quantity of Gas equal to the Annual Contract Quantity less the sum of the Scheduled Maintenance Quantities, Force Majeure Quantities, Shortfall Quantities, and if applicable less the Operational Flexibility Credit," (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Adjusted ACQ)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of ACQ: The ACQ is the sum of the daily contract quantities of the contract year.
> "the quantity of Gas equal to the sum of the Daily Contract Quantities determined using the" (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of ACQ)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Take or Pay Quantity: The take-or-pay quantity is a percentage of the Adjusted ACQ; the model leaves the percentage for the parties to insert.
> "quantity of Gas equal to [## INSERT] percent (##%) of the Adjusted Annual Contract Quantity for that Contract Year." (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Take or Pay Quantity)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of MaxDCQ: MaxDCQ is a stated percentage of the DCQ, left for the parties to insert.
> "a quantity of Gas equal to [## INSERT] percent (##%) of the applicable DCQ." (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of MaxDCQ)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Shortfall Quantity: The model computes the seller's shortfall as the properly nominated quantity less the delivery tolerance less the daily actual quantity.
> "the Properly Nominated Quantity less the Delivery Tolerance Quantity and less the Daily Actual Quantity determined using the following formula: SFQ = (PNQ − DTQ ) − DAQ" (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Shortfall Quantity)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Shortfall Quantity: The definition opens with a day on which the seller did not make the properly nominated quantity available.
> "means for any Day in the Delivery Period, during which Seller did not make available the Properly Nominated Quantity," (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Shortfall Quantity)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of BADQ: The buyer's annual deficiency quantity arises in a year the buyer takes less than the take-or-pay quantity.
> "means for any Contract Year in which Buyer does not take at least the Take or Pay Quantity," (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of BADQ)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Make-Up Aggregate: The make-up aggregate sums the deficiency quantities of prior years, less make-up already taken and make-up expired.
> "a quantity of Gas equal to the sum of Buyer’s Annual Deficiency Quantities in prior Contract Years less the sum of the Make-Up Quantities taken in prior Contract Years and expired Make-Up Aggregate" (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Make-Up Aggregate)

Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Make-Up Period: The make-up period is a stated number of contract years after the deficiency year.
> "Contract Years immediately following the Contract Year corresponding to such Buyer’s Annual Deficiency Quantity, within which Buyer has a right to make up" (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), definition of Make-Up Period)

Four rules the table shows:
- A contract term with no default is refused when it is missing, and the message says so: the make-up terms, the recovery order, each year's permitted reduction, the royalty terrain, the take-or-pay price, a shortfall price when a seller shortfall is stated, whether price control applies, and the domestic base price under price control.
- An input key a function does not read is refused at whatever level it sits (a top-level option, a year, a day, a formula, an S-curve, a schedule), with the path to the key and the full list of accepted keys; a key that is an index name is checked against the indices the series carries.
- A quantity that cannot be true is refused before anything is computed: gas taken above the gas made available, force majeure and maintenance above the DCQ, reductions above the ACQ, contract years or months that are not consecutive.
- Every figure inside a message is the shortest round-trip decimal of the value it was given.

# SECTION 6: What is graded, where the practicals run, and what is never graded (owned by Associate m01 l05 and Expert m06)

EVERY GRADED NUMBER IN THIS COURSE IS A RETURN VALUE OF THIS ENGINE ON FIXED INPUTS. A capstone field, a question key and a panel figure are each computed by a function in the table of section 1 on contract terms written down in advance. Nothing in the engine samples or searches, so the same terms give the same number on any machine, and there is exactly one right answer.

THE PRACTICALS RUN IN THE COURSE'S OWN CALCULATOR PANELS. This is an engine course with no Suite app. Each tier has a calculator panel that calls this same vendored engine: the quantity calculator (Associate), the ledger calculator (Professional) and the contract calculator (Expert). A learner types or pastes their own contract terms; the panel prints what the engine returns, every refusal in the engine's own words, and the reasons beside each figure.

A PRICED LEDGER IN A PANEL. Where a contract prices its gas by a formula, the panel prices every month with priceSeries and then copies each year's contract price and take-or-pay price from that year's annual row, by the basis the contract states: the annual average (the model agreement's Article 15.2.6 Alternative 1) or the last month's price (Alternative 2). The copy is a lookup; the engine computes every figure on both sides of it.

WHAT A CAPSTONE STATES. Each capstone runs its own synthetic agreement, which this digest does not print, and states every term a figure depends on: the quantities, the day counts, the take-or-pay percentage, the make-up period, the recovery order, the end-of-term rule, the carry-forward terms, the price formula with its averaging, lag, reset and rounding, the price basis of the ledger, the royalty terrain and share, the discount rate and base year. Each graded figure is quoted to six decimals as the panel prints it.

WHAT IS NEVER GRADED. No graded figure depends on a reading the engine states (section 18): every capstone field is the same number under each reading the engine takes and under the alternative it names. No graded figure uses the domestic base price, which is quoted only as reported (section 2).

WHAT A COMPUTED FIGURE DOES NOT SAY. A deficiency payment is what the stated clauses produce on stated takes; it is not a forecast of what a buyer will pay. An oil-indexed price follows the stated formula on the stated index series, which in this course is synthetic. An NPV depends on the discount rate and base year stated. Each figure is quoted with its terms for that reason.

A make-up period of N contract years after a deficiency year y runs to the end of year y + N inclusive: make-up is usable in that last year and the rest expires at its end, and none is available in year y + N + 1. RECOVERY ORDERS IN THE TEXTS. The model agreement takes make-up only after the Adjusted ACQ of the year is taken (Article 12.7.1); ESMAP describes make-up counted after the minimum-pay quantity for the year (para 6.59); HMRC names two orders, make-up in priority or only after the period minimum (OT05435). The engine computes all three and takes the order as a required input (section 11).

THE CARRY-FORWARD CAP IN THE TEXTS. ESMAP describes a cap on the use of carry-forward as a percentage of the annual quantity (para 6.62); the model agreement's Alternative 2 caps the credit as a percentage of the year's deficiency. The engine's capPct is a percentage of the deficiency.

THE SHORTFALL DEFINITION AND ITS FORMULA. The model agreement's definition of the Shortfall Quantity opens with a day the seller did not make the properly nominated quantity available, and its printed formula subtracts the quantity taken. The engine measures against the quantity made available (section 18).

PRINTED FIGURES AND EXACT FIGURES. HMRC prints the effective swing as 1.66 (the engine: 1.666667, section 8); the Energy Charter Secretariat prints the parity slope 0.172 (the engine on 5.8 MMBtu per barrel: 0.172414, section 16). A reason the engine prints carries the double it holds: the export refund reads, verbatim,

and the course quotes that refund as the numeric field at six decimals: 3700831.335000.

THE BTU AND THE CUBIC FOOT. The engine uses the International Table Btu, 1055.05585262 J, exact by definition (NIST SP 811 prints it as "1.055 056 E+03 J"). The validation record reads the model agreement's Btu as defined "at 59 F to 60 F", "about 1054.80 J, 0.02% smaller"; a contract on that Btu should state its heating value in those units. The cubic foot is exactly 0.028316846592 cubic metres, the cube of the international foot.

THE FOURTH SCHEDULE'S TABLE HEADER. The gazetted table heads its NRP column "US $/mmbtu" and, beneath it, "Net of transport Tariff US $/Kcf" (text); the formula above the table states NRP in US$ per MMBtu, and the engine holds NRP in US$ per MMBtu.

# SECTION 24: Vocabulary this course legislates before a word is written (owned by Associate m01 and Professional m01 and Expert m01)

Six terms in this course carry a narrower meaning than they have in conversation. The rule for each is binding on every lesson, bank question, key truth and panel.

| term | what it can mean elsewhere | the rule here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shortfall | any gap between what was wanted and what came | always qualified: a seller shortfall is gas the seller did not make available against a properly nominated quantity; a buyer shortfall is the adjusted DCQ the buyer did not take |
| deficiency | any shortage | the take-or-pay quantity less the quantity counted, when above zero; the money is the deficiency payment |
| make-up | any catching up | gas paid for in a deficiency year and taken in a later year of its make-up period |
| carry-forward | any balance brought forward | takes above the stated base credited against a later deficiency, capped, for a stated period |
| Adjusted ACQ | the ACQ | the ACQ less maintenance, force majeure, seller shortfall and any permitted reduction; the take-or-pay quantity is its stated percentage |
| domestic base price | the gas price | the price the Authority determines each year under PIA s.167(1); a required input, quoted only as reported |

A FIGURE THAT DEPENDS ON A TERM is quoted with it: a deficiency payment with its take-or-pay percentage and price; a make-up quantity with its order and period; a price with its formula, averaging, lag and reset; an NPV with its rate and base year.
