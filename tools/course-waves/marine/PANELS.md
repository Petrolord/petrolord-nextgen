# SC4 Offshore & Marine Logistics: the calculator panels

Read `BRIEF.md` first. Every number here is quoted from `digest.txt`, the only
teaching truth for this course. The engine's FINDINGS record, the oracle and
its golden, the fixture README and writer, and the engine's source comments
are PROVENANCE.

## AN APP COURSE

The Suite app is the Marine Logistics Planner, which runs the same engine file.
The four calculator panels below carry the course's practicals: they call the
vendored marine logistics engine on the Ekene cluster or on whatever the
learner pastes, so a learner without a Suite seat can work every exercise, and
the learning page says so in plain words.

## WHAT IS SHIPPED

Under `src/components/course/panels/marine/` in the NextGen repository:

* `marineLab.js`, the ONE teaching lab. It imports the vendored Ekene fixture
  (`packages/engines/test-data/supplychain/ekene-marine`) and the INPUTS of the
  vendored golden file, carries thin routes to the engine for whatever a
  learner types, the view routes, the block reader, the stated-input writer
  `setStated`, the rewrites (a route for a mode, a factor for a form), the
  berth sweep, a JSON parser and the teaching readers. It holds no graded
  answer, no tolerance and no capstone case.
* `gradedTolerance.js`, **the only place a grading tolerance is made**:
  `max(stated, half a unit in the last place the course prints that class)`.
  `fields.json` and `precision.json` are both written out of it.
* `gradedAnswerGuard.js`, every string shape a graded answer can reach a
  learner as.
* `VoyageCalculator.jsx` (`marine-voyage-calculator`), `DeckCalculator.jsx`
  (`marine-deck-calculator`), `BaseCalculator.jsx` (`marine-base-calculator`),
  `VariabilityCalculator.jsx` (`marine-variability-calculator`) and
  `panelBits.jsx`.

The learning page is `src/pages/apps/MarineLearningPage.jsx`, routed at
`/dashboard/apps/marine`; it names the Marine Logistics Planner. The panels
are registered in `src/content/courses/panelRegistry.js`, and
`panelRender.test.jsx` renders every view of every panel. The capstone case
files are generated by `gen_course.py --cases` into
`src/content/capstone-cases/marine` and are offered on the capstone card only.

## THE FOUR CALCULATORS

Every view takes the learner's own inputs as JSON, because that is how a
capstone is worked. A case file holds several calls under BLOCK KEYS: the
view's own name (`fleetSize`, `deckPlan`) or the view's name, a colon and a
name (`voyagePlan:milk-run`, `shoreBase:mdc`). Every view offers the blocks of
a pasted case file in a block selector, runs the block chosen, and writes every
control into that block; a box that holds one call's inputs has no blocks and
runs as it stands (no engine input key is a view name). `marineLab.test.js`
proves every block of every case file gives the whole case the result of the
block alone, and `panelCapstoneReadable.test.js` reads all eighteen graded
fields with each case file pasted WHOLE, as the capstone card offers it,
choosing the block in the selector. Every view shows the engine's own refusal
and reasons, verbatim, and a Source block with the engine's basis.

EVERY REQUIRED STATED INPUT HAS A VISIBLE CONTROL, and no panel holds a hidden
default. A control shows the input as the box states it, or "not stated", and
writes the learner's choice into the box through `setStated`; an input left
unstated reaches the engine unstated and the engine refuses by name.
`panelStatedControls.test.js` renders every view with a capstone case pasted
and finds a labelled control for every input the block carries. Each view
carries a start selector over the Ekene cases and the small cases the digest
teaches.

### `marine-voyage-calculator` (Associate and Professional)

The voyage plan: each voyage's legs and calm hours, its hours by activity with
the weather factor on the stated activities, its days, fuel by activity and
cost, its load and the utilisation of every capacity constraint, the binding
constraint and every overload, with the totals (the Ekene PSV milk run:
62.072727 hours, 19.876364 t, 17292.436364, deck area binding at 0.900000).
Fleet sizing for a period: each voyage set's voyages before and after rounding
and what drives them, voyage days and vessel-days, and the fleet's vessel-days,
vessels before and after rounding, capacity, spare and short vessel-days,
utilisation and fuel (the Ekene week: 3.100000 voyages of demand, 10.345455
vessel-days, 2 vessels). Controls for the vessel's speed, deck area, usable
fraction, deck load, deadweight, every tank and every fuel burn; each
product's kind and density; the route mode, the stops and every leg, or every
installation's distance; the port hours, the weather factor, the activities it
slows and the fuel price; each installation's field hours, cargo or demand and
bulk by product, and its minimum visits; the period, the available days and
both rounding rules.

### `marine-deck-calculator` (Professional)

The deck plan: each voyage's units, area, weight and utilisations, the units in
the order packed, every overflow unit with the engine's reason, the usable
area, the voyages used, the lower bound over the units that fit an empty
voyage, the units no voyage can ever carry and the packing order (the Ekene
voyage of deck cargo by first-fit decreasing: 599.229600 m2 carried and 11
units overflow). Controls for the deck area, the usable fraction, the deck
load, the voyages, the packing rule, and each item line's length, width,
weight and quantity.

### `marine-base-calculator` (Expert)

The berth queue: the service, lift and bulk hours, the arrivals an hour, the
offered load, the berth utilisation, the probability of waiting (M/M/c only),
the mean queue, the mean wait in hours and in working days, the mean time at
the base and the mean in the system; with a target, the fewest berths that
meet it and the wait there; and the same call at more berths, labelled as
stated probes (the Ekene base: 0.533333, a mean wait of 3.180124 hours as
M/M/c). Controls for the berths, the arrivals, the working day, every service
term, the concurrent choice, the model and the optional target.

### `marine-variability-calculator` (Expert)

The fleet under weather and demand variability: the plan at the modes, the
mean, P90, P50, P10, min and max of the vessel-days and of the vessels
required, the distribution of whole vessels, the probability of being short
and the expected short vessel-days for the planned vessels, every Monte Carlo
figure printed with its seed and its draws and marked as not graded (the Ekene
week: a P90 of 7.977705 on seed 20260927 and 20000 draws). Controls for each
factor's form and terms, the planned vessels, the draws and the seed, with
every fleet sizing control.

## THE RULES FOR EVERY PANEL

1. **No graded capstone answer, in any of four shapes**, no capstone name,
   label, input or series. `panelCapstoneGuard.test.js` sweeps the lab, the
   calculators, the bits and the learning page, and plants each shape to prove
   the sweep catches it. `panelCapstoneReadable.test.js` reads every graded
   field off a rendered panel with the capstone case pasted, at full precision.
2. **No refusal message is written in a panel or the lab.** Show `r.error`, and
   a reason as the engine returns it.
3. **No clock, no timer, no random number, no path under `/root`.** The one
   Monte Carlo runs inside the engine on the stated seed.
4. **No source prose.** A label or a note in a panel is the course's own words;
   `gate_no_source_prose.py` sweeps the panels and the page.
5. **The copy rule.** No em dashes, no en dashes, no "X, not Y" contrastive, no
   "rather than", no ", never", no "instead of", no "and not", no "and never".
   `gate_copy_rule.py` sweeps the panels and the page as well as the digest.
6. **The vocabulary**, digest section 30; no "AI".
