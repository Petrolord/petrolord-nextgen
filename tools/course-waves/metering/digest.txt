# SECTION 8: A DIFFERENTIAL THAT IS NOT A FLOW (owned by Associate m03)

A differential at or above the static pressure would put the downstream
pressure at zero or below. The repaired engine names that by itself rather
than letting it fall through to a message about the Reynolds number.

   > the differential of 500 in H2O is 18.06 psi, which is at or above the static pressure
     of 14.7 psia. The downstream pressure would be zero or negative, so this is a
     differential range or a static pressure that is wrong rather than a flow

The other three refusals this function carries, in its own words:

   > the orifice bore must be positive and smaller than the pipe bore
   > flow needs a positive differential, density and viscosity
   > a compressible flow needs a positive static pressure

# SECTION 17: THE VALVE, AND THE BOUNDARY EVERYTHING HAPPENS AT (owned by Professional m01)

A control valve is the one item of process equipment where the ordinary
sizing equation stops working exactly when the service gets difficult. Past
the choking boundary a larger coefficient buys nothing. The BELEMA valve is

A PRODUCT THAT BOILS AT AMBIENT IS REFUSED. The expansion factor has the
difference between atmospheric and the vapour pressure in a denominator, so
a product at atmospheric pressure has no expansion factor at all. The engine
refuses rather than returning a standing loss with a denominator at or below
zero, which a total would hide because the working loss does not carry it:
   > a true vapour pressure of 15.4 psia is at or above the stated atmospheric pressure of
     14.7 psia: the product boils at ambient and this is not a fixed-roof tank problem. It
     needs a pressure vessel or a refrigerated tank, and these relations do not apply to it
