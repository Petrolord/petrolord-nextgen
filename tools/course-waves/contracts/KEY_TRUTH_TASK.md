# SC5 Contract & Supplier Management: the key-truth writer's task

Read `BRIEF.md` first. `PACK.md` is the only teaching truth for this course.
A practice course has no engine, so the key-truth audit READS THE SOURCE PACK
in place of an engine digest (lead decision 5): every keyed answer must trace
to a cited passage, and the passage must say what the key says.

## TWO JOBS

1. **The key truths.** One sentence a learner should be able to say
   afterwards, with the source and locator that make it checkable.
2. **The second reader of every bank, before the migrations.** Read prompt,
   options, explanation, the passages the question's trace names, and the
   lesson, for every question, one tier at a time. For every key:
   * open each traced passage in `PACK.md` and confirm it states what the key
     states (a QUOTED passage word for word; a BY CONCEPT passage in substance);
   * where the key carries a legal figure, confirm the figure is printed by a
     traced passage, then open the fetched text under `sources/` at the
     passage's locator and confirm it there too;
   * confirm no distractor is also true on the pack.
   Record every question read in `AUDIT-<tier>.md` with its verdict and the
   passage ids checked. Swap roles where a distractor is supported.

## THE DEFECT CLASSES TO READ FOR

1. **A distractor that became true** under a regime or a reading the question
   did not rule out (the UK rule, the World Bank rule, the Nigerian public
   entity rule, the operator's contract).
2. **A reading keyed as the law**, or a practice source keyed as binding an
   Ekene contract.
3. **A key the traced passages do not support**, or a trace to the wrong
   passage.
4. **An invented figure**: a percentage, a day count, a threshold or a period
   no passage prints.
5. **A false superlative** ("always", "every contract") where the source
   speaks of one case or says "should".
6. **An exam question that near-duplicates a module question** (audit at
   Jaccard 0.45).
7. **A stage confused**: a tender-stage rule keyed at execution, a closing
   rule keyed at mobilisation.
8. **The answer printed before the work** in the prompt.
9. **The answer-length tell**, and three distractors opening alike.
10. **A licensed text quoted or closely paraphrased** (World Bank, AIPN,
    LOGIC, FIDIC, ISO 44001, CIPS, Kraljic).
11. **A passage id, the word "pack" or a pack section** in learner text.
12. **The regulatory rule broken**: a text named without its edition, a
    Nigerian rule cited without its section.
13. **Repair history**, and any claim that the course computes something.
14. **A scenario that is not marked synthetic.**

## THE KEY TRUTHS THIS COURSE OWES ITS LEARNERS

### Associate

1. The contract manager runs the contract day to day and produces its plan,
   and the senior business owner approves the plan (GovS 008, 4.6.6 and
   5.4.2).
2. A contract is managed according to its own terms (the World Bank Contract
   Management Practice guidance, by concept).
3. Every later oil and gas contract in Nigeria must conform to the content Act
   (the content Act, s.6).
4. Nigerians are given first consideration for training and employment on the
   work programme (the content Act, s.10(1)(b) and s.28(1)).
5. The operator files its annual Nigerian Content Performance Report within
   sixty days of the beginning of each year (the content Act, s.60), and its
   contractors are contractually bound to report content information to it
   (s.65).
6. Under the UK Act a public contract estimated above £5 million carries at
   least three KPIs (the Procurement Act 2023, s.52(1)), as UK practice.

### Professional

7. Suppliers are segmented by their commercial and business impact, and the
   strongest resource goes where risk and reward are highest (GovS 008, 6.3;
   Contract Management Principles, principle 4).
8. Performance is assessed against the KPIs at least once in every twelve
   months and on termination (the Procurement Act 2023, s.71(2)), as UK
   practice.
9. Contract changes are justified and controlled, with an audit trail (GovS
   008, 5.4.5).
10. A modification the UK Act does not permit on at least one ground needs a
    new procurement (the Contract Modifications guidance), as UK practice.
11. A succession plan puts a Nigerian understudy on each expatriate position
    for at most four years (the content Act, s.31(1)); at most five per cent of
    management positions may be expatriate positions (s.32).
12. Where performance falls short, preventative or corrective action is taken
    within the terms of the contract (GovS 008, 5.4.3).

### Expert

13. How a contract will be managed is a strategic decision taken early and
    written into the contract (the Sourcing Playbook, chapter 12).
14. A claim states its contractual or legal basis and is backed by records
    (the World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance, by concept).
15. Once mediation starts, the limitation period for the claim is suspended
    (the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023, s.71(1)).
16. Early termination is the last resort, after the contractual remedies are
    exhausted (GovS 008, 5.4.7).
17. An operator, contractor or sub-contractor who carries out a project
    contrary to the content Act commits an offence, liable to a fine of five
    per cent of the project sum or cancellation of the project (the content
    Act, s.68).
18. A conflict of interest is declared and the assessment kept under review
    until the contract ends (the Public Procurement Act 2007, s.57(10); the
    UK Conflicts of Interest guidance, as UK practice).
