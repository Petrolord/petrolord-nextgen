# SC3 Materials, Spares & Inventory Management: the key-truth writer's task

Read `BRIEF.md` first. Every number here is quoted from `digest.txt`, the only
teaching truth for this course. The engine's FINDINGS record, the oracle and
its golden, the fixture README and the engine's source comments are
PROVENANCE.

## TWO JOBS

1. **The key truths.** One sentence a learner should be able to say afterwards,
   with the number that makes it checkable.
2. **The second reader of every bank, before the migrations.** Read prompt,
   options, explanation, digest and lesson for every question, one tier at a
   time, and CALL THE ENGINE (through a calculator panel, or node on the
   vendored `packages/engines/engines/supplychain/inventory.js` through the
   wave's `materials_engine.mjs`) for every keyed figure. Swap roles where the
   explanation supports a distractor.

## THE DEFECT CLASSES TO READ FOR

1. **A distractor that became true** under an input the question did not rule
   out (another boundary rule, another rounding rule, another discount type,
   another service measure, a review period).
2. **A stated reading keyed as the law.**
3. **A concept-only item keyed to a number** the engine does not compute.
4. **A wrong failure-mode gloss.** "Refused" said of a result with a reason.
5. **A false superlative** ("always", "every item") where the digest shows one
   case.
6. **An exam question that near-duplicates a module question** (audit at
   Jaccard 0.45).
7. **A sentence characterising a relationship nobody computed.**
8. **The answer printed before the work** in the prompt or a table above it.
9. **A capstone name, input or value** anywhere.
10. **The answer-length tell**, and three distractors opening alike.
11. **Printed alike keyed as equal.**
12. **A boundary stated globally.** Each rule draws its own (the digest's
    boundaries section): a class minimum is met at or above it; the cover
    limit is exceeded only strictly above it; a lead-time demand equal to the
    reorder point is met; a tie takes the smaller quantity or fewer spares.
13. **A message quoted inexactly.**
14. **A digest section number in learner-visible text.**
15. **The regulatory rule broken**: a text named without its edition, a
    sentence of the lectures quoted or closely paraphrased, a figure the
    digest does not print.
16. **Repair history**: a sentence about former engine behaviour.
17. **A Monte Carlo estimate keyed as exact**, or quoted without its seed and
    draws.
