# SECTION 13: A count is a draw: the same observed rate at growing exposure (owned by Professional m01)

An event count is modelled by the Poisson count model: the true rate times the exposure gives a mean, and the count observed is one draw around it. The interval says how far the true rate could be from the observed one.

The IMO ladder holds the observed rate at 2.000000 per 200,000 hours and grows the exposure: each count is paired with 100000 hours per event (stated).

# SECTION 14: The Garwood interval, from the chi-square distribution (owned by Professional m02)

For a count N the limits are chi2(alpha/2; 2N)/2 and chi2(1 - alpha/2; 2N + 2)/2, times base over hours. The engine's method line: "Garwood (1936) exact Poisson interval, chi-square form: [chi2(alpha/2, 2N)/2, chi2(1-alpha/2, 2N+2)/2] x base / exposureHours".

| quantity at N = 7, 95 percent | value |
| --- | --- |
| chi-square lower quantile, 14 degrees of freedom | 5.628726103040 |
| half of it, the count lower limit | 2.814363051520 |
| engine countLower | 2.814363051520 |
| chi-square upper quantile, 16 degrees of freedom | 28.845350723405 |
| half of it, the count upper limit | 14.422675361702 |
| engine countUpper | 14.422675361702 |

The lower limit uses 2N degrees of freedom and the upper uses 2N + 2. The lower limit is the Poisson mean at which N or more events has probability alpha/2, which is a quantile of the gamma distribution of shape N; the upper limit is the mean at which N or fewer events has probability alpha/2, which is a quantile of the gamma distribution of shape N + 1. Twice a gamma of shape k is a chi-square on 2k degrees of freedom, which is where the halves and the two degrees of freedom come from.

# SECTION 15: Conservative by construction: the exact coverage of the 95 percent interval (owned by Professional m02 l03)

Derived from the engine: for a true mean mu, the probability of observing k events is Q(k + 1, mu) minus Q(k, mu), with the engine's `regularizedGammaQ` (and Q(0, mu) taken as 0). The interval for k covers mu when countLower(k) <= mu <= countUpper(k). The coverage is the sum of the probabilities of the k whose interval covers mu.

| true mean | coverage | total probability summed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.500000 | 0.985612 | 1.000000 |
| 1.000000 | 0.981012 | 1.000000 |
| 2.000000 | 0.983436 | 1.000000 |
| 3.500000 | 0.990126 | 1.000000 |
| 5.000000 | 0.979567 | 1.000000 |
| 10.000000 | 0.975386 | 1.000000 |
| 20.000000 | 0.956795 | 1.000000 |

The coverage is at or above 0.95 at every mean probed. A count is a whole number, so no interval built from it can cover exactly 95 percent at every mean; the exact interval is built so that it never falls short, and the coverage above 0.95 in the table is what that costs in width.

# SECTION 16: Confidence is a required fraction, and the upper tail is solved directly (owned by Professional m02 l04 and Professional m02 l05)

`confidence` has no default, and it must be a fraction strictly between 0 and 1. Section 4 tables the refusals for 95 and for a missing value.

WHAT FLOATING POINT DOES TO alpha. With confidence 0.95 the engine forms alpha as 1 minus 0.95, which in double precision is 0.050000000000000044; alpha over 2 is 0.025000000000000022. And 1 minus 0.025 is 0.975, which IS the double 0.975.

The engine solves the upper limit on the UPPER tail at alpha/2 directly, with `chiSquareQuantileUpper`, rather than on the lower tail at 1 minus alpha/2. At 0.025 the two routes agree closely; where the tail probability is small the lower-tail route runs out of digits, because 1 minus a tiny number rounds. Measured on 2 degrees of freedom:

| 0.025000 | 7.377758908228 | 7.377758908228 | 7.377758908228 | 1.20e-16 | 0 |

THE RULE OF THREE says an upper 95 percent limit at zero events is about 3 events. # SECTION 19: The central p-value, the minlike p-value, and why the engine chose central (owned by Professional m05)

UTOROGU north recorded 7 events in 355200 hours and south 6 in 1048900 hours (stated).

| what | value |
| --- | --- |
| engine pValue, central: twice the smaller tail, capped at 1 | 0.051759 |
| minlike p-value, derived: the probability of every count no more likely than the one observed | 0.025879 |
| engine rate ratio, north over south | 3.445148 |
| engine 95 percent rate-ratio interval | 0.991404 to 12.408545 |

The central p-value is above 0.05 and the rate-ratio interval includes 1: the two agree that the data do not rule out equal rates at 95 percent. The minlike p-value, the convention of R's poisson.test and of scipy's binomtest, is below 0.05 on the same counts and disagrees with that interval. The engine uses the central convention because it is the test the Clopper-Pearson interval inverts (Fay 2010). A reader who checks an engine p-value in R will see a different number on some inputs; that is this choice, and it is declared.

THE AGREEMENT, SWEPT. Every pair of counts from 0 to 15 north and 0 to 24 south on the UTOROGU hours, except both zero: 399 comparisons. The central p-value falls below 0.05 exactly when the engine's interval excludes 1 on 399 of them. The minlike p-value, derived, disagrees with the interval on 14 of them.

NO DIFFERENCE FOUND IS A WEAK CLAIM. On UTOROGU the rate-ratio interval runs from 0.991404 to 12.408545: it includes 1, and it also includes a north rate 12.408545 times the south one. A p-value above 0.05 says the data cannot tell; it does not say the rates are equal.

# SECTION 23: What a signal means, and revising the limits after a found cause (owned by Expert m03)

A signal is a question: something in that month may differ from the rest. It is not a finding about a cause. If investigation finds an assignable cause, the month is set aside and the chart is redrawn on the rest. On EGBEMA, with month 8 set aside:

| chart | months | centre | months flagged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| as drawn | 12 | 2.893273 | [8] |
| revised, month 8 set aside | 11 | 2.389523 | [] |

Setting a month aside needs a found cause. Setting it aside because it is high is removing the data that disagree.

NO SIGNAL IS A WEAK REASSURANCE. Month 3 on the drawn chart reads 8.227913 and does not signal, because with 97230 hours its upper limit is 10.211920. A quiet chart can be a chart with too little exposure per point to see anything.

# SECTION 26: The traps: denominators, contractors and reclassification (owned by Expert m05)

DENOMINATORS THAT MOVE. The second row adds the contractor hours and leaves out the contractor events: the rate falls with no change on site. Events and hours must come from the same workforce, both in or both out.

RECLASSIFICATION. Moving 2 recordables to first aid lowers the recordable rate from 1.161259 to 0.982603. The 58 days lost do not move, so the severity rate on the same hours is 5.181000 before the reclassification and after it. A rate that falls while the harm does not is a change in counting.

THE MEAN OF RATES, ONE LEVEL UP. A group that averages its sites' rates, or an industry body that averages its members' rates, makes the section 10 mistake at a larger scale. # SECTION 27: What the engine does not classify, and where the judgement stays (owned by Expert m06)

Every figure in this course starts from a count someone classified. The engine checks that a count is a whole number and that hours are positive; it cannot check whether an injury was recordable, whether a restricted day was a lost day, or whether a release crossed a Tier 1 threshold. Section 4 shows what it refuses; nothing in it refuses a count that was classified wrongly.
