WHAT THE ENGINE DOES NOT DO, checked here against its exports:
- It does not fill a missing value. A null, undefined or non-finite entry in X or y is refused by name, and filling or dropping it is the caller's decision.
- It fits linear models only: least squares, ridge and binary logistic regression. It builds no tree, no network, no neighbour rule and no cluster.
- It does not sample input distributions or give prediction intervals. The only random draws are the seeded shuffles of the splits and of permutation importance.
- It does not choose lambda, the features or the model. A course or an app does that with the folds groupKFold returns, and prints the scores it compared.
- Its exported names are, in full: DEFAULTS, METRICS, applyScaler, classificationReport, confusionMatrix, fitMinMaxScaler, fitStandardScaler, groupKFold, groupSplit, leakageDemo, learningCurve, logLoss, logistic, ols, permutationImportance, predict, randomRowSplit, regressionMetrics, ridge, rocCurve, solveSPD.

# SECTION 5: The seed, the sorted names and the shuffle (owned by Associate m02 l04)

Before any shuffle the engine sorts the well names. Names that are strings sort by UTF-16 code unit, character by character, so the nine sonic wells sort as: EKENE-1, EKENE-10, EKENE-2, EKENE-3, EKENE-4, EKENE-5, EKENE-7, EKENE-8, EKENE-9. EKENE-10 comes before EKENE-2 because the character 1 comes before the character 2; the engine does not read the number inside a name. Names that are all numbers sort ascending, and a mix of the two is refused (section 3).

The shuffle is Fisher-Yates from the end with one mulberry32 stream seeded by `seed`: for i from m - 1 down to 1, draw u, take j = floor(u x (i + 1)), and swap positions i and j. The draws for seed 5 and m = 9 (u from lib/stats mulberry32, printed to six decimals; j derived):

| i | u | j = floor(u x (i + 1)), derived | swap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 0.689775 | 6 | EKENE-9 with EKENE-7 |
| 7 | 0.772743 | 6 | EKENE-8 with EKENE-9 |
| 6 | 0.219763 | 1 | EKENE-8 with EKENE-10 |
| 5 | 0.623179 | 3 | EKENE-5 with EKENE-3 |
| 4 | 0.085137 | 0 | EKENE-4 with EKENE-1 |
| 3 | 0.592165 | 2 | EKENE-5 with EKENE-2 |
| 2 | 0.720102 | 2 | EKENE-5 with EKENE-5 |
| 1 | 0.458104 | 0 | EKENE-8 with EKENE-4 |

The shuffled order is EKENE-8, EKENE-4, EKENE-5, EKENE-2, EKENE-1, EKENE-3, EKENE-10, EKENE-9, EKENE-7, which is the `order` the engine returns. The first three are the test wells.

THE TEST SIZE is ceil(testFraction x count): here ceil(0.3 x 9) = 3 wells. A product within 1.00e-9 of a whole number is taken as that number before the ceiling. The engine's own case:

| call | testFraction x count in float, derived | test size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `groupSplit`, 25 groups, fraction 0.28 (golden) | 7.000000000000001 | 7 |
| `randomRowSplit`, 25 rows, fraction 0.28 (golden) | 7.000000000000001 | 7 |

In float, 0.28 x 25 is 7.000000000000001, which is 7 plus 8.88e-16 (derived, the float product less 7); a plain ceiling would hold out 8. 8.88e-16 is within 1.00e-9 of the whole number, so the engine takes 7. The engine's basis states the rule: "ceil(testFraction x count), a product within 1e-9 of a whole number taken as that number".

# SECTION 7: Ordinary least squares on the training wells (owned by Associate m04)

Least squares finds the coefficients that make the sum of squared residuals, sum (y - yhat)^2, as small as it can be. Here the target is DT (us/ft) and the features are GR (gAPI), RHOB (g/cm3) and NPHI (v/v), fitted on the 180 training rows of the teaching split, with an intercept.

| term | coefficient | unit | standard error | t value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| intercept | -0.552686 | us/ft | 30.261214 | -0.018264 |
| GR | 0.288005 | us/ft per gAPI | 0.066103 | 4.356927 |
| RHOB | 22.499915 | us/ft per g/cm3 | 12.289493 | 1.830825 |
| NPHI | 138.783590 | us/ft per v/v | 21.960664 | 6.319645 |

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| rows n | 180 |
| coefficients p, the intercept included | 4 |
| residual degrees of freedom n - p | 176 |
| residual standard error s (us/ft) | 5.823075 |
| RSS | 5967.842781 |
| TSS about the training mean | 18853.190000 |
| R-squared | 0.683457 |
| adjusted R-squared | 0.678062 |

READING A COEFFICIENT. Holding RHOB and NPHI fixed, one more gAPI of gamma ray moves the fitted DT by 0.288005 us/ft. NPHI is a fraction, so its coefficient 138.783590 is per whole unit of v/v; per 0.01 v/v it is 1.387836 us/ft (derived, the coefficient over 100). A coefficient carries the target's unit over the feature's unit, and its size says nothing about importance until the feature's own spread is known (section 21 measures importance directly).

THE INTERCEPT is the fitted DT where every feature is zero: -0.552686 us/ft, with a standard error of 30.261214. No rock has a bulk density of zero, so the intercept is a point far outside the data where the fitted plane happens to cross; it is there so the plane need not pass through the origin.

A coefficient depends on which other features are in the model. Fitted on NPHI alone, the NPHI coefficient is 11.127468 us/ft per v/v with R-squared 0.001327; beside GR and RHOB it is 138.783590.

STANDARD ERRORS are s x sqrt(diag((X'X)^-1)) with s^2 = RSS / (n - p); the basis says: "s x sqrt(diag((X'X)^-1)), s^2 = RSS / (n - p), p counting the intercept". They measure how far a coefficient would move between samples like this one, and the formula assumes independent residuals. Rows from one well share the well's offset (section 9), so that assumption does not hold row by row here, and the standard errors are exact only under it.

MORE ROWS THAN COEFFICIENTS. Least squares needs n > p: with n = p the fit passes through every row and leaves no residual degree of freedom to estimate s. The engine refuses n <= p (section 3).

# SECTION 8: RMSE, MAE and R-squared on the test wells (owned by Associate m05)

RMSE = sqrt(sum (y - yhat)^2 / n) and MAE = sum |y - yhat| / n are in the target's unit (us/ft). R-squared = 1 - SSE / sum (y - reference)^2 is a fraction of the variation about a reference mean. The OLS fit of section 7 scored on its own training rows and on the three test wells:

| scored on | rows | RMSE (us/ft) | MAE (us/ft) | R-squared | reference mean (us/ft) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| training rows (fitted values) | 180 | 5.758010 | 4.810983 | 0.683457 | 105.883333 |
| test wells, about the test mean (default) | 90 | 4.282693 | 3.526103 | 0.815322 | 105.214444 |
| test wells, about the training mean (referenceMean) | 90 | 4.282693 | 3.526103 | 0.816151 | 105.883333 |

The default reference for a test R-squared is the mean of the TEST targets, as scikit-learn's r2_score; the basis says: "1 - SSE / sum (y - mean of these y)^2 (scikit-learn r2_score); negative when worse than that mean". Passing the training mean as `referenceMean` asks how much better the model does than predicting the training average for a new well, which is the out-of-sample convention; here the two read 0.815322 and 0.816151. Name the reference every time an R-squared is quoted.

The three rows of the first table and the worse-than-mean case all read RMSE above MAE. ONE LARGE MISS. Move only the first test row's prediction 20 us/ft higher and score the 90 test rows again: RMSE goes from 4.282693 to 4.977801 and MAE from 3.526103 to 3.748326, rises of 0.695109 and 0.222222 us/ft (derived). Squaring weighs the one large miss more.

A REFUSED METRICS CALL RETURNS NO METRIC. `regressionMetrics` refuses the WHOLE call when the sum of squares about its reference is zero, so a refused call carries no RMSE and no MAE either, although both are defined on those rows. Without `referenceMean` that is any test set whose targets are all equal, one row included; with `referenceMean` the refusal comes only when every target equals it:

# SECTION 19: Separation, and the exact test before any iteration (owned by Expert m02)

When a hyperplane in the features puts every PAY = 1 row on one side and every PAY = 0 row on the other, the log likelihood keeps rising as the coefficients grow and the maximum likelihood coefficients are infinite. The Ekene pay rule does exactly that on its own input: among the 106 rows with RT at or above 10 ohm.m, PAY is 1 precisely when PHIC is at least 0.16. Logistic on PHIC alone for those rows is refused before any iteration:

> y is completely separated by a linear combination of the features (every row lies strictly on its own class side of a hyperplane), so the maximum likelihood coefficients are infinite: add an L2 penalty (l2 > 0) or remove the separating feature

WITH A PENALTY the fit is finite. With l2 = 1 on the same rows: PHIC coefficient 0.817700 log odds per v/v, intercept 1.881712, 6 iterations, converged true, and the result reports `separation.type` complete. The penalty (l2 / 2) x sum b_j^2 on the non-intercept coefficients makes the objective bounded; the separation is still there, and the coefficient is set by the penalty rather than by the data.

At l2 = 0.1 the PHIC coefficient is 6.814258: a weaker penalty, a larger coefficient, and no finite limit as l2 goes to zero.

QUASI-COMPLETE SEPARATION: a hyperplane puts every row on its own side or ON the plane, with rows of both classes on the plane. The engine's own case (golden `logistic-quasi-separation`), X [[1],[2],[3],[3],[4],[5]], y [0,0,0,1,1,1]:

> y is quasi-completely separated by a linear combination of the features (every row lies on or on its own class side of a hyperplane, some exactly on it), so the maximum likelihood coefficients are infinite: add an L2 penalty (l2 > 0) or remove the separating feature

THE EXACT TEST. The engine decides separation BEFORE iterating, with two linear programmes on S, the design with each row multiplied by +1 for y = 1 and -1 for y = 0 and each column divided by its largest absolute value. Gordan's theorem: either some beta has S beta > 0 (complete separation) or some w >= 0 with sum 1 has S'w = 0; the engine solves the second as a linear programme, and infeasible means complete. Stiemke's theorem, tested only when Gordan's programme is feasible: either some w >= 1 has S'w = 0 (no separation) or the separation is quasi-complete. Each pair of alternatives is a theorem, so the decision is exact up to the linear programme's feasibility tolerance. The basis says: "dual linear programmes on the column-scaled, sign-flipped design S (lib/lp): COMPLETE when S'w = 0, sum w = 1, w >= 0 is infeasible (Gordan); otherwise QUASI-COMPLETE when S'w = 0, w >= 1 is infeasible (Stiemke), else none; infeasible means an artificial sum above 1e-7 after phase one".

The pay model of section 14 reported `separation.type` none, with the certificate: "weights w >= 1 with S'w = 0 (Stiemke)".

# SECTION 20: Convergence and its stated rule (owned by Expert m03)

Logistic regression has no closed form. The engine takes Newton-Raphson steps from beta = 0: each step solves the Newton system (the information matrix against the score) with `solveSPD`, and the fit stops when the largest absolute component of the FULL Newton step is at most `tol`, default 1.00e-10, or after `maxIter` updates, default 100. The trace of the pay fit of section 14:

| iteration | largest component of the full Newton step | log likelihood after the step | step halvings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.024456 | -46.305377 | 0 |
| 2 | 2.796103 | -30.214668 | 0 |
| 3 | 5.791631 | -25.899408 | 0 |
| 4 | 7.440994 | -24.740838 | 0 |
| 5 | 5.014085 | -24.519775 | 0 |
| 6 | 1.434323 | -24.507083 | 0 |
| 7 | 0.097613 | -24.507027 | 0 |
| 8 | 0.000448 | -24.507027 | 0 |
| 9 | 9.80e-9 | -24.507027 | 0 |
| 10 | 1.14e-13 | -24.507027 | 0 |

The last step, 1.14e-13, is the first at or below 1.00e-10. Near the solution Newton's method roughly squares the error each step, which is why the step sizes collapse over the last few iterations. The rule is inclusive: a step exactly at tol stops the fit.

WHEN A FIT DOES NOT CONVERGE the engine returns the result with `converged` false and a warning; it is no refusal. The same pay fit with maxIter 3: converged false, 3 iterations, RT coefficient 0.196989 against 0.241141 at convergence. The engine's words:

> did not converge in 3 updates: the last full Newton step had a largest component of 5.791630774699914, above tol 1e-10

A REHEARSAL ON THE EKENE WELLS, for the Expert capstone brief: the pay model (RHOB, NPHI, RT, with an intercept) on all 300 rows of the ten wells, stopped at maxIter 3, returns the NPHI coefficient -5.491841 log odds per v/v (RHOB -5.579090, RT 0.193336), converged false. The value is the third Newton iterate: it stops by maxIter, and the same call at tol 1.00e-3 returns it unchanged (asserted).

THE STOPPING RULE IS IN COEFFICIENT UNITS. A feature in very small units has a very large coefficient, and an absolute tol must be set to match. The engine's own case (golden `logistic-compressibility-per-pa`): rock compressibility in 1/Pa, a feature whose largest value is 8.52e-10 (golden), 40 rows. With the golden's tol 0.1 it converges in 6 iterations with the compressibility coefficient 6.83e+9; at the default tol 1.00e-10 it runs to 100 updates, converged false, and warns:

> did not converge in 100 updates: the last full Newton step had a largest component of 0.0000014834152759636627, above tol 1e-10

A tol of 0.1 on a coefficient near 6.83e+9 is a relative 1.46e-11 (derived). Rescaling the feature (to 1/GPa, say) is the other remedy. The basis says: "converged when the largest absolute component of the full Newton step is at most tol 0.1 (in coefficient units: set tol to the scale of the coefficients when features are in very small or very large units); at most maxIter 100 updates".

STEP HALVING. A step that lowers the penalised log likelihood by more than 1e-12 x (1 + |l|) is halved, up to 30 times. Convergence is judged on the FULL step before any halving, so a halved step can never fake convergence. The pay fit halved 0 times; the basis says: "Newton-Raphson (IRLS) from beta = 0; a step is halved while it lowers the penalised log likelihood by more than 1e-12 x (1 + |log likelihood|), up to 30 halvings".

# SECTION 25: Conventions that are choices, and what is not built (owned by Expert m06)

Every convention below is a choice the engine states in its basis. Each has a real alternative in common use; name the choice when a number from this engine is compared with one from another tool.

| convention | this engine | a common alternative | why the engine chose it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scaler divisor | population SD (n) | sample SD (n - 1), which the data quality course uses for its z-score | ridge lambda then equals scikit-learn alpha on the same features |
| random draws | mulberry32(seed), Fisher-Yates from the end | a library generator (numpy, scikit-learn) | one canonical generator across the platform; no library reproduces its draws, so splits are compared by well list |
| group names | sorted by UTF-16 code unit before the shuffle | natural order (EKENE-2 before EKENE-10) | a stated order that needs no parsing of names |
| k-fold | round robin over shuffled groups: balances the number of wells | scikit-learn GroupKFold: balances rows and takes no seed | seeded and reproducible; every well tested once |
| test R-squared | about the mean of the test targets | about the training mean (referenceMean) | matches scikit-learn r2_score; the option is there |
| OLS refusal | scaled condition number above 1.00e+8 | fit anything and warn | at 1.00e+8 the worst-case bound reaches the coefficient itself |
| logistic stop | largest full Newton step at most 1.00e-10, in coefficient units | a relative or column-scaled rule | the full step cannot be faked by halving; the unit is stated in the basis |
| separation | decided exactly by two linear programmes before iterating, refused at l2 = 0 | iterate and watch the coefficients grow | a separated maximum likelihood fit has no finite answer to print |
| F1 | 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN) | the harmonic mean of precision and recall | equal wherever both are defined, and defined in more cases |
| ROC start | threshold null | scikit-learn prints infinity | JSON has no infinity |
| log loss clip | eps = 1.00e-15 | the float dtype epsilon (scikit-learn 1.9) | a stated constant |
| permutation drop | loss of score, positive when the feature matters | the raw permuted score | one sign for every metric |
| learning curve size | counted in wells | counted in rows | rows of one well are not independent |

WHAT IS NOT BUILT. The engine fits linear models only (least squares, ridge, binary logistic). There is no tree, forest, boosting, neural network, neighbour rule or clustering (clustering and facies are the electrofacies course's), no multiclass logistic, no nonlinear feature expansion, no prediction interval and no sampling of inputs (uncertainty is its own course's), no imputation (a missing value is refused), no stratified split and no time-ordered split (forecasting is its own course's), and no search over lambda or features: the course runs the folds and prints the scores it compared.

# SECTION 26: Vocabulary this course legislates before a word is written (owned by Associate m01 and Professional m01 and Expert m01)

Six words in this course carry a narrower meaning than they have in conversation or elsewhere in the academy. The rule for each is binding on every lesson, bank question, key truth and panel.

| word | what it can mean elsewhere | the rule here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| test | any check | rows the model was not fitted on; in this course a whole well held out, unless the text names a random-row split |
| standard deviation | either divisor | name the divisor: the scaler uses the population SD (n); the data quality course's z-score uses the sample SD (n - 1) |
| R-squared | one number per fit | name the rows and the reference mean: the training fit, the test rows about their own mean, or the test rows about the training mean |
| separation | any split of the data | the logistic property: a hyperplane puts the classes on their own sides (complete) or on or beside it (quasi-complete). A split is a split |
| importance | how much a feature matters in the rock | the loss of score when the feature is shuffled, for this fitted model, on these rows, with this seed and these repeats |
| machine learning | any automated judgement | a fitted statistical model named by its method (least squares, ridge, logistic); no lesson calls a model artificial intelligence |

No P label is used in this course. A probability from the logistic model is the probability of PAY = 1 for that row.
