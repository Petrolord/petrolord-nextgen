# SECTION 2: The five by five matrix, the product, and the four bands (owned by Associate m02)

- The scale runs from 1 to 5 on each axis, likelihood and impact. A score is the product of the two levels.
- The bands, as the engine carries them, highest first. Each band is found by its LOWER edge only:
| band | lower edge | upper edge as written |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Critical" | 15 | 25 |
| "High" | 10 | 14 |
| "Medium" | 5 | 9 |
| "Low" | 1 | 4 |

All twenty five cells, likelihood down the side and impact across the top, each cell the score and its band:
| likelihood \ impact | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 5 Medium | 10 High | 15 Critical | 20 Critical | 25 Critical |
| 4 | 4 Low | 8 Medium | 12 High | 16 Critical | 20 Critical |
| 3 | 3 Low | 6 Medium | 9 Medium | 12 High | 15 Critical |
| 2 | 2 Low | 4 Low | 6 Medium | 8 Medium | 10 High |
| 1 | 1 Low | 2 Low | 3 Low | 4 Low | 5 Medium |

- Cells in each band, counted from the grid above: "Critical" 6, "High" 4, "Medium" 7, "Low" 8.
- The grid holds 14 distinct scores. Of the whole numbers from 1 to 25, 11 are in no cell, because no two whole levels on this scale multiply to them: 7, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23, 24.

Scores read against the bands, including the edge either side of each band and scores no cell of the grid holds:
| score | band |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | "None" |
| 1 | "Low" |
| 4 | "Low" |
| 5 | "Medium" |
| 9 | "Medium" |
| 10 | "High" |
| 14 | "High" |
| 15 | "Critical" |
| 25 | "Critical" |
| 26 | "Critical" |
| 4.5 | "Low" |
| 14.5 | "High" |
| -3 | "None" |

- A score above the grid still bands: the band is found by the lower edge alone, so the upper edge printed in the band table is a label rather than a limit. A score of zero or below reads "None", the one band that means no score.

What the scale accepts as a level, each probe scored against the other axis:
| the likelihood given | as | impact | score | band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| two whole levels | 3 | 4 | 12 | "High" |
| two whole levels written as text | "3" | "4" | 12 | "High" |
| a level with spaces around it | " 3 " | 4 | 12 | "High" |
| a whole level written as text with a decimal point | "3.0" | 4 | 12 | "High" |
| the value true | true | 5 | 5 | "Medium" |
| a zero likelihood | 0 | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| a likelihood of 6 | 6 | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| a likelihood of 2.5 | 2.5 | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| a likelihood of 3.5 written as text | "3.5" | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| a blank likelihood | "" | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| no likelihood at all | null | 4 | 0 | "None" |
| a negative likelihood | -2 | 4 | 0 | "None" |

- The scale is five WHOLE levels. A fraction is off the scale and unscored, the same as a level of 6 or a blank: the engine returns a score of 0 and the band "None" rather than guessing a level. A whole number written as text or with a decimal point is still that level.

# SECTION 3: Inherent and residual, the per-axis fallback, and appetite (owned by Associate m03)

- The inherent score is the risk before any control. The residual score is the risk with its controls in place, scored from the residual likelihood and the residual impact.
- A residual axis that has not been assessed FALLS BACK to the inherent level on that axis alone. Null, an absent key and a blank form field all mean "not assessed". A residual axis that WAS assessed with a value off the scale does not fall back: it leaves the residual unscored.

| residual probe (inherent likelihood 4, impact 5) | residual likelihood given | residual impact given | residual score | residual band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| both residual axes assessed | 2 | 3 | 6 | "Medium" |
| neither residual axis assessed | null | null | 20 | "Critical" |
| residual likelihood only, impact left blank | 2 | "" | 10 | "High" |
| residual impact only, likelihood left blank | "" | 3 | 12 | "High" |
| both residual axes left blank | "" | "" | 20 | "Critical" |
| a residual likelihood of 2.5 | 2.5 | 3 | 0 | "None" |
| a residual likelihood of 7 | 7 | 3 | 0 | "None" |

- Appetite compares the RESIDUAL score with the risk's own target. The engine has three answers: "Within appetite", "Above appetite", "Not set".
| appetite probe | residual score | target | appetite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| residual below the target | 6 | 8 | "Within appetite" |
| residual equal to the target | 8 | 8 | "Within appetite" |
| residual above the target | 9 | 8 | "Above appetite" |
| no target set | 6 | null | "Not set" |
| a target of zero | 6 | 0 | "Not set" |
| a residual that cannot be scored | 0 | 8 | "Not set" |

- A residual EQUAL to the target is within appetite. With no target, or a target of zero, or a residual that cannot be scored, the answer is "Not set": the engine declines to report a pass it has no basis for.

- A calendar date is read at LOCAL midnight from its leading YYYY-MM-DD, and so is the as-of date, so a whole number of days separates any two of them in every time zone. Whole days from the as-of date, 2026-10-01:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the as-of date itself | "2026-10-01" | 2026-10-01 | 0 |
| the day before | "2026-09-30" | 2026-09-30 | -1 |
| the day after | "2026-10-02" | 2026-10-02 | 1 |
| the last day of October | "2026-10-31" | 2026-10-31 | 30 |
| the first day of November | "2026-11-01" | 2026-11-01 | 31 |
| the first day of the next year | "2027-01-01" | 2027-01-01 | 92 |
| a date one year earlier | "2025-10-01" | 2025-10-01 | -365 |

| 30 February, a date that does not exist | "2026-02-30" | null | null |

# SECTION 7: A change and its stages: the legal moves and the final stages (owned by Professional m01)

- The stages, in workflow order: "Draft", "Screening", "Review", "Approval", "Implementation", "Closed", "Rejected", "Cancelled".
- Active, still live work: "Draft", "Screening", "Review", "Approval", "Implementation". In effect, on the facility: "Implementation", "Closed". Terminal: "Closed", "Rejected", "Cancelled".
- Change types: "Permanent", "Temporary", "Emergency". The types that must carry an expiry date: "Temporary", "Emergency".

| from | legal next stages |
| --- | --- |
| "Draft" | "Screening", "Cancelled" |
| "Screening" | "Review", "Rejected", "Cancelled", "Draft" |
| "Review" | "Approval", "Rejected", "Cancelled", "Screening" |
| "Approval" | "Implementation", "Rejected", "Cancelled", "Review" |
| "Implementation" | "Closed", "Cancelled" |
| "Closed" | none |
| "Rejected" | none |
| "Cancelled" | none |

- Only "Approval" leads to Implementation. 3 stages lead nowhere ("Closed", "Rejected", "Cancelled"), and a change can step back from Screening, Review and Approval to the stage before.

Moves the engine refuses, with its own words:
- REFUSED, Review straight to Closed: A change in Review can only move to Approval, Rejected, Cancelled, Screening.
- REFUSED, Draft straight to Approval: A change in Draft can only move to Screening, Cancelled.
- REFUSED, Implementation back to Approval: A change in Implementation can only move to Closed, Cancelled.
- REFUSED, a Closed change moved anywhere: A closed change is final.
- REFUSED, a Rejected change moved anywhere: A rejected change is final.
- ALLOWED, Draft to Screening.

# SECTION 8: Approval levels: one signature a level, and a rejection that stops the gate (owned by Professional m02)

- Levels come from the approval rows themselves: every distinct level present must carry at least one Approved row, and no row at any level may be Rejected. A row with no level counts as level 1.
- Approval statuses: "Pending", "Approved", "Rejected", "Delegated".

| approval set | levels | outstanding | rejected rows | complete |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| three levels, the first signed | 1, 2, 3 | 2, 3 | 0 | no |
| three levels, all signed | 1, 2, 3 | none | 0 | yes |
| two signers at level 2, one of them signed | 1, 2 | none | 0 | yes |
| every level signed and one rejection | 1, 2 | none | 1 | no |
| a row with no level at all | 1 | none | 0 | yes |
| levels 1 and 3, no level 2 | 1, 3 | 3 | 0 | no |
| a delegated row | 1 | 1 | 0 | no |
| no approval rows | none | none | 0 | no |

- A Delegated row is not an Approved row, so its level stays outstanding. Two signers at one level need only one of them to sign. A rejection anywhere makes the set incomplete even when every level has an Approved row.

# SECTION 10: Actions and the two gates: into Implementation and into Closed (owned by Professional m04)

- Action types: "Pre-implementation", "Implementation", "Post-implementation". Action statuses: "Open", "In progress", "Complete", "Cancelled". Complete and Cancelled actions are finished; Open and In progress are not.
- Into Implementation: every approval level signed with no rejection (an Emergency change needs only its first level), no Pre-implementation action unfinished, and a Temporary or Emergency change needs a READABLE expiry date. Into Closed: no Implementation or Post-implementation action unfinished, and an Emergency change needs every level signed.

- REFUSED, ES-01 into Implementation as it stands, levels 2 and 3 unsigned: Approval levels 2 and 3 have not signed yet.
- REFUSED, ES-01 into Implementation with every level signed and one Pre-implementation action still Open: 1 pre-implementation action still open. They exist to be done before the change goes in.
- ALLOWED, ES-01 into Implementation with every level signed and its actions finished.
- REFUSED, a change with no approval rows at all: No approvers have been assigned, so there is nothing to approve. Add the approval levels this change needs.
- REFUSED, a change with every level signed and one rejection: An approver has rejected this change. It cannot be implemented.
- REFUSED, a Temporary change whose expiry reads "after the turnaround": A temporary change needs an expiry date before it is implemented. Without one it is a permanent change nobody decided to make.
- REFUSED, a Temporary change with no expiry date: A temporary change needs an expiry date before it is implemented. Without one it is a permanent change nobody decided to make.
- ALLOWED, a Temporary change with a readable expiry and every level signed.
- REFUSED, ES-02 into Closed with its Post-implementation action In progress: 1 implementation or post-implementation action still open.
- ALLOWED, ES-02 into Closed once that action is Complete.
- ALLOWED, ES-02 into Cancelled, which no action blocks.

# SECTION 11: Temporary and emergency change: expiry, the lead, and ratification (owned by Professional m05)

- REFUSED, an Emergency change in Implementation into Closed with level 2 still unsigned: Approval level 2 has not ratified this emergency change. It cannot close until every level has signed.
