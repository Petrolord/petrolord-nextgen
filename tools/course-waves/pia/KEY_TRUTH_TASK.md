# EC7 Petroleum Industry Act 2021 & Nigerian Fiscal Terms: the key-truth writer's task

Read `BRIEF.md` first. Every number here is quoted from `digest.txt`, the only
teaching truth for this course. The audit, the engine's FINDINGS record, the
oracle and its golden, and the engine's source comments are PROVENANCE.

## TWO JOBS

1. **The key truths.** One sentence a learner should be able to say afterwards,
   with the number that makes it checkable.
2. **The second reader of every bank, before the migrations.** Read prompt,
   options, explanation, digest and lesson for every question, one tier at a
   time, and CALL THE ENGINE (through a calculator panel, or node on the
   vendored `packages/engines/engines/economics/cashflow.ts` through the wave's
   `pia_engine.mjs`) for every keyed figure. Swap roles where the explanation
   supports a distractor.

A figure of sixteen or more significant digits is written comma-grouped, as the
digest prints it, and a quoted text comes from the digest section the tier owns
(the royalty texts and the texts behind the stack are Associate material).

## THE DEFECT CLASSES TO READ FOR

1. **A distractor that became true** under a setting the question did not rule
   out (the other base year, the other stated new-lease rate, another deep
   offshore reading, another framework override, another working interest).
2. **An open reading keyed as the law.** The base year, the new-lease rate and
   the deep offshore rate are never keyed on one reading presented as settled.
3. **A concept-only provision keyed to a number** the engine does not compute.
4. **A wrong failure-mode gloss.** "Refused" said of a result with a note.
5. **A false superlative** ("always", "never") where the digest shows one case.
6. **An exam question that near-duplicates a module question** (audit at
   Jaccard 0.45).
7. **A sentence characterising a relationship nobody computed.**
8. **The answer printed before the work** in the prompt or a table above it.
9. **A capstone name, term, row or value** anywhere.
10. **The answer-length tell**, and three distractors opening alike.
11. **Printed alike keyed as equal.**
12. **A boundary stated globally.** Each rule draws its own (digest section 23):
    a tranche edge belongs to the tranche below it, a price at the low benchmark
    pays nothing, a year that ends exactly on the allowance cap has no barrel
    after it.
13. **A message quoted inexactly**, or a quoted refusal carrying the sentence
    about the platform switch.
14. **A digest section number in learner-visible text.**
15. **The regulatory rule broken**: a law named without its edition, a
    licensed text quoted, a figure the digest does not print.
16. **Repair history**: a sentence about former engine behaviour.
