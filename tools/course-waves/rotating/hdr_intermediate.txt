-- What this tier owns: THE SUCTION SIDE, THE CHANGES, AND THE SECOND MACHINE.
-- A duty point that works on paper fails in three ways and this tier is all
-- three. It fails on suction, where NPSH available is built from the real
-- suction side and a margin over required is not the same thing as adequacy.
-- It fails on change, where a speed change follows the affinity laws exactly
-- and a trim does not. And it fails on the fluid, because a catalogue curve
-- is a water curve.
--
-- THE ENGINE FACT THIS TIER OWNS. Applying an affinity law to a duty point
-- does not give you a new duty point. The machine moves and the system curve
-- stays where it was, so the scaled point is off the system curve by a
-- visible head. The tier ends with two pumps in parallel delivering far less
-- than twice one pump, which is the result the engine exists to make visible.
--
-- HELD FOR LITERATURE, taught as a limit and never graded: the NPSH margin
-- rule is the larger of 3 ft and 35 percent of required, and those two change
-- places at a required NPSH of 8.571428571 ft, which the digest finds by
-- bisecting the engine rather than by quoting anybody. The trim shortfall
-- model and the Hydraulic Institute viscosity correction are taught here and
-- graded nowhere, which is why the capstone grades a SPEED change and states
-- no kinematic viscosity and no required NPSH at all.
--
-- The only numbers that travel from this tier to the next are none: the
-- Expert tier changes fluid, machine and method together.
