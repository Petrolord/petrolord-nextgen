THE PETROLEUM INDUSTRY ACT 2021:

PIA s.94(8)(b): In the marginal field section of the Act, a farm-out is an agreement that lets a third party explore for and produce petroleum in the holder's licence or lease area.
> "(b) “farm-out” means an agreement between the holder of a petroleum mining lease or petroleum prospecting licence and a third party, which permits the third party to explore, prospect, win, work and carry away any petroleum encountered in a licence or lease area" (PIA s.94(8)(b))

PIA s.94(4)(b): The holder of a marginal field may farm out the discovery with the consent of the Commission.
> "(b) with the consent of the Commission and on terms and conditions as the Commission may approve under regulations, farm out the discovery ; or" (PIA s.94(4)(b))

PIA s.94(5): The Commission's consent to a marginal field farm-out requires the farmee to present a field development plan.
> "(5) The consent of the Commission to the farm-out of a marginal field under subsection (4) (b) shall, amongst others, be subject to the farmee presenting a field development plan" (PIA s.94(5))

PIA s.95(2): The Minister consents to an assignment on the Commission's recommendation.
> "(2) The consent of the Minister under subsection (1) shall be granted upon the recommendation of the Commission." (PIA s.95(2))

PIA s.95(3): A change of control of a licence holder counts as an assignment.
> "(3) For the purpose of subsection (1), a change of control in the holder of a licence or lease under subsection (1) shall be deemed to be an assignment." (PIA s.95(3))

PIA s.95(4): A holder that wishes to transfer its interest applies to the Commission in the prescribed format.
> "shall make an application for approval of the transfer to the Commission in the format prescribed by the Commission," (PIA s.95(4))

PIA s.95(7)(b): If the Minister does not respond within 60 working days of the Commission's recommendation, consent is deemed granted.
> "(b) no response on the application has been received within 60 working days from the receipt of the recommendation of the Commission, the consent of the Minister under subsection (1) shall be deemed to have been granted." (PIA s.95(7)(b))

PIA s.95(11)(a): One condition the Minister may set is that the transferee is a company incorporated in Nigeria.
> "(a) is a company incorporated in Nigeria ;" (PIA s.95(11)(a))

PIA s.95(12): The Commission sets by regulation a fee on each assignment, a percentage of the value of the transaction and not tax deductible.
> "(12) The Commission shall make regulation to prescribe for payment of fees as a condition for any transaction under subsection (1), which fee shall be based on a percentage of the value of the transaction and shall not be tax deductible." (PIA s.95(12))

PIA s.95(14): A change of control is the acquisition of more than 50 percent of the holder's voting power by persons acting together.
> "“change of control” means any person or persons acting jointly or in concert, to acquire direct or indirect beneficial ownership of a percentage of the voting power of the outstanding voting securities of the holder, by contract or otherwise, that exceeds 50% at any time." (PIA s.95(14))

PIA s.95(15): A petroleum exploration licence is assigned only with the prior written consent of the Commission.
> "(15) A holder of a petroleum exploration licence shall not assign, novate or transfer his licence or any right, power or interest without prior written consent of the Commission." (PIA s.95(15))

PIA s.233(10): A farm-out agreement must provide for a decommissioning and abandonment plan funded by the incoming parties in whole or in part.
> "(10) Where the licensee or lessee is a party to a farm out agreement with one or more third parties, a decommissioning and abandonment plan funded in whole or in part by the applicable third parties shall be provided for in the applicable farm out agreement." (PIA s.233(10))

PIA s.264(f): For hydrocarbon tax, fees paid for assigning rights to another party are not deductible.
> "bonuses or fees paid for renewing petroleum mining lease or petroleum prospecting licence or marginal field or fees paid for assigning rights to another party ;" (PIA s.264(f))

PIA s.302(12)(c): For companies income tax, fees paid for assigning rights to another party are not deductible either.
> "signature bonuses or fees paid for renewing petroleum mining lease or petroleum prospecting licence or fees paid for assigning rights to another party including for marginal fields ;" (PIA s.302(12)(c))

THE NIGERIAN UPSTREAM PETROLEUM (ASSIGNMENT OF INTERESTS) REGULATIONS, 2024:

AOI Regulations 2024, the gazette cover (S.I. No. 67): The gazette cover lists the instrument under a shorter title.
> "67 Nigeria Upstream Petroleum (Assignment of Interest) Regulations, 2024" (AOI Regulations 2024, the gazette cover (S.I. No. 67))

AOI Regulations 2024, the arrangement of regulations: The arrangement at the front numbers the last four regulations from 22 to 25.
> "22. General provisions 23. Revocation 24. Interpretation 25. Citation" (AOI Regulations 2024, the arrangement of regulations)

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 23: The body's regulation 23 is on guidelines.
> "23. The Commission may from time to time, make and publish guidelines" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 23)

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 26 (the citation, as the body numbers it): The body numbers its citation regulation 26.
> "26. These Regulations may be cited as the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 26 (the citation, as the body numbers it))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 3(3): A change in the control of a holder needs the Minister's prior written consent, unless the holder is listed on a public exchange.
> "(3) A change in the control of a company that holds an interest in a licence or lease, (a “holder”), other than a licensee, lessee or indirect controller of a licence or lease listed on a public exchange, shall require prior written consent of the Minister." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 3(3))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(2): The application for the Minister's consent is made to the Commission.
> "(2) Application for consent of the Minister under the Act and these Regulations shall be made to the Commission in the manner prescribed in these Regulations," (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(2))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(4)(b): The notification of intention states the reason for the assignment, the method of the transaction and its expected benefits.
> "stating the reason for the intended assignment, the method intended to be used for the conduct of the transaction, and possible technical and economic benefits derivable from the transaction" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(4)(b))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(7): The Commission answers the notification within 15 working days, or it is deemed approved.
> "(7) The Commission shall within 15 working days from date of receipt of the notification of intention under sub-regulation (4)(a) of this regulation, approve or disapprove the transaction, failing which the application is deemed approved" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(7))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 16(c): For a petroleum exploration licence the consent is the Commission's.
> "(c) a change in control of a company that holds an interest in a PEL shall require prior written consent of the Commission." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 16(c))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 18(3): The Commission answers a PEL assignment application in writing within 60 working days.
> "the Commission shall communicate the reason for the refusal or approval of an assignment of an interest in a PEL in writing to the applicant within 60 working days of the receipt of the application." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 18(3))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(2): The Minister's consent costs seven per cent of the value of the transaction: two per cent processing fee and five per cent premium.
> "(2) The consent of the Minister in respect of an assignment pursuant to these Regulations shall be by the payment of seven per cent of the value of the transaction, comprising two percent processing fee and five per cent premium," (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(2))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(2), proviso: An intra group transfer pays the two per cent processing fee alone.
> "provided that consent to an Assignment in an intra group transfer shall only be subject to the payment of two per cent processing fee." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(2), proviso)

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(3)(a): The value of the transaction is either the amount payable to the assignor stated in the application or contract.
> "(3) The value of transaction pursuant to sub-regulation (2) of this regulation shall be by either the amount: (a) payable to the Assignor, as stated in the application or transaction contract; or" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(3)(a))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(3)(b): Or it is an amount the Commission prescribes from its metrics for good and valuable consideration.
> "(b) prescribed by the Commission, using the metrics for the determination of good and valuable consideration for the asset at the relevant time." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(3)(b))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(5): The processing fee, consent fee and premium are not tax deductible.
> "(5) Any payment made as processing fee, consent fee, or premium under these Regulations shall not be tax deductible." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(5))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(6): Consent is not granted until the application and processing fees are paid in full.
> "(6) The consent of the Minister and the Commission shall not be granted until the appropriate application and processing fees have been fully paid." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(6))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(7): The assignor pays the fee within 90 days of the notification of the grant of consent.
> "(7) Every Assignor shall be required to pay an applicable fee to an account provided by the Commission within 90 days of notification of the grant of the relevant consent" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(7))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(8): An assignor that has not paid in full within the 90 days has 30 days more.
> "the Assignor shall have an additional 30 days within which to pay or complete payment." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(8))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(9): After the 30 days, a surcharge of 0.01 per cent of the fee a day runs for 90 days, after which the consent is deemed withdrawn.
> "shall impose a surcharge of 0.01% of the stipulated amount per day on a straight-line basis for 90 days failing which the consent is deemed withdrawn." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(9))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Value of the Transaction": The interpretation regulation defines the value of the transaction again, as the amount the Commission determines the assignor receives.
> "“Value of the Transaction” means the amount determined by the Commission to be the value receivable by the Assignor for an Assignment," (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Value of the Transaction")

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Assignment" (d): An assignment includes one by a contractor or joint venture partner under any upstream arrangement.
> "(d) assignment by a contractor or joint venture partner under an upstream petroleum arrangement, including joint venture arrangement, production sharing contract, production sharing agreement, service contract, under a sole risk award or a marginal field." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Assignment" (d))

AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Petroleum Agreement": A farm-in agreement is one of the petroleum agreements the Regulations name.
> "“Petroleum Agreement” includes any or all of the following documents and or arrangements including, production sharing contracts, joint operating agreements, joint venture agreements, farm-in-agreements or any other document" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Petroleum Agreement")

HM REVENUE AND CUSTOMS, OIL TAXATION MANUAL (OPEN GOVERNMENT LICENCE V3.0):

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30020: A farm out is the disposal of a licence interest by its owner for consideration given by the incoming party.
> "A Farm Out involves the disposal of a licence interest by the owner - the Farmer Out in return for consideration given by the Farmer In." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30020)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021: On unexplored acreage the consideration is mainly the incoming party paying future costs such as drilling.
> "For relatively unexplored acreage consideration will generally consist wholly, or mainly, of the Farmer in undertaking to bear future costs (e.g. drilling)." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021: In a farm in the interest is assigned before the work is done.
> "The assignment is normally made, subject to government consent, before the work is undertaken and is called a Farm in." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021: In an earn-in the work is completed before the assignment.
> "In contrast, an agreement under which the work obligation is to be completed before the assignment is generally referred to as an earn-in." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021: The incoming party may also reimburse sunk costs in cash for the share it acquires.
> "The Farmer in may also pay a cash reimbursement to the farmer out for sunk costs relating to the proportionate interest acquired," (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30022: In a development carry the incoming party pays the development costs of the retained interest.
> "The Farmer in may therefore agree to “carry” the Farmer out by meeting the subsequent development costs relating to the farmer out’s retained interest." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30022)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30022: Those costs are usually recovered, with an interest element, from the production of the retained interest.
> "Those costs, probably with an interest element, will normally be recovered from the proceeds of the proportion of the production accruing to the Farmer out’s retained interest." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30022)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30023: An interest in a producing field is usually sold for cash or shares.
> "The consideration for the disposal of an interest in a producing field is generally in the form of cash or shares." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30023)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30048: A work programme obligation covers the incoming party paying its own share and the outgoing party's share of the work.
> "The phrase “obligation to undertake” is intended to cover all cases where the farmer in commits himself to bear his and the farmer out’s share of the costs of exploration or appraisal work" (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30048)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30081: A reimbursement of earlier costs is cash consideration for the interest.
> "The reimbursement should therefore be treated as cash consideration for the licence interest acquired." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30081)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30131: A right to future benefits is valued by their chance as well as their size.
> "The value of the right will need to reflect the degree of probability that future benefits will accrue as well as their extent" (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30131)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18320: The same manual calls the incoming party the increasing interest party, the farmer-in or the farmee.
> "The increasing interest party (farmer-in or farmee) undertaking a work programme at his own expense," (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18320)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360: A development carry is usually recovered with an addition for simple interest out of the carried party's production.
> "the increasing interest party may also stipulate in the agreement that he recover his costs, usually including an addition representing simple interest, out of production relating to the reducing interest party’s licence interest." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360)

HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360: The moment the carry is recovered is called payback.
> "When his costs are recovered this is referred to as payback." (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360)

THE ACT'S DEFINITION SITS IN ITS MARGINAL FIELD SECTION. # SECTION 6: What is graded, where the practicals run, and what is never graded (owned by Associate m01 l05 and Expert m06)

EVERY GRADED NUMBER IN THIS COURSE IS A RETURN VALUE OF THIS ENGINE ON FIXED INPUTS. A capstone field, a question key and a panel figure are each computed by a function in the table of section 1 on deal terms written down in advance. No graded figure comes from the Monte Carlo of riskSharing, so the same terms give the same number on any machine, and there is exactly one right answer.

THE PRACTICALS RUN IN THE COURSE'S OWN CALCULATOR PANELS. This is an engine course with no Suite app. Each tier has a calculator panel that calls this same vendored engine: the earning calculator (Associate), the deal calculator (Professional) and the valuation calculator (Expert). A learner types or pastes their own deal terms; the panel prints what the engine returns, every refusal in the engine's own words, and the reasons beside each figure.

WHAT A CAPSTONE STATES. Each capstone runs its own synthetic deal, which this digest does not print, and states every term a figure depends on: the parties and their participating interests, the farmor and the farminee, each event's gross cost, share paid, interest earned and cap, the vesting rule and the events completed, the cash bonus and past costs, the chance of success, the well costs and the success-case value, the signals and their cost, the interest priced and its basis and price, the carry's share, uplift and recovery share, the back-in target and costs, and the value of the transaction with its dates. Each graded figure is quoted to six decimals as the panel prints it.

WHAT IS NEVER GRADED. No graded figure depends on a reading the engine states (section 22): every capstone field is the same number under each reading the engine takes and under the alternative it names. No graded figure is a Monte Carlo draw: the chance of a loss and the low and high cases of section 19 are taught and never graded.

WHAT A COMPUTED FIGURE DOES NOT SAY. An EMV is what the stated chance and payoffs produce; a break-even is the point at which a stated EMV crosses 0; a value per percent is a stated value divided by 100; a transaction ratio is a stated price over a computed value. # SECTION 22: The readings the engine states, where each acts and what it would move (owned by Expert m05 l01)

Where a text leaves a choice open, the engine takes one and states it in its basis. The course quotes each reading verbatim and grades no figure that depends on it: every capstone field is bit-identical under each reading and under the alternative named beside it.

READING ONE: THE VALUATION TIMING. The engine's basis (deal-ekene), verbatim:
> the success-case value is at the valuation date; well costs, bonus, reimbursement and fees fall at the valuation date, undiscounted
It acts on every EMV of section 14: a deal whose well costs, bonus, reimbursement and fees fell a year after the valuation date would discount them at the success-case rate and give different EMVs. The Penn State problem has no time dimension at all. The engine values at one date and says so.

READING TWO: THE DAY COUNT OF REG. 19(7). The engine's basis (fee-ekene), verbatim:
> days from the notification of the consent to the payment: within 90 on time, 30 more of grace, then 0.01% of the fee a day straight line for up to 90 days, after which the consent is deemed withdrawn (reg. 19(7) to (9))
The regulation says "within 90 days of notification" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(7)). The engine counts from the notification date to the payment date without the notification day; counting that day as well would move a payment on day 90 into the grace days. It acts only at the day boundaries of section 17.

READING THREE: THE NINETIETH SURCHARGE DAY. The regulation imposes the surcharge "for 90 days failing which the consent is deemed withdrawn" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(9)). The engine charges the ninetieth surcharge day and deems the consent withdrawn from the ninety-first (fee-day-210 and fee-day-211); the other reading would withdraw it on the ninetieth.

READING FOUR: HOW A SIMPLE-INTEREST UPLIFT IS PAID. The engine's basis (devcarry-ekene-simple-ot18360), verbatim:
> 8% a year simple interest on the outstanding principal (one year per ledger period); accrued interest earns none; a recovery pays the accrued interest first, then the principal; a year's new cost earns none in its own year (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360: costs recovered "usually including an addition representing simple interest")
HMRC's manual says only that the recovery usually includes "an addition representing simple interest" (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18360); it does not say whether a recovery pays the interest or the principal first. The engine pays the accrued interest first, then the principal, and charges interest on the outstanding principal only; paying principal first would leave more principal outstanding to earn interest. It acts only when a carry states uplift type "simple".

THE VALUE OF THE TRANSACTION IS A STATED INPUT. The engine's basis (fee-ekene), verbatim:
> the value of the transaction is a stated input: the sum payable to the Assignor stated in the application or contract, or an amount the Commission determines (reg. 19(3)); the engine does not decide which consideration of a farm-out counts
It is no reading: the engine takes the amount and its source from the caller and charges the gazetted rates on it (section 16).

# SECTION 23: Reference texts and their quirks: one table with two numbers, a regulation numbered twice, one value defined twice (owned by Expert m05)

A TABLE WITH TWO NUMBERS. The Penn State EME 801 page labels its payoff table as Table 6.1 where it first prints it and refers to it as Table 10.1 when it computes the EMVs and the value at risk from the same figures (text, Penn State EME 801, Lesson 6, Expected Monetary Value and Value at Risk (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), numbers only). The course cites the page and its printed figures and calls the table by its first label.

THE REGULATIONS NUMBER THEIR LAST PROVISIONS TWICE. The arrangement at the front lists "22. General provisions 23. Revocation 24. Interpretation 25. Citation" (AOI Regulations 2024, the arrangement of regulations), while the body prints regulation 23 as guidelines ("23. The Commission may from time to time, make and publish guidelines", AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 23) and numbers the citation 26 ("26. These Regulations may be cited as the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum", AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 26 (the citation, as the body numbers it)). A citation of "reg. 25" follows the arrangement; the body prints no regulation of that number.
THE TITLE ON THE COVER. The gazette cover lists the instrument as "67 Nigeria Upstream Petroleum (Assignment of Interest) Regulations, 2024" (AOI Regulations 2024, the gazette cover (S.I. No. 67)); the citation names it the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum (Assignment of Interests) Regulations, 2024, the name this course uses.

ONE VALUE DEFINED TWICE. Reg. 19(3) makes the value of the transaction the amount payable to the assignor stated in the application or contract, or an amount the Commission prescribes; reg. 24 defines it as "the amount determined by the Commission to be the value receivable by the Assignor" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Value of the Transaction"). The engine takes the amount and its source as stated inputs (section 16).

PAID BEFORE AND PAID AFTER. Reg. 19(6) says consent is not granted until the application and processing fees are paid in full (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(6)); reg. 19(7) gives the assignor 90 days from the notification of the grant to pay the applicable fee (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 19(7)). The engine applies the timing of reg. 19(7) to (9) to the whole seven per cent and computes no application fee (reg. 19(1) leaves it to other regulations).

ONE PARTY, FIVE NAMES. The engine says farmor and farminee. The Act says "farmee" (PIA s.94(5)); HMRC's manual says Farmer Out and Farmer In (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30020) and, on another page, the increasing interest party, "farmer-in or farmee" (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18320). HMRC also separates a farm in, assigned before the work, from an earn-in, assigned after it (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021; HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30021); the engine's two vesting rules are those two orders.

A UK MANUAL FOR CONCEPTS. The HMRC Oil Taxation Manual is United Kingdom tax guidance published under the Open Government Licence; the course quotes it for what a farm in, an earn-in, a reimbursement and a development carry are, and applies none of its tax rules.

# SECTION 28: Vocabulary this course legislates before a word is written (owned by Associate m01 and Professional m01 and Expert m01)
