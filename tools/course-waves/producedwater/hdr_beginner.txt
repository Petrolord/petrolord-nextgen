-- FC7 ASSOCIATE DEEP SEED: Produced Water Treatment, beginner tier.
--
-- Six modules, 26 lessons, 132 questions (15 per module bank plus a 42
-- question final exam). The structure row is GENERATED from the tier's
-- manifest.json rather than hand typed.
--
-- What this tier owns: THE WATER, THE OIL AND THE GRAVITY FRONT END. What this
-- engine answers and the three ways it declines to; the water viscosity and
-- the brine density out of a temperature and a salinity, and the crude density
-- out of an API gravity; the droplets as a log-normal distribution in volume on
-- a grid the module reports back; the rise balance and the creeping flow band
-- it is honest in; an API 421 basin and a plate pack; and the UZERE stream read
-- end to end, down to a verdict the engine withholds.
--
-- THE ENGINE FACT THIS TIER OWNS. Oil in water is a DISTRIBUTION and not a
-- concentration, and a cut size is computed from the equipment and the fluids,
-- never looked up. The same device on finer water performs worse.
--
-- THE TRAP THE TIER IS BUILT ON. The depth of a basin cancels out of its cut
-- size and does NOT cancel out of its velocity check, so a reader who takes the
-- cut size as the whole answer builds a basin too shallow to work. The second
-- trap is the train: the stage figures of a train compound exactly as
-- survivals, and a figure measured on the RAW stream does not.
--
-- HELD FOR LITERATURE, taught as a limit and never graded in any tier: the
-- dissolved oil floor, the second half of the API 421 velocity rule, any
-- discharge limit, the grain exponent of a bed, the attachment efficiency and
-- the device shape constants one at a time. The capstone grades none of them.
-- OGULAGHA's basin sits under the fixed velocity limit, so the missing half of
-- the rule cannot decide anything, and every pinned constant a graded answer
-- reads (the short-circuit allowance, the plate efficiency factor) is stated
-- on the capstone page rather than taken as published.
