-- Six modules, 26 lessons, 132 questions (15 per module bank plus a 42

--
-- What this tier owns: THE GAS LINE. The moment the fluid compresses,
-- pressure stops being linear along the pipe and the whole method changes
-- shape into a p-squared driving group at base conditions that are 14.65 psia
-- and 520 degR and not atmospheric. Four published forms answer the same
-- question and disagree by a third on one line. One elevation group is shared
-- by all four and is the only place a static column enters a gas calculation.
-- And the outlet pressure has no closed form in this engine at all, so this
-- is where a reader first meets an answer that came out of a solver.
--
-- THE ENGINE FACT THIS TIER OWNS. The outlet-pressure bracket is itself a
-- piece of physics. Its ceiling is the inlet divided by the square root of e
-- to the s, which on a climb sits BELOW the inlet and on a descent sits ABOVE
-- it. A bracket that stopped at the inlet was wrong on both, and worst on the
-- commoner case. On a descent the drop comes back negative, because the line
-- arrives higher than it left.
--
-- THE TRAP THE TIER IS BUILT ON. Four numbers for one line is not four
-- opinions to average. Each form carries its own diameter exponent, its own
-- friction assumption and its own fitted range, and the question a reader has
-- to answer is which one is still honest on the line in front of them.
--
-- HELD FOR THE LITERATURE, AND THEREFORE NOT GRADED: the transmission
-- efficiency E, which is an unsourced multiplier. The capstone states its own
-- E as a condition of the line, so no graded value looks one up.
