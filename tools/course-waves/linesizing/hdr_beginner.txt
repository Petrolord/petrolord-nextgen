-- Six modules, 26 lessons, 132 questions (15 per module bank plus a 42
-- question final exam). The structure row is GENERATED from the tier's
-- manifest.json rather than hand typed, and every question row is generated
-- from the committed bank JSON, which reproduces byte for byte from its
-- committed generator.
--
-- What this tier owns: THE LIQUID LINE AND WHAT THE ENGINE REFUSES. The
-- liquid line is the only part of this engine that is closed form end to end,
-- so a reader can watch a whole answer assembled before anything iterates: a
-- bore becomes an area, an area and a rate become a velocity, a velocity
-- becomes a Reynolds number, and a Reynolds number and a relative roughness
-- become a friction factor. Then the three losses are kept apart, the
-- erosional limit stops being a wellhead idea and becomes the thing that
-- decides a bore, and a recommendation ranks the schedule.
--
-- THE ENGINE FACT THIS TIER OWNS. The three losses are three different
-- things and only one of them is what a bigger pipe fixes. Friction falls
-- steeply with bore, the fitting sum falls with it, and the elevation term
-- does not move at all, because a static column is a density and a height and
-- has no idea what bore it is standing in. A reader who treats the total as
-- one quantity sizes the wrong pipe.
--
-- THE TRAP THE TIER IS BUILT ON. Every limit in this method is a different
-- equation with a different domain. The friction factor has two branches and
-- nothing honest between Reynolds 2100 and 4000, the erosional limit is not a
-- pressure drop at all, and a number that prints perfectly can have come from
-- an equation standing outside the range it was fitted in.
--
-- HELD FOR THE LITERATURE, AND THEREFORE NOT GRADED: the API RP 14E c factor
-- rows, and the transition band between the two friction branches. Both are
-- taught here as limits. The capstone states its own site c factor and runs
-- above Reynolds 20000, so neither can reach a graded value.
