a Mscf/d on a critical rate, and under a hundredth of a foot on the maximum
slug. This file passed that guard.

# SECTION 4: THE DROPLET BALANCE, AND WHAT MOVES IT
# Drag against weight less buoyancy, with the largest stable droplet set by a
# critical Weber number. Eliminating the droplet diameter between the two leaves
# a velocity that depends on exactly three groups and nothing else: sigma to the
# quarter, the density difference to the quarter, and one over the square root of
# the gas density. Every row below is the engine, run on published golden fluid
# properties and published golden gas densities. Associate m03 owns all of it.
#
# READ THAT SENTENCE CAREFULLY, BECAUSE IT INVITES A CLAIM THAT IS FALSE.
# "Three groups and nothing else" is true of the SWEEP below, where the density
# difference is driven as an independent input. It is NOT true of a real
# station, because there the gas density appears TWICE: once on its own under
# the square root and once inside the density difference.
#
# The consequence a writer will trip on: the water-to-condensate velocity ratio
# is NOT a fluid constant. The 1.4626530609 printed below belongs to 1000.0
# psia and 620.0 degR and does not hold at 300.0 or at 2500.0 psia. Quote it
# with its station, always. A lesson writer nearly wrote it as an invariant,
# caught it, and asked for this note.
# The power laws, checked rather than asserted. Doubling sigma by sixteen doubles
# the velocity; doubling the density difference by sixteen doubles it; four times
# the gas density halves it.

# SECTION 15: THE GRADIENT CONSTANT, ITS EXACT FORM, AND WHAT THE ROUNDING COSTS
# plungerLift.PSI_PER_FT_SG is 0.433. The exact water gradient is one thousand
# kilograms per cubic metre times g, expressed in psi per foot per unit specific

# declares. The two are a tenth of a percent apart, the gate knows it and loosens
# that one assertion to a 5e-3 relative tolerance, and the golden publishes the
# ORACLE number for its own case, which the engine cannot reproduce. The cost
