# What reporting an audit needs

Reporting is the move that turns an audit into a deliverable, and canReportAudit asks for everything a report must stand on. The digest walks AUD-2026-007 from its record at the as-of date 2026-10-15 to a report the engine allows, one requirement at a time.

## The walk

| step | canReportAudit |
| --- | --- |
| as recorded | REFUSED |
| every item answered, items 10 and 11 given reasons | REFUSED |
| a finding raised from item 6 as well | REFUSED |
| and the conclusion written | ALLOWED |

## Every item answered

As recorded, the engine refuses:

"4 checklist items have no answer yet (10, 11, 13, 14). An audit reported with its checklist half blank says nothing about the items nobody looked at."

Items 13 and 14, signage current and emergency drill held this month, have no answer at all. Items 10 and 11 read Not applicable with no reason, and module four showed that the engine counts those as unanswered. The step clears when every item is answered and the two blank Not applicable answers are given reasons.

## Every failed critical item covered

With the checklist complete, the engine refuses again:

"Critical item 6 was answered Nonconformant with no finding raised. A critical question that fails needs a finding with a number, an owner and a due date."

Item 2 is the other critical question answered Nonconformant, and finding AF-2026-018 is raised from it, so the refusal names item 6 alone. The step clears when a finding is raised from item 6 as well.

## The conclusion

With every failed critical item covered, the engine refuses once more:

"Write the audit conclusion. It is the deliverable."

The checklist and the findings are the evidence. The conclusion is the auditor's statement of what they add up to, and the engine will not let an audit be reported without it. Written, the answer is ALLOWED.

## The lead auditor

The digest also asks the question from the other side. The same audit with no lead auditor named at all is refused:

"Name the lead auditor."

A report is somebody's report, and the lead auditor is the person answerable for it. Without a name in that role there is nobody to ask about the conclusion once the audit team has left site. Lesson four reads the rule about who may be the lead auditor. This row shows the simpler rule under it: there has to be one.

## What the order teaches

The four requirements build on each other. The engine asks for a complete checklist before it asks about findings, because a finding can only be raised from an answer. It asks for the findings before the conclusion, because the conclusion has to account for them. Each refusal names only the next thing missing, so the auditor works through the record in the same order the report will be read.

The walk also shows what the gate does not ask for. The digest prints no reporting refusal about the Major item 4, which reads Observation, or about the Conformant answers. Reporting needs every question answered and every failed critical question covered. It does not need the site to have conformed.
