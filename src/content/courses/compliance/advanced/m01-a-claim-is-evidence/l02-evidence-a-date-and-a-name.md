# Evidence, a date and a name

A clause marked Conformant is a claim. The isoCompliance module does not accept the claim on its word: canSetClauseStatus asks for three things on the record before it lets the status stand. This lesson reads that gate one request at a time, on clause 7.2 of the ORASHI register, at the as-of date 2026-10-15.

{{panel:compliance-readiness-explorer}}

## The vocabulary a clause may carry

isoCompliance.CLAUSE_STATUSES holds five words: Not assessed, Conformant, Partially conformant, Nonconformant and Not applicable. A word outside the list is refused outright. Asked to set clause 7.2 to Compliant, the gate answers:

REFUSED: Compliant is not a clause status.

Compliant is a status in the Associate tier's obligation register. It does not belong to a clause. Each register is held to its own vocabulary.

## Clause 7.2, one requirement at a time

Clause 7.2, Competence, is owned by u-obinna and reads Not assessed. Its row shows claims conformity false, evidence record false and assessed false.

- Conformant, with nothing else: REFUSED.
- Conformant, with an evidence reference only: REFUSED, with the same sentence.
- Conformant, with evidence, a date and an assessor: ALLOWED.

The refusal is the same sentence both times:

REFUSED: Name the evidence, the date it was assessed and who assessed it. Conformity is a claim about documented information; without those three it is an opinion in a dropdown.

An evidence reference on its own is still refused: the gate asks for all three together. Partially conformant is held to the same three: refused with nothing else, in the same sentence, and ALLOWED with evidence, a date and an assessor. Evidence with no date cannot be read against a certification cycle, and evidence with no assessor cannot be checked for independence, which module 2 teaches.

## Reading the three columns in the register

The ORASHI table prints three columns that answer the gate's question for each clause: claims conformity, evidence record and assessed. Read clause 4.1, owned by u-nneka: Conformant, with true in all three. Read clause 6.1.2, owned by u-tari: Partially conformant, and it also reads claims conformity true, evidence record true and assessed true.

Now read clause 5.2, Environmental policy, owned by u-obinna. Its status is Conformant and claims conformity reads true. Its evidence record reads false. Assessed reads true. This row is a conformity claim whose evidence is missing from the record, and module 5 shows the readiness list counting it.

The register counts the same thing in two places you will meet later in this tier. The readiness counts print evidenced 9 and conformant 9, and the summary prints evidenced claims 9 and unevidenced claims 1. Quote those figures as they print. Do not form a share or a percentage from them: the digest prints none.

## Why the gate stands where it does

An auditor reading a register asks what was seen, when, and by whom. Those are the three questions the gate puts, in the same order. The gate asks them when the status is set, so a register built through the engine cannot hold a Conformant clause the auditor cannot follow back to a document.
