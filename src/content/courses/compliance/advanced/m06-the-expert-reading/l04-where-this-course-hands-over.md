# Where this course hands over

This is the last lesson of "Compliance, Audit & Quality". It closes the course by stating what the course has taught, which apps it graded, and where its sibling course takes over.

## The one sentence

The course was built on one sentence. Nothing in these five apps is typed as a status: every status, count, age and verdict is derived from a dated record read against one stated as-of date, and every gate refuses until the evidence, the date and the named person it asks for are on the record.

The three tiers read that sentence at three scales. The Associate tier read it on an obligation register and a document library: a status derived from a due date, an expiry and a filing, and a review state derived from an issue date. The Professional tier read it on an inspection and test plan, its NCRs, a checklist audit and an audit programme: progress counted from the points and the answers, and gates that refuse until a date and a name are recorded. This tier read it on a management system: a conformity claim that needs evidence, a date and a name; an auditor who may not audit their own work; coverage counted over the cycle from reported internal audits; and readiness as a list of what the record still lacks.

## What this course owns

The course grades the calendar and five modules: complianceStatus, documentControl, qualityAssurance, auditManagement and isoCompliance. It owns audit independence, in both forms: the Audit & Findings Manager's lead auditor against the auditee, and ISO 19011 applied to every examiner in the ISO register. It owns the audit and finding lifecycles, and the root cause categories. It also teaches Document Control's reviewer rule, which says the author never reviews a revision.

## Where riskchange takes over

The sibling course in the assurance module is riskchange, "Risk, Change & Learning". It owns risk scoring, risk bands, risk appetite and heat maps. It owns the Management of Change and Peer Review apps, with their own approval rules and segregation of duties, and the Lessons Learned app.

The two courses meet at the finding. A finding raised in this course can feed a risk register or a lesson learned, and that is where riskchange begins. This course does not score the risk a finding describes, and it does not teach how a lesson learned is written or approved. Take those questions to riskchange.

## What to carry out of this course

Carry three habits. First, name the as-of date beside every status, count and age you quote, because each one is true at that date and at no other. Second, quote the engine's status words with their capitals, and treat two different words as two different statuses. Third, quote a refusal exactly or describe it, and never put your own words in quotation marks as though the engine wrote them.
