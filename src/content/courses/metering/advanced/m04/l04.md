# A factor of about 24, and what it would mean

The refusal in this module rests on one quantitative claim in the engine's own message: the two plausible forms of the missing relation differ by a factor of about 24. This lesson is about why that number settles the argument.

## The claim

The engine says it, in the refusal quoted in full in the previous lesson:

> the two plausible forms of it differ by a factor of about 24. An emergency vent sized 24 times too small is how a tank is destroyed, so no figure is offered here

That is the engine's own characterisation of its own uncertainty. It is quoted rather than computed, because the relation is not in the package and nothing in this course can evaluate either form of it.

## Why the magnitude decides the question

Engineering is full of quantities that are uncertain and still usable. A duty known to within a few percent supports a purchase, and a correlation with scatter can be used with margin. The judgement is always the same: is the uncertainty small enough that a decision made on the central value stays the right decision across the range.

At a factor of about 24 the answer is no. There is no margin policy that covers being wrong by that much in the unsafe direction, and no amount of conservatism elsewhere compensates, because the vent is the last line.

## What being undersized means here

An emergency vent exists for the case where a fire is boiling the contents of a tank. The vapour has to leave faster than it is generated or the tank over-pressures, and the relief path either passes the flow or it does not.

That is why this missing relation is the one the engine refuses on while it returns other figures with warnings attached. Elsewhere in this tier it extrapolates a thermal rate above a stated capacity and labels the result an extrapolation, which is reasonable for a figure a designer will check. An undersized fire vent is not a figure anybody checks by looking at it, because it looks exactly like a vent size.

## What the engine will still tell you

Everything up to the refusal is returned. On this tank the wetted area is 5881.0614 ft2, the band is above 2800 ft2 and the fire duty is 25892440.2513 Btu/hr. That duty is the input the standard's own relation needs, so the refusal costs one step rather than the whole calculation.

The engine also refuses to let the fire case be compared with the normal venting case:

> the fire duty is normally far above the normal venting duty, which is why an emergency vent or a weak roof-to-shell seam exists at all. The two cannot be compared as vent capacities here, because the vent capacity is withheld

That is the discipline of the tier in one sentence. A comparison you cannot compute is a comparison you do not make.
