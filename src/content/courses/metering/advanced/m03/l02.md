# One predicate, computed once

Once the two totals exist, one question remains: which direction governs. The engine forms a single predicate for that and computes it once, and the discipline in that sentence is worth more than the answer it produces.

## The answer on this tank

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| total outbreathing, scfh | 25689.2574 |
| total inbreathing, scfh | 23201.8178 |
| governing case | pressure (outbreathing) |

The digest prints the pair as a relation:

| RELATION: the two directions on this tank at its stated rates | value |
| --- | --- |
| total outbreathing, scfh | 25689.2574 |
| total inbreathing, scfh | 23201.8178 |
| difference (first less second) | 2487.4395 |
| ratio (first over second) | 1.107209 |

The difference is 2487.4395 scfh and the ratio is 1.107209, and both of those are quoted from the relation line rather than worked out here. At these rates the governing case is the word the engine returns: pressure (outbreathing).

## Why one predicate rather than two

A result like this usually carries more than one consumer. A label on a screen says which case governs. A warning fires when the governing case is the dangerous one. A downstream sizing step picks the total to size against. If each of those asks its own question about the same two numbers, they can answer differently, and the place they differ is exactly at the tie, where the two totals are equal or nearly so.

A tie is not a rare corner. It is the point every sweep passes through, and it is the point where the answer matters most, because it is where a designer is deciding between two cases. So the engine decides once, in one place, and everything else reads the decision.

## What that buys a reader

It means the word and the warning cannot disagree. When the engine says vacuum governs, the vacuum warning is the one that fires, because both come off the same predicate. A reader who sees a governing word can therefore treat the absence of the matching warning as information rather than as noise.

It also means the predicate is one thing to check. You can sweep it, bisect it, and find the crossover exactly, which is what the next lesson does with the draw rate. A quantity computed in three places cannot be swept, because there is no single answer to converge on.

## Where this pattern shows up elsewhere

The same argument applies to any result that carries a verdict beside a value. The shell calculation in the module before this one returns a governing candidate, and the same reasoning holds there: the thickness, the word and the note that explains it all come off one decision about which of three candidates is largest.

## The reading rule

When an engine returns a verdict word, find out whether the number, the word and the warning share a source. If they do, the word is trustworthy on its own. If they do not, treat the word as a display and go back to the numbers.
