# Nominal against working, on one tank

Two capacities come back from one tank and both of them are honest. Reading a result means knowing which question each one answers, and it means saying nothing about the size of the gap between them that the course has not computed for you.

## The two figures, and the one relation that is printed

This lesson prints the pair as a relation, with both values, their difference and their ratio:

| RELATION: the nominal and the working capacity of this tank | value |
| --- | --- |
| nominal, to the top of the shell, bbl | 19608.4845 |
| working, to the design liquid level, bbl | 18845.9323 |
| difference (first less second) | 762.5522 |
| ratio (first over second) | 1.040462 |

The difference between them is 762.5522 bbl and the ratio is 1.040462. Both of those are quoted from the relation line. Neither was worked out here, and that is a rule of this course rather than a style preference: a comparison between two figures is only ever stated where the source computed and printed it. If a relation line does not exist for a pair you want to compare, the honest move is to print both figures and say nothing about the gap.

## Where the gap comes from

The gap is geometric. The shell height is 36.000000 ft and the design liquid level is 34.600000 ft, so the working capacity stops below the top of the shell. On this tank the shell carries 544.6801 bbl for every foot of its height, which is the figure that turns a level in feet into an inventory in barrels.

That last figure is the one an operator actually uses. A gauge reading is a height. A tank ticket is a volume. Barrels per foot of shell is the bridge, and it is constant on a vertical cylinder because the cross section does not change with height.

## Which one belongs in which answer

The venting side of this tier is keyed to the nominal capacity, because the thermal rate in this package is stated per barrel of capacity, and the thermal inbreathing the engine returns for this tank is 19608.4845 scfh, computed from the nominal capacity at a stated rate of 1.000000 scfh of air per barrel of capacity and a latitude factor of 1.000000.

The inventory side is keyed to the working capacity of 18845.9323 bbl, because that is the volume the tank is designed to hold in service. A filling schedule works in barrels per hour against that working volume, and one that assumed the nominal capacity would plan to put product where the design liquid level says product does not go.

## The reading discipline

Take the two lessons of this module together. Quote figures at the precision the source prints them, because a tolerance is set around that rendering. State a relationship only where a relation line states it. Between them those two rules remove most of the ways a correct set of numbers turns into a wrong sentence.
