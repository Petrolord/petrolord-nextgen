# Standing loss and working loss

The total loss is the sum of two mechanisms with different causes. Separating them is what turns a loss figure into a decision, because the two are reduced by different equipment.

The standing loss is 3537.6108 lb/yr and the working loss is 73605.2059 lb/yr, and the table above prints their difference as -70067.5951 and their ratio as 0.048062. Both of those figures are quoted from the relation line rather than worked out here, which is the rule this course applies to every comparison it makes.

The total the engine returns is 77142.8167 lb/yr.

## What each mechanism is

The standing loss is breathing while nothing is moving. The vapour space warms during the day, the mixture expands, and some of it leaves. It cools at night, air comes back in, and the cycle repeats. It depends on the size of the vapour space and on how much product vapour that space holds.

The working loss is displacement. Liquid coming into the tank pushes the vapour space out ahead of it, and what leaves is a saturated or partly saturated mixture. It depends on throughput.

## Reading the pair before reading the total

A habit worth forming with any result that reports a total and its components: read the components first. A total is a single number that can arrive at the same value by very different routes, and on a tank the route is what a decision depends on. Two tanks with the same annual loss and opposite splits are two different problems with two different answers.

## Why the split decides the equipment

Control equipment does not treat the two halves alike. Something that reduces the vapour space or its daily cycling works on the standing loss. Something that captures displaced vapour during filling works on the working loss. On any given tank, knowing which half carries the loss tells you which family of equipment is worth pricing, and the split is the first thing to look at in a result of this kind.
