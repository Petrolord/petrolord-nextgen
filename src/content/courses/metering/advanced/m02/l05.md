# The crossovers, found by bisecting a word

This is the method lesson of the tier, and it is the thing worth taking away from the whole course. A threshold you need is often not printed anywhere. It can still be found exactly, as long as the engine will tell you which side of it you are on.

## What a returned word is

Three of the tank results in this tier come back with a word beside the number. The shell returns a governing candidate. The venting returns a governing case. The fire calculation returns a heat input band. None of those words is a number a reader can interpolate, and every one of them is a function of the inputs, which means every one of them changes somewhere.

That somewhere is a boundary. It is the gravity at which the water test stops governing, the draw rate at which vacuum starts governing, the wetted area at which one heat input band gives way to the next. The package prints the first two nowhere, because they are consequences of the relations in it. The band edges are different: the band names carry them, and bisecting the name confirms the engine switches where its names say it does.

## The procedure

Take two inputs you know return different words. Ask the engine at the midpoint. Keep the half that still straddles the change, and repeat until the interval is as narrow as you need. What makes it trustworthy is that every evaluation is the engine's own answer, so the boundary you converge on is the engine's own boundary rather than one reconstructed from a formula you think it is using.

## The two crossovers on this tank

The digest reports both of the tank crossovers this way, and says how each was found.

> on this tank the water test takes the bottom course below a gravity of 0.931727

> found by bisecting the engine's own governing word

> vacuum takes the case above a draw rate of, bbl/hr 1083.0319

> found by bisecting the engine's own governing word

Below a specific gravity of 0.931727 the water test governs the bottom course of this tank. Above a draw rate of 1083.0319 bbl/hr, at this tank's stated fill rate, the vacuum direction governs the venting. Both are properties of this tank at these conditions. Neither transfers to another tank, and that is the point of knowing how to find them rather than memorising them.

## What the method will not give you

Bisection finds a boundary the engine has. It cannot find a boundary the engine does not have, and it cannot repair one that is wrong. The fire venting module in this tier has a quantity the engine refuses to compute at all, and no amount of sweeping recovers it, because there is nothing on the far side of the refusal to converge on.

So the order of questions matters. First ask whether the engine answers at all. If it does, and it answers in a word, the boundary is one short sweep away.
