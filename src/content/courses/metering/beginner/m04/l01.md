# What a differential costs that is never given back

The differential across a plate is not free. Some of it is recovered downstream
as the flow spreads back out to fill the pipe, and some of it is gone. The part
that is gone is the permanent pressure loss, and it is a running cost on the
line for as long as the plate is in it.

| 0.350000 | 0.599062 | 0.862647 | 55.036882 |
| 0.500000 | 0.602677 | 0.733463 | 46.794924 |
| 0.600000 | 0.604689 | 0.629688 | 40.174094 |
| 0.670000 | 0.604249 | 0.549831 | 35.079209 |
| 0.750000 | 0.598985 | 0.452033 | 28.839688 |

Every row is at the ABOH differential, and the coefficient column is the one
computed for that beta rather than an assumed figure. The loss fraction is the
share of the measured differential that never comes back, and the last column is
that share expressed in the same inches of water the transmitter reads.

   ratio (first over second)                           2.186003

That is one half of the trade the high beta warning in module two names. The
other half, the uncertainty, is module six.

## What a permanent loss actually costs

Pressure on a gas line is either compression you have paid for or product you
will not be able to move later. A loss across a meter run is small next to the
loss across a control valve, but it is there every hour of every day, and it
does not appear on any invoice, which is precisely why it survives so long
without being questioned.

On a liquid export line the same loss appears as pump duty.

## Reading the table as a designer

There are two ways to arrive at this table. One is with the plate installed, in
which case your row is fixed and the table says what the run costs you now. The
other is at the design stage, where you are choosing between rows, and the loss
column is one of three things on the scale. The other two are the uncertainty
you will live with and the straight run the platform can give you.

Notice also that the coefficient column is not flat down the table. Each row was
evaluated at its own beta, so a loss calculation is quietly a coefficient
calculation and inherits everything module two established.

## The coefficient is a required argument

This function will not run without the discharge coefficient of the run. It asks
for it and says why when it is missing:

   > permanent loss needs the discharge coefficient of this run. This module exists to show
     that it is not a constant 0.61, so it will not assume one

So the working order is fixed. Compute the flow, read the coefficient off the
result, then compute the loss with that coefficient. The next lesson puts a
number on what skipping that step would have cost.
