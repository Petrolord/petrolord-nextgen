# What assuming a coefficient of 0.61 would cost

Module two argued that the discharge coefficient is computed rather than
assumed. This lesson does the arithmetic that the argument is worth on one run,
so that the size of the shortcut is a printed figure.

## The two ways of computing the same loss

The ABOH run has its own coefficient, which came back as 0.602223. The
convenient assumption is a flat 0.610000. Run the permanent loss both ways at
the ABOH beta and differential:

   with the run's own coefficient of 0.602223, in H2O            47.865230
   with an assumed coefficient of 0.610000, in H2O            47.689171
   difference (first less second)                      0.176059
   ratio (first over second)                           1.003692

That is the whole comparison, and it is the only one you may quote here. The
engine itself refuses to do the second calculation, because the coefficient is a
required argument; the digest performed it deliberately so that the cost of the
assumption is printed rather than asserted.

## Why the answer is not the point

The instinct on seeing a difference like that is to decide whether it is big. It
is the wrong instinct, and understanding why is most of this lesson.

The comparison above is one run, at one beta, at one Reynolds number. Look back
at the sweep in module two and you are looking at a surface of coefficients with
a real span across the cells inside the published range, reported there as a
difference of 0.072666 and a ratio of 1.122048 between the two ends. A single
assumed value is being compared against a quantity that moves across that
surface. Knowing what the assumption costs at one point on the surface tells you
what it costs at that point.

So the useful conclusion is procedural. The cost of assuming is not a fixed
penalty you can learn once and apply. It depends on where the run sits, and the
only way to know it for your run is to compute the coefficient, which is the
thing the assumption was trying to avoid.

## What the engine does instead

   > permanent loss needs the discharge coefficient of this run. This module exists to show
     that it is not a constant 0.61, so it will not assume one

A function that refused and said nothing would be an obstacle. This one names
the quantity it needs, names the position it is taking and says it will not
assume. A user who reads it knows where to get the missing input, because it is
on the result of the flow calculation they have already run.

## The habit worth taking away

When you meet an assumption in somebody's calculation, ask what the assumed
quantity actually depends on. If it depends on the thing being calculated, as a
coefficient depends on the beta and the Reynolds number of the run, then the
assumption is not a simplification. It is an unquantified error with a
comfortable number written on it.
