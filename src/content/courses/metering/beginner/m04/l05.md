# Straight run: a table, a refusal and a ceiling

{{panel:fc-withheld-explorer}}

An orifice plate measures a velocity profile as much as it measures a flow. Put
a bend, a valve or a reducer close upstream and the profile arriving at the
plate is not the one the correlation was fitted to. The fix is straight pipe,
and how much of it you need is a table value.

## The table

| fitting | beta 0.2 | beta 0.4 | beta 0.5 | beta 0.6 | beta 0.67 | beta 0.75 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| singleElbow | 10 | 14 | 18 | 26 | 36 | 44 |
| twoElbowsSamePlane | 10 | 16 | 22 | 42 | 44 | 44 |
| reducer | 5 | 5 | 8 | 9 | 12 | 13 |
| fullBoreValve | 12 | 12 | 12 | 14 | 19 | 24 |
| twoElbowsDifferentPlanes | withheld | withheld | withheld | withheld | withheld | withheld |

The figures are diameters of the pipe. Every answered row carries this note:

   > these are table values, not a calculation: they depend on the fitting and the beta,
     and a flow conditioner shortens them substantially. They are this engine's stated
     table data and are not cited to a document in this repository

That is the engine's own wording, quoted exactly. The rows are not derived from
anything, so there is no formula to check them against, and they are not cited
to a document in this repository, so a published table replaces them.

## The row that is refused

One row comes back withheld at every beta. The digest counts it over the five
fitting names, each asked at all six betas, with the rule that a fitting counts
as refused when the engine returns withheld true at every beta asked:

   upstream fittings this engine refuses to answer for: 1

And the reason, in the engine's own words:

   > the straight-run requirement for two elbows in different planes is withheld. The
     column that used to be here fell by 15 diameters between beta 0.5 and 0.6 and then
     rose by 20, and a published requirement rises with beta, so it cannot be right.
     Repairing it needs the ISO 5167 or AGA 3 table, which this package does not carry. Two
     elbows out of plane is the worst common upstream arrangement, so take the requirement
     from the standard or fit a flow conditioner

Nothing in this course recovers that column. It is withheld and it is taught
here as a limit. The engine calls two elbows out of plane the worst common
upstream arrangement, so the missing row is the one you will most want.

## Why a refusal is the useful answer here

A plausible number in that column would be used. A skid would be laid out
against it, the pipe would be cut, and the meter would be installed with less
straight run than the standard asks for. Nothing on the flow result would say
so, because the correlation does not know what is upstream of it.

## The ceiling

Above the last column the engine refuses rather than reading off the last row:

   > beta of 0.950 is above 0.75, where this table stops and where the flange-tap
     correlation this module is built on stops being published. Answering here would mean
     reading off the last row, which is not a requirement for this beta
