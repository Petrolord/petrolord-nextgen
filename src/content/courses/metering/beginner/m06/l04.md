# A lead with no margin is not a lead

Rank six numbers and one of them comes first. That is arithmetic, and it is true
even when the first two are separated by nothing that matters. A tool that names
a winner and stops has told you something that sounds like a finding and may not
be one.

## The engine returns the margin as well as the name

The budget result carries a flag saying whether the lead is clear, and the note
it writes differs in the two cases. Both are quoted here in the engine's own
words. The first is the clear case:

   > discharge coefficient contributes 76.9 percent of the variance against 12.3 percent
     for expansibility: spend on it first

And the second is the case where the flag comes back false:

   > discharge coefficient contributes 46.0 percent of the variance and differential
     pressure 40.4 percent. That is too close to call a dominant term: improving either one
     alone will move the total very little

Read the second one carefully, because it does more than hedge. It says what
follows from the closeness, which is that improving either one alone will move
the total very little. In a budget combined as a root sum of squares, two terms
of similar share have to be attacked together.

## Where the margin runs out

   the lead stops being clear at a reading of, in H2O              16.862007

Found on the same run as the crossover in the previous lesson. Between that
reading and the reading where the name changes, the engine is still naming a
dominant term while telling you the name is not worth much, and the clear column
in the previous lesson's table shows those rows.

## The margin is part of the result

Treat the flag as a field of the answer in the same way the total is. A ranking
of six numbers with no margin will name a winner on a photo finish, and the
photo finish and the landslide look identical on a screen that prints only the
name. When you copy a dominant term into a report, copy the flag beside it, and
when the flag is false, say so in the sentence rather than in a footnote.

## Why a tool should be built this way

Two failure modes are being avoided at once.

The first is the tool that reports only the winner. Its user reads a name,
spends money on that input, and finds the total has hardly moved, because the
second term is now the one holding the total up.

The second is the tool that reports a ranking and no verdict, leaving every user
to decide for themselves what counts as a clear lead. Some will decide
generously and act on a photo finish.

The engine here does neither. It names the term, states the two shares, and
returns a flag saying whether the lead is clear, so the user gets a verdict and
the evidence for it in the same result.
