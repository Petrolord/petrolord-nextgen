# Nine to one is three to one

Everybody in measurement has heard that a meter is good for about three to one.
Fewer people can say three to one in what. This lesson is about that sentence,
because the same rule written in two quantities gives two different numbers and
both are in circulation.

## Why there are two numbers

Flow through an orifice goes as the square root of the differential. Turn the
flow down by some factor and the differential falls by the square of it. So a
differential turndown and a flow turndown are different numbers describing the
same operating point, and a rule quoted without its quantity is ambiguous.

The engine holds both limits and returns both:

   flow turndown limit the engine holds                      3.000000
   differential turndown limit the engine holds              9.000000

   the differential turndown limit                            9.000000
   the flow turndown limit                                    3.000000
   difference (first less second)                      6.000000
   ratio (first over second)                           3.000000

Those are the same rule written in two quantities, and the digest prints the
relationship between them rather than leaving you to work it out.

## The engine says it in words too

Every call returns this note:

   > a differential turndown of 3.1 to 1 is a FLOW turndown of 1.77 to 1, because flow goes
     as the square root of the differential. The customary three-to-one flow rule is a
     nine-to-one differential turndown

The figures in that sentence are the ABOH run's own. Look back at the table in
the previous lesson and the row at 63.800000 in H2O carries a differential
turndown of 3.134796 and a flow turndown of 1.770536, which is what the note is
describing in the rounded form a screen shows.

## Which one the warning fires on

The warning fires on the flow turndown. The two limits are one rule written
twice, so they are reached at the same reading, as the figures below show. The
choice matters for anybody reading a screen, because the figure the warning is
about should be the figure the rule is written in. Apply the three-to-one figure
to the differential instead and you flag the ABOH row, whose differential
turndown is 3.134796 while its flow turndown is 1.770536, with the warning
silent.

   on a 200.000000 in H2O span the warning starts below a reading of, in H2O          22.222222
   the flow turndown there                                                      3.000000
   the differential turndown there                                              9.000000

Found by bisecting the reading at which the returned warning stops being null.
At that reading both limits are reached at once, which is what you would expect
from two statements of one rule.

## What to do with this

When somebody quotes you a turndown, ask in what. When you write one down, write
the quantity beside it, because the bare number carries a factor of three
depending on which quantity was meant. And when you specify a transmitter span,
remember that the span you pick decides where on this scale your ordinary
operating day sits, which is a choice made once and lived with for years.
