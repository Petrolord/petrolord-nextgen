# The ladder, and the screen it is read against

The authority is arithmetic. The verdict word beside it is a judgement, and this lesson is about who made that judgement and where the rungs sit.

## The ladder

On a system whose total drop is 120.000000 psi the engine returns the following. A valve drop of 10.000000 psi gives an authority of 0.083333 and the verdict `poor`. A valve drop of 25.000000 psi gives 0.208333 and `poor`. A valve drop of 40.000000 psi gives 0.333333 and `acceptable`. A valve drop of 55.000000 psi gives 0.458333 and `acceptable`. A valve drop of 70.000000 psi gives 0.583333 and `good`, as do 85.000000 psi at 0.708333 and 100.000000 psi at 0.833333.

The rungs are printed rather than inferred from the rows. The verdict stops being good below an authority of 0.500000, and it becomes poor below an authority of 0.250000.

## Whose rungs they are

The engine states the provenance of its own screen in the result:

> `the 0.5 and 0.25 boundaries are this engine's stated screen, not a value read from a standard`

That is a message worth reading twice, because a verdict word carries an air of authority that a bare fraction does not. Somebody reading `poor` on a screen will treat it as a finding. What the engine is saying is that the word is the output of a screen it states for itself, so the finding is the fraction and the word is a convenience laid over it.

There is a second reason the provenance line matters here. The verdict is a word with no units and no visible arithmetic behind it, so it travels further than the fraction does. It gets copied into minutes, into review sheets and into vendor correspondence, and by the time it arrives somewhere it is being read as though a standard had said it.

## Using it properly

Two habits follow. The first is to quote the authority alongside the verdict in any document that will be read by somebody who was not there. A reader given 0.458333 can apply their own company screen to it. A reader given `acceptable` cannot recover anything.

The second is to treat the boundaries as the place to argue rather than as the answer. An authority of 0.458333 sits below the rung at 0.500000 and above the rung at 0.250000. Whether that is a design to build depends on how much of the range the loop has to control over, how nonlinear the rest of the circuit is, and what the consequence of poor control at low flow actually is on that service.

This course grades no verdict word, for exactly the reason the engine's own message gives.

## Exercise

Write down the two authority boundaries the engine states, and the verdict it returns at authorities of 0.208333 and 0.583333. Then take the case at an authority of 0.458333 and say which rung it sits above and which it sits below, and what you would have to know before calling that design acceptable.
