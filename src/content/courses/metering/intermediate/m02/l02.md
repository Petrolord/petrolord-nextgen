# Sigma is computed on the drop the valve uses

There are two pressure drops available on a choked service and the cavitation index can be computed on either of them. Only one of the two answers means anything, and the engine says which one it used rather than leaving the reader to work it out.

## The basis line

Every cavitation index this engine returns carries a basis with it:

> `computed on the pressure drop the valve uses, which is the allowable drop once the service is choked`

That sentence is doing real work. The cavitation index is the pressure available to suppress vaporisation divided by the pressure drop being taken. Hand it the stated drop on a choked service and the denominator is a pressure drop the valve is incapable of taking, so the index that comes out belongs to a machine that does not exist.

## What it looks like in the march

Follow the index down the BELEMA march and watch where it stops. It reads 4.651599, 2.885714, 2.040786, 1.593572 and 1.307130 on the rows above the crossing. On the rows at outlet pressures of 60.000000, 40.000000, 28.740000 and 20.000000 psia it reads 1.217275 every time.

The index has stopped moving for the same reason the coefficient stopped moving at 19.148480. Both are computed on the drop used, and the drop used has been capped at the allowable drop of 179.220032 psi. A reader who expects the cavitation index to keep falling as the outlet pressure falls is expecting a number computed on the stated drop, which is the number the engine declines to give.

## The screen an empty box switched off

There is a second way to get a meaningless index, and it is an easy one to reach by accident. The index has the inlet pressure less the vapour pressure on top. Leave the vapour pressure out of the form and that term is the inlet pressure alone, the index is infinite, and the ladder hands back its safest rung for every liquid service at every pressure drop. The screen renders green and the screen has not run.

The engine refuses instead:

> `a true vapour pressure is needed. The cavitation index is (P1 - Pv) divided by the pressure drop, so with no vapour pressure it is infinite and every service reads as stable: the cavitation screen would not run at all. Every liquid has a vapour pressure at its flowing temperature, so state it`

The last clause is the one to argue with a datasheet over. There is no such thing as a liquid without a vapour pressure at its flowing temperature, so an empty box is a missing input rather than a property the fluid lacks.

## Exercise

Write down the cavitation index at outlet pressures of 80.000000 psia and 40.000000 psia, and say which of the two pressure drops each was computed on. Then say what value the index takes when no vapour pressure is given, and what regime word every liquid service would read as a result.
