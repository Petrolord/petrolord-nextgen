# What a screening band can and cannot tell you

Valve noise is a real engineering problem with a real calculation behind it, and this module does not do that calculation. What it offers is a screening band, and the value of it depends entirely on understanding which of the two things it is.

## The engine says so itself

The note comes back with every indication:

> `a screening indication only: a real noise prediction needs the IEC 60534-8-3 method with valve and pipe geometry. Use this to know whether to ask the question, not to answer it. The pressure ratio sets the band and the stream power caps and floors it, and both sets of thresholds are this engine's stated screen`

Four claims are packed into that. The result is an indication. A prediction needs a named method and geometry this package does not have. The band is there to tell you whether to ask the question. And the thresholds behind it belong to the engine rather than to a standard.

## What a screen is for

A screen is a triage tool. On a plant with several hundred control valves, the useful question at concept stage is which ten of them need a noise specialist, an acoustic lagging allowance or a low noise trim in the cost estimate. A screen that sorts the population into bands answers that question at a cost of nothing, and being approximately right about the ranking is enough for it.

What a screen cannot do is defend a number. It cannot support a guarantee, it cannot be offered to a regulator, and it cannot settle a dispute with a vendor about a decibel figure at a stated distance. Those need the method the note names, with the valve and pipe geometry that method requires.

## Reading a band without over-reading it

The practical rule is to act on the band and quote the method. A valve the screen flags is a valve to raise with the noise specialist, and what goes into the document is that the screening indication flagged it and that the proper calculation is outstanding.

The failure this course wants to prevent is the quiet promotion of a screening output into a design value, which happens when a band word gets copied out of a tool into a table with no note attached. Nothing in this course grades a noise band, and the reason is the same one the engine gives in its own note.
