# The style table, and that it is this engine own

Two numbers decide where every boundary in this module sits. The pressure recovery factor FL sets the allowable drop on liquid. The terminal pressure drop ratio xT sets the choking ratio on gas. Neither is calculated from anything. Both are read off a table of valve styles.

## The table this engine carries

| style id | label | FL | xT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| globeSingleFlow | Globe, single seat, flow to open | 0.900000 | 0.720000 |
| globeSingleClose | Globe, single seat, flow to close | 0.800000 | 0.550000 |
| globeCage | Globe, cage guided | 0.900000 | 0.750000 |
| globeAntiCav | Globe, multistage anti-cavitation trim | 0.970000 | 0.900000 |
| butterfly60 | Butterfly, 60 degrees open | 0.680000 | 0.380000 |
| butterfly90 | Butterfly, 90 degrees open | 0.550000 | 0.200000 |
| ballSegmented | Ball, segmented | 0.660000 | 0.300000 |
| ballFullBore | Ball, full bore | 0.550000 | 0.150000 |

The engine carries 8 valve styles, counted over the styles array the module exports, with every entry of the array counting and no filter applied.

Read the two columns as a description of what each style does with pressure. A style with a high FL recovers little of the pressure it took, so the pressure at the vena contracta stays well above the outlet pressure and the valve can take a large drop before anything vaporises. A style with a low FL recovers a great deal, which means the throat pressure dipped far lower than the outlet pressure suggests.

## The spread in the recovery factor

The digest prints the spread rather than leaving it to be described. The largest FL in the table is 0.970000 and the smallest is 0.550000. The difference, the first less the second, is 0.420000, and the ratio, the first over the second, is 1.763636.

That spread is the reason the style is a sizing decision and not a procurement detail. The allowable drop is built from FL, so the same service on two different styles chokes at two different outlet pressures, and a valve type chosen for cost or for space can move a boundary that the process never asked to move.

## Whose figures these are

The engine is explicit about the status of the table:

> `the FL and xT above are this engine's stated table values. They are not cited to a document in this repository, they are trim and vendor dependent by nature, and a certified vendor figure for the specific trim always replaces them`

Read that clause by clause. Stated table values means the engine is telling you where they came from. Cited to no document means you cannot follow them back to a standard from here. Trim and vendor dependent by nature means that even a perfect table would be approximate, because these are properties of a particular manufacturer's particular trim geometry. And a certified vendor figure always replaces them, which is the instruction.

Nothing in this course is graded on a number that rests on these table values, and that is a deliberate consequence of what the message says.
