# The pressure ratio sets the band

The screening indication is built in two steps. The pressure ratio across the valve proposes a band, and the stream power is then allowed to move it. This lesson is the first step.

## The thresholds

The pressure ratio bands the engine exports are moderate at 2.000000, high at 4.000000 and severe at 10.000000. Those three figures are the ladder a pressure ratio is read against.

The reasoning behind using the ratio is sound and simple. Noise from a throttling valve comes from the turbulent and, past the critical ratio, sonic dissipation of pressure energy inside and just downstream of the trim. The higher the ratio of inlet pressure to outlet pressure, the more violent that dissipation and the more of the energy ends up as acoustic power instead of heat.

## The three probes

The course runs three probes through the indication. A one scfh bleed at a pressure ratio near twelve has a ratio of 12.000000, and its ratio band is severe. A hundred million scfh at a pressure ratio near two has a ratio of 1.898734, and its ratio band is low. A moderate station flow at a pressure ratio near six has a ratio of 6.000000, and its ratio band is high.

Read those three ratio bands against the exported thresholds and you can see the ladder working. A ratio of 12.000000 sits above the severe threshold of 10.000000. A ratio of 6.000000 sits above the high threshold of 4.000000. A ratio of 1.898734 sits below the moderate threshold of 2.000000.

Notice that the ladder is read against the ratio of the pressures rather than against their difference. A valve dropping a large absolute pressure from a very high inlet may sit at a modest ratio, and a valve dropping a small absolute pressure from a low inlet may sit at a large one. It is the ratio that decides how far the gas expands through the trim, so it is the ratio the screen is built on.

## Why a ratio alone is not enough

Now look at what those three probes actually are. The first is a one scfh bleed, which is a trickle. The second is a hundred million scfh, which is a large transmission flow. On the ratio alone the trickle has been proposed as severe and the large flow has been proposed as low.

That is the correct behaviour of a ratio, and it is also obviously wrong as a statement about how loud these two services are. The ratio knows how hard the gas is being throttled and knows nothing whatever about how much gas there is. The second step of the screen exists to fix exactly that, and it is the subject of the next lesson.

Both failures are of the kind a screen must avoid, because a screen is trusted precisely where nobody has time to check it.
