# Equal percentage travel against linear travel

The characteristic module recommended a trim. This lesson shows what that recommendation is worth by putting the same duty on both curves and reading the travel off each.

## The same duty, two curves

The digest takes the normal duty on one valve and computes the travel it corresponds to on each characteristic. Equal percentage trim at a rangeability of 50 puts that duty at 74.737770 percent open. Linear trim puts it at 37.222222 percent open. The difference, the first less the second, is 37.515548, and the ratio, the first over the second, is 2.007880.

## What those numbers are saying

The duty has not changed. The flow, the pressure drop, the fluid and the required coefficient are all the same on both. The only thing that changed is the shape of the relationship between travel and coefficient, and the plug has ended up in a different place because of it.

That is the whole reason a characteristic is chosen. An inherent characteristic is a mapping, and choosing it decides where each duty lands on the stem.

## Why the equal percentage figure sits where it does

Equal percentage trim passes very little near the seat and a great deal near the top of its travel. A duty that a linear trim meets partway up the stem therefore needs more opening on equal percentage trim to pass the same coefficient. The stem position is the visible consequence of the curve.

Now put that beside what the authority module recommended. Equal percentage is recommended where the circuit absorbs most of the drop as flow rises, which flattens the installed curve at the top of the range. The trim's own curve rises steeply at the top of the range, and the two effects work against each other, leaving something closer to a straight line for the loop to work with.

## The practical reading

When a travel figure is quoted for a valve, the characteristic and the rangeability have to be quoted with it. The figures above are on equal percentage trim at a rangeability of 50, and a travel percentage without those two attributes is uninterpretable.

It also explains a common site observation. A valve that operates around three quarters open is often assumed to be undersized by somebody accustomed to seeing linear trim near the middle of its travel. On equal percentage trim that is a normal working position.

## Exercise

Write down the normal duty travel on equal percentage trim and on linear trim, with the difference and the ratio the digest prints between them. Then say which two attributes must be quoted alongside any travel percentage, and why a plug sitting around three quarters open is unremarkable on one of the two curves.
