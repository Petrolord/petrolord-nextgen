One teaching crew works 10-hour shifts, 5 a week, and carries three hazards. The action-level noise dose over the shift is 57.350093 percent against 50. The solvent mixture's index is 1.059500 on unadjusted limits and 1.513571 on limits adjusted for 10 h and 50 h a week. The one-hour time weighted WBGT is 29.066667 C, against the NIOSH heat REL at 300.000000 W of 28.213106 C, which is the NIOSH 2016-106 section 8.1 equation, checked for transcription only.

## What each row stands on

The rows do not carry equal weight, and reading them together starts with knowing why.

The noise exposure row stands on the Appendix A noise dose over the whole shift, a formula OSHA prints and the engine reproduces. The chemical limits stand on the Brief and Scala daily factor, which the BC regulation's printed factors reproduce; at 10 hours a day and 50 a week the daily 0.700000 governs. The unadjusted mixture index stands on the 1910.1000(d)(2) formula, which the engine reproduces on the regulation's own worked example.

The adjusted mixture index is different. It applies each limit's own reduction factor and then adds the terms. The adjustment and the index are each published; composing them is a judgement the hygienist makes and must state. The heat stress row compares a measured average with an equation checked for transcription only, which is a screening argument, and it is reported as one.

## No single number

There is no sum across the three hazards. A noise dose, a mixture index and a WBGT are measured against different criteria in different units, and adding them would mean nothing. The crew's record is three readings, each against its own line, each with its own evidence. A report lists them side by side and lets the reader see which is strongest, and the next lesson but one turns that list into a decision about what to measure again.
