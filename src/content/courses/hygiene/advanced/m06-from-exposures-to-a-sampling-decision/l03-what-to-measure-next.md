The IGBOMOTORU partial record covers 6.750000 hours and gives an 8-hour chemical TWA of 30.125000 ppm, against 35.703704 ppm averaged over the hours it covers. The short STEL record covers 11.000000 minutes of 15 and gives 92.333333 ppm. The OBEN day is 27.748183 percent of the OSHA PEL and 265.610944 percent of the NIOSH noise REL. The outdoor WBGT readings give 31.410000 C through the outdoor form and 32.560000 C through the indoor one, both the NIOSH 2016-106 section 9.3.2 weighting, checked for transcription only. Each pair is a reason to sample again before a control is chosen.

## The rule

What to measure next is decided by which reading is least certain and nearest its line. A reading close to its line with a known weakness in how it was taken is the one a decision should not rest on yet.

## Four weaknesses a record can carry

A record that covers under 8 hours. The chemical TWA always divides by 8, judgement J6, and the engine warns:

> the periods cover 6.75 h of 8: the remainder counts as zero exposure

A hygienist who knows the unsampled time was clean may accept 30.125000 ppm. One who does not has a record that cannot say: the true figure lies somewhere from 30.125000 ppm upward, and 35.703704 ppm is only what it would be if the unsampled time matched the sampled hours.

A STEL window that is short. The STEL divides by 15 and counts the missing minutes as zero, with a warning. The 92.333333 ppm is a floor on the fifteen-minute average, and the four missing minutes decide how far above it the real figure lies.

A noise dose read under the wrong criterion. The same record is 27.748183 percent of the PEL and 265.610944 percent of the NIOSH noise REL. A dosimeter set to the wrong criterion produces a correct number for the wrong question, and the only fix is to read the record again under the right one.

A WBGT built from a form that does not match the site. The indoor form on outdoor readings reads 32.560000 C against the outdoor form's 31.410000 C. If the form is in doubt, record which form the instrument used, or take the three readings again and state the form.

## Nearest its line

Distance matters as much as doubt. The teaching crew's action-level noise dose of 57.350093 percent sits above its 50 percent line by a margin the record's weaknesses could plausibly close; a mixture index of 1.513571 sits far enough above 1 that resampling would change the argument only if the model itself were wrong. The first is where the next dosimeter day should go.

## Measure before choosing a control

A control chosen on a weak record may address the wrong hazard. The sampling decision comes first, and it is written down with its reason, so that the next reader can see why a hazard was measured again and another was not.
