# Band summaries are not the equation

{{panel:hy-heat-stress}}

NIOSH 2016 Table 5-1 summarises the limits in bands of 30, 28, 26 and 25 C. At 233.000000 W the NIOSH heat REL equation gives 29.475407 C, against a band value of 30.000000 C, so the band sits 0.524593 C above the equation. At 465.000000 W the equation gives 26.024291 C and the band 26.000000 C, so the band sits 0.024291 C below it. Every equation value here is the NIOSH 2016-106 section 8.1 equation, checked for transcription only.

## What a band is

A band summary takes a continuous curve and prints one round value for a range of work. It is easy to read on site and easy to put on a poster. It is also, by construction, a different number from the equation at almost every rate inside the band. The golden does not gate Table 5-1 against the engine, because a band and a curve were never meant to agree at a point.

## Which way the bands lean

Three of the four band values sit above the equation at the band rate, by 0.524593, 0.542492 and 0.079422 C. One sits below, by 0.024291 C. A band above the equation is the more permissive number: a WBGT hour between the two would pass the band and fail the equation. So a hygienist who works from the band summary and one who works from the equation can reach different conclusions about the same hour, near the line, without either making a mistake.

The 349.000000 W row sits close to the worked example's 348.900000 W, and the band there, 28.000000 C, is close to the example's printed 27.800000 C. Three different routes through one document, the equation, the figure and the band, give three different limits for nearly the same work.

## What to write in a report

State which route produced the limit. If a site procedure uses the bands, say so, and quote the band as a band. If an assessment uses the equation, give the rate it was evaluated at and the transcription-only status. Never mix them: a margin computed against a band value and reported as a margin against the NIOSH heat REL is a margin against the wrong number.

## Why this matters more near the line

Far from any limit the choice of route changes nothing. A teaching hour whose WBGT is well below every version of the limit reads the same way on all three. Near the line, a few tenths of a degree decide the reading, and the table shows band and equation disagreeing by more than half a degree at two of the four band rates. That is the range in which a report has to be most explicit about which number it used.
