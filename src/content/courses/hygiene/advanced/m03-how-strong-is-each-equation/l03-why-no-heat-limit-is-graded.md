# Why no heat stress limit is graded

No graded field in this course passes through the RAL or the NIOSH heat REL, a margin against either, an exceedance verdict, or a WBGT built from globe, wet bulb and dry bulb readings. The two graded heat stress quantities are one-hour time weighted averages of readings a scenario states: the WBGT an instrument read out, and the metabolic rates. This lesson gives the three reasons, each measured.

## Reason one: the constants were copied, twice

The RAL constants 59.9 and 14.1, the NIOSH heat REL constants 56.7 and 11.5, and the WBGT weights 0.7 and 0.3 indoors, 0.7, 0.2 and 0.1 outdoors, are checked for transcription only. Each is the NIOSH 2016-106 section 8.1 equation or the section 9.3.2 weighting, checked for transcription only. The engine and the oracle each copied them from the same page. The provenance record states that planting the same error in both files left the suite green for the WBGT outdoor weights and for the NIOSH heat REL slope.

That result is the point. A graded answer that a shared misreading would still pass is graded on nothing. If both files held a wrong slope, every learner who evaluated the equation correctly would be marked right against a wrong key, and every learner who used the correct published slope would be marked wrong.

## Reason two: the document disagrees with itself

At 348.900000 W the section 8.1 equation, checked for transcription only, gives a NIOSH heat REL of 27.458939 C, and the section 1.1.3 worked example prints 27.800000 C, read off its figure. For the RAL the equation gives 24.047916 C and the example prints 25.000000 C. A learner who follows the document's figure would be marked wrong while doing exactly what the document shows.

## Reason three: the averages have nothing to copy

A time weighted average is ARITHMETIC BY DEFINITION. Its only constant is the sixty-minute window, so there is no page for two files to misread together. The teaching hour's 29.066667 C and 300.000000 W come out of the averaging and nothing else. That is why the averages are gradeable and the limits are not.

| quantity | evidence class | graded in this course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| one-hour WBGT of instrument readouts | ARITHMETIC BY DEFINITION | yes |
| one-hour metabolic rate | ARITHMETIC BY DEFINITION | yes |
| WBGT from thermometer readings | TRANSCRIPTION ONLY | never |
| RAL and NIOSH heat REL | TRANSCRIPTION ONLY | never |
| margin and exceedance verdict | TRANSCRIPTION ONLY | never |

## The same rule, applied elsewhere

The rule removes three more things, each oracle only with no printed value to set against it: the NIOSH hearing protector derating by type, the OSHA dual-protection 5 dB, and the Brief and Scala weekly factor on its own. Every verdict word is also left out, because the graded fields are numbers, and every licensed limit, because a limit here is always an input typed from a public value.

## What is taught instead

Every one of these is still taught. The equations appear in this tier as published, evaluated by the engine, with their status in the same sentence as every figure. The course asks you to know how strong each figure is while you use it.
