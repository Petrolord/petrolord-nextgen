# The one-hour average

{{panel:hy-heat-stress}}

The teaching hour is 30.800000 C of WBGT for 40.000000 minutes and 25.600000 C for 20.000000 minutes. Its time weighted WBGT is 29.066667 C over 60.000000 minutes. The plain mean of the two readings is 28.200000 C. The two numbers differ because the plain mean weights a twenty-minute rest the same as a forty-minute work period.

## Why an hour

NIOSH states its heat stress limits against a one-hour time weighted WBGT. A worker's body responds to the heat stress load over a period of work and rest, so a single reading at the hottest moment overstates the hour and a reading taken during a break understates it. The average over the hour is the quantity the limits are written against.

The engine's door is `wbgtTwaC`. It takes periods of WBGT and minutes, multiplies each reading by its minutes, sums, and divides by the total minutes.

| period | WBGT, C | minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 30.800000 | 40.000000 |
| 2 | 25.600000 | 20.000000 |

The golden's own case works the same way: 31.000000 C for 45.000000 minutes and 24.000000 C for 15.000000 minutes gives 29.250000 C.

## Arithmetic by definition

This door belongs to a different evidence class from the two weighting doors. The golden records `wbgtTwaC` as ARITHMETIC BY DEFINITION: a time weighted average whose only constant is the length of its window. There is nothing to transcribe beyond the averaging itself, so there is no page two people could both misread.

That is the property that makes the one-hour average safe to grade, and the two graded heat stress quantities in this course are exactly averages of this kind, taken of readings a scenario states. A WBGT figure an instrument reads out and a WBGT figure built from three thermometer readings through printed weights look the same on a report. Their evidence is different. The first passes only through the averaging. The second passes through the section 9.3.2 weights, which are checked for transcription only.

## The refusal

A record with no time in it has nothing to average, and the engine refuses it on `periods`:

> the periods total zero minutes: there is nothing to average

The door itself does not require exactly sixty minutes. It averages whatever window it is given. The sixty-minute requirement belongs to the NIOSH assessment door, which module two covers in its last lesson, and it is judgement J9.

## Reading the average against the mean

The difference between 29.066667 C and 28.200000 C is the whole reason to keep the minutes. A work pattern with a long hot period and a short cool one is dominated by the hot period, and the time weighted figure says so. A pattern with equal periods would make the two numbers agree, and only then.

## Exercise

Then do the same for the golden's case and confirm 29.250000 C. State in one sentence which evidence class the result belongs to and why no transcription-only constant enters it.
