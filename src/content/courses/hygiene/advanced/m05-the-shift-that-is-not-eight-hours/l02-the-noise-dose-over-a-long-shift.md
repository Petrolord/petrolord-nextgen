# The noise dose over a long shift

{{panel:hy-noise-dosimeter}}

Over the whole 10.000000 hours of the OLOMORO shift the action-level noise dose is 57.350093 percent, against a limit of 50. The PEL noise dose on the same record is 16.759307 percent. The TWA the action-level dosimeter reports is 85.989252 dBA, against a ten-hour action level of 83.390216 dBA. Rescaling the noise dose by 8 over 10 would give 45.880074 percent, which is not the quantity 1910.95 Appendix A defines.

## The record

The OLOMORO shift is four periods over ten hours. On the action-level setup the threshold is 80 dBA, inclusive by judgement J2, so the 78.900000 dBA period is not integrated. The other three contribute.

| period | level, dBA | hours | action-level contribution, percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 86.100000 | 3.100000 | 22.566713 |
| 2 | 83.400000 | 3.600000 | 18.024072 |
| 3 | 90.800000 | 1.200000 | 16.759307 |
| 4 | 78.900000 | 2.100000 | not integrated |

The three contributions add to 57.350093 percent. On the PEL setup, whose threshold is 90 dBA, only the 90.800000 dBA period is integrated, and its contribution is the same 16.759307 percent, because the two setups share the 90 dBA criterion level and the decibel exchange rate of 5 dB.

## The noise dose is not rescaled

Appendix A defines the noise dose as the sum, over every period of the shift, of the time at a level over the reference duration at that level. Nothing in that sum refers to eight hours. A ten-hour shift contributes ten hours of periods, and the noise dose is 57.350093 percent.

The tempting move is to scale the ten-hour noise dose back to an eight-hour equivalent, multiplying by 8 over 10. That gives 45.880074 percent, which reads under the 50 percent line. It is a number with no definition behind it. It answers the question of what the noise dose would have been had the worker left after eight hours at the same average, and the worker did not leave. The panel beside this lesson prints it beside the real figure and labels it as not a defined quantity.

## The two tests agree

Two comparisons are available: the noise dose of 57.350093 percent against 50, and the reported TWA of 85.989252 dBA against the ten-hour action level of 83.390216 dBA. Both say the shift is over the action level. They must agree, because both restate the same sum. The extended-shift action level is by construction the level that, held for the whole shift, gives exactly 50 percent.

What does not agree is the eight-hour action level. Comparing 85.989252 dBA with 85 dBA would reach the right verdict on this record by a margin of about one decibel, and it would reach it for the wrong reason. On a shift where the TWA sat between the eight-hour and the ten-hour action levels, the eight-hour comparison would pass a shift that the noise dose fails.

## Exercise

Add the three integrated contributions and confirm 57.350093 percent. Then multiply by 8 and divide by 10 to reproduce 45.880074 percent. Write one sentence explaining why the second figure is not a quantity Appendix A defines, and one sentence on which two comparisons must always agree for a ten-hour shift.
