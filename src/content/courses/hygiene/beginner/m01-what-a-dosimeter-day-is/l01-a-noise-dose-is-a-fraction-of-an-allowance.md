# A noise dose is a fraction of an allowance

One eight-hour dosimeter record, read against the OSHA PEL, gives a noise dose of 27.748183 percent. The same record read against the NIOSH noise REL gives 265.610944 percent. Neither figure is a mistake. A noise dose is a fraction of an allowance, the allowance belongs to a criterion, and so the number means nothing until the criterion is named.

This course calls that record the OBEN walkdown. It has six periods, each a sound level held for a stated number of hours, and almost every lesson in this tier reads it.

## The allowance is a time at a level

Each criterion says how long a worker may spend at a given sound level before the whole allowance is used. That time is the reference duration. On the OSHA PEL it is 8.000000 h at 90.000000 dBA, and on the NIOSH noise REL it is 8.000000 h at 85.000000 dBA. Spend the whole reference duration at one level and the noise dose is exactly 100 percent.

A period shorter than its reference duration uses part of the allowance. The third OBEN period sits at 94.600000 dBA for 0.800000 h. Its OSHA reference duration is 4.228072 h, so that period alone carries 18.921153 percent of the PEL noise dose. The day's noise dose is the sum of those parts, one term per period, written in percent.

## What the percentage says and what it leaves out

A noise dose of 100 percent means the allowance is used up exactly. Below 100 some of it remains, and above 100 the day has used more than one day's allowance. The percentage on its own says nothing about which period did the work or how loud the loudest moment was. The engine returns each period's contribution beside the total so that a reader can see where the percentage came from.

The engine behind this course exports 26 functions and 8 frozen tables, and every result it returns names the criterion or source it was computed against. That habit is the one this tier asks of you: write the criterion beside every noise dose you report.

## One word, used carefully

Other courses in this academy use the word dose for a corrosion inhibitor or a methanol injection. Here the quantity is always a noise dose, a percentage of a daily noise allowance, and a reading from the instrument is a sound level. Keeping the words apart stops a percentage from one course being read as a quantity from another.
