# What this tier leaves out

This tier has taught one metric family on one kind of record: the noise dose and its TWA, under three criteria, from 29 CFR 1910.95 Appendix A and NIOSH 98-126. The engine behind the course exports 26 functions, and this tier has used the handful that belong to the noise dose. The rest belong to the two tiers that follow, or to other courses, or to nobody in this academy.

## What the next two tiers teach

| tier | what it adds |
| --- | --- |
| Professional | the daily noise exposure level LEX,8h and exposure points, the weekly level, hearing protector estimates by method, the 8-hour chemical TWA, the short-term average and the mixture index |
| Expert | heat stress by WBGT and metabolic rate, the NIOSH heat REL and alert limit as published equations, the evidence behind every formula, errata in the sources, the shift that is not eight hours and the sampling decision |

None of those is needed to read a noise dose, and none of their figures belongs in an Associate answer. The LEX,8h in particular looks like a TWA and is a different quantity, an energy average with no threshold, so a number from that tier should never be set beside a number from this one as though they measured the same thing.

## What the course never grades

Some things are taught and never graded, in any tier. A verdict word is one: exceeds and passes depend on the criterion, and the course grades the numbers. A licensed limit is another: ACGIH values and ISO text are licensed, the engine embeds no limit table, and every limit is an input typed from a public OSHA or NIOSH value. The Expert tier explains the rest of the list, which is about heat stress equations and protector methods whose evidence is weaker.

The engine's judgement calls are taught by name and never tested at their boundary. No capstone input sits exactly on a threshold, so judgement J2 decides nothing in a graded answer. You still need to know it, because real records do put periods on the line.

## What belongs to other courses

Four subjects sit next to this one and are owned elsewhere. Cite them and stop.

- Flare and pool-fire thermal radiation belongs to the Separation and Relief courses. It is a radiant flux on a surface.
- BTEX as an emission from a glycol unit belongs to Gas Processing. A personal air sample is a different measurement.
- Incident and injury rates belong to Safety Performance Statistics, the first course of this module.
- The risk matrix belongs to Risk, Change and Learning.

## What the engine does not provide at all

Some questions have no door. The engine offers no octave-band protector method, no ISO 9612 uncertainty budget and no licensed limit table. When a question needs one of those, the honest answer is that this engine does not compute it.
