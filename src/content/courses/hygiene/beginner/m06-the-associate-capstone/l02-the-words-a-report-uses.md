# The words a report uses

A noise dose of 265.610944 percent on the NIOSH noise REL and a noise dose of 27.748183 percent on the OSHA PEL describe the same day. The numbers are only half of the report. The other half is the words around them, and in an academy where several courses share vocabulary, a careless word can make a correct number read as something else entirely.

This tier follows five rules, legislated before any lesson was written and binding on every lesson, question and panel.

## Five collisions

| write | never bare | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| noise dose | dose | Flow Assurance and Gas Processing dose inhibitors and methanol |
| noise exposure, chemical exposure, heat exposure | exposure | Field Development Planning, Uncertainty and Well Cost use it for money |
| sound level | noise, for a reading | Decline Curve Analysis, Well Test and Seismolord use noise for scatter in data |
| decibel exchange rate | exchange rate | the Economics courses have currency |
| heat stress | heat | Heat Transfer has heat duty; Separation and Relief have flare radiation |

Each rule costs a word or two and removes a whole class of misreading. A learner who has just finished a corrosion inhibitor course and reads "the dose was 72.054478 percent" has every reason to picture a chemical injection.

## Two misreadings on the same footing

"REL" means two different limits in this course. In this tier it is the NIOSH recommended limit for noise, with its 85.000000 dBA criterion level and 3 dB decibel exchange rate. In the Expert tier it is the NIOSH recommended limit for heat stress, a temperature. Always write "NIOSH noise REL" or "NIOSH heat REL".

"Action level" is the OSHA hearing conservation trigger, a TWA of 85 dBA or a noise dose of 50 percent. The European and UK rules use "action values", which are daily noise exposure levels of a different kind, taught in the Professional tier. The words look alike and the metrics are different, so never swap one for the other.

## Verdict words

The engine reports `exceedsLimit` true or false, and it is tempting to write "exceeds" or "passes" as the headline. This course never grades a verdict word. A verdict is only as good as the criterion behind it, and on the OBEN day the same record is false on one criterion and true on two others. If a report gives a verdict, it names the criterion and the limit noise dose in the same sentence: "72.054478 percent against the OSHA action level limit of 50 percent".

## Licensed limits

Some widely used noise limits are published under licence, and this course never quotes one. Every limit in the engine is an input typed from a public OSHA or NIOSH value, and the three presets in this tier are exactly those.

The same care applies to units. Write the dBA on every level and every TWA, and write percent on every noise dose. A level read in dBC is a different weighting and should never be written as though it were dBA.

## Exercise

Rewrite this sentence so that it breaks none of the rules above: "The REL dose was 265.610944 percent, so the exposure fails." Use the figures from this lesson, name the criterion in full, and give the limit noise dose it was read against. Then write a second sentence reporting the OSHA PEL figure of 27.748183 percent for the same day in the same style.
