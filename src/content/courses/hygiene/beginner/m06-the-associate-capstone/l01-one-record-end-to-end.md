# One record, end to end

{{panel:hy-noise-dosimeter}}

The OBEN record, walked from the instrument download to the report, produces six numbers on the OSHA PEL alone: 2 of 6 periods integrated, contributions of 18.921153 and 8.827030 percent, a noise dose of 27.748183 percent, a TWA of 80.752126 dBA and 173.404361 minutes left at 95 dBA. This lesson puts every step of this tier in order on that one record.

## The steps

| step | what you do | on the OBEN day, OSHA PEL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | name the criterion | OSHA PEL: 90.000000 dBA, 5.000000 dB, threshold 90.000000 dBA |
| 2 | drop periods below the threshold | 2 of the 6 periods integrated |
| 3 | find each reference duration | 4.228072 h and 2.265768 h |
| 4 | divide hours by reference duration | 18.921153 and 8.827030 percent |
| 5 | add the contributions | 27.748183 percent |
| 6 | restate as a TWA with 16.61 | 80.752126 dBA |
| 7 | find the time left at a level | 173.404361 minutes at 95 dBA |

## Step one decides the rest

Naming the criterion first is the habit that makes the rest mean something. Change step one to the OSHA action level and step two lets in 5 periods; the noise dose becomes 72.054478 percent and the TWA 87.635749 dBA. Change it to the NIOSH noise REL and every reference duration shortens; the noise dose becomes 265.610944 percent and the TWA 89.242460 dBA. The arithmetic in steps three to six never changes. The criterion changes every number it touches.

## Checks along the way

Before step two, look for refusals. A record whose periods total more than 24 hours is refused under judgement J4, and a period with a missing level or a negative duration is refused by position. Before step five, look for warnings. A period above 115 dBA or above 130 dBA still integrates under judgement J3, and its warning belongs in the report beside the number. At step two, remember judgement J2: a period exactly on the threshold counts.

At step six, remember judgement J1. The TWA uses the coefficient the source prints, 16.61 on OSHA and 10.0 on NIOSH. A hand check with the exact coefficient will differ slightly on NIOSH, and now you know why.

## What the report says

A report of the OBEN day on the PEL gives the criterion, the noise dose, the TWA and, if asked, the time left at a named level. It lists the contributions so a reader can see that the 94.600000 dBA period carries more than the 99.100000 dBA one. It carries any warning verbatim. It does not end in a verdict word standing alone; if a comparison with the limit is given, the criterion's limit noise dose is written beside it.

Keep the working as well as the answer. A reader who can see the reference durations and the contributions can check your arithmetic in a minute, and one who sees only the noise dose has to take it on trust.
