# The loudest period is one term

{{panel:hy-noise-dosimeter}}

The loudest OBEN period is 99.100000 dBA for 0.200000 h. It carries 31.811200 percent of the PEL noise dose. The rest comes from the other integrated period. The loudest reading on a dosimeter is one term in a sum, and on this record it is the smaller of the two terms the PEL counts.

Sound level meters and dosimeters both report a maximum, and it is the number people remember from a walkdown. This lesson is about why the maximum alone cannot tell you the noise dose.

## One term among several

| criterion | contribution of the 99.100000 dBA period, percent | day's noise dose, percent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OSHA PEL | 8.827030 | 27.748183 |
| OSHA action level | 8.827030 | 72.054478 |
| NIOSH noise REL | 64.980192 | 265.610944 |

On the PEL the loudest period's 8.827030 percent is 31.811200 percent of the day's 27.748183. The larger term belongs to the third period, 0.800000 h at 94.600000 dBA, which carries 18.921153 percent. Quieter, but four times as long.

## Why the loudest period is not the biggest

A contribution is hours over reference duration. The loudest period has the shortest reference duration, 2.265768 h on OSHA, so each minute there costs the most. It also has the fewest minutes. The third period's reference duration is 4.228072 h, a little under twice as long, and it lasts four times as long, so it wins.

On NIOSH the balance shifts but the lesson holds. The loudest period's reference duration there is 0.307786 h, and it carries 64.980192 percent, a big share. The third period still carries more, 91.895868 percent, and the second period, 1.900000 h at 89.800000 dBA, carries 71.996537 percent. Three periods on the NIOSH noise REL each carry more than half a day's allowance, and the loudest is the smallest of the three.

## What the maximum is good for

A maximum level is still worth writing down. It tells you whether a table top or a ceiling warning is in play, as the previous module showed, and it tells you where hearing protection matters most for the minutes it lasts. What it cannot do is stand in for the sum. Two records with the same maximum can carry very different noise doses, depending on everything else the day held.

When you read a dosimeter download, find the maximum, then find the largest contribution. They are often different periods, and the largest contribution is the one a control should reach for first.

The panel lets you test this directly. Choose a criterion and read the contribution column beside the level column. Sorting the periods by level and sorting them by contribution give two different orders on the OBEN day, on every criterion.
