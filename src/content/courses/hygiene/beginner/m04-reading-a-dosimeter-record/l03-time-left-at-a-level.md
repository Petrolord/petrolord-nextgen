# Time left at a level

{{panel:hy-noise-dosimeter}}

After the OBEN day the PEL noise dose stands at 27.748183 percent, so 72.251817 percent of the allowance remains. At 95 dBA the OSHA reference duration is 4.000000 h. The time left at 95 dBA is therefore 2.890073 h, which is 173.404361 minutes.

That is a derived quantity, the fraction remaining times the reference duration, and it answers the question a supervisor actually asks: how much longer can this worker spend in a louder area today before the PEL allowance is gone?

## The arithmetic in order

| step | quantity | value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | PEL noise dose after the day, percent | 27.748183 |
| 2 | allowance remaining, percent | 72.251817 |
| 3 | OSHA reference duration at 95 dBA, hours | 4.000000 |
| 4 | time left at 95 dBA, hours | 2.890073 |
| 5 | time left at 95 dBA, minutes | 173.404361 |

The arithmetic follows the definition of the noise dose. A contribution is hours over reference duration. Turn that round: the hours that would add a given share are that share times the reference duration. The share available is whatever the day has not already used.

## The criterion travels with the answer

The time left is only as good as the criterion behind it. The same record has used 72.054478 percent of the action level, whose limit is 50, so on that criterion nothing is left at all. On the NIOSH noise REL the day has used 265.610944 percent, already more than two and a half allowances. A figure of 173.404361 minutes belongs to the OSHA PEL and to nothing else, and it should never be quoted without saying so.

The level matters as much. At 95 dBA the reference duration is exactly 4.000000 h because 95 is one decibel exchange rate above the PEL criterion level of 90. At 100 dBA the OSHA reference duration is 2.000000 h, so the same remaining allowance buys half the time. At 90 dBA it is 8.000000 h and buys twice as much.

## The threshold still applies

A level below the criterion's threshold never uses allowance, so it has no time left to compute. On the PEL that means any level under 90.000000000000 dBA. Asking for the time left at such a level is asking a question the criterion does not frame. The engine's reference duration door answers it by reporting the level as below the threshold, and the time left should be reported the same way.

## What this number is not

Time left at a level is a planning figure for the rest of one day on one criterion. It assumes the rest of the day adds nothing else, and it assumes the new level is steady. A real afternoon rarely obliges on either count, so a hygienist uses the figure as a ceiling on planning and checks it against the next dosimeter record.

Write the level and the criterion into the same sentence as the minutes. "173.404361 minutes left at 95 dBA on the OSHA PEL" can be checked and acted on. A bare count of minutes cannot.

## Exercise

Compare your answer with the 173.404361 minutes at 95 dBA and say why the ratio between the two is exactly what the decibel exchange rate predicts.
