# Unsampled time counts as zero

{{panel:hy-protection-chemicals}}

The IGBOMOTORU partial record covers 6.750000 hours. Its 8-hour TWA is 30.125000 ppm. Averaged over the hours it covers, the same samples give 35.703704 ppm, a derived figure and a higher one. The engine returns the first and warns.

| record | hours covered | 8-hour TWA, ppm | average over the hours covered, ppm | warnings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| partial shift | 6.750000 | 30.125000 | 35.703704 | 1 |
| full shift | 8.000000 | 32.468750 | 32.468750 | 0 |
| ten-hour shift | 10.000000 | 42.156250 | 33.725000 | 1 |

## What the regulation writes

1910.1000(d)(1) divides the sum of concentration x time by 8. A record shorter than eight hours is still divided by 8, so the time nobody sampled enters the average as zero chemical exposure. The engine does what the regulation writes, and the judgement call J6 names it. It also says so, in its own words:

> the periods cover 6.75 h of 8: the remainder counts as zero exposure

## Two numbers, two claims

The partial record's samples are 38.000000 ppm for 2.500000 h, 64.000000 ppm for 1.250000 h and 22.000000 ppm for 3.000000 h. Divided by 8 they give 30.125000 ppm. Divided by the 6.750000 hours covered they give 35.703704 ppm. The first figure claims the unsampled hours were clean. The second claims those hours looked like the hours that were sampled. Neither claim is in the record.

A hygienist who knows the unsampled time was clean, because the worker left the area or the process stopped, may accept the first. One who does not know has a record that cannot say. The honest report states the hours covered beside the TWA and says what was assumed about the rest.

## The full shift settles it

When the fourth sample, 15.000000 ppm for 1.250000 h, is taken, the record covers 8.000000 hours and both averages read 32.468750 ppm. The unsampled time was not zero, and the true 8-hour TWA sits between the two figures the partial record offered. That is one case and it proves nothing general, but it shows why the warning exists: the zero is an assumption, and a measurement can overturn it.

## A shift longer than eight hours

The ten-hour record covers 10.000000 hours and is still divided by 8, giving 42.156250 ppm, well above its average of 33.725000 ppm over the hours worked. The engine warns again:

> the periods cover 10 h: the whole shift is divided by 8 as the regulation writes it, consider an unusual shift adjustment of the limit

The longer shift is a larger chemical exposure, and dividing by 8 says so, the same way the daily noise exposure level charges a long day against eight hours. Adjusting the limit for an unusual shift is taught in the Expert tier. Here, report the 8-hour TWA, the hours covered and the warning together.
