## One pattern under all six

Every average in this tier divides by a fixed reference, whatever the record covers. The daily noise exposure level divides by 8 hours, so the long EVWRENI day reads 87.207845 dBA where its own hours give 86.026852. The weekly level divides by 5 days. The chemical TWA divides by 8 hours, so the partial record reads 30.125000 ppm where its own hours give 35.703704. The STEL divides by 15 minutes. Learn the pattern once and every metric in this tier behaves as you expect: more time than the reference raises the figure, and less time counts the rest as nothing.

## Two different scales

The noise exposure figures are energy averages on a decibel scale, where three decibels roughly doubles the energy and the loudest task or day dominates. The chemical figures are arithmetic averages of concentrations, where every ppm counts the same. Do not carry intuitions across. A short loud task decides a LEX,8h; a short high sample moves a chemical TWA only in proportion to its minutes and its concentration.

## Name the criterion every time

A figure without its criterion is not a finding. "86.646970 dBA" means nothing until you add LEX,8h and the EU action values. "30.125000 ppm" means nothing until you add the 8-hour TWA, the hours covered and the limit it was set against. The Associate tier showed one dosimeter record reading three different noise doses under three criteria. This tier adds three more metrics and two more kinds of average, and the rule is the same.

## Warnings travel with the figure

Three of the six figures above come with a condition. The partial chemical record carries a warning that unsampled time counts as zero. The short STEL record carries a warning that its remainder counts as zero. The protector estimate carries its method. A report that drops any of those has quoted a number and lost the meaning.
