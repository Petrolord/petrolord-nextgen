The capstone for this tier asks you to work one site's noise exposure and chemical records end to end and to record six figures. Every one of them comes from a formula this tier reproduced against a printed value or built from an average whose only constant is its window.

| graded figure | the tier's own worked case | its evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a daily LEX,8h | L108 Figure 26, 86.613302 dBA | published, reproduced |
| a weekly LEX | the five-day week, 85.461288 dBA | the energy average of the daily LEX |
| a field derated protector estimate | the OTM example, 89.000000 dBA | published, reproduced |
| an 8-hour chemical TWA | 1910.1000(d)(1), 81.250000 ppm | published, reproduced |
| a STEL | the full window, 153.333333 ppm | arithmetic by definition |

## What it grades and why those things

It grades numbers, never a verdict word. It grades the field derating because the OSHA Technical Manual prints a value that fixes it. It does not grade the NIOSH derating by type or dual protection, because nothing printed fixes either. The weekly LEX is graded only where the statutory divisor of 5 equals the number of days given, so no constant the evidence cannot fix can move the answer.

## The conditions are stated

The brief states every sound level, duration, concentration, limit and label you need, and it declares how any time a record does not cover is to be counted. Follow what it states. Do not add a threshold to a LEX, do not divide a chemical record by its own hours, and do not trim or stretch a STEL window.

## How to work it

Build the daily LEX first from its tasks, then the weekly LEX from the days. Take the protector estimate by the field derating and name the method beside it. Compute the chemical TWA and the STEL by their fixed divisors, then the mixture terms and their sum. The course's precision convention is six decimals for concentrations, levels, durations and every dimensionless ratio, and twelve for a measured constant. It sets no rounding rule for your own working, so take this as practice: keep every intermediate figure at full precision and round only at the end, to those six decimals.

## Before you submit

Check each figure against the criterion it answers. A daily level in dBA, a weekly level in dBA, a protected estimate in dBA for the engineering controls question, two chemical averages in ppm and a dimensionless index. If a figure comes back from the panels with a warning, read the warning before you record the number. Then check the units once more: a level entered in the wrong weighting, or a duration typed in minutes where hours are asked for, gives a clean-looking number that answers nothing.

## Exercise

Before you open the capstone, rework three of the tier's own cases from the table by hand: L108 Figure 26 to 86.613302 dBA, the OSHA Technical Manual field derating to 89.000000 dBA, and the published mixture to 0.925000. For each, state the formula you used and the fixed reference or constant it rests on.
