# What a protector estimate is for

{{panel:hy-protection-chemicals}}

The OSHA Technical Manual worked example gives 98 dBA with an NRR of 25 as 89.000000 dBA under the field derating and 80.000000 dBA under Appendix B. The teaching case gives 87.600000 and 77.600000 dBA. In both pairs the protector and the noise exposure are the same, and the two answers differ by the question asked.

| question | method | OTM example, dBA | teaching case, dBA | evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| are engineering controls needed | OSHA_FIELD_50 | 89.000000 | 87.600000 | published, reproduced |
| is the protector adequate for hearing conservation | OSHA_APPENDIX_B | 80.000000 | 77.600000 | published, reproduced |
| what does NIOSH credit this type with | NIOSH_TYPE | none printed | 84.350000, earmuff | oracle only |
| what do two protectors give | OSHA_DUAL | none printed | 72.600000 | oracle only |

## Start from the question

Before you choose a method, write down the decision the estimate is for. If the decision is whether the employer must reduce the sound at source, the field derating answers it. If the decision is whether a protector already chosen is adequate within a hearing conservation programme, Appendix B answers it. If the decision is which kind of protector to issue, the NIOSH derating by type says how the kinds compare, with the evidence status it carries. Quote the method with the number every time.

## An estimate is an estimate

None of these figures is a measurement at the ear. Each is a label credit applied to a workplace level by a rule. The EU limit value of 87.000000 dBA applies to the noise exposure at the ear with protection taken into account, and the engine does not compute that. So a protector estimate is never compared with the EU limit value in this course, and never written as a verdict on one.

## Controls come first

The field derating is deliberately harsh on a high label, because the regulation's order of preference puts reducing the sound at source ahead of protecting the ear. On the teaching case the field derating leaves the protected estimate at 87.600000 dBA from a sound level of 97.600000 dBA, a credit of 10.000000 dB against the 20.000000 dB Appendix B would give the same protector. A hygienist who quotes the Appendix B figure to argue that engineering controls are unnecessary has answered the wrong question with the more generous rule.

## The evidence behind each row

Two methods are published and reproduced, and the OSHA Technical Manual's printed 89.000000 and 80.000000 dBA are the values that make them so. The NIOSH derating by type and the dual-protection 5 dB are oracle only: the engine and an independent oracle agree, and no printed value is known to set against either. Neither is ever graded in this course. When you report an oracle-only figure, say so.
