# When additivity is the wrong model

The teaching mixture's index is 1.059500 and its terms are 0.362500, 0.312000 and 0.385000. Whether that mixture exceeds depends on a question the arithmetic cannot answer: do the three components act on the same organ by the same mechanism? The index assumes they do. The engine computes the additive index only, and it cannot tell which case a mixture is in.

| how the components act | what to compare | the teaching mixture reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| on the same organ by the same mechanism | the sum of the terms against 1 | 1.059500, exceeds |
| independently | each term against 1 on its own | largest term 0.385000 |
| one potentiates another | the sum understates the hazard | above 1.059500, by an unknown amount |

## Three cases

Where the components act on the same organ by the same mechanism, their effects add, and the index is the right model. Where they act independently, on different organs or by different routes, each is compared against its own limit and the sum means nothing. Where one is known to potentiate another, so that together they do more harm than either alone, the sum understates the hazard, and a mixture below unity can still be a problem.

The engine offers one model because only one is written into 1910.1000(d)(2). The judgement of which case applies belongs to the hygienist, and it rests on toxicology the engine does not hold. No setting in the panel changes the model, so the choice has to be made, and written down, outside it.

## Reading the teaching mixture three ways

On the additive reading, the teaching mixture exceeds at 1.059500. On the independent reading, every component is under its own limit and the largest term is 0.385000. The two readings reach opposite conclusions from the same three samples. On a potentiating reading, the true figure is above 1.059500 by an amount no formula in this course supplies. The same record can therefore support three different findings, and the difference lies wholly in the assumption about mechanism.

## What to write when you are not sure

Default to the additive index where the regulation writes it, because it is the rule the source prints and the one an inspector will apply. State the assumption beside the figure: "additive index 1.059500, on the assumption that the three components act by the same mechanism". If there is evidence that the components act independently, report the terms as well, so the reader can see each one against its own limit. If there is evidence of potentiation, say that the index understates the hazard and that the figure is a floor.

## Where this course stops

A mixture that includes aromatic solvents also raises questions about emissions from process plant. BTEX as an emission from a glycol unit is owned by the Gas Processing course, and it is a different measurement of a different quantity from a personal air sample and its 8-hour TWA. Take that question there. This tier's concern is the person's breathing zone, the averages built from it, and the index those averages feed.
