# The neutral point is not a place

Three definitions, and the one that matters for buckling.

## The three

**The force neutral point.** Where axial tension is zero. Above it the string is in tension, below it in compression.

**The stress neutral point.** Where the axial stress equals the hydrostatic stress from the mud, so the pipe is in a state of pure hydrostatic compression rather than being loaded axially. It sits at a different depth from the force one.

**The buckling neutral point.** Where the EFFECTIVE compression, which includes the buoyancy contribution properly, first exceeds the buckling limit. Different again.

## Why three exist

Because "neutral" is a word rather than a definition, and three different questions all deserve one.

For a stress analysis, the stress definition is the right one. For a buckling check, the effective-compression definition is. For a torque and drag calculation, which asks whether the string is pushing or pulling, the force definition is.

This course uses the force definition throughout, and the buckling check compares the force-based compression against a limit derived on the same basis.

## Why it moves

The force neutral point moves with the operation.

On the vertical well rotating on bottom it is at 1940 m, because 89000 N of compression at the bit is carried by the bottom 60 m of drill collars.

Tripping out on the same well there is no compression anywhere, so there is no neutral point at all.

On the horizontal well sliding, the minimum tension is -422023.82665557245 N and the flag is at 0 m, meaning the compression is not confined near the bit at all: friction in the lateral is generating it along the whole length.

## The traditional rule, and where it fails

The rule is to carry the weight on bit entirely in drill collars, so the drill pipe is never in compression, with a safety margin.

It was written for vertical wells and it works there, because in a vertical well compression comes only from the weight on bit and nothing else.

In a horizontal well it fails completely. Compression there is generated by drag along the lateral, and no amount of collar at the bit removes it. The remedy is heavy weight drill pipe distributed along the lateral, or not sliding at all.

## The practical consequence

Asking "where is the neutral point" on a horizontal well is asking a question with no useful answer.

The right question is "over what interval is the string in compression, and how far past its buckling limit is it there", and the profile answers that directly.
