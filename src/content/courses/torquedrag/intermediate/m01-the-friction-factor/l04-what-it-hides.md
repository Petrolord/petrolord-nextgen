# What it hides

Four physical situations that all present as a high friction factor.

## Cuttings beds

In a hole above about 30 degrees, cuttings settle to the low side and form a bed. The string has to plough through it.

That force is not proportional to the normal force between the string and the wall, so it is not friction in any physical sense, and putting it into a friction factor is a fiction that happens to work.

It presents as a factor that rises with penetration rate, falls when you circulate, and falls when you rotate faster. None of those is how a coefficient of friction behaves.

## Differential sticking

The string lies against a permeable formation with mud pressure higher than pore pressure, and the pressure differential clamps it to the filter cake.

The force is proportional to the contact AREA and the differential pressure, and it grows with time in contact. It presents as a factor that rises the longer the string is stationary and drops when the string is worked.

## A ledge or a keyseat

A local geometric feature that the survey does not describe. The string hangs up on it at a particular depth.

It presents as a factor that appears suddenly and is depth-specific: hookload is normal until the bottom hole assembly reaches a certain depth, then jumps.

That depth specificity is the giveaway, and it is why plotting drag against depth is more useful than quoting one fitted factor.

## A stiff assembly in a dogleg

The soft-string model spread the contact force smoothly. A real stiff assembly concentrates it at the tangency points.

It presents as a factor that is higher when the bottom hole assembly is in a build section and lower when it is in a tangent, with no change in the hole at all.

## Why all four matter

Because the remedy is different for each, and the fitted factor does not distinguish them.

Cuttings need circulation and rotation. Differential sticking needs the string kept moving and the overbalance reduced. A ledge needs reaming at a specific depth. A stiff assembly needs a different assembly or a gentler dogleg.

Acting on a high factor without diagnosing which of these produced it is guessing.

## How to tell them apart

By what the factor does over TIME and over DEPTH, which a single fitted value throws away.

- Rises with rate of penetration, falls on circulation: cuttings.
- Rises with stationary time, falls on working: differential sticking.
- Appears at one depth and stays: geometry.
- Follows the assembly's position rather than the depth: stiffness.

That is the real skill of the Professional tier, and it needs the calibration done repeatedly rather than once.
