# The tangent that costs nothing

A straight section at angle, and why it is the cheapest hole to drill.

## The claim

A straight section, whatever its inclination, contributes NO curvature side force. Its only side force is the weight term, w sin(theta), and that is the smallest side force a deviated hole can have.

## The comparison

Consider two wells reaching the same target displacement:

**A** kicks off shallow, builds gently to 40 degrees, and holds a long tangent.

**B** stays vertical longer, then builds hard to 65 degrees and holds a short tangent.

B has less total measured depth. A has less total side force, because its build is gentler and its tangent is longer.

The slant well and the build-and-hold well in this course are roughly that comparison, and their worst side forces tripping in are 828.0432175391106 and 1167.5116395360324 N per metre.

## Why the tangent is cheap

In a tangent the string lies on the low side under its own weight and nothing pulls it harder into the wall. The side force per metre is a fixed w sin(theta), independent of how much tension is passing through.

So a tangent's contribution to total drag grows LINEARLY with its length and does not compound with depth. That is the opposite of a dogleg's behaviour.

## The catch

A tangent still costs measured depth, and measured depth costs weight, which costs tension everywhere above it.

And a long tangent at high inclination is where cuttings beds form, which raises the effective friction factor in exactly the section that has the most contact length.

So the cheapest hole geometrically is not always the cheapest operationally, and the hole-cleaning consideration usually caps the hold angle rather than the drag calculation.

## The design rule this supports

Build early, build gently, hold long.

That is the standard extended reach prescription and it comes directly out of the two side-force terms: gentle build minimises the curvature term where tension is high, and an early build puts what curvature there is where tension is low.

## What breaks the rule

A shallow build has to be drilled in soft formation, which does not hold a tangent direction well, and it puts the whole well at angle earlier, which means more hole to clean.

And a target that has to be reached vertically, or a shallow hazard to avoid, can force a shape that the drag calculation dislikes.
