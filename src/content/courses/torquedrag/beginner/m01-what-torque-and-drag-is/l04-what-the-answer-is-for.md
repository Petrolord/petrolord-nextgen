# What the answer is for

Six decisions that wait on a torque and drag run.

## Can the well be drilled at all

The most basic use. A trajectory that closes geometrically may still be one where the string will not reach bottom, or where the torque at total depth exceeds what the top drive can supply.

That check happens at the planning stage, before the rig is contracted, and it can change the well plan. The Expert tier's extended reach lesson is entirely about where that limit sits.

## What rig you need

Hookload sets the derrick rating and the drawworks. Torque sets the top drive. Both are contract terms, and both are set by a calculation rather than by measurement, because the well has not been drilled yet.

## Whether the casing will go in

A casing string is stiffer and heavier than a drill string and it cannot rotate freely. Running casing to bottom in a long reach well is often the binding constraint on the whole design, and the run is planned with the same model.

## What is wrong, while you are drilling

This is the use that matters day to day. The driller records the pick-up, slack-off and rotating hookloads at each connection. The model predicts them. When the measured values start departing from the predicted ones, something has changed in the hole: cuttings beds, a ledge, differential sticking, a washout.

A torque and drag model calibrated on the first thousand metres becomes an early warning system for the next three thousand. That is worth more than the planning use.

## How much of the casing is left

Rotating a tool joint against casing takes metal off it. The side force that produced the torque also produces the wear, and the Expert tier computes it. A worn casing string has a lower burst rating than the one that was run, and nobody can see it.

## Whether the string will fail

Tension and torque out of the model, capacities out of the pipe specification, and a utilization ratio between them. The answer is often surprising: on a horizontal well the pipe is near its torsional limit and nowhere near its tensile one, which is the opposite of the vertical well the ratings were written for.

## What none of it is

None of these is a measurement of the rock. The model has one calibrated number in it, the friction factor, and everything downstream inherits whatever that number absorbed.

That is not a reason to distrust the answers. It is a reason to know which parts of them are geometry, which are physics, and which are a fitted constant.

## Exercise

For each of the six uses above, write one sentence saying whether it needs an ABSOLUTE hookload or a CHANGE in hookload.

The answers are not all the same, and the distinction determines how much the friction factor's uncertainty actually matters in each case.
