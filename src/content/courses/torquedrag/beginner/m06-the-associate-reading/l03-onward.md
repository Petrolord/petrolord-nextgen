# Onward

From a hookload to the constant inside it.

## What the Professional tier adds

Everything in this tier took the friction factor as given: 0.25 in casing, 0.35 in open hole, no discussion.

The Professional tier is about that number. Where it comes from, what it actually multiplies, why it is calibrated rather than measured, and what a fitted value quietly absorbs on its way to becoming a prediction.

## What you will find there

**Torque properly.** Why rotating splits the friction, which radius carries the moment, and why using the pipe body radius instead of the tool joint radius understates the torque by a third.

**Side force where it is largest.** On the build-and-hold well the worst side force while tripping in is 1167.5116395360324 N per metre, and it is not at total depth.

**Calibration, and its danger.** Backing a friction factor out of an observed hookload always succeeds. The bisection converges on any target you give it, which means it will happily absorb a survey error, a wrong string description, a wrong mud weight or a missing physical effect, and then predict a torque that inherits all of them.

**The oracle comparison.** The goldens this course checks against were generated by an independent implementation. Over more than a hundred published values the worst relative disagreement is about seven percent and the worst absolute one is about 1632 N, both on the horizontal well. Refining the step halves the horizontal gap and does not move the slant one, which separates discretisation from a genuine model difference. And the vertical well, with its closed form, says which of the two is right.

## Before you go

Two habits from this tier.

**Run the degenerate case first.** The vertical well takes one run and catches unit errors, sign errors and setup errors before anything interesting is trusted.

**Use differences, not absolutes, for hole condition.** The absolute hookload grows every connection because the string is getting longer. The drag is what carries the information.
