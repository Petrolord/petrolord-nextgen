# What the model is for

Three decisions, and nothing else.

## The first: what mud weight

The primary output. Between the pressure that keeps the hole from collapsing and the pressure that splits it open, what should be in the hole at each depth.

Everything in this course leads to that answer, and the Expert tier walks it along a whole trajectory.

## The second: what trajectory

Less obvious and often more valuable. The window depends on the direction the hole is drilled, sometimes by a large fraction of itself.

The Professional tier shows a case where turning a hole from vertical to horizontal along the maximum horizontal stress costs about a quarter of the available window, with no rock property changing at all. Turning it the other way costs much less.

If the target allows a choice of approach azimuth, geomechanics is what makes the choice.

## The third: where to set casing

The same argument the well control course made from a different direction. A section has to be cased off before the mud weight it needs exceeds the mud weight it can stand.

Well control asks whether the shoe can hold a kick. Geomechanics asks whether the open hole can stand the mud. The tighter of the two answers sets the casing point.

## What it is not for

**It is not a fracture design tool.** Fracture initiation here is the pressure at which the wall goes into tension, which is a wellbore stability limit. Propagating a fracture into the far field is the stimulation course.

**It is not a sand production model.** The same rock strength appears in both, and the failure being asked about is different.

**It is not a subsidence or compaction model.** Those need the reservoir's pressure history and a poroelastic model of a volume rather than of a hole.

## Who reads the output

The drilling engineer writing the mud programme, the well planner choosing the trajectory, and the casing designer choosing the shoe depths.

All three want a mud weight window against depth, and all three want to know which bound is doing the work at each depth, because the remedies differ.

## The shape of the deliverable

One chart per well: measured depth on one axis, equivalent mud weight on the other, with a lower bound, an upper bound and the planned mud weight between them.

The Expert tier builds exactly that chart, and it is the whole product.

## Exercise

For each of the three decisions, say what would go wrong on a well if nobody had done the calculation.

Then say which of the three is hardest to fix after the fact.
