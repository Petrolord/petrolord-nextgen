# The ramp below 1500 m

Where the profile stops being hydrostatic, and what changes.

{{panel:gm-stress-explorer}}

## The shape

| true vertical depth | pore pressure EMW |
|---|---|
| 50 m to 1500 m | 1030 kg/m3 |
| 1550 m | 1041.935483870968 kg/m3 |
| 1800 m | 1091.6666666666667 kg/m3 |
| 2050 m | 1129.2682926829268 kg/m3 |
| 2300 m | 1158.695652173913 kg/m3 |
| 2600 m | 1186.5384615384614 kg/m3 |

Flat, then a rise that steepens at first and then flattens again.

## What flat means

Hydrostatic. 1030 kg/m3 is a saline formation water, and a column of it connected to surface gives exactly this gradient.

A normally pressured section is one where the pore fluid has been able to escape upward as fast as the sediment compacted, so the fluid pressure is just the weight of the water above it.

## What the rise means

The escape stopped working. Below 1500 m the fluid is carrying part of the load that the grain framework would otherwise carry.

## The effect on effective stress

Effective vertical stress is the overburden less the pore pressure. In equivalent mud weight terms this profile has:

| depth | overburden | pore pressure | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 m | 2300 | 1030 | 1270 kg/m3 |
| 1500 m | 2300 | 1030 | 1270 kg/m3 |
| 2000 m | 2300 | 1122.5 | 1177.5 kg/m3 |
| 2600 m | 2300 | 1186.5384615384614 | 1113.4615384615386 kg/m3, which is 2300 less the pore pressure gradient |

The difference is CONSTANT above the ramp and FALLS below it.

## Why that is the important line

Because effective stress is what the rock feels. Above 1500 m the rock is being loaded more heavily with every metre of depth. Below it, the rate of loading slows and then reverses in these units.

Deeper rock in an overpressured section can be geomechanically weaker than shallower rock in a normally pressured one, which is not what depth alone would suggest.

## What it does to the mud window

It squeezes it from below. The pore pressure is one of the two candidates for the lower bound, and it is rising while the fracture gradient is rising more slowly.

The Expert tier finds one of the two wells in this course tightest at total depth for exactly that reason.

## What to watch for in the field

A rising pore pressure ramp is the classic setting for a casing point. It is also the setting where a kick and a loss can happen in the same hole section, which is the narrow-margin drilling problem.
