# The story so far

Five modules, two wells, and a parameter nobody measures deciding the answer.

## The claim

The mud window is a property of the whole trajectory, and the thing that decides how wide it is turns out to be the input with the least evidence behind it.

## What each module established

**Module 1.** A stability answer at one depth is not an answer. The walk steps 30 m of measured depth at a time, interpolates the profile onto each true vertical depth and runs the full calculation, giving 99 rows on the slant well and 92 on the horizontal one. The tightest row is what the mud programme is designed around, and it is at total depth on one well and at the KICK-OFF POINT on the other, 1020 m into a 2800 m hole. The window there narrows above the kick-off and widens below it, because the rock trend and the trajectory trend pull in opposite directions.

**Module 2.** The lower bound is the larger of the collapse gradient and the pore pressure, and which one wins changes everything downstream. The slant well is pore pressure bound at its tightest point, by 773.5521036358621 kg/m3, and its remedy is a casing point. The horizontal well is collapse bound at its, by 333.0134212003486 kg/m3, and its remedies include the trajectory, the open hole time and the mud chemistry as well as the mud weight.

**Module 3.** The Poisson ratio is the dominant sensitivity: sweeping it from 0.20 to 0.35, which is this course's own sandstone-to-shale seed range, moves the slant well's window by 956.2616032453875 kg/m3 and the horizontal well's by 1153.222084676116. Everything else moves it less, and the friction angle moves the slant well by EXACTLY ZERO because that well's tightest point is pore pressure bound and neither of its bounds contains a friction angle.

**Module 4.** The deliverable is a four-curve chart and a per-section table. This course's fracture gradient and pore pressure gradient go to well control, its casing point competes with the kick tolerance one and the shallower governs, and its window is STATIC so the circulating uplift, the surge and the swab all have to fit inside it.

**Module 5.** Five omissions: inelastic and anisotropic behaviour, time, chemistry, thermal stress, and natural fractures and bedding. The first makes the zero-breakout-width criterion very conservative; the second means open hole time cannot be represented at all; the last means the computed fracture gradient is an over-estimate for lost circulation wherever fractures exist.

## The numbers to carry

- The vertical closed forms, which are the check on everything: collapse at Pp plus (3 SH less Sh less UCS) over (1 plus q), and fracture at Pp plus (3 Sh less SH) plus T0, all in effective terms.
- Slant well tightest: 3000 m MD, window 1041.4600436889584 kg/m3, pore pressure bound.
- Horizontal well tightest: 1020 m MD, window 1364.1863204876872 kg/m3, collapse bound.
- The Poisson ratio moves the answer more than any other input, and it usually comes from a table.

## The one sentence

Which bound binds decides which parameter matters, and on these two wells that single fact reorders the whole list of what to measure.
