# Collapse, or pore pressure

Two candidates for the floor, and why the answer matters more than the number.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## The two

**Pore pressure.** Mud below it lets formation fluid into the well.

**Collapse pressure.** Mud below it lets the wall fail in shear.

The lower bound is the larger of the two, and the walk records both separately at every row.

## Why they are different in kind

The pore pressure is a property of the FLUID in the rock. It does not care about the hole, its direction, its size or the rock's strength.

The collapse pressure is a property of the ROCK and the HOLE. It depends on the strength, the stress field, the inclination, the azimuth and the friction angle.

## What that means for the remedies

**Pore pressure bound.** One lever: mud weight. The amount needed is known exactly and nothing else helps.

**Collapse bound.** Several levers. Mud weight helps, and so does a different trajectory, a shorter open-hole time, better hole cleaning, and a mud chemistry that does not weaken the shale.

## What that means for the uncertainty

**Pore pressure bound.** The uncertainty is the pore pressure's uncertainty, which is an upstream question and often well constrained by offset data.

**Collapse bound.** The uncertainty is the whole model's: the stresses, the Poisson ratio, the friction angle, the UCS correlation, the breakout width tolerated. Much larger, and much harder to reduce.

So a pore-pressure-bound window is a firmer number than a collapse-bound one of the same width.

## What that means for the consequence of being wrong

**Too light in a pore-pressure-bound section.** A kick. Detected quickly, procedures exist, and the well control course is the whole response.

**Too light in a collapse-bound section.** Cavings, then tight hole, then possibly stuck pipe. Slower to detect, and much more expensive to fix.

## Why a single lower bound hides all this

A report that gives one number has thrown away which of the two produced it, and with it the lever, the uncertainty and the failure mode.

Two columns instead of one. It costs nothing and it changes what a reader can do with the answer.
