# Different bounds, different remedies

The practical payoff of the whole module.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## The table

| | pore pressure bound | collapse bound |
|---|---|---|
| what fails | the well takes an influx | the wall fails in shear |
| first sign | flow out rises, pit gain | cavings, torque, tight hole |
| speed | minutes | hours to days |
| mud weight helps | yes, exactly | yes, and not exclusively |
| trajectory helps | no | often, substantially |
| drilling faster helps | no | yes, and the model cannot show it |
| mud chemistry helps | no | yes, and the model cannot show it |
| uncertainty | the pore pressure's | the whole model's |
| conservatism in the number | none | large: zero breakout width |

## The asymmetry

A pore pressure bound is a hard number with one remedy. A collapse bound is a soft number with several.

That asymmetry should change how each is used. A pore-pressure-driven floor is a specification. A collapse-driven floor is a recommendation with a stated conservatism, to be weighed against the other levers.

## What happens when they are treated the same

Two failure modes, in opposite directions.

**Over-conservatism.** A collapse gradient taken as a specification can push the mud weight up until it threatens the fracture gradient, closing a window that was never really that narrow.

**Complacency.** A collapse gradient dismissed as soft, in a section where it really is the binding constraint, produces the hole full of cavings.

The way to avoid both is to state the assumption: what breakout width was tolerated, and what the number would be at a different one.

## Where the crossover is on these wells

On the horizontal well, collapse binds from the top of the walk down to somewhere in the build, and the pore pressure takes over as the rock strengthens.

On the slant well the pore pressure binds at the bottom and the crossover is higher up, in the same weak shallow rock the other well has its problem in.

Both wells go through the same rock. They just have their tightest point on different sides of the crossover.

## What to write in the programme

For each section: the lower bound, WHICH candidate produced it, the upper bound, and the tolerated breakout width if collapse is binding.

Four items instead of two, and the reader can then act on the one that is actually constraining them.
