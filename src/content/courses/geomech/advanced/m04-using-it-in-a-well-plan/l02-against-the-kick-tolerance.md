# Against the kick tolerance

Two courses, one shoe depth, and they must agree.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## The two calculations

**This course** asks: what mud weight does the open hole need, and what can it stand?

**The well control course** asks: with that mud weight in the hole, how big an influx can be circulated out without fracturing the shoe?

They use the same fracture gradient and they answer different questions with it.

## Where they meet

At the casing point.

A section has to be cased off before EITHER calculation runs out of room. The shoe goes at the shallower of the two answers.

## The geomechanics answer

The depth at which the mud weight the open hole needs approaches the mud weight the open hole can stand. On the slant well the window narrows steadily to total depth and the trend continues below it.

## The well control answer

The depth at which the kick tolerance falls to the design kick size. That calculation uses the fracture gradient AT THE SHOE and the mud weight in the hole, and the well control course walks it against mud weight.

## Why they can disagree

Because they are limited by different things.

The geomechanics window closes when the pore pressure or the collapse gradient meets the fracture gradient IN THE OPEN HOLE.

The kick tolerance runs out when the shoe cannot take an influx, and the shoe is much shallower than the open hole. A shallow shoe with a low fracture gradient can run out of kick tolerance while the open hole below still has a comfortable window.

## Which usually binds

On a well with a rising pore pressure ramp, usually the kick tolerance, because the mud weight rises against a fixed shoe fracture gradient.

On a well with weak shallow rock, usually the geomechanics window, because the collapse gradient is the problem and it has nothing to do with the shoe.

## What this course hands over

The fracture gradient at every depth, which becomes the shoe's fracture equivalent mud weight once the shoe depth is chosen. And the pore pressure gradient, which sets the kill mud weight.

Both are inputs to the well control calculation, and both carry this course's uncertainty into it.

## The check to run

Compute both casing points and take the shallower. Then state which one governed, because it tells the next engineer which calculation to redo if anything changes.
