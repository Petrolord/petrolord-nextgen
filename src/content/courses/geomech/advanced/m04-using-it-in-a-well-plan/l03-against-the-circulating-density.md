# Against the circulating density

The mud weight is not what the formation feels.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## The gap

Everything in this course is a STATIC mud weight. The window bounds are pressures with the pumps off.

While circulating, the pressure at any depth is higher than the static value by the annulus friction above it. The Drilling Hydraulics course computes that as an equivalent circulating density.

## What that does to the window

It eats into it from the top.

The fracture gradient has to be compared against the EQUIVALENT CIRCULATING DENSITY, not against the static mud weight. So the usable window is narrower than the one this course produces, by the circulating uplift.

## How much

The hydraulics course's numbers are the answer, and the uplift depends on the flow rate, the mud rheology and the annulus geometry. At drilling rates in a tight annulus it is not small.

## What that means for the deliverable

A geomechanics window is an upper bound on the room available. The drilling engineer subtracts the circulating uplift from the top of it before choosing a mud weight.

Quoting the window without that caveat invites somebody to plan a mud weight right at the fracture gradient and then lose returns the moment the pumps come on.

## The other direction

Surge, from running pipe. The Drilling Hydraulics course computes that too, and it is another transient addition on top of the static mud weight.

A trip in at speed can exceed the fracture gradient with a mud weight that is comfortably inside the static window.

## And the other way again

Swab, from pulling pipe. That SUBTRACTS from the bottom hole pressure and takes the well toward the lower bound.

So the static window has to accommodate the static mud weight PLUS the circulating uplift PLUS the surge, and MINUS the swab. Four numbers in one window.

## The trip margin, from the other side

The well control course's trip margin exists to keep the well overbalanced against swab. This course says what that extra weight costs: it moves the mud weight up toward the fracture gradient and takes room out of the window.

Two courses, opposite pressures on the same decision, and the well plan has to hold both.

## The honest deliverable

A window, and a statement that it is static. The drilling engineer owns the transient corrections and needs to know which numbers already have them and which do not.
