# What goes in the programme

The whole output of this course, listed.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## Per hole section

- The **tightest point**: measured depth, true vertical depth, inclination.
- The **lower bound** there, and **which of the two candidates** produced it.
- The **upper bound** there.
- The **window width**.
- The **breakout angle** at the tightest point, in the borehole frame.
- The **tolerated breakout width** the collapse number assumes, which for this engine is zero.

## Per well

- The **four-curve chart** against measured depth for the whole trajectory.
- The **quality score and clamp count** on the stress model.
- A **sensitivity band** on at least the Poisson ratio, and on the stress azimuth if the tightest points are deviated.
- A statement of **what was measured and what was seeded**.

## What goes to other disciplines

**To well control:** the fracture gradient at each shoe and the pore pressure gradient, which are the two inputs its calculations take from here.

**To casing design:** the geomechanics casing point, to be compared against the kick tolerance one, with the shallower governing.

**To the well planner:** the window as a function of inclination and azimuth at the critical depths, so the trajectory decision can be made on evidence.

**To the drilling engineer:** the window, with a clear statement that it is STATIC and that the circulating uplift, the surge and the swab all have to be accommodated inside it.

## What does NOT go in

**A number without its provenance.** A Poisson ratio from a table produces a window that has to be labelled as such.

**A curve above the shallowest calibration depth.** The stress model extrapolated upward is not evidence, and this fixture's top 1150 m is the demonstration.

**A collapse gradient without a breakout width.** Zero width is a large conservatism and the reader has to know it is there.

## The one-page version

If the programme has room for one thing, it is the four-curve chart with the planned mud weight drawn on it and the tightest point marked.

Everything else is support for reading that chart.

## What the next well gets

The calibration. Where breakouts formed and how wide, where returns were lost and at what circulating density, and where the hole gave trouble.

Feed those back into the model, adjust it so it would have predicted them, and the next well starts from a calibrated model rather than a seeded one. That loop is the whole practice of field geomechanics.
