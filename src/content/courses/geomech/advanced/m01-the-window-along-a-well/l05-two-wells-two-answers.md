# Two wells, two answers

The same rock, and completely different problems.

## Four differences, all of them consequential

**Where.** Total depth against the kick-off point. Nearly 2000 m apart.

**Why.** Deep and pore-pressure limited against shallow and strength limited.

**How wide.** The horizontal well's tightest window is 31 percent wider than the slant well's, so on this measure it is the EASIER well.

**Which lever.** Different bounds, different remedies, which is the next module.

## What the slant well's answer says

Its problem is the pore pressure ramp. The window narrows steadily to total depth because the pore pressure keeps rising while the fracture gradient falls slowly with the effective stress.

That is a classic deep-well profile, and the remedy is a casing point.

## What the horizontal well's answer says

Its problem is weak shallow rock. The window is narrowest where the hole is still nearly vertical, and it opens up as the well builds angle and gets into stronger rock.

The remedy is not a casing point at 1020 m. It is a mud weight that clears the collapse gradient there, which on this well is a comfortably achievable 1363.0134212003486 kg/m3.

## The lesson for a study

Do not assume the reservoir section is the hard part.

On this pair, the well with the deeper target has its problem at the bottom and the well with the shallower target has its problem near the top. A study that evaluated total depth on both would have got one of them right by accident.

## The lesson for a mud programme

Both wells need a window per SECTION rather than a single number. The tightest point sets the design for the section it falls in, and a well with four sections has four designs.
