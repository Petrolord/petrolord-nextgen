# The tightest point

The one row the mud programme is designed around.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## The definition

The row whose window width is smallest, where the width is the fracture initiation gradient less the LARGER of the collapse gradient and the pore pressure gradient.

## Why the larger

Because the mud has to clear both. A window measured from the collapse gradient when the pore pressure is above it would be a window that includes mud weights that take a kick.

## Why the kick-off point

Look at the rows either side:

| measured depth | inclination | collapse | window |
|---|---|---|---|
| 930 m | 0 deg | 1412.0868058920203 | 1381.5951094488616 |
| 990 m | 0 deg | 1379.3212170589932 | 1367.3362050962469 |
| 1020 m | 5.333333333333236 deg | 1363.0134212003486 | 1364.1863204876872 |
| 1080 m | 21.33333333333332 deg | 1327.532261064919 | 1386.4198049425286 |
| 1200 m | 53.333333333333336 deg | 1277.2367873694418 | 1464.2526230645142 |

The collapse gradient falls steadily all the way through, because the rock is getting stronger with depth faster than the stresses are growing.

The window narrows above the kick-off and WIDENS below it. The minimum is where the two trends meet.

## Reading that

Above the kick-off the well is vertical and the only thing changing is the rock, which is improving. Below it the well is building angle, and here the deviation happens to widen the window rather than narrow it.

Deviating in this stress field at this depth helps, exactly as the Professional tier's 30 degree case did. So the tightest point is at the last moment before the help starts.

## The general shape

A window profile is a competition between the rock trend and the trajectory trend. The tightest point is wherever they cross, and on a well that lands and turns it is frequently in the build rather than at the bottom.
