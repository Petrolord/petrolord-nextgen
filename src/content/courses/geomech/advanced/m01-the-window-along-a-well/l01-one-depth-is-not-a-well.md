# One depth is not a well

Why the Professional answer is not yet an answer.

{{panel:gm-window-explorer}}

## What the previous tier produced

A collapse pressure and a fracture initiation pressure at ONE depth, for ONE hole attitude.

That is a complete answer to a question nobody asks. A well is drilled through many depths at many attitudes, and the mud programme has to work at all of them.

## The three things that vary along a well

**The rock.** The stresses, the pore pressure and the strength all change with depth, and they change independently of each other.

**The attitude.** A well that builds angle presents a different face to the stress field at every station.

**Which of them dominates.** At one depth the rock may be the problem and at another the trajectory.

## Why the tightest point is not obvious

Because the two effects can pull in opposite directions.

Going deeper generally makes the rock stronger, which widens the window. Going deeper on a building well also means more inclination, which usually narrows it.

Which wins is a question about a particular well through particular rock, and it has to be computed.

## What the walk produces

A row every 30 m of measured depth from the shallowest usable station to total depth, each with a measured depth, a true vertical depth, an inclination, a pore pressure gradient, a collapse gradient, a fracture gradient and a breakout angle.

The slant well gives 99 rows and the horizontal well gives 92.

## The output that matters

The TIGHTEST row: the one where the window is narrowest.

That is the depth the mud programme is really designed around, because a mud weight that works there works everywhere else on the same section.

## The two answers in this course

The slant well is tightest at total depth, 3000 m, with a window of 1041.4600436889584 kg/m3.

The horizontal well is tightest at 1020 m, with a window of 1364.1863204876872 kg/m3. That is barely a third of the way down a 2800 m hole.

## Why that second answer is the interesting one

Because it says the hardest place to drill that well is nowhere near the reservoir. A study that evaluated the target depth and stopped would have missed it entirely.
