# The breakout rotates

Where the wall fails, and why it is not always where you expect.

{{panel:gm-stability-explorer}}

## The vertical case

Breakout at 90 degrees from the SHmax direction. Fixed, predictable, and the basis of stress interpretation from image logs.

## What happens on a deviated hole

At 2500 m, along the north azimuth of 0 degrees:

| inclination | breakout angle from the high side |
|---|---|
| 0 deg | 150 deg |
| 30 deg | 122 deg |
| 60 deg | 96 deg |
| 90 deg | 90 deg |

The breakout walks steadily from 150 degrees round to 90 as the hole is laid over.

## What 90 degrees means on a horizontal hole

The high side of a horizontal hole points straight up. Ninety degrees from it is the SIDE of the hole.

So a horizontal well drilled north in this stress field breaks out on its flanks rather than on its roof and floor.

## Why that is worth knowing

Because it changes what the cavings do. Material spalling from the roof falls into the hole and gets carried away, or does not. Material spalling from the flanks sits on the low side and forms a bed.

The hole cleaning problem and the stability problem interact, and where the failure is determines how they interact.

## The azimuth dependence

Along 60 degrees, the SHmax direction, the breakout sits at 90 degrees from the high side at every inclination from 30 upward. Along 150 degrees it starts at 0 and jumps to 90 by 60 degrees of inclination.

A breakout at 0 degrees from the high side is on the ROOF of the hole, and one at 90 is on the flank. Those are different problems.

## The interpretation trap

A breakout orientation on a deviated well does not directly give the minimum horizontal stress direction.

It gives an angle in the borehole frame, and recovering a stress azimuth from it requires the full rotation, the inclination, the azimuth and a stress magnitude model. That is a genuine inversion rather than a reading.

Plenty of stress interpretations have been made by applying the vertical-well rule to deviated data, and they are wrong by whatever the rotation was.

## The engine's convention

Theta is measured from the high side of the hole, and the reported breakout angle is the theta at which the Mohr-Coulomb margin is worst just below the collapse pressure.

For a vertical hole the high side is defined by the nominal azimuth, which is why the first lesson in this module could use that as a consistency check.
