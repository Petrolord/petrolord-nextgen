# Along SHmax, and along Shmin

The two extremes, and the size of the choice.

{{panel:gm-stability-explorer}}

## The comparison

At 2500 m, two horizontal holes through the same rock, differing only in the direction they point:

| azimuth | collapse EMW | fracture EMW | window width |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 deg, along SHmax | 545.5972311612414 | 2016.5561719173554 | 1470.958940756114 |
| 150 deg, along Shmin | 508.1480946245233 | 2494.5481467507584 | 1986.400052126235 |

## The size of it

The Shmin direction gives 515.44 kg/m3 more window than the SHmax direction. That is 35 percent more, from a decision that costs nothing at the planning stage.

## Where the difference comes from

Almost all of it from the fracture end. The collapse pressures differ by only 37.45 kg/m3; the fracture pressures differ by 477.99.

## Why the fracture end dominates

Because a hole drilled along Shmin has SHmax and Sv in its cross section, and a hole drilled along SHmax has Shmin and Sv.

The least hoop stress goes as three times the smaller cross-sectional stress less the larger. Drilling along Shmin puts the LARGER horizontal stress in the section, so the smaller of the two cross-sectional stresses is bigger, and the least hoop stress is higher. The wall takes more pressure before it goes into tension.

## The conventional wisdom, and its qualification

The rule usually quoted is that a horizontal well should be drilled along the minimum horizontal stress for stability.

This fixture agrees. It is worth knowing that the rule is regime-dependent: in a reverse faulting field, where the overburden is the SMALLEST stress, the conclusion is different, and the general answer is to compute it.

## The other consideration

Hydraulic fracturing wants the opposite. A well drilled along Shmin creates transverse fractures perpendicular to the wellbore, which is what a multistage completion is designed for; a well along SHmax creates longitudinal ones.

So the stability optimum and the completion optimum can point the same way or opposite ways, and the well plan has to decide which matters more. This course supplies one half of that argument.

## The 30 degree case

Not on the list above, and worth noticing: at 30 degrees toward Shmin the window is 2240.374586622351 kg/m3, wider than either horizontal case and wider than vertical.

If the geometry allows a moderately deviated approach rather than a fully horizontal one, this stress field rewards it substantially.
