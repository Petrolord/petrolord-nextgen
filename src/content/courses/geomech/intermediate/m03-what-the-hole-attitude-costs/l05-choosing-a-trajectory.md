# Choosing a trajectory

What this module is actually for.

{{panel:gm-stability-explorer}}

## The decision

A target has to be reached. Usually there is a choice about how: which azimuth to approach on, how much inclination to build and where, whether to land shallow and drill a long lateral or land deep and drill a short one.

Traditionally that choice is made on drilling efficiency, torque and drag, and where the rig can be placed.

## What this module adds

That the choice also changes the mud window, sometimes by a third of it.

At 2500 m in this stress field, a horizontal well along Shmin has 1986.400052126235 kg/m3 of window and one along SHmax has 1470.958940756114. Same rock, same depth, same everything except the compass bearing.

## When it matters most

When the window is already narrow.

A well with 500 kg/m3 of window can afford to lose 35 percent of it. A well with 100 kg/m3 cannot, and on those wells the trajectory decision is a stability decision first and a drilling one second.

## The other lever: inclination

Not just direction but how much. This fixture rewards a moderate deviation toward Shmin over both vertical and horizontal at 2500 m, with 2240.374586622351 kg/m3 at 30 degrees.

That is the kind of result that only shows up if somebody sweeps it, and it is a good argument for producing the whole surface rather than evaluating the planned trajectory alone.

## What to hand the well planner

Not a single number. A map: window width against inclination and azimuth at the critical depths, with the planned trajectory marked on it.

A planner looking at that map can see immediately whether the plan is on a ridge or in a valley, and how much freedom there is to move.

## The constraint the map does not have

Everything else. Anti-collision, target tolerance, dogleg limits, casing wear, torque and drag, and where the surface location can go.

Geomechanics supplies one input to a decision with many, and its job is to make its own contribution legible rather than to make the decision.

## Where it is used most

Deepwater, where the windows are narrow because the overburden is reduced by the water column. Depleted reservoirs, where the pore pressure has dropped and the fracture gradient with it. And any field where the offset wells have a history of stuck pipe in one particular section.
