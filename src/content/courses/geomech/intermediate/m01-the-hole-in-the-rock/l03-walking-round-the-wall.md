# Walking round the wall

Where the peaks are, and what sits at them.

{{panel:gm-stability-explorer}}

## What the panel shows

Open the wall view and it plots the largest and smallest wall stress against the angle round the hole, from zero to 360 degrees, with the flat radial line across it.

## The shape

Two full cycles in one turn. The largest wall stress peaks twice, at points diametrically opposite each other, and dips twice a quarter turn away.

That is the cosine of twice the angle doing its work, and it is why breakouts come in pairs on opposite sides of a hole.

## Where the peaks are

For a vertical hole, at 90 degrees to the maximum horizontal stress direction.

The rock is squeezed hardest where the far-field squeeze is trying to close the hole most, and that is across the direction of the largest stress rather than along it.

## The consequence for image logs

A breakout is a pair of enlargements 180 degrees apart, and its orientation tells you the direction of the MINIMUM horizontal stress.

That is one of the few field observations that constrains the stress azimuth, and it is why an image log or a four-arm caliper is worth running on a well where the stresses matter.

## Tensile fractures are the other pair

At the angle where the hoop stress is SMALLEST, a high enough mud weight puts it into tension and the wall splits.

Those drilling-induced tensile fractures appear 90 degrees from the breakouts, which means along the maximum horizontal stress direction. Seeing both on the same image log constrains the azimuth twice over.

## What raising the mud weight does to the picture

The whole hoop curve moves down by the change in differential pressure, and the flat radial line moves up by the same amount.

The peak is still a peak and the dip is still a dip: the pattern does not change shape, it just slides. That is what makes the two criteria bounds on a single number.

## What deviating does to the picture

The shear term switches on and the pattern tilts. The peak is no longer at a fixed angle from the stress direction, and it moves as the inclination and azimuth change.

The panel's wall view at 60 degrees of inclination shows it: the curve is no longer symmetric about the high side.

## Exercise

In the panel's wall view, set a vertical hole and note the angle of the largest wall stress. Then set 60 degrees of inclination on the same azimuth and note it again.

Then say what the difference implies for interpreting a breakout on a deviated well.
