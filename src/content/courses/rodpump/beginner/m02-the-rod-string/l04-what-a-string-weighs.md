# What a string weighs

Three weights matter and they are not interchangeable: weight per foot, weight in air, and the buoyed weight the polished rod actually lifts.

## The catalog is the ground floor

| Size | Area, in2 | Published weight, lb/ft | Published over bare steel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 0.196349541 | 0.726000 | 1.086608611957 |
| 5/8 | 0.306796158 | 1.135000 | 1.087207294388 |
| 3/4 | 0.441786467 | 1.634000 | 1.086941213308 |
| 7/8 | 0.601320469 | 2.224000 | 1.086914062177 |
| 1 | 0.785398163 | 2.904000 | 1.086608611957 |
| 1 1/8 | 0.994019551 | 3.676000 | 1.086793390486 |

A published rod weight is not the weight of a cylinder of steel. It includes couplings, which is why every published weight stands above the bare steel weight of the same rod. The catalog states a single coupling allowance of 1.087, and the real ratios sit near it without matching it and without climbing steadily with size: 1/2 and 1 share 1.086608611957, while 5/8 is highest at 1.087207294388 and 3/4 sits at 1.086941213308.

A 7/8 rod weighs 2.224000 lb/ft published against 2.046159929 lb/ft as bare steel at 490 lb/ft3. Computing a string weight from density and area is the mistake, and it is short by that gap on every foot.

## What a string weighs in air

Weight per foot times length, summed. The published taper is 6672.000000 lb of 7/8 over 3000 ft on top of 3268.000000 lb of 3/4 over 2000 ft, so 9940.000000 lb, an average of 1.988000000 lb/ft. The published uniform string is 13344.000000 lb over 6000 ft, an average of 2.224000000 lb/ft, which is simply the rod weight because nothing is mixed. ODUMA-4, three sections deep, weighs 10692.200000000 lb at an average of 2.227541667 lb/ft.

## The comparison that is easy to get wrong

The taper weighs 3404.000000 lb less in air than the uniform string, which is 25.509592 percent, and it is stiffer by 12.365341 lb/in, which is 4.854369 percent. Lighter and stiffer at once looks like a free lunch and it is not one: the uniform string is 6000 ft and the taper is 5000 ft. Depth is doing most of the work in both columns, and the taper's own contribution can only be read against another string of the same length.

## What the polished rod carries at rest

8673.757961783 lb on the published taper in fluid of specific gravity 1.00, and 11644.127388535 lb on the uniform string in the same fluid, both from a buoyancy factor of 0.872611464968. ODUMA-4 in lighter fluid carries 9466.342675159 lb.

Neither figure includes the fluid load standing on the plunger, and neither includes a pound of acceleration. A string weight is a number about steel and a fluid, and a polished rod load is a number about a moving machine, so the second is never read off the first.
