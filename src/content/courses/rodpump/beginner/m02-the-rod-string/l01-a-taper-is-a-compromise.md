# A taper is a compromise

Heavier rods at the top carry what hangs below them, lighter rods at the bottom are less to lift, and there is no split that does both.

The published taper is the 3000 ft split. Run the sweep to its end, all 7/8 for the full 5000 ft, and the string weighs 11120.000000 lb in air with a spring rate of 305.671238333 lb/in and 16.357443 in of stretch under the same load.

## Why there is no best row

Weight and stiffness climb together, step for step, and the stretch falls with them. Every foot of 7/8 put at the top adds stiffness and adds weight in the same breath, so no split is stiff and light at once, and a designer who sweeps for a minimum finds none.

That is what makes a taper a compromise rather than an optimisation. The heavy top exists because the top section carries the buoyed weight of every rod below it plus the fluid load; the light bottom exists because that steel has to be lifted every stroke and it carries almost nothing.

## What the compromise is actually settled on

Neither of them. It is settled on stress, section by section, and stress is a load over an area, which means the top section can be justified only once the loads it carries are known. Buoyed weight follows the same monotone climb, 7129.235669 lb at all 3/4, 8673.757962 lb at the published split, 9703.439490 lb at all 7/8.

## The mistake

Picking the lightest string. It exists, all 3/4 over the full 5000 ft, and it is also the softest in the sweep: 8170.000000 lb in air and 22.264298 in of stretch under 5000 lb against 16.357443 in for the heaviest. That stretch is surface stroke the plunger never gets. Steel saved at the top of a design is paid for at the bottom.
