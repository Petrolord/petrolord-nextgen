# Onward

A rod pump design is a card and a set of readings taken off it. What this tier taught is how to tell those two apart.

## What the reading earns

It separates a number that would survive a different solver from one that would not. On ODUMA-4 the plunger stroke moves 0.044644 percent across six node counts while the minimum load moves 10.864298 percent of the smallest, so a produced rate can be defended and a load range has to be qualified.

It also puts a size on every convention. The service factor decides the verdict at 0.828733084 with the card unchanged. The fillage warning separates 301.389964 bbl/d from 301.354487 bbl/d. Neither of those is physics, and both are now numbers rather than opinions about numbers.

## Where it hands over

The engine models a string, a linkage, a pump and a wave. It does not model rod buckling, tubing movement, fluid friction on the plunger, valve slippage beyond the efficiency a caller types, gas interference, deviated hole side loading, rod on tubing wear, gearbox and belt and motor losses, or the fatigue history that turns a Goodman percentage into a service life. A design that passes every check here can still fail on any of those, and none of them will appear in a warning.

## The repairs that cost no arithmetic

Several of the things this tier priced are reporting faults rather than physics faults. A design function could forward the three inputs it already accepts. A counterbalance effect could be given the crank offset the balance around it already holds, which at 20.0 deg would move it from 12393.212710 lb to 11287.785540 lb. A load could be reported with the sampling that produced it. None of that changes an equation, and all of it changes what a reader is entitled to believe.

## What you have finished

You can say which numbers in a rod pump design are measurements, which are conventions and which are artefacts of how the answer was sampled. You can prove an input is unread in one extra call. You can refuse a tempting optimum on the grounds of the solver's own noise, which is the habit worth carrying furthest.

## The question to keep asking

Of any number on a design sheet: what sampled it, what convention set its threshold, and would it survive a grid parameter that changes nothing about the well.

## Exercise

Write the three things this engine does not model that would most change a rod pump design in the field, and say why no warning covers them.

Then write one number you would now refuse to quote without a second number beside it, and name the second number.
