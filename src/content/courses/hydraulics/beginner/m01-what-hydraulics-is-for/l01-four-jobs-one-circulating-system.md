# Four jobs, one circulating system

What the mud is for, and why the four jobs pull against each other.

## The four

**Carry the cuttings out.** The bit makes rock into chips and the mud has to lift them to surface. That wants flow rate.

**Hold the hole up and keep the formation fluids in.** That wants density.

**Clean and cool the bit.** That wants velocity through the nozzles, which wants flow rate and small nozzles.

**Do all of it without breaking the formation.** The pressure the mud exerts at any depth has to stay below what the rock will take. That wants LESS density and LESS flow rate.

## Why they conflict

The fourth job is in direct opposition to the first three.

More flow rate cleans better and cools better, and it raises the pressure in the annulus, which raises the pressure the formation feels. More density holds the hole up and raises the same pressure further.

So drilling hydraulics is not an optimisation with a single objective. It is finding a flow rate and a density that satisfy four constraints at once, and on a difficult well there is no such pair.

## The quantity that ties them together

The equivalent circulating density: the pressure at a point while circulating, expressed as the mud weight that would produce the same pressure statically.

It is the number the formation actually experiences, and almost everything in this course is about what moves it.

## What this course computes

**Rheology.** Four dial readings from a viscometer, fitted to three models of how the mud resists shear.

**Pressure loss** in every element of the circulating path: down the inside of the string, across the bit, back up the annulus.

**Equivalent circulating density** from the annulus loss.

**Hole cleaning:** how fast the cuttings actually travel and how many of them are in the annulus at any moment.

**Surge and swab:** what moving the string does to the same pressure, with no pumps running at all.

## What it does not compute

Temperature. Compressibility. Gas in the annulus. Cuttings beds forming and sliding. The transients when a pump starts. All of those are real, all matter, and the Expert tier names them properly.
