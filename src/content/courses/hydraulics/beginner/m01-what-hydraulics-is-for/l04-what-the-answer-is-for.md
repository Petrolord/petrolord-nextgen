# What the answer is for

Six decisions that wait on a hydraulics calculation.

## What pump you need

Pump pressure sets the liner size and the horsepower. It is a contract term and it is decided before the well is drilled, from a calculation.

## What flow rate to circulate at

Too little and the cuttings do not come out. Too much and the equivalent circulating density breaks the formation, or the pump cannot supply the pressure.

This is the single most common day-to-day use, and it is the trade the whole course is about.

## What nozzles to run

The nozzle area is the one lever that changes how the pump pressure is split. Smaller nozzles put more of it across the bit and less into the system, which cleans the bit better and costs pump pressure.

## What mud weight to use

Chosen to sit between the pore pressure and the fracture pressure. The hydraulics calculation then has to fit the circulating and tripping pressures around that choice, and sometimes it cannot.

## How fast to run in and pull out

Surge and swab. On a narrow-margin well the trip speed is a computed limit rather than a matter of preference, and exceeding it either fractures the formation or brings a kick into the hole.

## Whether the well can be drilled at all

If the flow rate needed to clean the hole produces an equivalent circulating density above the fracture gradient, there is no answer. The well needs a different hole size, a different mud, managed pressure drilling, or a different plan.

## What each of them needs

| decision | needs |
|---|---|
| the pump | pump pressure at the design rate |
| the flow rate | cleaning and ECD together |
| the nozzles | the pressure split |
| the mud weight | pore and fracture, from geomechanics |
| the trip speed | surge and swab against the same window |
| whether it can be drilled | all of the above at once |

Two of the six need information this course does not produce: the pore and fracture pressures come from the geomechanics course.

## The habit

Every one of these is a comparison between a computed pressure and a limit. So every hydraulics answer should be quoted with the limit it is being compared against, and a pressure with no limit attached has not been used for anything.

## Exercise

For each of the six, say whether it is decided before the well is drilled or while it is being drilled.

Then say which of them would change if the mud engineer changed the mud without telling anybody, and which would not.
