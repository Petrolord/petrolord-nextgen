# What the bit does not tell you

Four things a nozzle calculation cannot settle.

## Rate of penetration

The whole point of bit hydraulics is to drill faster, and the calculation predicts nothing about how fast the bit will drill.

Rate of penetration depends on the rock, the bit type, the weight on bit, the rotary speed and the bottom hole cleaning, and the hydraulics calculation touches only the last of those, and only as a proxy.

## Bit balling

Soft sticky formations pack the cutting structure with rock. Jets are one defence and mud chemistry is another, and neither is in the pressure equation.

A bit that balls up shows as a rate of penetration collapse with no change in pressure at all, which is exactly the case the hydraulics model cannot see.

## Nozzle erosion

Nozzles wear. Their area grows over a bit run, which lowers the bit pressure drop and the jet velocity progressively.

That is a real and measurable effect: standpipe pressure falls slowly through a long bit run. The calculation assumes a fixed area.

## Whether the jets reach the rock

The nozzle equation computes the pressure and the jet velocity AT the nozzle. Whether that jet still has any energy by the time it reaches the formation depends on the standoff distance, the crossflow under the bit and the cuttings already there.

The classical extended-nozzle work exists precisely because that standoff matters, and none of it is in a total flow area.

## What the calculation IS good for

**The pressure.** It is exact, it is a square law, and it lets a measured pump pressure be decomposed.

**Detecting a change.** A plugged nozzle raises pump pressure; a washout lowers it. Both are seen by comparing measured against computed.

**Ranking nozzle choices** against each other on power or impact force, which is a screening exercise rather than a prediction.

## The habit

Quote the bit numbers as pressures and velocities, which the model computes, rather than as a predicted improvement in drilling performance, which it does not.

## Exercise

For each of the four omissions, name the rig observation that would tell you it is happening.

Two of the four move the standpipe pressure and two do not, and knowing which is which is the diagnostic.
