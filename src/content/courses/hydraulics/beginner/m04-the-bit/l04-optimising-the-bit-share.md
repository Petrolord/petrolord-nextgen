# Optimising the bit share

The one lever, and what it moves.

{{panel:hy-rheology-explorer}}

## The lever

The nozzle total flow area. It is the only input that changes the SPLIT of the pump pressure without changing the flow rate.

| total flow area | bit pressure | jet velocity | bit power |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0003 m2 | 5540166.204986151 Pa | 83.33333333333334 m/s | 138504.1551246538 W |
| 0.000461814 m2 | 2337927.902432598 Pa | 54.13434846063567 m/s | 58448.19756081496 W |
| 0.0007 m2 | 1017581.5478545991 Pa | 35.714285714285715 m/s | 25439.53869636498 W |

All at 0.025 m3/s on the slant well.

## What happens elsewhere

Nothing. The pipe loss and the annulus loss are unchanged, because the flow rate is unchanged and the geometry outside the bit is unchanged.

So the whole effect of the nozzle choice lands on the pump pressure and on the bit.

## The trade

Smaller nozzles mean a faster jet, more power at the bit and better bottom hole cleaning, and they cost pump pressure.

If the pump is already at its limit, smaller nozzles mean a LOWER achievable flow rate, which means worse hole cleaning in the annulus.

So the bit and the annulus are competing for the same pump, and the nozzle is where that competition is settled.

## The bit share on this system

At the standard nozzles and 0.025 m3/s the bit takes 19.8616 percent of the pump pressure.

Classical optimisation aims for something like 48 to 65 percent. This system is nowhere near that, because the drill collars' 0.05715 m bore is consuming so much pressure that there is little left for the bit.

That is a realistic situation and it is a real finding: on this string, tightening the nozzles to reach an optimal bit share would push the pump pressure far beyond anything a rig would supply.

## The conclusion the numbers force

The bit hydraulics on this well are limited by the STRING, not by the nozzle choice.

Fixing it means a bigger bore in the bottom hole assembly, not smaller nozzles. That is the sort of conclusion a hydraulics calculation exists to produce, and it is not the conclusion a bit optimisation formula on its own would give.
