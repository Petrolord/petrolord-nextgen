# Pack-off

The annulus closing around the string.

## The event

Cuttings, cavings or a collapsed hole fill the annulus around the bottom hole assembly. The flow area shrinks and the pressure needed to push mud past it rises sharply.

## Why it is a hydraulics event

Because the pressure loss in an annulus goes as the velocity squared or nearly so, and the velocity is the flow rate over the area.

Halve the area and you double the velocity and roughly quadruple the loss. A pack-off can take the equivalent circulating density from comfortable to fracturing in seconds.

## The signature

**Standpipe pressure rising** with no change to the flow rate. That is the first and clearest indicator.

**Torque and drag rising** at the same time, because the material in the annulus is also in contact with the string.

**Returns reducing**, if the pack-off is complete enough to divert flow.

The combination of a pressure rise and a torque rise with no other change is a pack-off until proved otherwise.

## Why it is dangerous

Three ways.

**It can fracture the formation**, because the equivalent circulating density spikes.

**It can stick the string**, because the material around the assembly is holding it.

**It hides itself**, because stopping the pumps relieves the pressure and the pack-off may partly clear, so the evidence disappears while the cause remains.

## What causes it

Poor hole cleaning: cuttings that were not carried out accumulating until they bridge.

Wellbore instability: cavings from a shale that is failing.

A combination: cavings are large and irregular and are far harder to carry than drilled cuttings, so an unstable hole cleans badly even at a good flow rate.

## What this course can say

The transport ratio and the cuttings concentration are the leading indicators. A concentration above a few percent means the annulus has a lot in it, and the more that is in it the less has to happen to bridge.

What the course cannot say is when it will bridge, because that is a mechanical process with no model here.

## What is done

Reduce the flow rate immediately if the pressure spikes, to avoid fracturing. Work the string. Then circulate carefully at a rate that clears the material without breaking down.

The instinct to pump harder is exactly wrong and it is what fractures the formation.
