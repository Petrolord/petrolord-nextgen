# Losses

Mud going into the formation, and what it does next.

## The event

The pressure in the wellbore exceeds what the formation will take, and mud flows out of the well into the rock.

It can be a fracture that has been created, a natural fracture that has been opened, or a very permeable formation that simply takes fluid.

## The three severities

**Seepage losses:** a fraction of a cubic metre an hour. Annoying and manageable.

**Partial losses:** returns to surface are reduced but present. The well is still under control and mud is being consumed.

**Total losses:** no returns at all. The annulus level is falling and the pressure at the bottom is falling with it.

## Why total losses are a well control event

Because the hydrostatic column is what holds the formation fluids in. If mud is leaving faster than it is being pumped, the level falls, the pressure falls, and eventually it falls below the pore pressure somewhere.

Then formation fluid enters the well while mud is leaving it, which is the worst combination there is: an underground blowout, where one formation is feeding another through the wellbore.

## The hydraulic causes

**Too much mud weight**, which is a static problem and a mud weight decision.

**Too much equivalent circulating density**, which is a flow rate decision and is what this course computes.

**Surge**, which is a trip speed decision.

All three are in this course, and all three are compared against a fracture gradient this course does not compute.

## The signature at surface

Flow out less than flow in. Pit volume falling. Pump pressure falling slightly, because part of the circuit now has a shorter path.

The pit volume is the primary indicator and it is monitored continuously for exactly this reason.

## What is done

Stop pumping, which removes the circulating uplift immediately. Then reduce the mud weight if it can be reduced, and pump a lost circulation material pill to bridge the fracture.

The first action is free and it is the one the hydraulics calculation predicts the effect of.

## Why the calculation matters before the event

Because the difference between the static mud weight and the equivalent circulating density is the margin you lose the moment you start the pumps.

If that difference is most of the window, then the well is fine when static and loses returns every time circulation is established, which is a specific and diagnosable pattern.
