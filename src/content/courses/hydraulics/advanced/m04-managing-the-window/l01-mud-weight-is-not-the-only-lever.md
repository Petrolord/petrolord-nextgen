# Mud weight is not the only lever

Six things that move the four pressures.

{{panel:hy-surge-explorer}}

## The lever everybody reaches for

Mud weight. It moves all four pressures together, up or down, by the same amount.

That is its strength and its weakness: it cannot change the SPREAD between them, only where the spread sits.

## The five that change the spread

**Flow rate.** Moves the equivalent circulating density and nothing else. Lower rate, smaller spread, worse cleaning.

**Rheology.** Moves the equivalent circulating density, the surge and the swab together, because all three are annular pressure losses. A thinner mud reduces all three.

**Trip speed.** Moves the surge and the swab and nothing else.

**Open or closed string.** Moves the surge and the swab by about a fifth on this well.

**Annulus size.** Moves everything except the static weight, and it is fixed by the time the question is asked.

## The ranking for a narrow window

**Trip speed first**, because it is free and it is entirely under the driller's control.

**Rheology second**, because it moves three of the four and a mud can be thinned.

**Flow rate third**, because it moves only one but it moves it a lot, and it costs cleaning.

**String state fourth**, because it is often not a choice: casing has a float.

## The one that changes the problem rather than the answer

Managed pressure drilling, which adds a fifth lever: surface back pressure.

That is a genuinely different thing, because it can be adjusted in real time and it can be applied when the pumps are OFF. Every other lever here is fixed once the operation starts.

## The interaction that catches people

Thinning the mud to reduce the equivalent circulating density also reduces the transport ratio, so the flow rate has to rise, which raises the equivalent circulating density again.

On this course's two muds, the light mud's annulus loss at 0.025 m3/s is 1110436.3963266218 Pa against the heavy mud's 1434707.6543385487. But the light mud's transport ratio is 0.7699923145452399 against 0.8284815558593573, and matching the heavy mud's cleaning would need a substantially higher rate.

Whether the net effect is a lower equivalent circulating density depends on the numbers, and the only way to find out is to run both.
