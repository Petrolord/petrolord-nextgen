# Four pressures, one window

The complete picture, on one axis.

{{panel:hy-surge-explorer}}

## The four

**The static mud weight.** What the formation feels when nothing is happening. It has to be above the pore pressure and below the fracture pressure on its own.

**The equivalent circulating density.** The static weight plus the annulus friction, felt while circulating. It has to be below the fracture pressure.

**The surge.** The static weight plus the surge pressure, felt while running in. Also below the fracture pressure.

**The swab.** The static weight less the surge pressure, felt while pulling out. It has to be above the pore pressure.

## The picture

    pore pressure
      |
      |  swab
      |
      |  static mud weight
      |
      |  ECD while circulating
      |  surge while running in
      |
    fracture pressure

Two of the four go up from the mud weight and one goes down. The static weight is not in the middle of the window; it is offset toward the pore side to leave room above it.

## The numbers on the slant well

Mud weight 1440 kg/m3.

| pressure | value |
|---|---|
| swab at 0.5 m/s | 1400.0934648506984 kg/m3 |
| static | 1440 kg/m3 |
| surge at 0.5 m/s | 1479.9065351493016 kg/m3 |
| ECD at 0.025 m3/s | 1498.3349880149756 kg/m3 |

So the full spread is from 1400 to 1498, which is 98 kg/m3 of window used up by ordinary operations.

## What that means

A window narrower than 98 kg/m3 on this well cannot accommodate both circulating at 0.025 m3/s and tripping at 0.5 m/s.

Something would have to change: a slower trip, a lower flow rate, or a different mud.

## Which of the four is largest

The equivalent circulating density, on this well at these settings. It is 58.33 kg/m3 above the mud weight against 39.91 for the surge.

That ranking is not general. On a well with a low flow rate and a fast trip, the surge is larger. On a horizontal well the two are closer, because both are divided by the same true vertical depth.

## The one that is easiest to forget

The swab, because it is the only one BELOW the mud weight and it is easy to check the fracture side and forget the pore side.

Most kicks happen while tripping out. Forgetting the swab is how.
