# Pore and fracture

The two limits everything else fits between.

## Pore pressure

The pressure of the fluid in the rock's pores.

If the pressure in the wellbore falls below it, formation fluid flows into the well. That is a kick, and it is the beginning of every blowout.

## Fracture pressure

The pressure at which the rock breaks and takes fluid.

If the pressure in the wellbore rises above it, mud is lost into the formation. That is lost circulation, and beyond costing mud it lowers the level in the annulus, which lowers the pressure, which can then let a kick in.

## Where they come from

Not from this course. They come from geomechanics: from offset well data, from leak-off tests, from log-derived pore pressure prediction, and from the seismic velocity field before any well is drilled.

This course takes them as given and asks whether the hydraulics fit inside them.

## The window

The gap between them, expressed as an equivalent mud weight so that both are in the same units as the mud.

A wide window is 200 kg/m3 or more and the drilling is easy. A narrow window is 50 kg/m3 or less and every decision in this course becomes a constraint.

## Why the window narrows

**With depth**, generally: pore pressure rises toward the overburden and the fracture gradient rises more slowly.

**With depletion**: a reservoir that has been produced has a lower pore pressure than the shales around it, and drilling through it means the mud is overbalanced on the reservoir and possibly underbalanced on a shale above.

**In deep water**: the overburden includes a column of seawater, which is much lighter than rock, so the fracture gradient is low relative to the depth.

Those three cases are where managed pressure drilling and dual-gradient techniques come from.

## What this course adds to the window

Four pressures that all have to fit inside it: the static mud weight, the equivalent circulating density, the surge and the swab.

The mud weight is chosen first, and then the other three have to fit around it. Sometimes there is no choice of mud weight that lets all four fit, which is the finding the next lessons are about.
