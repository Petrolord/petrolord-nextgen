# Beds, and what moves them

Four mechanisms, none of them in the model.

## What a bed is

A layer of cuttings lying on the low side of an inclined hole. It has a thickness, it occupies part of the annulus, and it reduces the area available to flow, which raises the velocity over it.

That raised velocity is the only thing that stops it growing without limit: the bed grows until the flow over it is fast enough to carry away as much as arrives.

## Mechanism one: flow rate

More flow means more velocity over the bed and more scour. It works, and it works less well than it does in suspension: doubling the rate does not halve the bed.

And it is limited by the pump and by the equivalent circulating density, which is the whole trade this course is about.

## Mechanism two: rotation

Rotating the drill string stirs the bed mechanically. The pipe lies in the low side, on top of or inside the bed, and turning it throws cuttings up into the flow.

This is the single most effective bed-removal mechanism in a deviated hole and it is entirely absent from every model in this course, because the model assumes concentric annuli with no pipe rotation.

That is why a hole that will not clean at 120 rpm cleans at 180, with no change in flow rate.

## Mechanism three: pipe movement

Reciprocating the string, or making a wiper trip, drags the assembly through the bed and mobilises it.

It is why a wiper trip cleans a hole and why a stationary string in a deviated hole accumulates a bed under it.

## Mechanism four: mud properties

Not viscosity in the simple sense. A high-viscosity sweep pumped as a pill lifts cuttings in suspension; a low-viscosity turbulent sweep scours a bed. Both are used and they work by opposite mechanisms.

Which one to use depends on the angle, which is exactly the variable this model does not have.

## The ranking in a deviated hole

Rotation, then flow rate, then pipe movement, then mud. That ranking is roughly the reverse of the ranking in a vertical hole, where flow rate dominates and rotation does very little.

## What this means for the model's answer

A transport ratio computed here is an upper bound on how well a deviated hole is being cleaned. The real answer is worse, and the amount worse depends on the angle, the rotation and the pipe movement, none of which is an input.

## Exercise

For each of the four mechanisms, say whether it would raise or lower the equivalent circulating density while it was working.

Two of them raise it, which is a real conflict: the things that clean the hole make the pressure problem worse.
