# The hole cleaning toolkit

What is actually done, and which parts the model can price.

## The tools

**Flow rate.** Priced by this model, through the transport ratio and the concentration, and by the pressure calculation through the equivalent circulating density.

**Rotation.** Not priced. The most effective tool in a deviated hole.

**Reciprocation and wiper trips.** Not priced. They also cost rig time and carry a surge and swab risk, which the Expert tier does price.

**Sweeps.** Not priced. A high-viscosity pill or a low-viscosity turbulent pill, pumped as a slug.

**Mud rheology.** Partly priced: the model computes what a thicker mud does to the slip velocity and to the annulus loss. It does not compute what a thicker mud does to a bed.

**Time.** Circulating bottoms up before a trip, and circulating clean. Not priced, because the model has no time in it.

## What can be decided from this model

The flow rate, and only against the vertical-transport criterion and the equivalent circulating density.

That is genuinely useful in a vertical or lightly deviated hole and it is one input among several in a deviated one.

## What is decided from experience and from rules of thumb

Everything else. Rotation speed, sweep design, circulating time before a trip, and the acceptance criterion for a clean hole.

Those rules exist because the models are not good enough, and the honest position is to say which of your numbers came from a calculation and which came from a rule.

## The measurable indicators

**Cuttings at the shakers.** How much is coming out, and whether it matches what the bit made.

**Torque and drag.** A dirty hole raises the friction factor, which is exactly the effect the previous course said the friction factor absorbs. A rising fitted friction factor with no other change is a hole cleaning signal.

**Equivalent circulating density measured downhole.** A pressure-while-drilling tool measures it directly, and a rise above the computed value means the annulus contains more than the model assumed.

That third one is the closest thing to a direct measurement of hole cleaning that exists, and it is why pressure-while-drilling tools are standard on difficult wells.

## The connection back to the previous course

A cuttings bed is the largest single thing a fitted friction factor absorbs. So the torque and drag course's most important unmodelled effect is this course's most important unmodelled effect, seen from the other side.

Two models, one missing mechanism, and the way to see it is to watch both of them drift at once.

## Exercise

You have a rising fitted friction factor and a rising measured equivalent circulating density, and no change to the mud or the flow rate.

Write down what that pair of observations says, and what you would do about it in order.
