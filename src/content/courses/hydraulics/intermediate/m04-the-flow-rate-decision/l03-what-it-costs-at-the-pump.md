# What it costs at the pump

The other end of the same decision.

{{panel:hy-cleaning-explorer}}

## The chain

A target transport ratio gives a flow rate. That flow rate gives a pump pressure and an equivalent circulating density.

So a cleaning target has a price in both currencies, and the panel computes both from one number.

## The prices on the slant well with kcl_polymer

A transport ratio of 0.9 needs 0.04677230885801645 m3/s.

At 0.040 m3/s, which is close to it, the pump pressure is 26434148.02942923 Pa and the equivalent circulating density is 1541.0015905878527 kg/m3.

Against 11771089.324261548 Pa and 1498.3349880149756 kg/m3 at 0.025 m3/s.

So raising the transport ratio from 0.83 to 0.90 costs more than double the pump pressure and 43 kg/m3 of equivalent circulating density.

## The asymmetry

The pump pressure MORE than doubles and the equivalent circulating density rises by under three percent.

That asymmetry is the reason the pump is usually the binding constraint. The formation is far more tolerant of a flow rate increase than the pump is.

## Which is the real limit

On this well, the pump. 26 MPa is 3800 psi, which is beyond most rigs' working pressure, and 39 MPa at 0.050 m3/s is beyond all of them.

So the achievable flow rate on this string is capped somewhere around 0.030 m3/s by the pump, and the transport ratio that goes with it is what the hole gets.

## What to do when the pump caps you

**Change the string.** A larger bore in the assembly is the biggest lever on pump pressure, and it does not touch the annulus at all.

**Change the nozzles.** Bigger nozzles free up pump pressure at the cost of bit hydraulics.

**Change the mud.** A thinner mud costs less pump pressure and cleans worse, which is a trade rather than a fix.

**Accept a lower transport ratio and manage it** with rotation, sweeps and circulating time, none of which this model prices.

## The reporting habit

A flow rate recommendation should carry three numbers: the transport ratio it achieves, the pump pressure it needs, and the equivalent circulating density it produces.

One of the three on its own has not made a decision.
