# The profile, not the number

Why one equivalent circulating density is not enough.

{{panel:hy-cleaning-explorer}}

## What the engine returns

Not a number: a profile. One entry per annulus element, each with a measured depth, a true vertical depth and the equivalent circulating density there.

The summary quotes the deepest one, because that is usually the largest, and the profile is where the useful information is.

## Why the maximum is usually at total depth

Because the annulus loss accumulates from the bit upward, so the deepest point has the most of it below... no, above it. The pressure at a depth is the hydrostatic to that depth plus the friction of everything from there to surface.

So the friction contribution grows downward and the true vertical depth grows downward too, and the ratio between them usually grows as well, because the deepest annulus sections are the tightest.

## When it is not at total depth

When a shallower section has a much tighter annulus or a much lower true vertical depth per metre of hole.

The clearest case is a horizontal well: in the lateral, every metre adds friction and no true vertical depth, so the equivalent circulating density climbs steeply through the lateral even though the hole is going sideways.

## Why the whole profile matters

Because the formation that matters is not always the deepest one.

A weak shallow shale exposed at the top of the open hole sees a lower equivalent circulating density than total depth does, and it may still be the one that fractures, because its fracture gradient is lower.

So the comparison is between the ECD PROFILE and the FRACTURE PROFILE, point by point, and a single number at total depth cannot make it.

## The shape it takes

Starting at the mud weight at surface, rising smoothly through each annulus section, with a slope change at every geometry change.

The slope is the annulus loss per unit of true vertical depth, so in a lateral where the true vertical depth is not changing, the profile rises vertically on a plot against depth.

## The reading habit

Plot it against true vertical depth, with the pore and fracture gradients on the same axes. That is the picture that answers whether the well can be circulated, and a single number at total depth cannot.
