# Only the annulus reaches the rock

The one loss that matters to the formation, and why the other two do not.

{{panel:hy-cleaning-explorer}}

## The picture

The pump pushes mud down the inside of the string. The string is a closed pipe: whatever pressure the mud has inside it is contained by the steel, and the formation is on the other side of that steel.

At the bit the mud leaves the string and enters the annulus. From there to surface it is in direct contact with the hole wall.

So the formation sees the pressure IN THE ANNULUS, and nothing else.

## What that means for the three losses

**The pipe loss** is inside the string. The formation never sees it.

**The bit loss** happens at the nozzles. It is the pressure the mud gives up to become a jet, and it is upstream of the annulus.

**The annulus loss** is the pressure needed to push the mud up past the formation, and it is added on top of the hydrostatic column at every depth.

## The numbers

Slant well, kcl_polymer, 0.025 m3/s: the pump reads 11771089.324261548 Pa and the annulus loss is 1434707.6543385487 Pa.

So of the 11.8 MPa the pump supplies, 1.4 MPa is what the rock feels as an addition. The other 10.3 MPa is entirely a pump problem.

## The commonest reading error

Taking a pump pressure as a measure of how hard the well is being pushed.

It is not. A well with a very restrictive string has a high pump pressure and may have a low equivalent circulating density. A well with a wide bore and a tight annulus has the reverse.

## The one connection between them

Through the pump's limit. If the pump cannot supply the pressure needed for a flow rate, that flow rate cannot be used, and if that flow rate was needed to clean the hole, the hole does not get cleaned.

So the pipe loss and the bit loss matter to the formation indirectly, by capping what is achievable, and not directly.

## Where the annulus loss actually accumulates

From the bit upward, so the pressure added at any depth is the loss BELOW that depth. At surface the added pressure is zero, because there is no annulus above surface.

That is why the equivalent circulating density is a profile that starts at the mud weight and grows downward, and why its maximum is at total depth.
