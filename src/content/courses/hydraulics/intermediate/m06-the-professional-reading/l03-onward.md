# Onward

From circulating to moving the string.

## What the Expert tier adds

Everything in this tier assumed the pumps are running and the string is still.

The Expert tier turns the pumps off and moves the string, which produces pressure of its own, and then puts all four pressures into one window and asks whether they fit.

## What you will find there

**Surge and swab.** Running in pushes mud out of the way and raises the pressure; pulling out does the reverse. The two are symmetric about the static mud weight, and the pressure is not proportional to the trip speed because the annular flow is partly turbulent.

**The displaced volume, and the clinging mud.** A closed string displaces its whole outside area and an open one displaces only its steel, which is the entire difference between the two and it is worth about a fifth on this well. On top of that, a clinging constant of 0.45 accounts for the mud dragged along by the pipe wall.

**The window.** Pore pressure and fracture pressure bound everything, and four pressures have to fit between them: the static mud weight, the equivalent circulating density while circulating, the surge while running in and the swab while pulling out.

**The speed limit.** Given a window, the engine solves for the fastest trip that stays inside it. On a narrow-margin well that number is what the driller is actually managing.

**What goes wrong.** Losses, a kick on a trip, and a pack-off, with the signature each one leaves at surface.

**Where the model stops.** Steady state with no transients, no temperature, no compressibility, no gel strength and no eccentricity or rotation. Five omissions, named and graded nowhere.

## Before you go

Two habits from this tier.

**Name the pressure.** Pump, bit, annulus and equivalent circulating density are four different numbers with four different audiences.

**State the scope with the answer.** A transport ratio on a deviated well is an upper bound, and saying so takes one sentence.

## The one sentence

You can now say what the rock feels while the pumps are running. The next tier is what it feels when they are not.
