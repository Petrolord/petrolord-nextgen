# The volume is not the answer

A planning volume is a target. What the acid actually does is decided by three things the model does not contain.

{{panel:st-acid-explorer}}

## Placement

The calculation assumes the acid is distributed evenly over 100 m of interval. Nothing makes it behave that way. Acid goes where the resistance is least, which is the cleanest, highest permeability rock, which is precisely the rock that needed it least.

Left alone, a large fraction of the job disappears into a few feet of thief zone and the damaged intervals see almost nothing. The planning volume is then correct and the treatment is still a failure.

## Diversion

Diversion is the engineering that fixes placement: ball sealers on the perforations, particulate diverters, viscoelastic self diverting acid that gels as it spends, foam, or mechanical isolation and coiled tubing placement stage by stage.

None of that appears in the engine. The volume it returns is what a perfectly diverted job would need. Getting close to perfect diversion is usually more of the design work than choosing the volume was.

## Spending rate

Acid reacts as it travels, so the distance live acid penetrates is a race between the flow rate and the reaction rate. Raise the temperature and the acid spends sooner. Raise the strength and the reaction is faster as well as more capable. Raise the rate and you push live acid further, until you are above the matrix ceiling and fracturing instead.

The model contains no time, no rate, no temperature and no mineralogy. It is a volumetric bookkeeping rule with a placement factor bolted on. It cannot tell you whether the acid was still alive when it arrived.

## What the number is good for

It is a budget and a benchmark. If someone proposes 8 m3 for the published sandstone case, you can say immediately that even a perfectly placed job would leave most of the damaged annulus untouched, because reaching 0.6 m takes 29.55 m3 and clearing the damage entirely takes 67.72 m3.

It also sets the question the rest of the design has to answer. You now know how much acid, and every remaining decision is about getting that acid to the rock that needs it before it dies.
