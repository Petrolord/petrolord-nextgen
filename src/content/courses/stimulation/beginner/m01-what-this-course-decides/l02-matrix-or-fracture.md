# Matrix or fracture

The choice is not a preference. It follows from one question about the well, and the well usually answers it for you.

{{panel:st-acid-explorer}}

## The question

Is this well damaged, or is it just tight?

Both conditions look identical from the surface, a well producing far below what the reservoir map promised. They are distinguished by where the resistance sits, and a pressure transient test is what tells you: a large positive skin with a reasonable permeability says damage, a small skin with a poor permeability says tight rock.

## Why the answer decides the treatment

Damage lives in the first metre. In the published case it lives inside a radius of 0.9 m, with the rock there five times less permeable than the rock beyond it. Everything that treatment has to reach is within arm's length of the casing. Acid pumped below the fracture pressure will reach it, because that is exactly the volume the fluid enters first.

Low permeability is not a near wellbore condition at all. It is a property of every cubic metre out to the drainage radius, 300 m in the published case. A treatment that penetrates 0.9 m has touched a vanishing fraction of that. There is no near wellbore action that changes a reservoir property.

## What each route can actually buy

| Route | Published result |
| --- | --- |
| Damage present, untreated | skin 8.481054145 |
| Sandstone acid to a front at 0.6 m | skin 1.6218604324326575 |
| Sandstone acid reaching the full 0.9 m | skin 0 |
| Carbonate wormholes, 8 m3 pumped | skin -1.287406553 |
| Fracture, 150 m half length | skin -5.3116380662677045 |

Read the first three rows as one route. Matrix acid is a way of unwinding a number you created. Its best case is to give back what was taken, and in sandstone the arithmetic stops there.

The last row is a different mechanism. It does not remove anything. It adds a plane of conductivity, which is why it can take the skin somewhere the acid route cannot go.

## The honest overlap

A damaged tight well can want both, and a damaged high permeability well almost never wants a fracture. Cost decides the rest, and cost is not in this engine.
