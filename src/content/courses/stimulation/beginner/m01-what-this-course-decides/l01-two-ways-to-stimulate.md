# Two ways to stimulate

There are two things you can do to a well that will not flow, and they answer two different problems.

## The well that is choked at the sandface

Drilling, cementing, perforating and completion fluids all leave the rock nearest the wellbore worse than it was. Mud filtrate invades, fines migrate and plug pore throats, clays swell, scale precipitates. The reservoir a hundred metres out is untouched. The first metre is ruined.

That is a near wellbore problem, and it has a near wellbore answer. You pump a reactive fluid below fracture pressure, it goes into the rock through the pore network, and it either dissolves the plugging material or dissolves a path around it. Nothing is broken. The rock stays rock.

The published case in this course carries a permeability contrast of 5 between the undamaged rock and the damaged ring, with the damage reaching 0.9 m from a wellbore of radius 0.108 m. That costs a skin of 8.481054145. The whole of that number is manufactured, and in principle the whole of it can be given back.

## The well that was never going to flow

The second problem is different in kind. The rock is not damaged. It is simply tight. The published case sits in 1 mD, and 1 mD is 1 mD whether or not anyone ever drilled through it.

No amount of acid fixes that, because there is nothing to remove. The answer is to manufacture a flow path the reservoir never had. You pump above the fracture pressure, split the rock, hold the split open with proppant, and give the well a wide conductive plane to drain into instead of a 0.108 m hole.

## The two answers do not reach the same place

That difference shows up in the arithmetic. Take the damage away completely and the damage skin becomes exactly 0. That is the ceiling on a sandstone matrix job. Acidise a carbonate and the wormholes reach past the wellbore, so the published carbonate job returns a skin of -1.287406553.

The fracture is in another class. The published 150 m half length gives a fracture pseudo skin of -5.3116380662677045, equivalent to producing from a wellbore of effective radius 21.889652014700083 m.
