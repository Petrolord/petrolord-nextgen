# A wormhole is not dissolution

If carbonate acid had to dissolve rock evenly, nobody could afford to pump it.

{{panel:st-acid-explorer}}

## The thought experiment

Imagine enlarging the well by dissolving carbonate uniformly, a growing cylinder of rock taken away in every direction. To open the hole out by even a metre over 100 m of interval you would have to dissolve the whole solid volume of that shell, not just its pore space, and a cubic metre of acid dissolves only a fraction of a cubic metre of rock.

Put the sandstone numbers beside it for scale. There the acid only has to FILL the pore space of the annulus out to 0.6 m, and that already takes 29.55 m3. Dissolving the grains as well is a job of an entirely different order.

Uniform dissolution is not a design. It is a reason not to acidise carbonates at all.

## The instability

What saves it is that uniform dissolution does not happen. Acid dissolves fastest where the most acid arrives, which is wherever the flow is fastest. Dissolving there widens that path, which lowers its resistance, which sends still more of the next acid down it.

That is a positive feedback, and it runs away. A few channels outrun everything around them and drive far into the formation, while the rock between them is barely touched. Those channels are wormholes.

## Why that makes the job economic

You are no longer buying a dissolved shell. You are buying a small number of highly conductive channels that reach past the damaged zone, and the acid spent per metre of penetration collapses.

The published carbonate case is 8 m3 into the same 100 m interval, and the engine reports an effective radius of 0.39 m with a skin of -1.29. The well behaves as though it were drilled substantially larger.

## The rate that is not in this model

Wormholing only happens in a window. Pump too slowly and the acid spends at the rock face, widening the hole and going nowhere, which is face dissolution. Pump too fast and the acid fills everything ahead of it, which is closer to uniform attack and just as wasteful.

Between those is an optimum injection rate, and the number of pore volumes needed to break through is smallest there. The engine does not compute it. Selecting it is a laboratory result, and the next three lessons show exactly where it enters.
