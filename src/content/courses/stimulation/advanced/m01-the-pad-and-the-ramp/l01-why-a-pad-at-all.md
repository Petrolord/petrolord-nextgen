# Why a pad at all

The first seven tenths of this treatment carries no proppant at all, and that is the design working rather than failing.

{{panel:st-pack-explorer}}

## What the pad is

The pad is clean fluid, pumped ahead of any slurry, whose only job is to create a fracture that proppant can enter.

A fracture starts at zero width. It opens because the fluid inside it is at a pressure above the closure stress, and the width it reaches is set by the net pressure and the rock stiffness. On this case the PKN average width during pumping is 0.004015981871358954 m and the maximum at the wellbore is 0.006391633661942177 m. Those widths exist only while fluid is being pumped.

Nothing in that width appears instantly. It grows with the fracture, so at the first moment of the job there is nowhere for a grain to go.

## Why proppant cannot lead

A proppant grain that meets an aperture narrower than a few of its own diameters does not squeeze through. It stops, and the grains behind it stop against it, and the entrance bridges.

A bridge at the perforations is a screenout at the worst possible place. Pressure rises, the pumps come off, and the fracture that was going to be propped closes on clean fluid with a plug of proppant at its mouth.

So the rule is simple. Open first, fill second. The pad exists to buy the aperture that the first grain of the ramp will pass through.

## The pad is spent on purpose

Most of the pad never comes back. It leaks into the formation through the fracture faces, and that loss is exactly what the material balance in the tier below measured. On this case the injected volume is 209.09714590747427 m3, of which 172.95330906524367 m3 leaks off and only 36.143836842230584 m3 is still in the fracture when pumping stops. The fluid efficiency is 0.1728566723633056.

Calling the pad wasted misreads the job. It is the price of a fracture that is wide enough and long enough to be worth filling. What matters is that you pay the right price and no more.

## The published pad

At a leakoff coefficient of 0.0001 and a pump time of 3945.2291680655526 s, the schedule puts the pad fraction at 0.7052381992848291. That is 2782.3263142525348 s of clean fluid, or 147.46329465538435 m3 at the injection rate of 0.053 m3/s.

The remaining 1162.9028538130178 s carries every kilogram of proppant the job will place.
