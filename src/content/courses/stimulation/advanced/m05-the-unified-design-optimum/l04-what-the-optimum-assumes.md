# What the optimum assumes

The result is real. It is also answering a narrower question than the one you were asked.

## Fixed proppant volume

The optimum only exists because the proppant is fixed. That constraint is what puts length and width in competition, and without it the search has no minimum to find. More proppant is always better in this model.

So 1.6 answers how to spend a purchase, not whether to make it. The published case places 28915.069473784468 kg. Ask the same engine how much proppant to buy and it has nothing to say.

## A contained height

Every row of the sweep uses the same fracture height, 30 m in the published case, from a 30 m half-length to 440 m. The pack is spread over a taller or shorter fracture at no point in the calculation.

Height containment is an assumption about the bounding stresses, and it is the assumption most likely to fail in the field. It also fails in a direction the optimum does not see. Net pressure rises with length, from 2076588.0505800513 Pa at a 40 m half-length to 3436494.6050675157 Pa at 300 m on the PKN model, and rising net pressure is exactly what drives height out of the interval. The long fractures in the sweep are the ones least entitled to a fixed height.

## Steady radial flow

The pseudo-skin is defined against a well producing at steady state from a circular drainage area, 300 m in the published case, into a wellbore of radius 0.108 m. The effective wellbore radius means the radius of an unfractured well in that same geometry that would produce the same rate.

Nothing here is transient. A fractured well spends its early life in linear and bilinear flow, where the fracture is worth more than this steady comparison suggests, and none of that is in the number. The optimum is a late-time statement.

## One phase and one permeability

The formation is a single permeability, 1 mD in the published case, producing one phase. The pack is a single retained permeability, uniform from the wellbore to the tip.

Real packs are not uniform. Proppant settles, the last of it never reaches the tip, and the near-wellbore region carries every barrel the fracture collects while the tip carries almost none. The damage factor of 0.5 in the published case is the entire allowance made for all of that, applied as one multiplier.

Multiphase flow in the pack is absent too. Gas breaking out in the fracture reduces the conductivity to liquid exactly where the flux is highest.

## No economics at all

This is the important one. The optimum maximises a productivity index for a given proppant volume. It contains no proppant cost, no pumping cost, no oil price, no discount rate and no decline.

The design question an engineer is actually asked is which job makes the most money, and that question needs the cost of the proppant, the cost of the horsepower to place it, the incremental production the skin buys and what that production is worth over time. The answer to that question is not always the physics optimum, and where prices are low or proppant is expensive it can sit a long way from it.

Read 1.6 as the best you can do with what you have already decided to buy. Everything before that decision, and everything about whether the well earns back the job, is outside this model.
