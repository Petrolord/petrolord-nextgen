# Conductivity is a product

A pack is worth exactly two numbers multiplied together, and neither of them means anything on its own.

{{panel:st-pack-explorer}}

## The quantity the reservoir actually feels

Fracture conductivity is the pack permeability multiplied by the propped width. The engine calls it kfw and returns it in cubic metres, because a permeability in square metres times a width in metres is a volume.

Read that product as a flow capacity along the fracture. Darcy flow down the channel scales with the permeability of the material in the channel and with how much channel there is to flow through. Widen the channel and you gain capacity. Improve the grains and you gain capacity. The reservoir cannot tell which of the two you did.

The published job carries a retained pack permeability of 6.512770069532134e-11 m2 and a propped width of 1.5115434 mm. Multiply them and you have the published conductivity of 9.84433461550515e-14 m3, which is 99.747717 mD.m in the units a conductivity chart usually uses.

## Why a thin good pack equals a thick poor one

Because the two terms enter as a product, they trade against each other one for one. Halve the permeability and you need double the width for the same conductivity. That is the whole reason proppant selection and proppant volume are one decision and not two.

The catalogue makes the trade visible. The published 20/40 ISP ceramic is tabulated at 250 darcy at 2 kpsi of closure and at 70 darcy at 8 kpsi. The same pack material, crushed harder, needs proportionally more width to deliver the same conductivity. If you cannot get that width, a better proppant is the only lever left.

## Where the width comes from

Width is not free either. The engine builds it from the placed mass spread over both faces of both wings, giving an areal loading of 3.212785497087163 kg/m2 for the published job, and then divides by the density of the solid framework to get 1.5115434 mm of propped width.

So conductivity is really mass, geometry and grain quality collapsed into one number. Everything upstream in this course exists to feed that product.
