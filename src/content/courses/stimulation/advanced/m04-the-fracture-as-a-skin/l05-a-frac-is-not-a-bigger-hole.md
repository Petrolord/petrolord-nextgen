# A frac is not a bigger hole

The effective radius is an accounting device. Believe it too literally and you will misread the first month of the well.

{{panel:st-pack-explorer}}

## What the equivalence actually claims

The claim is narrow. Under stabilised radial flow, at the same drawdown in the same rock, a well of radius 21.889652014700083 m with no fracture would deliver what the published fractured well delivers with a drilled radius of 0.108 m.

That is all. It is a statement about one flow regime and one steady comparison. The correlation behind it was fitted to the stabilised solution, so the equivalence inherits exactly that scope and nothing wider.

## Where it does not hold

Early transient flow. A fractured well does not start in radial flow. It starts with flow perpendicular into the fracture face, and with a finite conductivity pack it passes through a bilinear period while pressure drops along the channel and into the face at the same time. A large hole shows none of that. The pressure transient of the published well early on looks nothing like a 21.889652014700083 m borehole, which is why fracture diagnostics read the early data and not the late data.

Cleanup. The equivalence assumes the pack is at its retained permeability, which the published job takes as half of the catalogue value. Immediately after the job the fluid is not broken, the fracture is loaded with gel, and the real conductivity is far below the design value. The effective radius is a promise about the settled well and not about the flowback.

Multiphase flow. The whole apparatus is single phase Darcy flow. Put a second phase in the fracture, drop below bubble point in the channel, or produce water into the pack and the relative permeability that appears is not in the correlation anywhere. Fines and proppant flowback do the same thing from the other direction.

## How to use it honestly

Quote the effective radius when you are handing a stabilised inflow relationship to someone else. Quote the pseudo-skin when you are comparing stimulation options. Do neither when you are forecasting the first weeks of production or interpreting a build-up, because those live in regimes the correlation never described.
