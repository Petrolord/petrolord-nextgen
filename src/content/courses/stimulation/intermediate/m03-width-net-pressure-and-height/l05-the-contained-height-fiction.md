# The contained height fiction

Real fractures grow out of zone. Both models are built on the promise that this one will not.

## What the models are pretending

PKN assumes the fracture reaches its full height immediately and then extends only in length. KGD assumes something stronger still, that height is so small relative to length that the vertical dimension can be ignored in the elasticity.

Neither is what happens. A fracture grows vertically wherever the net pressure exceeds the stress contrast at the boundary of the pay. Height is an outcome of the stress profile, and it changes through the job as net pressure changes.

## What the pretence buys

Closed form width, a single net pressure, an analytic material balance and an answer in the time it takes to press a key. That is not a small prize.

It is what lets you sweep half-length across six values and read the trend, which is the only way anyone builds an intuition for how these variables trade. A planar three dimensional simulator gives a better single answer and a far worse feel for the surface.

## What the pretence costs

Four things, in the order they hurt.

Height runs first and length falls short. Fluid that went into unwanted height did not go into the length you designed, so the actual half-length is less than the model's.

The extra area leaks more, so efficiency is worse than the balance says and the pad is undersized.

The proppant is spread over more face, so the propped width is thinner and the conductivity lower than designed.

Finally the pressure record misleads you. A rising net pressure is read as contained growth, and a flat or falling one as height growth, but that diagnosis only holds if the underlying model is the right one.

## The check the model cannot do for you

The published PKN net pressure is 2889735.9944400033 Pa. Ask whether the bounding formations carry a minimum stress higher than the pay by more than that. If they do not, the fracture will not stay in the 30 m you assumed, and the design's half-length is optimistic.

That check lives outside the engine, in the stress profile from your geomechanics work. The two dimensional result is a starting point to bracket, not a length to quote as though it were measured.
