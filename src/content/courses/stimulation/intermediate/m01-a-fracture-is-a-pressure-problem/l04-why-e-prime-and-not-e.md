# Why E prime and not E

Because the rock beside the fracture is not free to move sideways, and a rock that cannot move sideways behaves as a stiffer rock.

## What Young's modulus assumes

Young's modulus is measured on a sample that is free at its sides. Squeeze it along one axis and it bulges outward on the other two. That bulging is what the Poisson ratio counts, and it is why the sample deforms as easily as it does.

Uniaxial stress with free lateral faces is the condition E belongs to. Nothing about a fracture at depth looks like that.

## What confinement does

Open a fracture and the rock at its face is pushed back. It would like to bulge along the fracture, but the rock beside it is already there. It cannot expand into ground that is occupied.

Restrain that lateral movement and the same push produces less displacement. The material has not changed. Its response has, because the geometry removed one of the ways it was allowed to give. That is the factor of one over one minus nu squared.

Plane strain is therefore a statement about geometry, not about the rock. It says that in one direction the strain is zero because the surroundings will not allow it, and everything then happens in the remaining plane.

## Which plane, in each model

The two models make the same statement about different directions, and this is the cleanest way to hold them apart.

PKN assumes plane strain in vertical cross sections across the fracture length. The fracture is long, so a slice taken across it is confined by the slices on either side and deforms as if the length were infinite.

KGD assumes plane strain in horizontal planes. The fracture is short and tall, so a horizontal slice is confined above and below, and the whole height comes with it.

Both then divide by the same E prime. The confinement direction differs. The stiffening factor does not.

## The consequence for width

E prime is always larger than E, so a plane strain calculation always gives a narrower fracture than a naive uniaxial one. Not much narrower, because of the fourth root, but always in that direction.

If you meet a width calculation that used E where the published coefficient expected E prime, you know without checking the arithmetic that the width came out too wide.
