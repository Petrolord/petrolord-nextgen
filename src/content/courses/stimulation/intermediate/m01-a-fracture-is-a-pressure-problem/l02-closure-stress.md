# Closure stress

The rock sets this number, not the designer. Your job is to find out what it already is.

{{panel:st-frac-explorer}}

## What closes a fracture

A hydraulic fracture opens against the minimum principal stress and closes when the fluid pressure falls back below it. At normal depths that minimum principal stress is horizontal, so closure stress is the minimum horizontal stress at the depth of the fracture.

The fracture plane orients itself perpendicular to that minimum stress. This is not a choice either. It is the cheapest plane to open, so it is the plane you get.

In the published case closure is 38.13195 MPa at a mid perforation true vertical depth of 2124.897478 m.

## Why it is read, not assumed

The temptation is to take a gradient off a chart and multiply. That is a starting guess and nothing more.

The minimum horizontal stress varies bed to bed. Two formations at the same depth with different stiffness, different pore pressure or a different tectonic history hold different stresses, and the differences decide whether a fracture stays in your zone or leaves it. A shale with a higher closure stress above and below your sand is what contains the height, and that containment is a stress contrast you either measured or invented.

So closure is read from a stress profile over the interval, anchored by a step rate test, a minifrac or a diagnostic fracture injection test. The profile is the deliverable. A single number pulled from it is only the value at one depth.

## True vertical depth, not measured depth

Stress is a function of true vertical depth, because it is the overburden and the pore pressure at that vertical position that set it.

The published interval runs from 2450 m to 2550 m measured depth, mid point 2500 m, but the mid point true vertical depth is 2124.897478 m. The well is deviated, and using the measured depth would place the perforations more than three hundred metres deeper than they are, with a closure stress to match. Every stress lookup takes the vertical depth.

## What it does to the treating pressure

Bottomhole treating pressure is closure plus net. Closure is the large fixed part the earth contributes, net pressure the small model dependent part the treatment contributes.

An error in closure moves the predicted treating pressure by the same amount, one for one, with no damping at all.
