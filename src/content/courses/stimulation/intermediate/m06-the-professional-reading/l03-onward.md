# Onward

You can make a fracture. The Expert tier fills it, and then makes it earn its keep.

## Four things you have not done

The pad. You know that leakoff removes most of the injected fluid, and that the efficiency on the published case is 0.1728566723633056. What you have not done is act on it. Proppant pumped into a fracture that has not yet been created is proppant that bridges, so a job starts with clean fluid whose only job is to open width and lose itself to the rock. How much of the treatment that pad should be follows from the efficiency alone.

The ramp. After the pad, the proppant concentration rises through the job rather than sitting flat, so that the last slurry in arrives at a fracture that is closing behind it. Choosing that ramp, and stepping it for a blender, is the next piece of arithmetic.

The pack. Once pumping stops the fracture closes onto the grains, and what is left is a propped width and a conductivity, both smaller than the dynamic width you computed here. The pack also loses conductivity to crushing, embedment and gel residue, carried as a damage factor rather than pretended away.

The skin. A fracture is only worth what it does for the well. The propped conductivity is compared with the reservoir through a dimensionless conductivity, which converts into a pseudo-skin and an effective wellbore radius. That is where the design becomes a production forecast, and where a published optimum dimensionless conductivity of 1.6 tells you that longer is not automatically better.

## What does not get fixed

The Expert tier does not repair module five. It still fixes the height, has no tip physics and spreads proppant uniformly. The extra work happens downstream of the same geometry, so carry the limits forward with the method. A designer who quotes a half-length without saying which model produced it, what height was assumed and what the efficiency was has quoted a number rather than a design.
