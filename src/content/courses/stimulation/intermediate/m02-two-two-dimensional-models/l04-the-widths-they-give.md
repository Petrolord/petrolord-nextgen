# The widths they give

Same rock, same rate, same fluid, same target length. Two models, and one of them says the fracture is two and a half times fatter.

{{panel:st-frac-explorer}}

## The sweep

Both models were run at six half-lengths with everything else held at the published values, and these are the average widths.

| Half-length, m | PKN w_avg, mm | KGD w_avg, mm | KGD / PKN |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 40 | 2.885918 | 5.403467 | 1.872357 |
| 70 | 3.319277 | 7.148115 | 2.153516 |
| 100 | 3.628849 | 8.543632 | 2.354364 |
| 150 | 4.015982 | 10.463769 | 2.605532 |
| 220 | 4.419515 | 12.672254 | 2.867340 |
| 300 | 4.775834 | 14.798004 | 3.098517 |

At the published design point, a half-length of 150 m, KGD gives 2.605532 times the average width of PKN. Nothing physical has changed between the two columns. Only the assumption about which direction the rock is confined in.

## Maximum as well as average

At that same point the maximum widths are 6.391634 mm for PKN and 13.322885 mm for KGD.

The gap in maxima is not the same as the gap in averages, because the two models take different shape factors. PKN averages with pi over 5, KGD with pi over 4, so KGD keeps a larger share of its own maximum. That shape factor difference alone puts a further 5 to 4 on top of the maximum width ratio.

The reason is the geometry from the two previous lessons. PKN tapers twice, elliptically across the height and again towards the tip. KGD tapers once, in plan view only, because its whole height opens as a rectangle.

## Why the ratio grows with length

Read the right hand column downwards. The disagreement is not a constant offset. It widens from 1.872357 at 40 m to 3.098517 at 300 m.

That follows from the equations. PKN width goes as the half-length to the one quarter, KGD width goes as the half-length to the one half. Divide one by the other and what is left is the length over the height to the one quarter, multiplied by the fixed ratio of the coefficients 3.22 to 2.31 and by the 5 to 4 of the shape factors.

So the two models agree best in the geometry neither of them is comfortable in, and disagree most in the long fracture that real designs aim for.

## Exercise

Divide the published maximum widths, 13.322885 mm by 6.391634 mm, and check that multiplying by 5 over 4 recovers the average width ratio of 2.605532.

Then hold the half-length at 150 m in the panel and raise the fracture height. Only one of the two widths moves. Say which, say why, and say how tall the fracture would have to become before the two models agreed.
