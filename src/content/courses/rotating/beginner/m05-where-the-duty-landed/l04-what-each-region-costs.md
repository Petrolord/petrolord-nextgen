# What each region costs

A label on its own tells a reader where the duty landed. The note tells them what it costs to leave it there. There are four such notes, one for each band that is not preferred, counting the two ends of the range separately, and this lesson reads all four in the engine's own words.

{{panel:fc-pump-explorer}}

## Below the preferred band

At 60.000000 percent, labelled "allowable, low", the note reads: "below 70 percent of best efficiency flow: suction and discharge recirculation begin here, and bearing and seal life shorten".

Recirculation is flow going the wrong way inside the machine. Too little flow through an impeller designed for more leaves the passages partly stalled, and fluid turns back on itself at the eye and at the periphery. It is unsteady, so it loads the shaft in a direction that changes, and that is why bearings and seals come into it. Notice that this is the one sentence in the whole package that says anything at all about seals and bearings, and it says it in prose.

## Far below it

At 30.000000 percent, labelled "outside": "far below best efficiency flow: this pump is being throttled hard, which wastes power and damages the machine. A smaller pump, a trim or a variable speed drive is the answer".

This note is unusual in the module because it prescribes. A duty this far down the curve usually means a control valve is absorbing the difference between what the pump makes and what the station needs, and that difference is paid for in electricity. The three remedies it offers are three ways of making the machine smaller instead.

## Above the preferred band

At 130.000000 percent, labelled "allowable, high": "above 120 percent of best efficiency flow: the required NPSH climbs steeply with flow here, and this module carries NPSHr as a single number rather than a curve, so the vendor curve has to be read at THIS flow before the suction margin means anything".

That note names a limit of the module it lives in, and the next lesson is entirely about it.

## Far above it

At 160.000000 percent, labelled "outside": "far above best efficiency flow: the pump will run out of NPSH and may cavitate, and the driver may overload".

Two failures in one sentence, and they arrive from opposite ends of the machine. The suction side runs out of margin because the required NPSH climbs with flow, which is the same mechanism the allowable-high note names. The driver side overloads because the machine is being asked to move far more fluid than it was selected for. A duty this far to the right threatens both at once.

## The mistake

Taking the label and discarding the note. The note is the only part of this return that says what the band means physically, and in the high case it is also the only part that tells you the return you are reading is incomplete.

## Exercise

Give the four notes and say which region each belongs to. Then name the two separate failure modes the far-above note warns about, and say which end of the machine each one belongs to.
