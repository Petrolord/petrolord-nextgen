# Fuel out of the stream being compressed

A gas-engine or turbine driver burns gas, and on a compressor station the gas it burns is usually taken out of the stream the machine is there to move. That makes fuel a subtraction from throughput rather than a line on a utilities bill.

## What the SOKU driver burns

At a heat rate of 8100.000000 Btu per hp hr on a fuel gas of 985.000000 Btu per scf, the driver for the SOKU train burns 36296606.9250 Btu per hr, which is 0.884384331 MMscfd. Its thermal efficiency is 31.412760 percent.

The station is compressing 26.000000 MMscfd, so that fuel is 3.401478 percent of the stream being compressed. Part of what arrives is consumed making the rest of it move, and that part is gas the field does not sell.

## The chain that produces it

The brake horsepower of the train sets the shaft work. The heat rate converts shaft work into fuel heat, and it is the driver's characteristic rather than the compressor's. The heating value converts fuel heat into a fuel volume, and it is a property of the gas being burned.

Each of those three is somebody else's number. The brake power came from the staging and the stages. The heat rate comes from the driver vendor at the site conditions. The heating value comes from the gas analysis. A fuel figure quoted without all three stated cannot be reproduced by the person reading it.

Across those six rows the fuel and the share of throughput rise with the heat rate while the thermal efficiency falls. Moving from 6200.000000 to 11000.000000 Btu per hp hr takes the share from 2.603601 to 4.619291 percent.

## Why it is worth the arithmetic

Fuel gas is the part of a compression scheme that is easiest to leave out of an economics case and hardest to leave out of the real plant. It reduces sales volume every day for the life of the station, it is the reason a more efficient driver can be worth a large capital premium, and on a field where the gas has a contract price the share of throughput is directly a revenue number.

It also compounds with the staging. More machines means more brake power, and more brake power at the same heat rate means more of the stream going up the stack.

## The mistake

The mistake is quoting fuel in Btu per hour and stopping there. Btu per hour is what the driver needs and MMscfd is what the field loses, and the second number is the one a commercial reader can act on. The second mistake is comparing two drivers on heat rate alone when the fuel gas heating values differ.
