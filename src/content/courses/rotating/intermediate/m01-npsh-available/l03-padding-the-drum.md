# Padding the drum

Of the three parts of NPSH available, one can usually be changed on an installation that is already built. Raising the pressure on the suction drum is that one.

## The sweep

The OKONO liquid, its vapour pressure of 0.950000 psia, its gravity of 1.040000, the source standing 6.000000 ft above the pump and the 5.500000 ft of suction friction are all held. Only the drum pressure moves.

The two columns walk together because the static column and the friction are held on every row. Padding the drum reaches one of the three parts and leaves the other two exactly where the survey put them.

## What the sweep is really showing

The reason to run a sweep rather than a single call is that a suction question is almost never asked as a yes or a no. It is asked as how much, and the answer is a curve of available head against the one condition anybody can change without moving steel.

At 14.700000 psia the drum leaves 31.040865 ft of available head. At 24.500000 psia it leaves 52.808173 ft, and at 60.000000 psia it leaves 131.659135 ft. Those are three different suctions on one installation, and which of them is worth having depends on something this call has not been told.

## The other two parts, and what they cost

The static column and the friction can be changed too, and the difference is what it takes. Raising the source, lowering the pump or shortening and enlarging the suction line are all real answers, and all of them are steel. Padding is a valve and a gas supply.

That is why a suction survey is worth assembling part by part. The parts are not interchangeable as engineering even when they are interchangeable as arithmetic, and a figure that arrives as one number hides which of them is available.

## The mistake

The mistake is running the sweep and then reading a row as a verdict. Not one column here says whether any of these suctions works. The sweep is the supply side of the question, and every row of it is still waiting on a required NPSH from the pump vendor before it means anything at all.

The second mistake is sweeping the drum pressure while quietly moving something else. A sweep whose gravity or vapour pressure drifts between rows is a set of different liquids, and the shape it draws is not the shape of padding.
