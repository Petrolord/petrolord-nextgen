# Onward

Everything in this tier had a curve to work with. A user typed catalogue points in, the engine fitted them, and the answer was a crossing. The Expert tier gives that up.

## A fluid that will not hold still

Compression is the same discipline with the fluid no longer incompressible and the machine no longer describable by a curve anyone can type in. Answers stop coming out of an intersection and start coming out of a thermodynamic path.

That changes what a stage is. A compressor stage makes a pressure ratio, and what it costs in work and in temperature depends on the path taken to get there. The Expert tier works that path, the exponent behind it, the compressibility at both ends, and the two heads a stage reports so neither gets quoted as the other.

## A count that has to be discovered

A pump station's machine count is an input. A compressor's stage count is an answer, out of two limits at once, with the engine naming which governed. The stages then chain into a train with a real cooling duty between them, and the trade between cooling and power reverses across a change in the stage count.

## Where both engines stop

Eight things in this course are held for the literature: the viscosity correction, the trim shortfall model and its implied efficiency, the operating-region bands, the NPSH margin rule, the machine-screening thresholds, the 300 degF default discharge limit, what the implied water density is away from real water, and every published golden case. The affinity speed band is held on the same footing, as a sanity bound.

Four things are absent from the package altogether, and the course names the seam rather than papering over it. There is no compressor surge line, no surge margin, no recycle valve and no anti-surge control. There is no seal and no bearing calculation. There is no machine curve, no wheel selection, no valve dynamics and no rod loading. And there is no required-NPSH-against-flow curve, which is why the vendor curve has to be read at the duty flow before any suction margin means anything.

## What it does answer

The duty a vendor should be quoting against, the power and the stage count to expect, the suction margin a selection has to survive, and the reasons behind all four. That is a smaller claim than a selection package usually makes, and every figure in this course stands behind it.
