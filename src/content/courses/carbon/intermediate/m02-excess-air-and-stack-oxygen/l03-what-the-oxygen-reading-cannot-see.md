# What the oxygen reading cannot see

excessAirFromFlueOxygen works under one stated assumption, which SECTION 12 prints. This lesson reads that sentence word by word, because it limits every excess air figure in SECTION 12 and, through them, every efficiency and saving later in this tier.

{{panel:carbon-efficiency-explorer}}

## The assumption, verbatim

SECTION 12 prints the engine's assumption: "Complete combustion. An oxygen reading alone cannot see carbon monoxide, so a stack making CO will read as if it had more excess air than it has."

It is three statements in two sentences.

The first is the model. The engine takes every fuel molecule in the invented Isiokpo analysis to burn completely, and the products of SECTION 11 are the products of that complete burn: products.co2PerKmolFuel 1.098000 and products.h2oPerKmolFuel 2.033000 kmol per kmol fuel.

The second is the limit of the instrument. The engine is given one measurement, dry stack oxygen, and nothing in that measurement is carbon monoxide.

The third is the direction of the error. A stack making CO will read as if it had more excess air than it has. The engine's sentence names the direction and gives no size, and the digest prints no case with carbon monoxide in the flue gas, so this course gives no size either.

## Where the direction matters

The relation of lesson two turns a reading into excess air, and SECTION 12 prints a higher excess air at every higher reading in its table: 13.9199 percent at 2.8 percent oxygen, 32.1223 percent at 5.5 percent. A stack reading as if it had more excess air than it has is a stack whose reported excess air sits too high on that table.

Module four prices the move from 5.5 percent to 2.8 percent oxygen. In practice, tuning a heater down toward low oxygen brings it toward the point where it makes carbon monoxide, which is why the oxygen reading and the burner's combustion test are read together.

## The floor the engine asks for

The engine does not guess where carbon monoxide begins. SECTION 14 prints its refusal when the minimum safe stack oxygen is left blank: "A minimum safe stack oxygen is required and is not defaulted. Below some excess air a burner makes carbon monoxide, and where that point sits depends on the burner, the fuel and the draught control."

The two sentences belong together. The assumption says the reading cannot see carbon monoxide. The refusal says the point where carbon monoxide begins depends on the burner, the fuel and the draught control. Neither is something the engine reads from the stack, so the floor is declared by the caller. At Isiokpo that floor is 2 percent, declared after a combustion test the digest marks invented. Module four reads it in full.
