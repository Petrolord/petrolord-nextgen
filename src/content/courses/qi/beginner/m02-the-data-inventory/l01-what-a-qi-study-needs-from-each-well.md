# What a QI study needs from each well

Before a single spectrum or substitution, a study lists what each well carries. The list is short. Rock physics needs Vp, from a sonic, and density. A shear sonic lets every shear-dependent result be measured at the well; without one, shear has to be estimated. Porosity, shale volume and saturation feed the substitution. Checkshots tie the well to the seismic in time. The elevation and the deviation survey place the well in depth.

Of the 10 wells with logs, 10 carry a shear sonic, 9 a density log and 10 two or more checkshot levels. Ekene-9 is the well without density; its curves are CALI, GR, DT, DTS and RT.

## Reading the inventory against the needs

Sonic and density decide whether rock physics can run at all. Every logged Ekene well has a sonic, and every one except Ekene-9 has density, so nine wells can carry an elastic set.

Shear is complete. With a measured DTS at every logged well, the shear trend of a later module can be fitted on the field's own rock, and the published regression becomes a comparison.

Checkshots are plentiful: the vertical wells carry 21 or 25 levels and the deviated wells 24. A study that later takes the wells to the seismic has its time-depth control in place.

Deviation matters for depth. Ekene-8, Ekene-9 and Ekene-10 are deviated, so their depths along the hole differ from their vertical depths and each needs its survey. The four pack wells, Ekene-1 to Ekene-4, are vertical, so their measured depths read straight down.

## What the pack adds

The pack carries more than the headers. For Ekene-1 to Ekene-4 it holds Vp, Vs and density from DT, DTS and RHOB, GR, and the kit's own PHIT, VSH and SW over the Ekene Sand and the Ogbia Shale above it, sampled every 0.500000 ft. The three petrophysical curves are the interpretation the kit was built from. In practice those curves come from Petrophysics Studio before a QI study starts, and the pack carries them for that reason. The next lesson shows what happens to a well whose files do not carry them.

## Why list it at all

An inventory is the first part of a study, and it shapes every later step. A well without density drops out of every elastic result; a well without shear forces an estimate into every shear-dependent figure; a well without checkshots cannot be placed on the seismic. Writing the list first means each later figure can say which wells it rests on.
