# The number in proportion

The last thing this module does is put 0.656868 MMstb next to everything else you have measured in this course. The comparison decides how much effort a property model deserves.

## What the table says about effort

The property model as a whole sits below the fault position, below the boundary convention and below the cross fault control, all of which came from the tier below and none of which usually appears in a report.

Its genuinely spatial part, 0.252229 MMstb, is smaller still. It is about a quarter of what a tie break in a labelling routine is worth, and about an eighth of what the map's ignorance of the fault is worth.

Meanwhile the choice of population method, from constant to krige, is worth 0.793817 MMstb, which is more than the property model is credited with in total. The decision about how to model the property is larger than the decision about whether to.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean property modelling is a waste of time, and the table can be misread that way.

Three things are worth saying against that reading.

The uplift is not the uncertainty. The property model moves the answer by 0.66 MMstb, and the uncertainty on the porosity data itself is larger. Six wells with one to two porosity units of uncertainty each give an uncertainty on the mean of around 0.6 porosity units, which is 3 percent of the booking, or 0.36 MMstb, and that exists whether or not you model spatially.

The effect grows with property contrast. Ekene's porosities span 0.17 to 0.23, a 29 percent spread. A field with porosities spanning a factor of two would show a far larger effect, and fields like that exist.

And the property model does something the constant cannot, which is to say where the good rock is. A booking is not the only output. A map of modelled porosity informs well placement, and being right about that is worth more than 0.66 MMstb.

## The general lesson

Rank the effects before choosing where to spend the study.

The habit worth taking from this course is to compute the sensitivities early and cheaply, then allocate the effort. At Ekene that ranking says: pin the contact first, then decide whether the fault seals and where it is, then worry about the properties.

That ordering is not what a typical study does, because the contact is somebody else's problem and the property model is the part with interesting software attached.

## Worked example

Allocate a study budget using the table.

Suppose you have four weeks. One week can buy a pressure survey that pins the contact to within 3 m. One week can buy a seismic reprocessing that halves the fault position uncertainty. Two weeks can buy a detailed property model with facies.

Pinning the contact to 3 m reduces an uncertainty of roughly 8 to 10 MMstb per 10 m to about 2.5 to 3 MMstb. That is a reduction of several million barrels of uncertainty for one week.

Halving the fault position uncertainty is worth about 0.45 MMstb of uncertainty on the block split, and it does not change the field total at all.

The detailed property model addresses a total effect of 0.25 MMstb of spatial variation and would reduce, not eliminate, the uncertainty within that.

The contact wins by a factor of ten, and it would win even if the property work took a day.
