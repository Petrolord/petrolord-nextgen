# The top of the ladder

This is the last lesson of the last tier of this course. It is worth saying what the three tiers together have built and where the limits now sit.

## The three tiers

The Associate tier booked one volume from one map and one contact: 12.139208 MMstb over 169 cells, with four constants.

The Professional tier divided that volume between compartments that will be developed separately, and priced the conventions involved: 117 cells and 9.855617 MMstb west, 52 and 2.283591 east, with a tie break worth 0.901423 MMstb and a map that borrowed 1.989889 MMstb of western booking from a well across the seal.

The Expert tier let a property vary across the field and measured what that did: 12.796077 MMstb, an uplift of 0.656868, of which 0.404640 was only a better constant.

Each tier made the one below it more demanding rather than replacing it, and none of them changed the rock.

## What you can now do that you could not

Take a mapped accumulation and a fault and produce a defensible compartment booking with its conventions priced.

Take a set of well property values and populate a grid from them, choosing a method you can justify, and say what the choice was worth.

Distinguish three averages of a varying property, know which one a booking implies, and recover it from somebody else's report by dividing their pore volume by their net volume.

Decompose a modelling change into the part that is arithmetic and the part that depends on the model.

And rank the sensitivities of a volumetric study, which is the habit that decides where the next week of work should go.

## What this course still cannot do

Three limits are worth naming, since the value of knowing them is that you will not reach for this course when the answer is elsewhere.

Water saturation is still a constant, and it is the property with the strongest physical reason to vary and the largest likely effect. A saturation height model is the obvious next piece of work on Ekene and it is a different body of technique.

The model is deterministic. Every number in three tiers has been a single value: one contact, one fault position, one plane. Every one of them has an uncertainty, several of them have been priced here one at a time, and none of them has been propagated together. That is what a probabilistic volumetric study does, and the sensitivities computed in these tiers are exactly its inputs.

And nothing here is recoverable oil. Every number in this course is oil in place. What fraction of it can be produced, over what time, at what cost, is the subject of the reserves and economics work downstream.

## What the downstream inherits

Every assumption made in this course travels with the number.

A contact assumed rather than measured becomes a development plan built on rock nobody has seen. A compartment booked from a map that did not know about its own fault becomes a well planned against a volume that partly belongs to the other side. A property model quoted without its residuals becomes a well placed on the strength of a plane fitted to six points.

None of those failures announce themselves downstream, because what travels is a number and what stays behind is the provenance. The single most useful habit this course can leave you with is attaching the provenance to the number every time, even when it makes the report longer and less confident.

## Worked example

The Ekene field in the form this course would report it.

The Ekene SAND holds 12.796077 MMstb of stock tank oil initially in place at an assumed oil water contact of 1560 m, booked over 169 cells of a 100 m grid with net to gross 0.8, a trend porosity model averaging 0.210822 by volume, water saturation 0.35 and a formation volume factor of 1.2.

The contact dominates every other uncertainty: 10 m either way moves the field by plus 9.905243 or minus 8.303393 MMstb.

The field is cut by a sealing fault at an easting of about 1800 m into compartments holding 81 and 19 percent of the volume, which are not in communication, so the total is not a drainable volume. The fault position is uncertain by at least 100 m, worth 0.901423 MMstb transferred between compartments, and the structural surfaces were gridded without knowledge of the fault.

The porosity model is a least squares plane fitted to six well values, improving westward at 0.020437 per kilometre, which honours none of the six and misses four by more than 8 percent. It raises the booking by 0.656868 MMstb over the handed out constant of 0.20, of which 0.404640 is attributable to that constant having been below the well average and 0.252229 to the spatial model.

Water saturation remains a constant, which is the largest outstanding modelling gap.

That paragraph is what three tiers were for.

## Exercise

Name the one number in the report above you would most want to improve, and the measurement that would improve it.

Self check: the contact, since it moves the answer by roughly eight to ten million barrels per ten metres, an order of magnitude more than anything else in the study. A pressure survey giving a free water level in each compartment would improve it, and it would also test whether the fault seals, since two compartments in communication must share a contact.
