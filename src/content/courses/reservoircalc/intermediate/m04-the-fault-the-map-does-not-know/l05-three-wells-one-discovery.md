# Three wells, one discovery

The fault divides the six Ekene wells evenly, three to a side. That symmetry is the last thing in this module worth taking apart, because it is the kind of fact that gets into a summary table and misleads everybody who reads it.

## The table that misleads

| Block | Wells | Cells | STOIIP (MMstb) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| West | 3 | 117 | 9.855617 |
| East | 3 | 52 | 2.283591 |

Read that and the natural conclusion is that both compartments are equally well controlled and that the east is simply smaller. Add one column and the conclusion collapses.

| Block | Wells | Oil at 1560 m | Dry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| West | Ekene-1, Ekene-3, Ekene-5 | 3 | 0 |
| East | Ekene-6, Ekene-2, Ekene-4 | 1 | 2 |

Ekene-2 tops at 1565 m and Ekene-4 at 1590 m, both below the contact. The east block has three wells and one discovery.

## What a dry well does and does not tell you

A dry well is not a wasted well for mapping purposes. Ekene-2 and Ekene-4 are two of the six control points that built the surfaces, and their deep picks are what defines the eastern flank of the structure. Without them the map would have no idea how fast the surface falls away to the east.

What they cannot do is confirm that the eastern compartment holds oil. Only Ekene-6 does that, and Ekene-6 is 100 m from the fault, at the very western edge of its own block.

So the eastern compartment's 2.283591 MMstb rests on one well, sitting at the extreme edge of the compartment, whose influence on the mapped surface is shared with the block next door.

## The other direction

The dependency runs both ways and the previous lessons measured the return leg. The west block's booking falls by 1.989889 MMstb when Ekene-6 is removed.

Put the two together. Ekene-6 is the only proof of oil in the east block and it supplies a fifth of the west block's volume. One well, on one side of a sealing fault, is load bearing for both compartments.

## What happens with block by block control

There is one more experiment worth reporting. Grid the surfaces using only the three western wells, then apply the same contact and the same fault.

The west block collapses to 48 cells and 3.406726 MMstb, and the east block to nothing at all.

The east falling to zero is expected: with no eastern control, no eastern node is within 800 m of a well, so no eastern node is live. The west falling from 117 cells to 48 is the surprise, and it is the same lesson again in a stronger form. More than half of the west block's mapped area is live only because eastern wells are within reach of it.

A model built strictly block by block, with each compartment mapped from its own wells, would book about a third of what the shared map books for the west and nothing for the east. That is not a better answer. It is the opposite extreme, and the truth is between the two.

## Reading it off the panel

Set the capstone configuration and read the well posts.

{{panel:rc-block-explorer}}

Each well carries a W or an E for its block, and dry wells are drawn as hollow red circles. You should see three white circles in the blue block, one white circle in the amber block hard against the fault, and two red circles out east.

Now set the east contact to 1570 m. Ekene-2, at 1565 m, turns from dry to oil bearing, and the east block jumps to 73 cells. A ten metre change in an assumption converts a dry hole into a discovery, on paper. That is worth sitting with, and it is exactly what module five is about.

## Worked example

Count the evidence supporting each block's booking, treating a well as evidence only for the block it sits in.

The west block's 9.855617 MMstb is supported by three oil bearing wells inside it, spread across the block, with tops of 1548, 1541 and 1552 m. Those three agree with each other closely, so the western surface is well constrained and its oil is confirmed.

The east block's 2.283591 MMstb is supported by one oil bearing well at the block's edge, plus two dry wells that constrain the surface but confirm no oil. The eastern surface is constrained and the eastern oil is not.

Those two bookings are quoted to the same number of digits and are not remotely equally certain. Any honest report of this partition says so.

## Exercise

State what the east block's booking would rest on if Ekene-6 were reinterpreted as belonging to the west block, for example because the fault is remapped 200 m further east.

Self check: it would rest on no oil bearing well at all. Both remaining eastern wells are dry at the contact, so the east block's oil would be an entirely mapped quantity with no direct confirmation, while the west block would gain both the well and the volume. That is a large change in confidence produced by moving a line 200 m, and it is why the fault position deserves to be reported with its uncertainty.
