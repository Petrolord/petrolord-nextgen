# Booking two blocks honestly

This module has shown that a partitioned field carries one contact assumption per compartment and that the compartments respond to those assumptions very differently. This lesson is about how to write that down.

## What goes wrong with a single number

A report that says the field holds 12.139208 MMstb is now three separate claims wearing one number.

It claims that the western compartment holds 9.855617 MMstb, which rests on a western contact at 1560 m and on a map that borrowed an eastern well.

It claims that the eastern compartment holds 2.283591 MMstb, which rests on an eastern contact at 1560 m that no observation supports directly and that the data brackets only between about 1546 and 1565 m.

And it claims that adding those two together describes something, which it does not, because no well can produce both.

## The shape of an honest report

Four things belong in the summary of a compartmentalised booking, and none of them is difficult.

State each compartment separately, with its own volume, its own contact and the evidence for that contact. Two lines rather than one.

State the field sum if you must, and say explicitly that it is an arithmetic sum across compartments that are not in communication, so it is not a drainable volume.

State the partition assumptions: where the fault is, what its position uncertainty is worth, and which side the boundary cells were assigned to.

State the map's relationship to the fault. If the surfaces were gridded across the fault, say so and say what it is worth.

For Ekene those four are all quantities this tier has already produced.

## A worked report

Here is the whole thing written out for the capstone configuration.

The Ekene SAND is cut by a north to south sealing fault at an easting of about 1800 m into a western and an eastern compartment.

The western compartment holds 9.855617 MMstb of stock tank oil initially in place over 117 grid cells, at an assumed oil water contact of 1560 m, with a mean oil column of 15.45 m. Three wells lie in this compartment and all three found oil.

The eastern compartment holds 2.283591 MMstb over 52 cells at the same assumed contact, with a mean column of 8.06 m. Three wells lie in this compartment and only one found oil; the eastern contact is bracketed by well data between about 1546 and 1565 m, over which range the compartment's volume runs from under half a million to about 2.9 MMstb.

The two compartments sum to 12.139208 MMstb. That sum is not a drainable volume, since no well can produce both compartments.

The fault position carries an uncertainty of at least 100 m, which is worth about 0.9 MMstb transferred between compartments. Cells lying exactly on the fault easting have been assigned to the eastern compartment; the alternative convention would move a further 0.901423 MMstb west. The structural surfaces were gridded from all six wells without regard to the fault, and removing the single eastern well that found oil reduces the western compartment by 1.989889 MMstb, so a fifth of the western booking depends on control across the seal.

## Why this is not hedging

That report contains more numbers than the single figure it replaces, and every one of them is a computed result rather than a caveat. It tells a reader which compartment to develop first, which number to trust, what would change the answer, and what to measure next.

The single figure told them none of that while sounding more certain. Precision without provenance reads as confidence, and it is the most common way for a volumetric study to mislead.

## Worked example

Rewrite the eastern compartment line for a case where the eastern contact has been measured at 1552 m by a pressure survey.

The eastern compartment holds oil above a measured contact at 1552 m. Reading between the panel's 1550 and 1560 m cases, the volume is a little above 0.327859 MMstb, in the region of half a million barrels, over roughly 20 to 25 cells.

Now the compartment's contact is evidence rather than assumption, its range collapses, and the development question answers itself: half a million barrels will not carry a well. The measurement was worth more than any amount of further mapping, which is the kind of conclusion a good volumetric study should be willing to reach about itself.

## Exercise

Write the two compartment lines for a fault at 1900 m with both contacts at 1560 m, using the panel for the numbers. Include for each compartment the volume, the cell count and the mean column, and add one sentence on what the fault position uncertainty is worth.

Self check: at 1900 m the western compartment holds 10.757040 MMstb over 130 cells and the eastern compartment 1.382168 MMstb over 39 cells, summing to the same 12.139208 MMstb. Moving the fault back to 1800 m transfers 0.901423 MMstb from west to east, so 100 m of fault position uncertainty is worth about 8 percent of the field and about 65 percent of the eastern compartment.
