# Why contacts differ across a seal

Everything up to here held both blocks at 1560 m. That was a convenience, and it is the last simplification this tier removes. Two compartments that do not communicate have no reason to share an oil water contact.

## Where a contact comes from

An oil water contact is the level at which the buoyancy of the oil column is balanced against the water pressure. Where it sits depends on how much oil arrived, how much the trap could hold, and how the pressures in the oil and water phases behave.

Each of those is a property of a compartment rather than of a field.

How much oil arrived depends on the charge path from the source rock. A compartment that lies along the migration route fills first and may fill to its spill point; one that is shielded may receive less, or none.

How much the trap can hold depends on the compartment's own geometry: its spill point, and the capacity of the fault that bounds it.

How the pressures behave depends on the compartment's own aquifer and its own production history.

Compartments that have never been in fluid communication have therefore had independent histories, and the contact is the visible record of that history. There is no mechanism that would force them to agree.

## What equal contacts would mean

If two compartments do share a contact, that is itself a finding, and it usually means one of two things.

Either they are in communication after all, at least over geological time, so the fault leaks or leaked, and the compartments equilibrated.

Or they are separate but were charged from the same source through the same path to the same fill level, and their spill points happen to coincide. That happens, and it is a coincidence rather than a rule.

So the assumption this tier has been making, that both blocks share 1560 m, is not a neutral default. It is a claim about the field.

## What the evidence looks like

Contacts are established from a small set of observations, and it is worth knowing which ones can distinguish compartments.

A contact seen directly in a well, from logs or from a pressure gradient change, applies to that well's compartment and to no other. Ekene's 1560 m contact came from somewhere, and if it came from a western well, it constrains the western compartment.

Pressure gradients measured in the oil and water legs can be extrapolated to their intersection, which gives a free water level for that compartment. Two compartments with genuinely different contacts usually show different pressure regimes as well, which is a second and independent signature.

The lowest known oil and the highest known water bracket the contact in each compartment separately. In the east block, the lowest known oil is at Ekene-6 and the highest known water is at Ekene-2, at 1565 m, so the eastern contact is bracketed only very loosely.

## Worked example

Apply that bracket to the Ekene east block honestly.

Ekene-6 found oil with its top at 1546 m. Its base is at 1580 m, so it has oil at least down to wherever the contact is within that well, which the picks alone do not resolve.

Ekene-2 has its TOP_SAND at 1565 m and is dry, meaning water at 1565 m and below in that well.

So the eastern compartment holds oil somewhere above 1565 m and water at 1565 m in the east of the block. That brackets the eastern contact between roughly 1546 m and 1565 m, a range of 19 m.

Read the sensitivity that follows off the next two lessons: over that range the eastern booking runs from close to nothing to well above 2.283591 MMstb. The eastern contact is the single largest uncertainty in the eastern block's volume, and the well data does not pin it down.
