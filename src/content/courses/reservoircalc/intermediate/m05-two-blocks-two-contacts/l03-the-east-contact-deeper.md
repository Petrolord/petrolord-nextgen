# The east contact ten metres deeper

Now take the eastern contact the other way, down to 1570 m, and the compartment behaves as violently in the opposite direction.

## The numbers

| East contact | East cells | East STOIIP (MMstb) | Field sum (MMstb) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1550 m | 18 | 0.327859 | 10.183477 |
| 1560 m | 52 | 2.283591 | 12.139208 |
| 1570 m | 73 | 5.810977 | 15.666594 |

Over a 20 m range of assumption, the eastern compartment runs from 0.327859 to 5.810977 MMstb, a factor of seventeen and a half.

The west block sits at 9.855617 MMstb throughout, because its own contact never moved.

## The field total stops being a number

Look at the last column. Adding the two compartments gives 10.18, 12.14 or 15.67 MMstb depending on an assumption about one of them.

At 1570 m the field sum is 29 percent higher than the Associate tier's booking, and every barrel of the difference is in the east block. Nothing about the map changed, no new well was drilled, and the western compartment is identical.

This is the point at which a single field number stops being reportable. The Associate tier could write 12.139208 MMstb and attach one assumption to it. A partitioned field carries one contact assumption per compartment, and the total inherits all of them.

## Why deepening is not the mirror of shallowing

Compare the two directions from 1560 m. Going up 10 m costs 1.955732 MMstb. Going down 10 m gains 3.527386 MMstb. The gain is nearly twice the loss.

That asymmetry is structural. Deepening the contact does two things at once, just as raising it did, but both work in your favour: new cells become oil bearing at the edges, and every cell that already held oil gains 10 m of potential column. The eastern flank is broad and gently dipping, so a 10 m drop floods a large new area.

The general point is that contact sensitivity is not symmetric and cannot be quoted as a plus or minus. On a flank, the downside is bounded by the oil running out and the upside is bounded only by the spill point.

## Reading it off the panel

Set the fault to 1800 m, the west contact to 1560 m and step the east contact through 1550, 1560 and 1570 m.

{{panel:rc-block-explorer}}

Watch the amber area grow eastward and southward. At 1570 m the amber cells reach Ekene-2, which is no longer dry, since its top at 1565 m now sits above the contact.

That is the state change worth noticing. At an eastern contact of 1570 m the model says Ekene-2 found oil. The well did not find oil, and the model is not lying: it is showing you the consequence of an assumption you supplied, which contradicts an observation. A contact deeper than 1565 m in the east block is inconsistent with Ekene-2 being dry.

## Worked example

Use the well data to bound the eastern contact, which is the discipline this whole module is building toward.

Ekene-2 is dry with its TOP_SAND at 1565 m. If the eastern contact were below 1565 m, that well would have found oil. It did not. Therefore the eastern contact lies above 1565 m.

So the 1570 m case in the table is ruled out by an observation already in hand, and with it the 5.810977 MMstb booking and the 15.666594 MMstb field sum.

The eastern contact is bounded above by the oil at Ekene-6 and below by the water at Ekene-2, so it lies between roughly 1546 and 1565 m. Over that range the eastern booking runs from very little up to about 2.9 MMstb.

That is the honest statement of the eastern compartment: somewhere under 3 MMstb, possibly very small, and the data in hand cannot narrow it further. It took a dry well to produce the upper bound, which is the most useful thing Ekene-2 has done all tier.
