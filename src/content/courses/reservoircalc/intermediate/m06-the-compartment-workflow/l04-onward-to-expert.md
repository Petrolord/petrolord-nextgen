# Onward to Expert

This tier took one booked volume and divided it. The Expert tier takes the same volume and changes what it is made of.

## What this tier established

You can now partition an accumulation. More usefully, you know what a partition is worth and what it costs.

The arithmetic is a label per node and a separate accumulator per label. The checks are completeness, closure, invariance and coverage, and you know that all four can pass on a model that is wrong in the two ways that matter most.

The findings that transfer are these. Area and barrels split in different proportions, at Ekene 69.2 percent against 81.2 percent, because the block holding the crest holds taller columns. A tie break on the boundary column is worth 0.901423 MMstb. A hundred metres of fault position is worth about the same. And a fifth of the western compartment's booking rests on a well on the other side of the seal, because the map was built before anybody mentioned the fault.

That last one is the finding worth carrying into any partitioned model you meet.

## What the Expert tier changes

Every volume in this tier and the tier below was computed with four constants: net to gross 0.8, porosity 0.20, water saturation 0.35 and a formation volume factor of 1.2. The Associate tier said out loud that real rock does not work that way, and this tier did not touch it.

The Expert tier replaces the constant porosity with a value at every node, fitted from the porosity measured at each of the six wells. The same contact, the same map, the same chain, and a grid where there was a number.

The booking moves from 12.139208 to 12.796077 MMstb, an increase of 0.656868 MMstb.

## Two things to notice about that number

The first is its size. It is smaller than the tie break convention you priced in module two and a third of what the cross fault control is worth. A whole tier is built on a quantity that this tier's labelling conventions outweigh, which is a useful piece of perspective to carry into it: the property model is not where the leverage is, and knowing that is part of knowing when to spend effort on it.

The second is that the number is not what it appears to be. Most of that 0.656868 MMstb is not spatial at all. The Expert tier decomposes it, and the larger part turns out to come from something much simpler than a property model. That is where the tier starts, and it is worth arriving without a firm expectation.

## What the Expert tier inherits from this one

Two of this tier's ideas reappear immediately in a different costume.

The first is that an average stops being a single number once a quantity varies across a field. This tier met that with area against barrels: the west block held 69 percent of one and 81 percent of the other, and the gap was the mean column. The Expert tier meets it with three different averages of the same porosity, all defensible, differing by two percent, only one of which belongs in a booking.

The second is that a model can invent detail. Module four showed the map interpolating across a fault it did not know about. The Expert tier shows a porosity model fitting a smooth plane through six measurements and honouring none of them, which is the same phenomenon in a different variable.

## Where the ladder goes after that

The Expert tier is the top of this course, and the course is one step in a longer chain. Correlation supplies the picks, mapping turns them into surfaces, this course turns surfaces and a contact into barrels in place, and reserves and economics decide how many of those barrels are recoverable and worth recovering.

Everything downstream inherits the assumptions made here. A compartment booked without saying that its contact was assumed rather than measured becomes a development plan built on a contact nobody checked, and the further down the chain that assumption travels the harder it becomes to see.
