# The tax so far

{{panel:pia-hct-calculator}}

Five modules have built the hydrocarbon tax as a system. Before the capstone, this lesson lays the pieces out in the order the tax meets them, each with the provision that puts it there, so you can read any year of any lease against the texts.

## The system in one table

| step | what it decides | where the text says it | module |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scope | crude oil and condensate in, gas out; deep offshore out in a year under the Act alone, frontier out | PIA s.260(1)(a), (3); NTA s.65 | two |
| royalty deduction | the tranche rate on crude plus condensate, and the royalty by price | Seventh Schedule para 10; s.263(1)(b) | one and three |
| community and levy deductions | HCDT and the NDDC levy at the liquids share, outside the cap | s.240(2), s.263(1)(h) | three |
| the cap | opex, capital allowance and carried cost up to 65 percent of liquids revenue | Sixth Schedule para 2 | three |
| allowances | the capital allowance over five years; the production allowance per barrel | Fifth Schedule para 17; Sixth Schedule para 1 | four |
| rate | 30 percent for converted leases, 15 percent for prospecting licences and converted marginal fields, a stated reading for new-acreage leases | s.267 | two |
| beside it | companies income tax on oil and gas, with the hydrocarbon tax kept out of its base | s.302(5), (11) | five |

## Three ideas that carry the rest

The rate and the base are separate. A licence type or a stated reading moves the rate; the royalty, the cap and the allowances move the base. On the Ekene new onshore lease the chargeable profit is 151411548.913043 in 2026 under either stated reading, and only the hydrocarbon tax line differs.

Every carry has its own rule. The cost price ratio carry is capped each year by para 2(2)(b) and forfeited at the end by para 2(2)(c): 93000000.000000 on the Ekene CPR case. A loss sits in its own pool for its own tax under s.265. The two thirds carry in companies income tax waits on its own limit.

Two taxes, two bases. On the same lease in the same year the hydrocarbon tax can find a profit where companies income tax finds a loss, because each base deducts a different list.

## Questions to put to any year

Read a year of hydrocarbon tax with four questions, in this order. Is the stream inside the tax, and is the terrain? Which royalty rate did the daily rate earn? Did the cap bind, and if so what was carried out? Which allowances came off, at which tier? Only then look at the rate, and ask whether it is a rate the texts print or a stated reading. A figure you can trace through those four questions is one you can defend line by line. A figure you cannot trace is usually an input you have not checked, most often the barrels produced before the ledger or the preceding year's opex.
