# Production sharing contracts under the Act

{{panel:pia-ledger-calculator}}

The Petroleum Industry Act 2021 writes terms for production sharing contracts in Part IV of its Seventh Schedule, para 14. The engine of this course does not compute them: production sharing under the Act is concept-only here, taught from the text and never graded on a number. The fiscal course designs production sharing in the abstract; this lesson reads what the Act itself fixes.

## Two cost limits

A contract on new acreage may recover cost from at most 70 percent of production. Para 14(4) reads: "(4) For new acreage any production sharing contract shall have a cost limit of 70% based on total oil production, and where applicable condensates". A contract that comes in through conversion is held tighter. Para 14(9) reads: "(9) For a production sharing contract subject to a conversion contract under this Act, the cost limit shall be 60%."

Conversion reaches production sharing contracts too, and the converted contract carries the lower cost limit.

## The government's minimum share of profit oil

Para 14(4) goes on to set a floor under the government's share of profit oil, rising with cumulative production per field:

| cumulative production per field, as printed | minimum government share |
| --- | --- |
| up to and including 50 million barrels | 5% |
| over 50 up to and including 100 million barrels | 10% |
| over 100 up to and including 350 million barrels | 15% |
| over 250 up to and including 750 million barrels | 25% |
| over 750 up to and including 1500 million barrels | 35% |
| over 1500 million barrels | 45% |

The fourth band is quoted as the gazette prints it: "(d) over 250 million barrels up to and including 750 million barrels: 25% ;".

## Reading a misprint

Look at the lower bounds. Each band starts where the one above it ended, except the fourth. Band (c) closes at 350 million barrels, so band (d) should open "over 350". As printed, the two bands overlap between 250 and 350 million barrels, and a field in that range would sit in both. The course reads 250 as a misprint for 350, because the scale's own steps need it. The quotation stays as printed and the error is named beside it.

The same habit applies to the Petroleum Royalty Regulations 2022, whose middle benchmark column does not follow their own 2 percent rule; the engine states that in a note. Check a schedule against its own rule before relying on it.

## What the Nigeria Tax Act touched

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 deletes one paragraph of this Part. NTA s.197(1)(d) reads: "(d) paragraph 14 (6) of Part IV of the Seventh Schedule." The course does not print para 14(6), so it teaches only that the deletion exists.
