# Onward to Expert

This tier taught two things and taught them together. You can measure the misalignment between a synthetic and an observed trace with a scan rather than an eye, and report it with the correlation that justifies it. And you can read an amplitude or a peak time off a synthetic knowing that both of them belong to the wavelet as much as to the rock, so that neither leaves your notes without its frequency attached.

On the teaching well that means a suggested bulk shift of 8 ms with a correlation of 1, against 0.621742 at zero lag where nothing was shifted at all. It means a strongest amplitude of 0.1573149710893631 at 1580 ms TWT with a 15 Hz wavelet and 0.0362229160964489 at 1646 ms TWT with a 40 Hz one. And it means a strongest reflection coefficient of 0.017688043415546417 in absolute value at 1582 ms TWT that did not move while all of that was happening.

That is a complete skill, and it is narrow in one direction on purpose. Everything you did was a single trace against a single trace, with a wavelet you chose but never interrogated. The tier above turns the wavelet itself into the subject.

## The Expert tier makes tuning quantitative

Module 5 showed you that the wavelet moves the peak and left it there. It told you how big the effect is on this well and how to protect a pick from it, but it did not tell you how to predict it, because predicting it needs a controlled model rather than one well.

The Expert tier builds that model. It runs a wedge, a layer thinned steadily from thick to zero, with an equal and opposite reflection pair at its top and base: a positive coefficient at the top and a negative coefficient of the same size at the base. That pairing is chosen because it isolates the interference from any difference in reflection strength, so whatever the amplitude does as the layer thins is caused by the two wavelet copies meeting and nothing else.

What comes out is a tuning curve, amplitude against thickness, with a maximum at a particular thickness. The tier measures that thickness from the model and compares it against the classical theoretical value for a Ricker wavelet, then repeats the exercise at more than one frequency to watch the resolution limit move with bandwidth. That comparison against an independently known result is what turns a model into a measurement.

The connection to this tier is direct. Your 66 ms was a symptom. The wedge is the mechanism behind it, run under conditions where every variable is controlled.

## The Expert tier chains fluid substitution into AVO

The second half of that tier changes what varies. Instead of holding the rock fixed and varying the wavelet, it holds the geometry fixed and varies the fluid, taking a sand whose pore fill has been substituted and following what that does to the seismic response.

That leads into AVO screening, which asks how the reflection from an interface changes with offset rather than only at normal incidence. Everything you have built so far, including every reflection coefficient in this course, has been the normal incidence case, a single number per interface. AVO replaces it with a behaviour across angle, and the shape of that behaviour is what carries fluid information.

The chain is the point. A substituted sand produces an impedance change, the impedance change produces a reflectivity change, and the reflectivity change produces an AVO response that can be screened for. Each link is a step you have already run in a simpler form.

Those are destinations rather than material. Do not go looking for their numbers now, and do not try to reach them with the shift panel, which works one trace at a time on a fixed set of logs.

## What to carry up

Three habits from this tier are what make the Expert work readable.

Measure rather than eyeball. The scan exists because a shift found by dragging cannot be reproduced or defended. A tuning thickness read off a curve by eye has the same weakness, and the Expert tier compares its measurement against theory for the same reason this tier compares its correlation against the rest of the scan.

Quote the bandwidth with the number. It becomes more important above, not less, because the tuning thickness is a function of frequency and a resolution statement without a frequency attached says nothing.

Separate the rock from the experiment. That is the whole of module 5 in one line, and it is the premise the Expert tier runs on. A wedge model and an AVO screen are both experiments performed on a rock, and both are only interpretable if you can say which part of the answer came from which.

## Where this leaves you

The Associate tier built the synthetic. This tier aligned it and taught you what the wavelet is doing to what you read off it. The Expert tier quantifies what the seismic can and cannot resolve, and turns the response into a fluid question.

Above all three sit mapping and volumetrics, which consume tied wells without rechecking them. The tie you record is the thing they trust.
