# Onward

Where this course sits, and what the rest of the module does with it.

## What the three tiers gave you

**Associate.** Three measured numbers per station and a dozen computed ones. Minimum curvature and what the methods it replaced cost. The survey listing, its two dogleg conventions, and the difference between closure and vertical section. Four trajectory profiles built from arcs and holds, solved in closed form, compiled into station lists and checked.

**Professional.** Position uncertainty from a published model, reproduced against its own validation workbook to machine precision. Twenty-seven sources, three propagation modes, and the fact that most of them are systematic so the error does not average down. The borehole frame, in which the lateral uncertainty of a long horizontal well is four times the highside. And a budget whose ranking changes completely between a vertical station and a horizontal one.

**Expert.** Two wells, one frame, and a separation factor with every term named. Eleven published standard cases. The thresholds and what the margin above 1 is for. The reference frame underneath everything, and what a one degree error in it does.

## Where it goes next

This is the first course of the Drilling and Completions module, and everything after it puts an engine over a wellbore this course defines.

**Torque and drag** takes the survey and computes what the string feels: the dogleg severity from module 3 of the Associate tier is its primary input, and casing wear is computed from the contact forces a dogleg produces.

**Hydraulics** takes the trajectory and the hole sizes and computes pressures along it: every equivalent circulating density is at a true vertical depth this course computes, and hole cleaning depends on the inclination profile.

**Well control** takes the shoe depth, which is a TVD crossing from module 5, and the mud window.

**Geomechanics** takes the inclination and azimuth at every station and computes the stresses around the hole in that orientation, which is why the survey is its input rather than its output.

**Casing, cementing and completion design** all take the trajectory and the dogleg profile, because everything run into the hole has to bend through it.

None of those courses recomputes a survey. They consume this one.

## Two habits worth keeping

**State the frame.** Depth reference, north reference, position origin, vertical section azimuth, and for anything uncertain the model, the parameter set, the confidence factor and the station. Every one of them changes a number by more than its printed precision.

**Read the shape, not the summary.** A ladder rather than a minimum, a budget with a depth attached, a curve rather than an extremum. Every summary statistic in this course loses something specific, and each lesson named what.

## The one sentence

Three numbers are measured at a depth and everything else, including how wrong they might be and how close that puts you to the neighbour, is a chain of published models with named assumptions.
