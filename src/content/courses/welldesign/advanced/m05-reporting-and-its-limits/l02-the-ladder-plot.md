# The ladder plot

Separation against depth, and why the minimum is not the whole answer.

{{panel:wd-clearance-explorer}}

## What it is

The separation factor plotted against the reference well's measured depth, with the no-go and review thresholds drawn as horizontal lines.

The panel's clearance view is exactly this, and stepping through the eleven standard offsets shows the range of shapes it takes.

## Why the whole curve

**A single deep excursion** is a point to manage: survey more often there, monitor, and pass.

**A long stretch inside the review band** is a different problem: hundreds of metres drilled with reduced margin, more opportunities for the actual to diverge from the plan, and no single point at which to concentrate attention.

Both can have the same minimum. The plans are not equally acceptable and the minimum alone does not distinguish them.

## What the shape tells you

**A sharp V** is a crossing. Brief, well defined, and the depth of the minimum is where the surveys need to be dense.

**A long shallow trough** is a near-parallel approach. The most demanding, because the exposure is an interval rather than a point.

**A decay from a shallow minimum** is two wells leaving the same structure. The problem is at the top, in the section where the surface position term dominates and the survey contributes almost nothing.

**A curve that rises and then falls again** is two conflicts, and reporting only the smaller one hides the other.

## Reading the standard set

Step through the offsets in the panel. Two are clear throughout, four dip into the review band, and five go below one.

Among the five, the shapes differ: one is very near zero over a narrow interval, one is negative, and the others are broad. They would attract different mitigations even though they share a classification.

## What to plot alongside it

**The centre-to-centre distance**, on the same depth axis. The factor is a ratio, and seeing the numerator separately says whether a low factor is a close well or an uncertain one.

**Both wells' pedal radii.** If the offset's uncertainty is three times the reference's, the mitigation is on the offset, and no amount of gyro work on the new well will help.

The engine returns all of these, which is what makes the diagnosis possible rather than only the verdict.

## The violation list

The engine also returns an explicit list of every station below the review threshold, with its depth and its level.

That list is what goes into the drilling programme: not "minimum separation factor 1.2" but "between 780 and 940 m the factor is below 1.5, reaching 1.2 at 860 m, with the offset bearing 40 degrees toolface".

## The misconception to avoid

"The minimum separation factor is the result." It is one number from a curve. Two plans with the same minimum can present very different risk, and the interval spent inside the review band is often the more important quantity. Report the curve, the interval, and the geometry, and let the minimum be the headline rather than the whole message.
