# A printing slip in the beta factor

{{panel:lp-proof-test}}

The third inference is the uncomfortable one, because it says a printed number in the source is wrong. The paper's coverage table prints a beta factor of 10 percent against the pressure transmitters. Computed at 10 percent the row does not reproduce. Computed at 15 percent it reproduces exactly. The heading of that table explains why, and the conclusion is that the printed figure is a slip. This lesson works through the evidence, because asserting that a published figure is a misprint is a claim that has to be earned.

## The row, computed at both values

| beta factor and betaD | engine PFDavg | at three significant figures | printed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1, as printed | 0.000473588152 | 4.74E-04 | 6.76E-04 |
| 0.15, the value raised by half | 0.000676060358 | 6.76E-04 | 6.76E-04 |

The printed value is 6.76E-04. At the printed beta factor the engine returns 0.000473588152, which rounds to 4.74E-04 and misses by far more than rounding can explain. At a beta factor of 0.15 the engine returns 0.000676060358, which is the printed value to all three figures.

## Why 15 percent is the value the table meant

The table is headed as one that applies the MooN multiplication to the beta factor. That multiplication raises the base beta factor by half for the voted architectures, which takes 10 percent to 15 percent. So the heading of the table describes an operation that turns the printed figure into the reproducing figure. The same conclusion is confirmed elsewhere in the paper's own material: the pressure transmitter row without coverage, computed at a beta factor of 0.15, returns 0.000344085754 and reproduces its own printed 3.44E-04.

Two independent rows reproduce at 15 percent. Neither reproduces at 10 percent. The heading names the operation that produces 15 percent from 10 percent. The reading that the printed 10 percent is a slip in the SOURCE is the one the golden records.

## One row that was left out

Honesty about a slip means honesty about its limits. One further row in the raised beta factor table, the analogue input card, reproduces only with a detected common cause figure that looks like a rounded print. That is too ambiguous to stand as evidence, so the golden leaves it out. A reproduction argument is built from the rows that are clean, and the rows that are not clean are named and set aside in the open.

## What a learner takes from this

A published table is evidence about the equations for the rows it reproduces, and the inferences behind those rows are facts about the SOURCE. None of this reaches a graded answer, because every capstone states its own beta factor and its own betaD. What it teaches is the standard of proof: two rows, an independent explanation from the document itself, and an ambiguous row declared.

## Exercise

Take the printed 6.76E-04 and the engine's 0.000473588152 at the printed beta factor of 10 percent. Work out how many times larger the printed figure is than that value. Then say how large a rounding error would have had to be for the printed figure to be consistent with 10 percent, and write one sentence on why that comparison settles the question.
