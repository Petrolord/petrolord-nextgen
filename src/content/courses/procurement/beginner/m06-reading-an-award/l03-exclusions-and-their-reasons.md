# Exclusions and their reasons

{{panel:pr-envelope-calculator}}

An award names one bid. An evaluation report must also account for every bid that did not reach the award, saying at which stage it left and why. The engine returns every exclusion with its stage and a reason in its own words, so the report can quote them as they stand. This lesson reads the exclusions of the well services tender and the stages at which a bid can leave.

## The well services exclusions

| excluded bid | stage | the engine's reason, verbatim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WS4 | technical | technical score 65 is below the pass mark 70; the commercial envelope is not opened |
| WS6 | technical | failed the mandatory requirement signed-bid-form; the bid is not scored and its commercial envelope is not opened |

Both left at the technical stage, for different reasons. WS6 failed a mandatory requirement and was never scored. WS4 was scored and fell below the pass mark. Neither price envelope was opened, and neither bid's prices touch any other bid's evaluated cost.

## The stages a bid can leave at

At the technical stage a bid leaves for a failed mandatory requirement or a score below the pass mark. At the commercial stage a bid leaves when it carries a stated rejection reason, such as a major deviation, or when it offers completion beyond the tender's maximum weeks. Each exclusion carries its stage, so the report can say how far the bid got.

## A result with a reason

An exclusion is a result. The engine did not refuse to evaluate WS4 or WS6; it evaluated them and returned them excluded, with the reason beside each. A refusal is something else: the engine declining a bad input and returning no result at all. The report records exclusions; a refusal is fixed before the evaluation runs.

## Why the words matter

A bidder who is excluded may ask why, and in a public tender the answer is a record that can be reviewed. A reason in the engine's own words is exact: it names the requirement, the score and the mark, and says what happened to the price envelope. Paraphrasing risks changing the condition, "below" for "at or below", and a paraphrase that is wrong about a boundary can be wrong about the award. So the course quotes reasons as they stand.

## Writing the exclusions down

For each excluded bid, a report gives three things: the bid code, the stage at which it left, and the engine's reason in quotation marks. For the well services tender that is two lines, WS4 and WS6, both at the technical stage.

## When an exclusion moves another bid

A commercial exclusion can change a bid that stays in. With WS2 given a rejection reason and WS1 offering 11 weeks, both leave at the commercial stage, and WS5 is the only responsive bid left that quotes nitrogen. WS3's omitted nitrogen is priced again from WS5 alone:

> item nitrogen omitted; the average of the 1 price quoted by the other responsive bids, 33600, is added

WS3's evaluated cost becomes 956190.000000 and its combined score 97.049258 against WS5's 87.647059, so the award stays WS3 while WS3's own figures move.
