# A price envelope that is never opened

{{panel:pr-envelope-calculator}}

WS4 quotes the lowest total of the six well services bids, 763200.000000. It also scores 65.000000 on the technical envelope, below the pass mark of 70. Under a two-envelope process its price is never read, and nothing it quoted takes any part in the rest of the evaluation. This lesson follows WS4 out of the tender and shows what that means downstream.

## The rule says only passing bids

The engine's basis says only passing bids have their commercial envelope opened, citing the World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) para 6.29 and the Nigeria Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) s.51(2). The whole-tender call opens no commercial row for WS4, and it lists WS4 among the exclusions at the technical stage.

| WS2 | 849400.000000 | 75.000000 | opened |

## Its prices do not reach anyone else

WS4 priced a nitrogen line like every bid except WS3. When WS3's missing nitrogen is priced later, the engine uses the other responsive bids only. WS4 is not responsive at that stage, so its nitrogen figure is not in the average. A failed bid's prices cannot move another bid's evaluated cost.

## Why this protects the tender

If a low price could rescue a weak proposal, bidders would learn to bid low and write thin method statements, and the technical scoring would stop meaning anything. The pass mark says the company will not buy this work from a contractor it judged unable to do it, whatever the price. Sealing the price until the technical scores are fixed also keeps the price from colouring the marks.

## A low price is a question for later

A bid that passes the technical envelope and is priced far below the others raises a different question: can the bidder really do the work at that price? That test, for abnormally low bids, is taught at the Professional tier. At this tier the rule is simpler: a bid below the pass mark is out, and its price is irrelevant.
