# One envelope, then the other

{{panel:pr-envelope-calculator}}

A two-envelope tender asks each bidder to seal the technical proposal and the price separately. The technical envelopes are opened and scored first. Only the bids that pass have their price envelopes opened. This lesson walks the order of the stages, names the rule and its sources, and fixes three words the whole course uses in a narrow sense.

## The order of the stages

The engine states its stages in one sentence, which the envelope calculator prints under the whole tender:

> technical envelope (mandatory requirements, then the pass mark), then the commercial envelope of the passing bids only, then the award

First, each bid's mandatory requirements are checked: a bid security, a signed bid form. A bid that fails one is excluded and never scored. Second, the remaining bids are scored against the criteria and compared with the pass mark. Third, the price envelopes of the passing bids are opened, their bills corrected and their evaluated costs built. Fourth, the award is made on the stated basis.

## Why the price waits

Scoring quality with the price in view invites a panel to favour the cheaper bid in its technical marks. Sealing the price until the technical scores are fixed keeps the two judgements apart. The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) describe the two-envelope process at para 6.29, and the Nigeria Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) provides for separate envelopes at s.48 and for comparing prices only at or above the threshold at s.51(2). The engine cites para 6.29 and s.51(2) as the source of its technical stage.

## What the well services tender does at each gate

| stage | bids that enter | bids that leave | why they leave |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mandatory requirements | 6 | 1 | WS6 did not meet signed-bid-form |
| pass mark 70 | 5 | 1 | WS4 scored 65.000000 |
| commercial envelope | 4 | 0 | none rejected |
| award, combined at technical weight 0.7 | 4 | none | WS3 is most advantageous |

WS4 quotes the lowest total of the six bids, and its price is never read. That is the two-envelope rule doing exactly what it says.

## Three words with a narrow meaning

In this course each of these words carries one meaning only.

**Responsive** names a bid still in the evaluation at that stage: it passed the technical envelope and was not rejected at the commercial stage. WS4 answered the invitation, and at the commercial stage it is not responsive.

**Lowest evaluated cost** names the lowest evaluated cost as the engine builds it: the corrected price, less any discount, plus priced deviations, omissions and the completion-time adjustment. It is never the quoted price. On the well services tender it belongs to WS5, at 862141.000000, with omissions at the average and the fixture's completion schedule.

**Most advantageous** names the highest combined score under a stated technical weight and stated methods. On the same tender that is WS3, at 96.998434, with technical weight 0.7, the lowest-ratio commercial score and the relative technical score.

The two awards differ, and module 6 reads why.
