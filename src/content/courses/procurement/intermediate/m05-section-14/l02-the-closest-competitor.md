# The closest competitor

{{panel:pr-award-calculator}}

Once the s.14 group is formed, the Act looks for the bid with the highest Nigerian content in it and compares that bid's content with its "closest competitor". Closest in price, or closest in content? The engine states its answer in every s.14 result.

## The engine's reading

Verbatim, as it appears in every s.14 reason:

> "its closest competitor" is read as the bid with the next-highest Nigerian content in that group

So the competitor is found by content. The leader is the bid in the group with the highest overall content; the runner-up is the bid in the group with the next-highest.

The alternative reading, the bid closest in evaluated cost, could compare the leader with a bid of far lower content than the runner-up. The engine reads the phrase as a comparison of contents and prints the reading.

## The materials tender

The s.14 group is MS4 and MS2. Their overall contents, weighted by each bid's quoted amounts:

| bid | evaluated cost | overall content | role (both readings) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MS2 | 547863.577232 | 61.584657 | leader |
| MS4 | 546244.982386 | 57.043637 | runner-up |

With two bids in the group the runner-up is simply the other bid. The leader and the runner-up are the same under both readings of "at least 5% higher": the readings differ only in how the lead between them is measured, which is the next lesson.

## When the lowest also leads

The reading matters with three or more bids in the group. A stated case, run under the points reading: LO at 2000000 with 88%, L2 at 2006000 with 70%, and L3 at 2012000 with 75%.

| bid | evaluated cost | content | role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LO | 2000000 | 88% | leader |
| L3 | 2012000 | 75% | runner-up |
| L2 | 2006000 | 70% | third |

L2 is closer to LO in price; L3 is closer in content, and L3 is the runner-up. The lead, read as points, is 13.000000. The section applies and selects LO, which was already the lowest evaluated cost. The engine's reason, as the course prints it after the case label and before the three readings, verbatim:

"LO is the lowest evaluated cost and also leads on Nigerian content, 88% against 75% (L3), a lead of 13 percentage points; s.14 confirms it"

When the lowest evaluated cost also has the highest content, s.14 confirms the award.

## Why the runner-up matters

The lead test compares the leader with one bid only. A different runner-up means a different lead, and a different lead can pass or fail the 5% test. In the case above, a comparison with L2 would give a larger lead than the comparison with L3. The engine's reading takes the harder comparison: the bid whose content is closest to the leader's.
