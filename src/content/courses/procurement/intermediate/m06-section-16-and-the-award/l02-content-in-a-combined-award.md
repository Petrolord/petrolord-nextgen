# Content in a combined award

{{panel:pr-award-calculator}}

The s.14 preference acts "at commercial stage", among bids whose evaluated costs are within 1% of each other. That wording fits an award to the lowest evaluated cost. A combined award is decided by a weighted sum of a technical and a commercial score, and the engine does not force s.14 onto it.

## The refusal, and what it asks for

When the whole tender is evaluated in one call with a combined award and a Nigerian content rule, the engine refuses the content rule and says what to do instead, verbatim:

> nigerianContent applies s.14 at the commercial stage of a lowest-cost award; with award 'combined' state Nigerian content as a rated criterion with its weight instead

In a combined award, Nigerian content can still count: it enters the technical envelope as a rated criterion, with a weight and a maximum score like any other criterion.

## Content as a rated criterion

The technical envelope of the Associate tier has the machinery: the technical percentage is the sum of weight x score / maxScore over the criteria, and the weights must sum to 100. Adding a content criterion means taking weight from the others and stating how a bid's content turns into a score. Both are the buyer's choices, fixed in the tender document.

Two things change.

First, content now counts across the whole field of passing bids. Under s.14 it only matters for bids within 1% of the lowest evaluated cost, and only when a leader clears the 5% test. As a rated criterion it moves every bid's combined score by its weight.

Second, the reading question of s.14 disappears. The criterion's scoring rule is stated in the tender, so there is no "at least 5% higher" to read.

The combined award also brings back the weighting matrix of module 2. A content criterion is part of the rated criteria, so the technical weight that carries it must still sit inside its para 5.50 cell of the World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers, Seventh Edition, September 2025.

## Why the engine refuses

The engine reads s.14 as acting at the commercial stage of a lowest-cost award, and a combined score is a different kind of ranking. It chooses no reading of its own for that case: it refuses the content rule and names the alternative.
