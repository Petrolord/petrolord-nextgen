# Writing the evaluation report

{{panel:pr-contract-calculator}}

An evaluation ends in a report, and the report is what an approving authority, an auditor or a losing bidder reads. Every figure the course has computed only counts if the report lets a reader rebuild it. That means each figure travels with the rule, the source and the settings that produced it.

## What the report names

1. The tender, its scope and its award basis.
2. Every source applied, with its edition and the date it was read.
3. The criteria, their weights and maximum scores, and the pass mark.
4. Each bid's technical percentage, and every exclusion with its stage and its reason in the engine's words.
5. The arithmetic corrections, with the rule applied to each.
6. Each term of every evaluated cost, with the omission rule, the schedule rate and its base, and the life-cycle years and rate.
7. The technical weight inside its para 5.50 range, and the price and technical methods.
8. Any bid to clarify as abnormally low, and the approach used.
9. Nigerian content by item and overall in its measured units, with the s.14 reading stated and the s.16 rows.
10. For a contract comparison: the seed, the iterations, the plan, and the percentile definition beside every cost percentile.
11. The should-cost, with its contingency, its NPT fraction and its band.

## The well services tender, as a report would state it

A short extract shows the habit. Every figure is quoted with its settings:

| item | as the report states it |
| --- | --- |
| award | WS3, most advantageous at 96.998434 (combined, technical weight 0.7, priceMethod lowest-ratio, technicalMethod relative) |
| lowest evaluated cost | WS5 at 862141.000000 (omission rule average; schedule minWeeks 6, maxWeeks 10, ratePerWeek 0.005 of the corrected price less the discount) |
| exclusions | WS6 at the technical stage for a failed mandatory requirement; WS4 at the technical stage below the pass mark of 70 |
| corrections | WS2, unit rate prevails, correction 18000.000000; WS5, decimalMisplaced recorded, quoted amount governs |
| abnormally low | none flagged; absolute approach (four responsive bids) against the should-cost |
| should-cost | 895361.041667, contingency 0.1 of the base, NPT fraction 0.15, band 0.8 to 1.25; WS3's ratio 1.069948 |
| contract comparison | day rate P90 cost 837897.131515, the LOW cost, and P10 1029964.481686, on seed 20270211 with 20000 iterations |

Two things in the extract matter most. The award and the lowest evaluated cost are different bids, and the report says why: the technical weight. The cost P90 carries its label and definition, so no reader can take it for the high figure.

## Quoting the engine in a report

A reason or a refusal goes into the report verbatim, in quotation marks, as the engine's words. A figure the report reasons with comes from the numeric field at six decimals. A reason string is no source for it, since there the engine prints the shortest decimal of the double it holds. The two agree, and only the field is the figure.

## Settings a reader would change

A good report anticipates the question a challenger will ask: which stated setting, changed, would change this award? On the well services tender the price method or the award basis would; on the materials tender the s.14 reading would. A report that names the deciding setting, and shows the award under its alternative, answers the challenge before it is made.
