# Plugged, and washed out

Two string faults with opposite signatures.

## Plugged

A nozzle blocked with junk, or the bit balled up with soft formation.

The flow area at the bit has shrunk, so the pressure needed to push the same rate through it has risen.

**Drill pipe pressure:** UP, and it stays up.

**Casing pressure:** unchanged. The extra pressure is spent inside the string and never reaches the annulus.

**Bottom hole pressure:** unchanged, if the choke has not been touched. The extra drill pipe pressure is exactly the extra loss inside the string.

## Washed out

A hole in the drill pipe, usually at a connection, from erosion or fatigue.

Flow takes the shortcut into the annulus, so less has to be pushed through the bit and the friction inside the string falls.

**Drill pipe pressure:** DOWN.

**Casing pressure:** unchanged or slightly up.

**Bottom hole pressure:** unchanged at first, and the annulus below the washout is no longer being displaced.

## Why both leave the casing pressure alone

Because both are inside the string, upstream of the annulus. The annulus sees the same flow rate and the same fluids arriving at the same times.

That is the diagnostic: a fault that moves one gauge and not the other is inside the string.

## The trap that follows

The operator sees the drill pipe pressure off the schedule and moves the choke to bring it back.

**For a plug:** opening the choke lowers the bottom hole pressure while the gauge comes back to the schedule. The well goes underbalanced with the gauge looking correct.

**For a washout:** closing the choke raises the bottom hole pressure with the gauge looking correct, which puts extra pressure on the shoe.

In both cases the gauge is right and the interpretation is wrong, and correcting the gauge makes the well worse.

## What is done instead

Recompute the schedule from the NEW observed drill pipe pressure, treating the fault as a permanent change to the string's friction.

That is exactly the same procedure as for a stale slow circulating rate pressure: the observed pressure is the truth and the sheet has to be shifted to match it.

## The washout's second problem

The annulus below the washout is not being circulated. Whatever influx is down there stays down there, and it will still be there when the kill appears to be finished.

That is discovered by a flow check at the end, and it is why the flow check is not optional.

## Exercise

For each of the two faults, describe what the well does if the operator brings the drill pipe gauge back to the schedule with the choke.

Then say which of the two is detected by the end-of-kill flow check and which is not.
