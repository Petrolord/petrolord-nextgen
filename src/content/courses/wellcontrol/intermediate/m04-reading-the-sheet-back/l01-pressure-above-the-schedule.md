# Pressure above the schedule

Four causes, and how to tell them apart.

## What it means

The bottom hole pressure is higher than the formation pressure. The well is overbalanced.

That is the safe direction in the sense that no more influx is entering, and it is not safe: the extra pressure is being applied to the shoe as well.

## One: the choke is too closed

The commonest and the simplest. The operator has over-corrected or the choke has plugged.

**Distinguishing feature:** the casing pressure is also above what was expected.

## Two: something in the string has restricted

A plugged nozzle or a bit balled up. The friction inside the string has risen, so more surface pressure is needed to push the same rate.

**Distinguishing feature:** the casing pressure is normal. The extra pressure is being spent inside the string and is not reaching the annulus.

That distinction is the whole diagnosis: a string restriction raises the drill pipe pressure and NOT the casing pressure.

## Three: the pump rate has risen

More friction at a higher rate.

**Distinguishing feature:** the stroke counter's rate has changed, and both pressures have moved.

## Four: the kill mud is heavier than planned

If the mud being pumped is heavier than the kill weight, the head in the string grows faster than the schedule assumed, so the schedule under-predicts.

Wait: a heavier mud gives MORE head, which needs LESS surface pressure. So this cause puts the pressure BELOW the schedule, not above it.

That correction is worth making explicitly, because the instinct runs the other way.

## The order to check

**The pump rate**, because it is a glance at the counter.

**The casing pressure**, because it separates a choke problem from a string problem.

**Then the string**, if the casing pressure is normal.

## The action while diagnosing

Do not open the choke to bring the drill pipe pressure back down until you know why it is high.

If the cause is a string restriction, opening the choke lowers the bottom hole pressure while the drill pipe gauge still reads high, and the well goes underbalanced with the gauge looking fine.

That is the single most dangerous misreading in a kill.
