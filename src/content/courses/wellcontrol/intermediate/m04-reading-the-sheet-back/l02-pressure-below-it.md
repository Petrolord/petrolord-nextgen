# Pressure below it

The dangerous direction, and its four causes.

## What it means

The bottom hole pressure is below the formation pressure. The well is underbalanced and more influx is entering.

This is the direction that makes the situation worse rather than merely uncomfortable, and it has to be corrected immediately rather than diagnosed first.

## The immediate action

Close the choke slightly, bringing the drill pipe pressure back onto the schedule, and then find out why.

That order is the reverse of the previous lesson's, and the reason is the asymmetry: an overbalance is applying pressure to the shoe and an underbalance is admitting more formation fluid.

## One: the choke is too open

Over-correction or a washed-out choke.

**Distinguishing feature:** the casing pressure is also low.

## Two: a washout in the string

A hole in the drill pipe above the bit lets mud take a shortcut into the annulus. The friction inside the string falls, so less surface pressure is needed for the same rate.

**Distinguishing feature:** the casing pressure is normal or slightly high, and the drill pipe pressure fell without the choke being touched.

**Why it is serious:** part of the flow is bypassing the bit, so the annulus below the washout is not being displaced. The influx down there is not moving.

## Three: the pump rate has fallen

Less friction at a lower rate.

**Distinguishing feature:** the stroke counter's rate has changed.

## Four: the kill mud is heavier than the schedule assumed

More head in the string than the schedule allowed for, so less surface pressure is needed to reach the same bottom hole pressure.

**Distinguishing feature:** the pressure drifts progressively below the schedule as the string fills, rather than stepping.

This one is not a fault at all: the well is being overbalanced slightly, and the drill pipe pressure being low is the correct response.

Distinguishing it from a real underbalance takes the mud weight going in, which the mud engineer knows.

## The check that resolves most of it

The casing pressure. A choke problem moves both gauges; a string problem moves only the drill pipe one.

## What NOT to do

Do not simply hold the choke and accept the low reading. An underbalance that is left alone brings in a second influx on top of the first, and the operation gets substantially harder.

## Exercise

For each of the four, say what the casing pressure does and what the stroke rate does.

Then say which of the four is the only one where the correct response is to leave the choke alone.
