# A second influx

What it looks like, and why it happens.

## The cause

The bottom hole pressure fell below the formation pressure during the kill, for long enough for more formation fluid to enter.

Every one of the four causes in the previous lesson can do it, and the commonest is an operator holding a low drill pipe pressure without correcting it.

## What it looks like

**The pit gain rises again**, over and above the displacement being returned.

**The casing pressure rises** more than the expansion of the original influx would explain.

**The drill pipe pressure** may look entirely normal, if the choke was being used to hold it there.

That last point is the trap: the schedule can be followed perfectly while the well takes a second kick, if the schedule itself is being followed against a wrong slow circulating rate pressure or a wrong pump rate.

## Why it is worse than the first

**More total influx** to circulate out, so a higher peak casing pressure and a longer operation.

**Two influxes at different depths**, so the single-column assumption that every calculation here rests on is now wrong.

**A higher formation pressure than the sheet assumed**, if the second influx came from a different and stronger zone.

## What is done

Shut in again. Read the pressures again. Recompute the sheet.

That is a real setback and it is the correct action: the sheet on the desk describes a well that no longer exists.

## Why recomputing matters

The new SIDPP may be higher, which means a higher kill mud weight. The new pit gain is larger, which means a different kick tolerance check.

Continuing with the old sheet is continuing to kill a well that is not the one in front of you.

## How it is prevented

By correcting a low drill pipe pressure immediately rather than diagnosing first, and by checking the initial circulating pressure against the computed one when the pump comes up.

That check at the start is what catches a stale slow circulating rate pressure before it produces a whole circulation of underbalance.

## The connection to the earlier lesson

The sheet's first check is the ICP. If the observed and computed values disagree, the observed one is right and everything downstream should be recomputed from it.

Skipping that check is the most common route to a second influx.

## Exercise

The observed ICP is 0.4 MPa below the computed one, and the crew proceeds with the computed schedule.

Compute how underbalanced the well is throughout the circulation, and say whether that is enough to admit a second influx on a permeable formation.
