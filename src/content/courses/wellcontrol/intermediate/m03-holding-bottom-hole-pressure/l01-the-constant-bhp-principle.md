# The constant bottom hole pressure principle

One rule, and everything that follows from it.

## The rule

Hold the pressure at the bottom of the hole at or just above the formation pressure, for the whole operation.

If that is held, no further influx enters and the well is controlled. If it is not, either more influx enters or the formation somewhere is being overpressured.

## Why it is not automatic

Because the annulus is changing continuously.

The influx is rising and expanding, which displaces mud out of the well and lowers the hydrostatic column. Kill mud is going down the string, which raises the head there. Original mud is being pushed up the annulus.

Left to itself the bottom hole pressure would wander, and it would wander downward as the gas expanded.

## What holds it

The choke. Closing it raises the pressure everywhere in the annulus, including at the bottom; opening it lowers it.

So the choke is a variable back pressure applied at surface, and the operator moves it continuously to compensate for what the annulus is doing.

## Why the drill pipe gauge is watched instead

Because the drill pipe side is calculable and the annulus side is not.

At any moment the pressure at the bottom of the hole is the drill pipe surface pressure plus the hydrostatic head of whatever is in the string plus the friction of circulating.

All three of those are known: the surface pressure is on the gauge, the head follows from the strokes pumped, and the friction is the slow circulating rate pressure.

So keeping the drill pipe gauge on the schedule keeps the bottom hole pressure constant, without knowing anything about the annulus at all.

## That is the whole trick

The annulus is the complicated side and the string is the simple one, and the string is connected to the same bottom hole pressure.

Every kill procedure in the industry is an application of that observation.

## What breaks it

Anything that changes the string side without changing the schedule: a washout in the string, a plugged nozzle, a pump rate change, or gas in the drill pipe.

Module 4 is about detecting each of those from the gauge.
