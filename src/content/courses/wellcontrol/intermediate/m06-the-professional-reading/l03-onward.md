# Onward

From killing the well to whether it can be killed.

## What the Expert tier adds

This tier assumed the kill would work. The next one asks whether it will.

## What you will find there

**MAASP.** The maximum pressure the casing shoe can be exposed to at surface, which is the fracture pressure there less the mud column above it. It is the limit every casing pressure in a kill is compared against, and it FALLS as the mud weight rises.

**Kick tolerance.** How large an influx the well can circulate out without fracturing the shoe. Two cases, the influx shut in at the bottom and the same influx expanded up to the shoe, and the smaller one wins.

**Two wells with opposite answers.** On the slant well the shut-in case binds; on the horizontal well the circulated case does, because its shoe is only 42.515647195 m of true vertical depth above its bit.

**The bubble.** Boyle, isothermally, on a single column. Why a gas kick of a given pit gain is far worse than a liquid one, and what the single-bubble assumption costs.

**Planning with it.** The mud weight at which kick tolerance reaches zero is the mud weight at which the section has to be cased off, and that is a well design decision made months before the well is drilled.

**The five things the model does not do**, including the surface BOP assumption that makes every number in it wrong on a floating rig.

## Before you go

Two habits from this tier.

**Watch the drill pipe gauge and diagnose with the casing gauge.** A fault that moves one and not the other is inside the string, and correcting it with the choke makes the well worse.

**Check the observed initial circulating pressure against the computed one.** It takes a minute, it catches a stale slow circulating rate pressure, and skipping it is the commonest route to a second influx.
