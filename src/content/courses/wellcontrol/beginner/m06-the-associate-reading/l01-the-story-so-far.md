# The story so far

Five modules, two gauges and one kill sheet.

## The claim

A shut-in well tells you the formation pressure through one gauge and the influx through the other, and everything after that is arithmetic on volumes you knew before the kick.

## What each module established

**Module 1.** The mud column is the primary barrier and the blowout preventer is the secondary one, and closing the BOP does not kill the well. A kick feeds itself, because the influx is lighter than the mud it displaces, so shutting in quickly is arithmetic rather than caution: on a 1440 kg/m3 mud a 240 kg/m3 gas costs about 11.8 kPa of bottom hole pressure per metre of annulus it occupies.

**Module 2.** Volumes are a span walk and they are the only exact part of the calculation. The string side uses inside diameters and the annulus side uses hole and outside diameters. Volumes take MEASURED depth and pressures take TRUE VERTICAL depth. The annulus is two to three times the string, so bottoms up is much longer than pumping to the bit. And the capacity at the bit is about half the capacity at the shoe, so the same influx is nearly twice as tall where it starts.

**Module 3.** The drill pipe is a manometer and the annulus is not, which is why one gauge gives the formation pressure and the other does not. Their difference is exactly the weight the influx is not providing. Four things break the manometer, three of which make the reading low and therefore the kill mud light.

**Module 4.** The kill mud weight is the mud plus the SIDPP over the head, and the same shut-in reading needs a much larger weight increase on a shallow well. The trip margin is real and is added AFTER the kill, not during it. And there are three ways the kill weight is not achievable, all of which are prevented at the design stage by setting casing before kick tolerance runs out.

**Module 5.** One principle, constant bottom hole pressure, and two methods. The driller's method needs no schedule in its first circulation because the string never changes; wait and weight needs one because it does. Both share every number this course computes, and the slow circulating rate pressure that both sit on is measured rather than calculated.

## The numbers to carry

- Formation pressure is the mud head plus the SIDPP. Kill mud weight is the same statement as a density.
- ICP is the slow circulating rate pressure plus the SIDPP, exactly.
- FCP is the slow circulating rate pressure times the kill mud over the original mud.
- On the horizontal well: 24.23065791 m3 of string, 67.365410592 m3 of annulus, 2019.221492463 strokes to the bit and 5613.784216004 bottoms up.
- Its shoe is only 42.515647195 m of TVD above its bit, which is why the Expert tier's answer for it is so different.

## The one sentence

Twelve numbers are known before the kick and three are measured during it, and the kill sheet is what turns fifteen numbers into an operation.
