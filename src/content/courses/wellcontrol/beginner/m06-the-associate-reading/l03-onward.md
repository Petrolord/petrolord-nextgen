# Onward

From a shut-in pressure to a full kill sheet.

## What the Professional tier adds

This tier computed the kill mud weight and named the two circulating pressures. The next one builds the whole sheet and reads it back.

## What you will find there

**The six inputs and four outputs**, worked properly, with the schedule between the two circulating pressures and what happens after the bit.

**What the influx actually is.** Its height from the pit gain and the annulus capacity, its density from the difference between the two shut-in gauges, and the classification the engine applies to that density.

**A finding worth the module.** One of the two published scenarios in this course is NAMED as a gas kick and computes as a LIQUID one, because the difference between the two shut-in pressures is too small for the influx height. The label was the author's and the classification is the engine's, and they disagree.

**Holding bottom hole pressure constant**, which is the principle both kill methods share, and what the choke is actually doing.

**Reading the sheet back**, which is the skill that matters during the operation: what it means when the drill pipe pressure sits above the schedule, below it, or moves when nothing was changed.

**The oracle**, including a hand-built example with round numbers whose answers have closed forms a reader can check without a computer.

## Before you go

Two habits from this tier.

**Measured depth for volumes, true vertical depth for pressures.** Every confusing-the-two error in well control is one of those two applied in the other's place.

**Fill in the standing part before the kick.** Twelve of the fifteen numbers are known in advance, and a kill sheet that has to be built from scratch under pressure is a kill sheet that will have an error in it.

## The one sentence

You can now read a shut-in well and say what mud will kill it. The next tier is the sheet that gets it there.
