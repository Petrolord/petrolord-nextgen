# Two methods

One principle, two ways of applying it.

## The principle

Hold the bottom hole pressure constant, at or slightly above the formation pressure, for the whole operation.

Everything else is bookkeeping. The influx comes out because it is displaced; the well stays controlled because the pressure at the bottom never falls.

## Why the pressure has to be held rather than left alone

Because as the influx rises up the annulus it expands, and as it expands it displaces mud out of the well, and that lowers the hydrostatic column.

Left alone, the bottom hole pressure would fall as the influx rose, and more influx would enter. The choke is what stops that.

## Method one: the driller's method

Two circulations.

**First circulation:** circulate the influx out with the ORIGINAL mud, holding bottom hole pressure constant on the choke. At the end the well is full of original mud and still shut in, with a casing pressure equal to the shut-in drill pipe pressure.

**Second circulation:** circulate kill mud in, again holding bottom hole pressure constant. At the end the well is dead.

## Method two: wait and weight

One circulation. Build the kill mud first, then circulate it in and the influx out at the same time.

The drill pipe pressure follows the schedule as the kill mud goes down the string, then is held constant while the annulus is displaced.

## The trade

| | driller's | wait and weight |
|---|---|---|
| circulations | two | one |
| waiting before starting | none | while the mud is built |
| peak casing pressure | higher | lower |
| complexity for the choke operator | lower | higher |
| total time | usually longer | usually shorter |

## Which is taught first

The driller's, because the first circulation uses the original mud and therefore needs no schedule at all: the drill pipe pressure is simply held at the initial circulating pressure throughout.

That makes it the easier of the two to execute and the one that can be started immediately.

## What both share

The kill sheet's standing part, the shut-in readings, the kill mud weight, and the two circulating pressures. Everything computed in this course applies to both.
