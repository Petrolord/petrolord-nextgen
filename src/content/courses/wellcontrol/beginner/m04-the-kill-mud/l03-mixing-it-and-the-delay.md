# Mixing it, and the delay

The operational constraint that changes which method is used.

## The problem

A kill mud weight is a number. Turning it into a tank of mud takes barite, mixing equipment, and time.

Weighting a large active system up by 170 kg/m3 is tonnes of barite and can take hours.

## Why that matters

Because the well is shut in the whole time, with an influx in the annulus.

**Gas migrates.** A gas influx rises at a hundred metres an hour or more, and in a shut-in well it carries its pressure up with it, raising both surface gauges.

**The casing pressure rises** as a result, and it may approach or exceed the MAASP.

**The influx may be dissolving** in an oil-based mud, which changes what happens when it comes up.

So waiting is not free, and the longer the wait, the worse the shut-in condition becomes.

## The two responses

**Wait and weight.** Build the kill mud first, then circulate once, killing the well in a single circulation. Fewer circulations and a lower peak casing pressure, at the cost of the waiting.

**The driller's method.** Circulate the influx out immediately with the ORIGINAL mud, holding bottom hole pressure constant, then circulate kill mud in a second pass. No waiting, two circulations, and a higher peak casing pressure.

The next module is about both.

## The arithmetic of the delay

Volumetric control is the procedure used while waiting: as gas migrates and the pressure rises, mud is bled from the annulus in measured increments to hold the bottom hole pressure constant.

It works, it is slow, and it requires careful measurement of what has been bled. It is a genuine skill and it is outside this engine.

## What decides which method

**How long the mud will take** against **how much casing pressure the shoe can stand**.

If the mud can be built in half an hour and the shoe has margin, wait and weight is better. If the mud will take four hours and the casing pressure is already close to the MAASP, the driller's method starts immediately.

## What the engine says about it

Nothing. The kill sheet is the same for both methods: the same kill mud weight, the same circulating pressures, the same schedule. The difference is when the kill mud is introduced, and that is a procedure rather than a calculation.
