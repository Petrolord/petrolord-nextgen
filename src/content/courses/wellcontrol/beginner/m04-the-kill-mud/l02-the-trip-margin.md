# The trip margin

The overbalance that is not in the kill sheet.

## What the kill mud does

Balances the formation exactly. With kill mud in the hole and the well static, the pressure at the bottom equals the pore pressure and no more.

## Why exact balance is not enough

Because as soon as the string moves, the pressure changes.

Pulling out swabs, which lowers the pressure below the static value. With exact balance, any swab at all takes the well underbalanced and the next kick begins.

## The trip margin

An extra mud weight above the kill weight, chosen so that the expected swab does not take the well below the pore pressure.

    trip margin = expected swab pressure / (g x TVD)

Typical values are 30 to 60 kg/m3, or a quarter to half a pound per gallon.

## Where the swab number comes from

The Drilling Hydraulics course, which computes it from the trip speed, the string geometry, the mud rheology and the annulus.

That is a genuine handover: this course produces the kill weight and that one produces the margin to add to it.

## Why it is not in the kill sheet

Because the kill sheet is written for the kill, and during the kill the string does not move. Adding a trip margin during the kill would raise the bottom hole pressure above the formation pressure everywhere, which risks fracturing the shoe.

The margin is added AFTER the kill, before the next trip, by weighting the mud up further once the well is dead.

## The trap

Killing the well with kill mud plus a trip margin already in it. That is a common instinct and it is wrong: it raises the pressure at the shoe throughout the circulation, and the shoe is the weakest point.

Kill with the kill weight. Weight up afterwards.

## The other margin

Some operators use a riser margin in deep water, which covers the loss of hydrostatic head if the riser is disconnected. It is a different quantity for a different failure and it is also not in the kill sheet.
