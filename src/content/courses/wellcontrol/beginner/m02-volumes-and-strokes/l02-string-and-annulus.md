# String and annulus

Two volumes, two purposes.

{{panel:wc-volume-explorer}}

## The string volume

How much mud it takes to fill the drill string from surface to the bit.

Its purpose in a kill is the STROKES TO THE BIT: how long it takes for the kill mud to reach the bottom of the hole. Until it arrives, the drill pipe pressure has to follow the schedule.

## The annulus volume

How much mud it takes to fill the space between the string and the hole, from the bit to surface.

Its purpose is BOTTOMS UP: how long it takes for whatever is at the bottom of the hole to reach surface. That is when the influx arrives at the choke.

## The two together

The full cycle: pump a marker at surface and count the strokes until it comes back. It is the sum of the two.

On the horizontal well: 2019.221492463 strokes to the bit and 5613.784216004 bottoms up, for a full cycle of 7633.005708467.

## Which is bigger, and why

The annulus, almost always, and by a factor of two or three.

The string is a narrow tube. The annulus is the space between that tube and a hole substantially larger than it. Even though both are the same length, the areas are very different.

## Why that matters during a kill

Because the kill mud reaches the bit in the first third of the operation and the influx reaches surface in the last two thirds.

So a kill is a long operation, and the pressure schedule only applies to the first part. After the kill mud passes the bit, the drill pipe pressure is held constant for the remaining bottoms up.

## The one that changes during the well

Both, continuously, as the hole gets deeper. That is why the standing part of a kill sheet is updated at every connection or at least every stand, and why a stale kill sheet is a hazard.
