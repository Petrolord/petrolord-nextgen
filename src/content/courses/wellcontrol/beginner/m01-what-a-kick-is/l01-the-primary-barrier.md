# The primary barrier

The mud column, and what happens when it is not enough.

## The barrier

A well is kept under control by the weight of the mud in it. The hydrostatic pressure of that column at any depth has to exceed the pore pressure of the formation there.

That is the PRIMARY barrier, and it is the only one working while the well is being drilled. It is a column of fluid, and its strength is entirely its density and its height.

## The secondary barrier

The blowout preventer. It is closed when the primary barrier has failed, and it does not stop the influx: it stops it reaching surface while the primary barrier is repaired.

That distinction matters. Closing the BOP does not kill the well. It buys time to work out what the formation pressure is and to build a mud that will balance it.

## What a kick is

An influx of formation fluid into the wellbore, because the pressure in the hole fell below the pore pressure somewhere.

Once it is in, it displaces mud, which lowers the hydrostatic column further, which admits more influx. That feedback is why a small kick becomes a large one if it is not shut in quickly.

## Why the influx is lighter

Formation fluids are lighter than drilling mud, always. Gas is far lighter; oil and formation water are lighter than a weighted mud.

So the influx reduces the average density of the annulus, and the pressure at the bottom of the hole falls. The lighter the influx, the worse.

## What a blowout is

A kick that has not been controlled: formation fluid flowing to surface at rate, with no barrier holding it.

Every blowout begins as a kick, and almost every kick is controlled routinely. The difference between the two is what this course is about.

## What this course computes

**The volumes and strokes** the well holds, which everything else needs.

**The shut-in pressures** and what they say about the formation and the influx.

**The kill sheet:** the mud weight that balances it, the circulating pressures, and the schedule between them.

**Kick tolerance:** how large an influx the well can take before the weakest point fractures.

## What it does not compute

The dynamics. Gas migration rate, gas solubility in an oil-based mud, choke response, temperature, and everything that happens on a timescale. The Expert tier names them properly.
