# What the answer is for

Five decisions, and who makes each of them.

## What mud weight to kill with

The output of the kill sheet, and the decision that ends the event. It comes from one measurement, the shut-in drill pipe pressure, and one number the driller already knows, the true vertical depth.

## What pressure to hold on the choke

The schedule. It is a sequence of drill pipe pressures against pump strokes, and holding it keeps the bottom hole pressure constant while the kill mud goes down.

This is the decision being made continuously during the kill, by the choke operator, from a sheet worked out beforehand.

## Whether the well can be killed at all

Kick tolerance. If the influx that came in is larger than the well can circulate out without fracturing the shoe, the standard procedure will break the formation down.

That check is made at the planning stage and again when the pit gain is known.

## Where to set the next casing

The mud weight at which kick tolerance reaches zero is the mud weight at which the section has to be cased off. That is a well design decision made months before the well is drilled, and it uses the same calculation.

## Whether the crew is ready

Drills. A well control drill is timed from the first indicator to the well being shut in, and the time is recorded. The arithmetic in the previous lesson is why.

## What each decision needs

| decision | needs |
|---|---|
| kill mud weight | SIDPP and the TVD |
| choke schedule | volumes, strokes, and the two circulating pressures |
| can it be killed | pit gain, shoe TVD, fracture gradient |
| casing point | fracture gradient and a planned kick size |
| readiness | none of the above |

Two of the five need a fracture gradient, which this course takes as given from the geomechanics course.

## The habit

Every number in a kill sheet is filled in BEFORE the kick, on the standing part of the sheet: the volumes, the strokes, the slow circulating rate pressures, and the shoe data.

Only three numbers are filled in during the event: the two shut-in pressures and the pit gain. That division is deliberate, and it is why a kill sheet can be completed in minutes.
