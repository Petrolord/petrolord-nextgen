# When the drill pipe lies

Four situations where the manometer stops working.

## One: a float valve

A non-return valve in the string stops flow up the drill pipe. The pressure below it cannot reach the surface gauge, so the drill pipe reads zero or something meaningless.

**What is done.** Pump slowly against the closed well and watch the drill pipe pressure. It rises with the pump and then holds; the pressure at which it stops rising is the pressure needed to open the float, which is the pressure below it. Subtract the pump's contribution and you have the SIDPP.

That procedure has several names and it is standard, and it takes minutes rather than seconds.

## Two: a plugged bit or nozzle

The string is no longer open to the formation, so there is no connection between the formation pressure and the drill pipe gauge.

**What is done.** There is no good answer. The casing pressure and the pit gain are what you have, and killing on casing pressure alone is a much harder operation.

## Three: the pressure has not stabilised

Immediately after shut-in the pressures are still rising. Reading them too early gives a low SIDPP and therefore a low kill mud weight.

**What is done.** Wait. Five to fifteen minutes is usual, and the criterion is that the gauges stop moving rather than a fixed time.

## Four: gas migration

Gas rises through the mud whether the well is circulating or not, at something like 100 to 300 m per hour. As it rises without expanding, in a shut-in well, it carries its pressure with it and BOTH gauges rise together.

So a stabilised reading taken half an hour after shut-in is higher than the true one, and it rises indefinitely.

**What is done.** Read the pressures promptly once they have stabilised, and if there is a delay, use a volumetric procedure to bleed mud and hold the bottom hole pressure constant while the gas rises.

## The pattern

Three of the four make the SIDPP read LOW, which gives a kill mud weight that is too light. One of them makes it read HIGH.

A kill mud that is too light means the well is still underbalanced when the kill is finished, which is discovered when it flows again.

## What the engine does about it

Nothing. It takes an SIDPP and uses it. All four of these are procedures at the wellsite rather than terms in a calculation, and the calculation is only as good as the reading it is given.
