# What a zero means

The end of the section, in one number.

{{panel:wc-tolerance-explorer}}

## The condition

Kick tolerance is zero when the shoe is at its fracture pressure with the hole full of mud and no influx at all.

That is the mud weight equalling the fracture equivalent mud weight, and on both wells in this course it is at or a little above 1680 kg/m3: the slant well reaches zero there exactly and the horizontal one is still at 0.08613710397051996 m3.

## What it means operationally

The well cannot be shut in on ANY influx without fracturing the shoe.

Not a large influx. Any influx at all, because the margin that would absorb it has already been spent on the mud.

## What it means for the section

It has to be cased off before the mud weight reaches that point, with enough margin left to tolerate the design kick.

So the practical limit is not the zero crossing but the crossing of the design kick size, which is earlier.

## The two numbers a designer needs

**Where the tolerance crosses the design kick.** That is the casing point.

**Where it crosses zero.** That is the absolute limit, useful as a check that the casing point has adequate margin.

The gap between them is the design's safety margin, and quoting both is more informative than quoting either.

## What a negative would mean

The engine clamps the tolerance at zero rather than returning a negative.

That is a presentation choice, and the underlying arithmetic does go negative: the required influx height would be negative, meaning the shoe is already over-pressured with the hole full of mud.

Clamping is defensible because a negative volume has no meaning, and it does lose the information about HOW far past the limit the well is.

## The check at the wellsite

Compare the actual pit gain against the kick tolerance as soon as both are known.

If the pit gain exceeds the tolerance, circulating the influx out by the standard procedure will fracture the shoe. That is the check that decides whether the standard procedure is available, and it takes two numbers.

## What to do if the pit gain exceeds it

The options are all specialist and all worse: bullhead the influx back into the formation, set a barite plug, or accept fracturing the shoe and manage the losses.

None of them is in this course, and knowing that the check exists and what it means is the point.
