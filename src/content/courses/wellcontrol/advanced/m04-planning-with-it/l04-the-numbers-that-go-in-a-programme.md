# The numbers that go in a programme

What this course produces for a drilling programme.

## The list

**Per hole section:**

- The shoe's true vertical depth and its fracture equivalent mud weight, from the leak-off test.
- The MAASP at the planned mud weight.
- The kick tolerance at the planned mud weight and the design kick intensity.
- The mud weight at which the tolerance reaches the design kick size, which is the section's limit.

**Per well, updated as it is drilled:**

- The string and annulus volumes and the three stroke counts.
- The two true vertical depths.
- The slow circulating rate pressures, at two rates.

## Why the second list is the one that goes stale

Because it changes every stand. The first list changes when a casing string is run or a leak-off test is repeated, and the second changes continuously.

A kill sheet's standing part IS the second list, and keeping it current is a routine duty rather than a calculation.

## What is NOT in the programme

**A kill sheet.** It cannot be written until there is a kick, because three of its inputs are measurements made during the event.

**A prediction of the casing pressure history.** This engine does not compute it and the programme does not carry it.

**Which kill method to use.** A procedure choice made at the time.

## The one number a supervisor remembers

The kick tolerance at the current mud weight, in cubic metres, and how it compares against the pit volume that would trigger a shut-in.

If a well is being drilled with a 3 m3 tolerance and the pit alarm is set at 2 m3, the alarm gives a margin. If the alarm is set at 4 m3, it does not, and that mismatch is worth catching.

## The check that connects them

Pit alarm setting against kick tolerance. It is two numbers and a comparison, it is rarely written down, and it is the most direct link between the design calculation and the operation.

## What this course hands to the rest of the module

**To casing design:** the casing point, from the tolerance sweep.

**To hydraulics:** nothing, and hydraulics hands this course the swab that causes the kick and the equivalent circulating density that has to be re-established afterwards.

**To the well plan:** the maximum mud weight each section can be drilled with.

## Exercise

Write out the two lists for the slant well at 1440 kg/m3 mud, filling in every number from the panels.

Then say which of them you would expect to be wrong on a rig where the standing part had not been updated for a day.
