# Two wells, two answers

The comparison, and what it says about well design.

{{panel:wc-tolerance-explorer}}

## The headline

Same mud, same fracture gradient, same kick intensity, same string, same casing size.

    slant:      2.783680489 m3
    horizontal: 1.078825342 m3

The horizontal well tolerates less than half as much influx.

## Where the difference comes from

Not from the mud, not from the rock, and not from the string. Entirely from where the shoe sits relative to the bit in TRUE VERTICAL depth.

    slant:      1225.671108990 m of gap
    horizontal:   42.515647195 m of gap

## What that says about horizontal wells

The lateral adds measured depth and no true vertical depth. So it adds annulus volume, which means more influx can enter for a given underbalance, and it adds no room for that influx to expand into.

Both effects go the wrong way, and neither is fixable by anything except a deeper shoe, which a horizontal well by definition does not have.

## The design responses

**Set the casing shoe deeper before landing.** More true vertical depth between the shoe and the lateral. This is the only one of the four that moves the number, because the tolerance is geometric on this well rather than pressure-limited: the sweep in module 4 shows it is almost flat against mud weight.

**Reduce the kick intensity the well is designed for**, which is really an admission that the well cannot tolerate a large kick and has to be drilled so as not to take one.

**Manage the pressure precisely**, which is the case for managed pressure drilling on long laterals.

**Accept a smaller tolerance and drill carefully**, which is what usually happens.

## The connection to the previous courses

The same geometric fact appears in all three: a lateral adds contact length and no true vertical depth in torque and drag, adds annulus friction and no true vertical depth in hydraulics, and adds annulus volume and no expansion room here.

One property of a horizontal well, three consequences, three courses.

## The number to quote

Both the tolerance and which case bound it. A tolerance of 1.08 m3 bound by the circulated case is a different problem from the same number bound shut in, and the responses are different.
