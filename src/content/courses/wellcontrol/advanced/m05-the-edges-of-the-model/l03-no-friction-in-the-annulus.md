# No friction in the annulus

The term that is on one side of the sheet and not the other.

## The asymmetry

The kill sheet's drill pipe side has a friction term: the slow circulating rate pressure, measured and added.

The annulus side has none. The casing pressure is compared against a MAASP computed from a static mud column.

## Why the drill pipe side has one

Because the schedule is a surface pressure, and a surface pressure has to account for everything between the gauge and the bottom of the hole. That includes the friction of pushing mud down the string.

Measuring it is easy, and it is measured.

## Why the annulus side does not

Because the annulus friction depends on what is in the annulus, and during a kill that is mud, influx and expanding gas at unknown positions.

There is no equivalent of a slow circulating rate pressure for it, because it is not a fixed system: it changes continuously through the operation.

## What that means for the MAASP comparison

The MAASP is a static limit. Comparing a circulating casing pressure against it is comparing a number that includes no annulus friction against a limit computed with none.

That is consistent, and it is not conservative: the pressure AT THE SHOE while circulating is higher than the gauge plus the static column, by the friction above the shoe.

So the shoe is closer to its limit than the comparison suggests.

## How much closer

At a kill rate, a few bar. Against a MAASP of tens of bar, that is a few percent.

Small, systematic, in the unsafe direction, and standard practice everywhere.

## Why it is tolerated

Because measuring or computing it during an event is not practical, because the error is small at kill rates, and because the working margin operators apply to the MAASP, often 10 or 20 percent, absorbs it comfortably.

That is a good example of a known small error absorbed by a policy margin rather than by a correction.

## When it would not be tolerable

At a high circulating rate, or in a very tight annulus, or on a well with a narrow margin at the shoe.

All three push the annulus friction up relative to the MAASP, and all three are reasons to circulate more slowly.
