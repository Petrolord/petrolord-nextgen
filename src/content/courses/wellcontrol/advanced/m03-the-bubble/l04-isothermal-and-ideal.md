# Isothermal, and ideal

Two gas-law simplifications, and which way each one goes.

## Isothermal

Boyle assumes constant temperature. A gas rising up a well does not stay at constant temperature.

**The formation is hot** and the surface is cool, so a rising bubble is moving into cooler surroundings.

**Expansion itself cools a gas**, if it does work against the pressure around it.

So the real gas is cooler than the isothermal assumption at every point above where it entered.

## Which way that goes

A cooler gas is DENSER at the same pressure, so it expands LESS than Boyle predicts.

So the isothermal assumption OVERSTATES the expansion, which understates the kick tolerance, which is the conservative direction.

## How much

Over a thousand metres of rise with a geothermal gradient of 25 degrees per kilometre, the gas cools by 25 degrees from its surroundings alone, from perhaps 370 K to 345 K.

That is a seven percent reduction in absolute temperature, so about seven percent less expansion than isothermal.

Not negligible, and small compared with the other uncertainties.

## Ideal

Boyle is the ideal gas law with temperature held constant. A real gas at tens of megapascals is not ideal.

The compressibility factor z accounts for it, and for methane at 30 MPa and 100 degrees it is around 0.85 to 0.95, depending on the composition.

## Which way that goes

A z below one means the gas is MORE compressible than ideal, so it occupies less volume at high pressure than the ideal law predicts.

Coming up, from a high pressure to a low one, z rises toward one. So the real expansion is LARGER than the ideal law gives.

That is the non-conservative direction, and it partly cancels the temperature effect.

## The two together

Temperature makes the real expansion smaller and non-ideality makes it larger, by roughly comparable amounts on a deep hot well.

So Boyle isothermally is a reasonable approximation not because either simplification is small but because the two errors partly offset.

That is an accident rather than a design, and it does not hold on every well.

## What a proper treatment needs

A real gas equation of state, a temperature profile, and the composition of the influx.

The first two are available and the third is not, at the time the calculation matters.
