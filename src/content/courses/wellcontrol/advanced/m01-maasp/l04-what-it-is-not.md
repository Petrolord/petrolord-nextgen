# What it is not

Four things MAASP is mistaken for.

## Not a casing burst limit

MAASP is a ROCK limit. It is the surface pressure at which the formation below the shoe fractures.

The casing's burst rating is a separate and usually much larger number, and the surface pressure is limited by whichever of the two is smaller.

On most wells the rock is weaker, which is why MAASP is the number quoted. On a shallow casing string with a strong shoe it can be the other way round.

## Not a blowout preventer limit

The BOP stack has a working pressure rating, typically 34 or 69 MPa. That is a third limit, and it is usually far above both of the others.

Three limits, and the operational limit is the smallest.

## Not a fixed number

It changes with the mud weight, as the previous lesson showed, and with the fracture gradient if a leak-off test is repeated.

A MAASP written on a kill sheet is a MAASP at the mud weight that was in the hole when it was written.

## Not a limit on the drill pipe pressure

The drill pipe gauge is connected to the string, not the annulus. Nothing about it is limited by the shoe.

Confusing the two is a specific error and it leads to a choke operator refusing to raise the drill pipe pressure onto the schedule because they think it is approaching a limit.

## What it IS

The maximum reading the CASING gauge may show, at the current mud weight, before the shoe fractures.

One gauge, one limit, one condition attached.

## The reading habit

Write it on the kill sheet next to the casing pressure box, with the mud weight it was computed at.

That places the limit next to the number it limits and records the condition, and it takes one line.

## The check at shut-in

Compare the SICP against the MAASP as soon as both are known.

If the SICP already exceeds it, the shoe was fractured while the well was being shut in, and the situation is not the one the standard procedure was written for.
