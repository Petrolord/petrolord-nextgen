# Onward

A string that has been set has not yet been unloaded, and the questions that follow are all about what happens while it is.

## What the Expert tier adds

**The sequence, stage by stage.** Each stage drops the casing, uncovers the next valve and is supposed to shut the one above it. Whether it does is the closing test run at every stage on every valve above the point of injection, and the published cases carry 28 stage rows and 85 closing margin rows of it.

**The margin the verdict sits on.** On midDecrementKnifeEdge, spaced on 26.75 psi per valve, the stage 5 verdict on valve 4 turns on 0.149791635 psi at valve depth and 0.124769727 psi at surface, on a string running at 1164.7 psia. It flips between 26.80 and 26.90 psi per valve of decrement. A boolean that consequential is not a detail of the output, it is the output.

**A verdict that was never checked.** The independent oracle's unloading routine was a stub: it walked the valves and appended an empty open valve list at every stage without evaluating the condition, and no unloading key reached the goldens at all. The verdict was ungated while looking gated, which is worse than ungated.

**The deepest injection point, and a residual that cannot see its own error.** The crossing is found by straight line between tabulated points, and both sides of the residual come off the same pair of chords. On the published case the answer lands 1.318735072 ft away with a reported residual of 4.8890e-3 psi against a true residual of 1.5833e-1 psi, a ratio of 32.386. On a curved teaching traverse the same mechanism gives 60.420814470 ft of error at a reported residual of 1.5907e-2 psi, true 1.0789e+1 psi, a ratio of 678.26. It is the ratio, not the magnitude, that is the finding.

## Before you go

**Nothing from this tier is superseded.** The depths still come from a recursion, the domes are still thermometers, the spread is still R times a differential, and the throughput is still an upper bound. And every boolean in the output has a number behind it, which is the part you can defend.
