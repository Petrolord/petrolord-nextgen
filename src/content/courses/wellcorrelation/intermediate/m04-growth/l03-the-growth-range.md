# The growth range

The growth range is the maximum interval across the section minus the minimum interval. For A-to-SAND across the four Ekene wells it is

$$60 - 46 = 14$$

so the growth range is 14 m. It is one of the six graded readings, to a tolerance of 0.01 m. This lesson gets the number, then spends the rest of its length on what the number carries and what it does not, because a range is a summary and every summary throws something away.

## Getting it

Three steps, and none of them can be skipped safely.

**List the intervals.** From the previous lesson, 48, 53, 46 and 60 m for Ekene-1 through Ekene-4. Compute all four before comparing any of them, because a maximum found by eye on a panel is a guess.

**Identify the ends.** The maximum is 60 m in Ekene-4 and the minimum is 46 m in Ekene-3. Write the well names down beside the numbers. A range with no wells attached cannot be checked, and the wells are the part a reader will ask about.

**Subtract.** 60 minus 46 is 14 m. The result is a thickness difference, so it is in metres and it is never negative.

## The check on the panel

Because this section is flattened on TOP_A, the displayed TOP_SAND column is the datum plus the interval, so the spread of that column is the spread of the intervals. The displayed sand picks run 1498, 1503, 1496 and 1510 m, and 1510 minus 1496 is 14 m. The same number, arrived at without touching the interval column.

That gives you a fast reading and a slow reading of the same quantity. The slow one computes four intervals from eight measured picks and subtracts. The fast one measures the vertical spread of the sand line on the flattened panel. They must agree, and if they do not, a shift is wrong. Use the fast one to check the slow one rather than to replace it, because the fast one only works on a panel flattened on the top of the interval in question.

## What the range tells you

It tells you that the interval is not constant across this section. A growth range of zero would mean every well carries the same thickness between TOP_A and TOP_SAND. This one is 14 m, so they do not.

It tells you the size of the variation in the units of the thing itself. Fourteen metres against a thinnest well of 46 m is a large variation: the thickest well carries roughly thirty percent more section between the two surfaces than the thinnest. A range of 14 m would mean something quite different against intervals of 500 m, so quote the range next to the intervals it came from rather than on its own.

It tells you which two wells set the extremes, provided you wrote them down. Ekene-4 is the thick end and Ekene-3 the thin end. That pairing is often the most useful part of the result, because it points at where to look next.

## What the range does not tell you

**It does not say where the section thickens.** The range is a spread, not a direction and not a gradient. Ekene-3 and Ekene-4 sit next to each other on this panel and carry the two extremes, so the strongest change is between adjacent wells, but the range alone would never have told you that. Only the ordered list of intervals does.

**It uses two of the four numbers and discards the rest.** Ekene-1 at 48 m and Ekene-2 at 53 m contribute nothing to the range. Two sections with the same range can have quite different distributions in between, and the range cannot distinguish them.

**It can only grow.** Add a fifth well to the section and the range either stays at 14 m or increases, because a new interval can extend an end but cannot pull one in. So a range is a statement about the wells you have, not about the field, and it is biased low as an estimate of the field's variation. Quote the well count with it, as you learned to do with relief.

**It carries no rate and no duration.** Fourteen metres of growth says nothing about how fast, how long or when. Those questions need ages, and this section has none. Do not compute one.

**It is not a relief.** The range is a variation in an interval, which is two surfaces in each well. Relief is the spread of one surface across wells. They are different quantities and they answer different questions, which is the subject of the last lesson in this module.
