# Growth against relief

Two numbers describe how a section varies across wells and they are constantly swapped for one another. Relief is the spread of one surface. Growth is the variation of an interval. A learner who confuses them will quote a relief where a growth is wanted, and the answer will be wrong by a factor of three in this very section. This is the distinction that has to land.

## Relief

The structural relief of a surface across a section is its deepest measured occurrence minus its shallowest measured occurrence, over the wells that carry it. It involves one named surface and it uses one pick per well.

For the four Ekene surfaces:

| surface | relief | wells used |
|---|---|---|
| TOP_A | 35 m | 4 |
| TOP_SAND | 49 m | 4 |
| BASE_SAND | 45 m | 4 |
| TOP_B | 34 m | 3 |

TOP_B is carried by three wells only, because Ekene-4 reached total depth above it, so its 34 m must be quoted with the well count every time it is written down.

Relief is a structural quantity. It describes where a surface sits, and it is defined on measured depths, so flattening cannot touch it. Flatten on TOP_A, on TOP_SAND, on anything, and TOP_SAND still has 49 m of relief, from 1541 m in Ekene-3 to 1590 m in Ekene-4.

## Growth

Growth is variation in an interval across the wells, and the growth range is its maximum minus its minimum. It involves two named surfaces and it uses two picks per well.

For A-to-SAND: intervals of 48, 53, 46 and 60 m, and a growth range of 14 m.

Growth is a stratigraphic quantity. It describes how much section accumulated between two surfaces, and it says nothing directly about where either surface now sits. Flattening does not change it either, since intervals survive flattening, but flattening is what makes it visible, by putting every well's top surface on one line so the intervals can be compared by eye.

## Side by side

| | relief | growth |
|---|---|---|
| what varies | one surface across wells | one interval across wells |
| picks per well | one | two |
| kind of quantity | structural | stratigraphic |
| defined on | measured depths | measured depths, seen on displayed ones |
| changed by flattening | no | no |
| revealed by flattening | no, it is removed from the view | yes, that is the point |
| value here, TOP_SAND and A-to-SAND | 49 m | 14 m |

The last row is the one to memorise, because both numbers are about the sand top and they differ by 35 m.

## The same surface, two numbers

Look at TOP_SAND from both sides. In measured depth its picks are 1541, 1548, 1565 and 1590 m, spread over 49 m. On the panel flattened on TOP_A at 1450 m its picks display at 1496, 1498, 1503 and 1510 m, spread over 14 m.

Neither reading is wrong and neither has changed the surface. They answer different questions. How deep does the sand top sit across this section is answered by 49 m of relief. How much does the section above the sand vary in thickness is answered by 14 m of growth. Flattening on TOP_A removed the structure from the view, and what remained in the sand line was the growth.

The relationship between the two numbers is worth one line, because it explains the sizes. TOP_SAND's measured depth in each well is that well's TOP_A depth plus that well's interval. So the spread of TOP_SAND is built from the spread of TOP_A, which is 35 m, and the spread of the interval, which is 14 m. Here they add exactly, since 35 plus 14 is 49, and they add exactly because Ekene-4 carries both the deepest TOP_A and the thickest interval while Ekene-3 carries both the shallowest TOP_A and the thinnest. In general the sum is an upper bound rather than an equality, since the two extremes need not fall in the same wells. Either way, the relief of a lower surface mixes structure and growth together, and separating them is what the flattening was for.

## The two ways this is asked

**Asked for growth, answering with relief.** Someone asks how much the interval between TOP_A and TOP_SAND varies across the section. The answer is 14 m. Answering 49 m quotes the relief of the lower surface, which is roughly three times too large, and it attributes to thickness change something that is mostly structure.

**Asked for relief, answering with growth.** Someone asks how much structural relief TOP_SAND has across these wells. The answer is 49 m, measured. Answering 14 m quotes the displayed spread from a flattened panel, which is the growth. This is the more dangerous direction, because the number came off a screen and looks authoritative, and because a structural question answered with a flattened number will flatter the structure and understate the closure.

The habit that prevents both is to count surfaces in the question. One surface named means relief and measured depths. Two surfaces named means an interval, and variation in it is growth.

## Exercise

For BASE_SAND, give the relief and then give the growth range of the TOP_A to BASE_SAND interval, from the measured picks. Say which of the two is structural and which is stratigraphic, and state what each one would be after flattening on TOP_A at 1450 m.

Self-check: BASE_SAND picks are 1580, 1601, 1570 and 1615 m, so the relief is 1615 minus 1570, which is 45 m over four wells, and it is structural. The A-to-BASE_SAND intervals are 1580 minus 1500, which is 80 m; 1601 minus 1512, which is 89 m; 1570 minus 1495, which is 75 m; and 1615 minus 1530, which is 85 m. The maximum is 89 m in Ekene-2 and the minimum is 75 m in Ekene-3, so the growth range is 14 m, and it is stratigraphic. After flattening, both are unchanged, at 45 m and 14 m, because both are defined on measured depths. What flattening changes is that the growth becomes visible as the spread of the displayed BASE_SAND line while the relief disappears from the view.
