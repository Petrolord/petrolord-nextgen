# What growth means geologically

You have a number, 14 m of growth range in the A-to-SAND interval, and four intervals behind it: 48, 53, 46 and 60 m. This lesson turns that into a geological statement, and it is careful about where the statement stops. The section gives thicknesses and nothing else, so the reading stays qualitative.

## The null model first

Start with what the section would look like if nothing interesting had happened. A layer-cake section is one where every layer has the same thickness at every well. Sediment arrived evenly, the room available to fill was the same everywhere, and any structure came later and tilted the finished stack as a rigid body.

A layer-cake section has three signatures, and they are the same signature seen three ways. Every well shows the same A-to-SAND interval. The growth range is zero. And after flattening on TOP_A, every well's TOP_SAND displays at the same depth, so the sand line is as flat as the datum line and the whole stack below the datum lies flat.

That is the reference case. It is worth carrying because it is the thing your eye is testing against whenever it looks at a flattened panel, whether you have said so or not.

## What this section shows instead

The intervals are 48, 53, 46 and 60 m, so the growth range is 14 m rather than zero, and on the flattened panel the sand line is not flat. It sits 48 m below the datum at Ekene-1, 53 m at Ekene-2, 46 m at Ekene-3 and 60 m at Ekene-4. The stack below TOP_A does not lie flat after flattening, and that is the observation.

The interpretation follows directly and it is short. Accommodation, meaning the room available for sediment to accumulate in, was not the same at all four locations while the section between TOP_A and TOP_SAND was being deposited. There was more of it at Ekene-4 than at Ekene-3, and the difference amounts to 14 m of section.

That sentence is the whole geological content of the growth range. Everything else in this lesson is either a refinement of it or a warning about going further.

## The pattern with structural position

One refinement is available here and it is worth having, because it is the classic case. Line the wells up by the measured depth of TOP_A and the intervals sort themselves: Ekene-3 at 1495 m carries 46 m, Ekene-1 at 1500 m carries 48 m, Ekene-2 at 1512 m carries 53 m, and Ekene-4 at 1530 m carries 60 m. The interval increases as TOP_A gets deeper, across all four wells with no exception.

The reading is that the interval thickens towards the structurally low side of the section. Sediment accumulates preferentially where there is more room to accumulate in, and there is more room where the surface is sinking faster. So the structure and the deposition were not separate events in sequence. Some part of the structure was already forming while this interval was being laid down, and the interval recorded it by thickening into the low.

This is what the Associate tier was pointing at when it noted that TOP_SAND has 49 m of relief against TOP_A's 35 m. That extra relief on the lower surface and this thickening are the same fact, read from two directions.

## Candidate mechanisms

Several processes produce thickening into a structural low, and four thicknesses cannot separate them. Fault movement during deposition, with the downthrown side taking more section. Growth of a fold or the flanks of a rising feature, with the syncline filling faster than the crest. Differential subsidence or differential compaction of what lay beneath, so one location kept sinking while another did not. Depositional topography that was there before the interval started and was gradually filled in.

Any of these fits. Naming one requires evidence this section does not contain: seismic across the wells to see whether a fault or a fold is present, log character to see whether the thicker section is more of the same or something different, and dips to see the geometry between the wells. Report the growth as an observation, list the mechanisms as candidates, and go and get the data that separates them.

## Where the reading stops

**No rates.** Fourteen metres of extra section says nothing about how quickly it accumulated. A rate needs a duration and this section carries no ages.

**No ages and no durations.** There is nothing in the pick table that dates TOP_A, dates TOP_SAND or bounds the time between them. Do not invent one, and do not import one from a general expectation about how long a sand takes to arrive.

**No shape between wells.** Four wells are four samples. The change from 46 m at Ekene-3 to 60 m at Ekene-4 may be a smooth ramp, a step at a fault, or something less tidy. The data does not decide it.

**Nothing beyond the section.** The growth range describes these four wells. It is a lower bound on the variation in the field, since another well can only extend the range.

## Growth is only as good as the correlation

One prerequisite, carried from the Associate tier. All of this rests on TOP_A being the same surface in all four wells and TOP_SAND being the same surface in all four wells. If one TOP_SAND pick is on a different sand, its interval is wrong and the growth range measures a picking error rather than accommodation. A large, isolated growth number in one well is a reason to go back to the logs before it is a reason to write about accommodation.

## Exercise

Write two sentences about the Ekene section. The first states the observation from the intervals and the growth range, using the numbers. The second states the interpretation, and stops where the data stops. Then list two statements that the numbers do not support.

Self-check: an acceptable observation is that the A-to-SAND interval measures 48, 53, 46 and 60 m across the four wells, a growth range of 14 m, and thickens systematically as TOP_A deepens. An acceptable interpretation is that accommodation varied across the section while that interval was deposited, with more room at Ekene-4 than at Ekene-3, which is consistent with structural growth during deposition, though the mechanism is not determined by four thicknesses. Unsupported statements include any rate of growth in metres per unit time, any age or duration for the interval, any claim about how the thickness behaves between the wells, and any named fault or fold.
