# The teaching section

Every number in this tier comes off one dataset: four wells named Ekene-1 to Ekene-4, four named surfaces, and a single tops table with one gap in it. You met these wells at the Associate tier. Meet them again with a measuring eye, because from here on you are expected to reproduce readings from this table rather than recognise them.

## The picks

All depths are measured depths in metres. Every Ekene well is treated as vertical, so measured depth stands in for true vertical depth throughout.

| well | TOP_A | TOP_SAND | BASE_SAND | TOP_B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 Ekene-1 | 1500 | 1548 | 1580 | 1640 |
| W2 Ekene-2 | 1512 | 1565 | 1601 | 1662 |
| W3 Ekene-3 | 1495 | 1541 | 1570 | 1628 |
| W4 Ekene-4 | 1530 | 1590 | 1615 | missing |

Fifteen picks across four wells. Learn the shape of the table rather than the individual numbers, because the shape is what the rest of the tier argues about.

The wells are drawn left to right in well-number order. That order is a labelling convention and carries no distance information, so nothing on the panel is a gradient and no dip angle can be quoted from it.

## Reading down a column

Read a column and you are reading structure. TOP_A runs 1500, 1512, 1495 and 1530 m, so the shallowest occurrence is in Ekene-3 and the deepest is in Ekene-4. The structural relief of TOP_A across the section, deepest minus shallowest over the wells that carry it, is 35 m.

The other three columns give 49 m on TOP_SAND, 45 m on BASE_SAND and 34 m on TOP_B. Read those four relief figures as a group: 35, 49, 45 and 34 m. They do not agree with one another, and if the section were a rigid slab that had been tilted they would have to agree, because tilting a rigid body moves every surface in it by the same amount. The disagreement is the first hint that something happened during deposition rather than only after it.

Note the well count against the last one. The 34 m on TOP_B is a three-well figure, and it belongs beside the other three only with that qualification attached. Ekene-4 is the deepest well on every surface it reaches, so the surface it did not reach is missing precisely the well that would have extended its range.

## Reading across a row

Read across a row and you are reading thickness. The interval between TOP_A and TOP_SAND is 48 m in Ekene-1, 53 m in Ekene-2, 46 m in Ekene-3 and 60 m in Ekene-4.

That row-wise reading is the one this tier is built on. It is a property of each well and it owes nothing to the display, which is why it survives every datum choice you will make in the next four modules. Keep it in view while you learn the flattening arithmetic, because the arithmetic exists to make this reading visible on a panel rather than to produce it.

## The fourth well stops short

Ekene-4 reached total depth above TOP_B, so it carries three tops rather than four. That is a drilling fact and not a geological one. Nothing was faulted out and nothing was eroded. The bit stopped, and the surface below the bit was never seen.

Three consequences follow, and all three are readings this tier asks you to produce.

The count of wells carrying all four tops is 3. That number is graded with no tolerance at all, which is the grader's way of saying it is a definition rather than a calculation. Any analysis that needs the complete column from TOP_A to TOP_B is a three-well analysis on this section, and saying so is part of reporting the result.

The TOP_B correlation line reaches three wells and stops. It is not carried across the gap, not interpolated, and not dragged to a default depth. A line that stops short is a correct statement about what has been observed.

Any interval measured to TOP_B is undefined in Ekene-4. Not zero, not small, not assumed. Undefined, and excluded from the calculation with the exclusion reported.

## Why this is the reason the Expert tier exists

The gap in Ekene-4 is the question the whole course ladder climbs toward. The Associate tier taught you to see it and to refuse to fill it. This tier teaches you to measure the thing that would govern any filling of it, which is how the section thickens and thins between the wells that do carry a surface. The Expert tier then uses those growth numbers to estimate where TOP_B would have been in Ekene-4, gets more than one answer, and treats the disagreement between them as the honest uncertainty on the estimate.

So this table is not a section with an inconvenient hole in it. The hole is the teaching device, and every tier handles it at the level of maturity it has earned. At this tier that means you count it, you exclude it, and you name it in the result.

## What four wells can and cannot support

Four wells is a small section, and the readings it supports should be described as what they are. Growth measured across four wells is a trend in four control points, not a mapped thickness surface. A relief figure computed over these wells describes this section and would change if a fifth well were added to it. Both statements are still worth making, because a small honest reading beats a large invented one, but neither is improved by being quoted without its sample size.

## Exercise

Copy the pick table out by hand, then answer four questions from your copy without looking back. Which well supplies the shallowest pick on every surface it carries, and which supplies the deepest. What is the structural relief on TOP_A, and over how many wells. How many wells carry all four tops. And what is the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval in each of the four wells.

Self-check: Ekene-3 supplies the shallowest pick on every surface and Ekene-4 the deepest on every surface it reaches. TOP_A relief is 35 m over four wells. Three wells carry all four tops, because Ekene-4 reached total depth above TOP_B. The TOP_A to TOP_SAND intervals are 48, 53, 46 and 60 m for Ekene-1 through Ekene-4. If you wrote a fourth interval for TOP_B in Ekene-4 rather than leaving it undefined, that is the exact habit this tier is built to break.
