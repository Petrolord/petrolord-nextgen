# Why every shift is negative here

All four Ekene shifts came out negative: -50, -62, -45 and -80 m. That is a property of this particular setting rather than a law of flattening, and knowing which of the two it is protects you from two opposite mistakes. One is expecting negative shifts everywhere. The other is treating a positive shift as a symptom of something wrong.

## The reason, in one sentence

The datum is 1450 m and every well's TOP_A pick is deeper than that: 1500, 1512, 1495 and 1530 m. The shift is the datum minus the pick, so subtracting a larger number from a smaller one gives a negative result every time, in all four wells.

There is no geology in that observation. It follows from where the datum line was placed relative to the picks, and the datum line was placed by you.

## What a negative shift does to the display

Depth increases downward on the panel, so adding a negative number to a measured depth produces a smaller displayed depth, which is a position higher on the picture. A negative shift moves a well up the display.

Every Ekene well therefore moves up, and each one moves up by its own amount. Ekene-4 travels furthest at -80 m and Ekene-3 least at -45 m. Say what that means physically: Ekene-4 was the deepest well at TOP_A, at a measured 1530 m, so it had the longest journey to reach the 1450 m datum line, and Ekene-3 at a measured 1495 m had the shortest. The magnitude of a shift is the distance between that well's flattening pick and the datum, and nothing more.

Because all four moved in the same direction, the section did not turn inside out. The relative order of the wells on TOP_A is preserved, the well that was deepest before is the one that moved most, and the four end up level at 1450 m displayed. Uniform sign is a convenience, not a requirement.

## When a shift is positive

A shift is positive when the datum is deeper than that well's pick of the flattening top. The well then moves down the display, toward larger displayed depths.

You would see one in three ordinary situations.

**A datum chosen below the picks.** Put the flattening datum at a depth deeper than every TOP_A pick, deeper than 1530 m, and all four Ekene shifts turn positive at once. The panel geometry is identical to the one you have now, because moving the datum depth alone slides the whole picture, and every interval, the growth range and the displayed span are all unchanged. Only the axis labels differ. That is the cleanest demonstration that the sign of a shift is a statement about the datum rather than about the section.

**A deep flattening top with a shallow datum kept.** Flatten the same wells on BASE_SAND while keeping a shallow round datum and the shifts are more negative still, because the picks are deeper. Flatten on TOP_A while keeping a datum you had chosen for a deeper surface and they go positive. The sign tracks the pairing of the two choices, not either one alone.

**A structurally high well against a mid-range datum.** This is the case worth recognising, because it produces mixed signs in a single view. Choose a datum that falls inside the range of the flattening picks and the wells shallower than it take positive shifts and move down, while the wells deeper than it take negative shifts and move up. Nothing is broken. The wells converge on the datum line from both sides, and they still all land on it.

A shift of exactly zero is the boundary case. It means that well's flattening pick already sits at the datum depth, so the column does not move at all. It is also what the structural view is, expressed in this tier's language: every well given a shift of zero, displayed depth equal to measured depth everywhere.

## Why this matters beyond bookkeeping

Three practical points follow from taking the sign seriously.

**The sign is part of the answer.** The graded Ekene-4 reading is -80 m. Writing 80 m is not a rounding difference or a presentation choice. It is the claim that the well moved down the display when it moved up, and at this tier it is wrong.

**A sign that surprises you is a check worth running.** If you expect all-negative shifts on a shallow datum and one comes back positive, either that well's flattening pick is shallower than the datum, which is a fact you can verify in the table, or the pick itself is wrong. Both are worth two minutes.

**Uniform sign is not evidence of anything geological.** All four Ekene wells moving up says the datum is shallow. It does not say the wells are similar, that the structure is simple, or that the flattening is good. A section with a badly mispicked flattening top produces four tidy same-signed shifts just as readily.

## What does not depend on the sign

Nothing this tier grades except the shift itself depends on which way the wells moved.

Intervals are differences between two picks in one well, and both picks carry the same shift, so the sign cancels. The TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval is 48, 53, 46 and 60 m whatever the datum. The growth range of 14 m follows from those intervals and is equally indifferent. The count of 3 wells carrying all four tops is a fact about the tops table. Even the displayed span of 150 m is a difference between two displayed depths and survives any move of the datum depth.

So the sign discipline in this lesson is about reporting a shift correctly and reading a panel correctly. It is not a hazard to the measurements, and that is a good example of the general shape of this tier: the display conventions are strict, and the quantities that matter are built to be robust against them.
