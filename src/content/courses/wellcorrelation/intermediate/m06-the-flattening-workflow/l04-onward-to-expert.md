# Onward to Expert

This tier taught one thing and taught it completely. You can take a section, hang it on a datum of your choosing, and measure what the structure was hiding. On Ekene that means four shifts of -50, -62, -45 and -80 m from a TOP_A datum of 1450 m, an A-to-SAND interval of 48, 53, 46 and 60 m that flattening leaves untouched, a growth range of 14 m, a displayed span of 150 m, and the standing fact that 3 of the 4 wells carry all four tops.

That is a complete skill and it stops at a definite edge. Everything you produced was measured from picks that exist. The tier above starts where the picks run out.

## The Expert tier predicts what was never drilled

Ekene-4 has no TOP_B, because the well reached total depth above that surface. Three wells carry TOP_B: Ekene-1 at 1640 m, Ekene-2 at 1662 m and Ekene-3 at 1628 m, all measured. The Expert question is where TOP_B would have been in Ekene-4 if the hole had gone deeper.

It is answered two independent ways from those three wells, and the two answers do not agree. They differ by 11 m.

Both of those sentences are a destination rather than material. Do not go looking for the method now and do not try to reach it with the flattening panel, which shows shifts, displayed depths, intervals and a growth range and does not predict a missing pick. The panel measures. Prediction is a different piece of machinery and it belongs to the tier that teaches it properly.

## Why the disagreement is the point

The instinct when two methods disagree is to treat the gap as a fault, decide which method is better, or split the difference and move on. The Expert tier does none of those. The 11 m is not noise around a true answer that better arithmetic would recover. It is the measured uncertainty on the prediction, and it is the most informative number the exercise produces.

You already know why the two answers can differ, because this tier gave you the reason. A section with growth in it is not a layer cake. The A-to-SAND interval ranges over 14 m across four wells, which means the surfaces bounding it are not parallel. Any prediction that leans on one reference surface will disagree with a prediction that leans on another, and the size of that disagreement is a readout of how far from parallel the section is.

So the growth you measured here is what makes the Expert prediction uncertain, and it is also what lets you put a number on the uncertainty. Average the two estimates into one and you throw away the only evidence you had about how much to trust either.

## What carries up

Three habits from this tier are what make the Expert work honest rather than merely clever.

**Say which frame a number is in.** Measured and displayed are different depths for the same surface. Ekene-2's TOP_SAND is 1565 m measured and 1503 m displayed, and both are correct. A prediction that quietly mixes the two frames is wrong before the geology enters into it.

**Say how many wells are behind a number.** The growth range of 14 m is a four-well number. Anything touching TOP_B is a three-well number, including everything the Expert tier does, since the prediction is built from the three wells that carry the surface. That label travels with the prediction.

**Keep the estimate beside the data.** Module 5 set this boundary deliberately and the Expert tier lives on the far side of it. The prediction is an estimate. It is reported as an estimate, with its range. Ekene-4's TOP_B slot stays blank in the tops table, the correlation line still stops after the third well, and the count of wells carrying all four tops stays at 3 no matter how good the prediction turns out to be.

## The progression

Three sentences hold the ladder. The Associate tier reads a section. This tier flattens it and measures how the section grows. The Expert tier predicts what a well did not reach and states how much that prediction can be trusted.

Each tier works the same four wells, the same tops table and the same engine. What changes is the question. The measurement here is the arithmetic of the tier below applied on a different anchor. The prediction above is this tier's measurements plus an assumption about a surface nobody observed, and the assumption is exactly the part that has to be declared.

## Where this course sits

Well Correlation is one rung of a longer ladder. Beneath it is Well Data Manager, which is where the wells, the deviations and the tops table came from. A tops table that was never quality-controlled at that level cannot be rescued at this one.

Above it sit mapping and volumetrics. Your correlated tops become the control points somebody contours into surfaces, and the intervals you measured become the gross rock volume a resource number is built on. That is why the labelling discipline in this module matters more than it looks. A displayed depth handed upward as a measured one becomes a wrong contour and then a wrong volume, and by the time anybody notices, the section it came from is a year old.

## Exercise

Write down, in one paragraph and without doing any prediction arithmetic, why a section with a 14 m growth range should be expected to produce two disagreeing estimates for a missing top, and what you would report if a colleague handed you a single predicted depth for Ekene-4's TOP_B with no range attached.

Self-check: growth means the interval between two surfaces varies across the wells, so the surfaces are not parallel, so a prediction anchored on one surface and a prediction anchored on another will place the missing top differently, and the size of the difference reflects how far from parallel the section is. A single predicted depth with no range attached is not more accurate than two that disagree, it is less informative about its own reliability, so you would ask what reference it was built from, which wells it used, and what the alternative construction gives, and you would report the result as an estimate with a range rather than as a depth. In either case Ekene-4's TOP_B stays blank in the tops table and the well count stays at 3.
