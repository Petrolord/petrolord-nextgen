# Projecting from TOP_SAND

You have the anchor and you have the borrowed interval, so the prediction is one addition. Ekene-4's measured TOP_SAND is 1590 m and the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval across the three wells that carry both is 92 m.

$$1590 + 92 = 1682$$

The predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4 is 1682 m. It is one of the six graded readings in this tier, to a tolerance of 0.01 m.

## The same move on a different anchor

Compare the two constructions side by side, because the point of this module is that they are the same method rather than two competing techniques.

From TOP_A: anchor 1530 m, borrowed interval 141 m, predicted TOP_B 1671 m.

From TOP_SAND: anchor 1590 m, borrowed interval 92 m, predicted TOP_B 1682 m.

Both take a measured depth in Ekene-4 and add an average measured in Ekene-1, Ekene-2 and Ekene-3. Both use every well that can contribute and exclude the well being predicted. Neither one involves a fudge factor, a weighting or a judgement call about which well to trust. If you handed the two recipes to two competent people who had never spoken to each other, both would be reproduced exactly.

What differs is only the starting marker, and with it the stretch of section whose thickness is being assumed rather than measured.

## What the number is anchored to

The anchor is Ekene-4's own TOP_SAND at 1590 m, a measured depth in that wellbore. Two things follow.

The first is a frame check. This is a measured depth, not a displayed one. The Professional tier flattened this section on a 1450 m datum and Ekene-4's shift was 80 m up the display, which would put its TOP_SAND at 1510 m on that panel. Adding the borrowed interval to that displayed depth produces a number that is not a prediction of anything, because a displayed depth belongs to a datum somebody chose rather than to the earth. Predictions are built in measured depth and reported in measured depth, and the panel you happened to be looking at when the idea occurred to you is not part of the arithmetic.

The second is that the anchor carries no assumption at all. Everything above 1590 m in Ekene-4 is known from that well, including the fact that its TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval is 60 m, the thickest in the section. This prediction never has to guess at that stretch, because it starts below it. Hold on to that observation, since the last lesson in this module is built on it.

## Checking it for sense

A predicted depth that nobody can sanity check is a liability, so check it against what the section already tells you.

The three measured TOP_B picks are 1640 m in Ekene-1, 1662 m in Ekene-2 and 1628 m in Ekene-3. The predicted 1682 m in Ekene-4 is deeper than all three. That is worth noticing rather than worrying about, and the section explains it.

Ekene-4 is the structurally deepest well in the section on every surface it carries. Its TOP_A of 1530 m is deeper than the 1495 to 1512 m of the other wells, and its TOP_SAND of 1590 m is deeper than the 1541 to 1565 m range. A well that sits low on every marker it reached should be expected to sit low on the next marker down. A predicted TOP_B shallower than all three measured picks would have been the result worth stopping over.

Do not push the check further than it goes. It confirms the direction and the rough magnitude. It does not confirm the 1682 m, and no arithmetic performed on the three wells that carry TOP_B could, because none of them is Ekene-4.

## How to write it down

The number never travels alone. A useful form carries four things: what it is, how it was made, what it was made from, and what it is not.

Predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4, 1682 m, by projection of the three well mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m from the measured TOP_SAND at 1590 m. Estimated, not picked. Ekene-4's TOP_B slot in the tops table stays blank.

Every word of that is doing work. The label estimated stops the number being read as a measurement. The method lets a reader reproduce it or disagree with it. The three well count says how much evidence stands behind it. And the last sentence keeps the tops table honest, which is the discipline the tier below spent a module establishing and which nothing in this tier is allowed to overturn.

## Exercise

A colleague rechecks Ekene-3 and repicks its TOP_B 3 m deeper, at 1631 m. Recompute the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval and the predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4, then explain why the prediction moved by less than the repick did. Separately, say what is wrong with a reported predicted TOP_B of 1602 m for Ekene-4.

Self-check: Ekene-3's interval becomes 1631 minus 1541, which is 90 m, so the three intervals are 92, 97 and 90 m, the mean is 279 divided by 3, which is 93 m, and the predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4 becomes 1590 plus 93, which is 1683 m, estimated rather than picked. The repick moved one well by 3 m but the prediction moved only 1 m, because the borrowed interval is an average of three wells and a change in one of them is divided by three before it reaches the anchor. That damping is a real property of the method and it cuts both ways, since it also means the average is slow to respond to a well that genuinely is different. The reported 1602 m is wrong because it was built in the wrong depth frame, by adding the 92 m interval to Ekene-4's displayed TOP_SAND of 1510 m on a section flattened to a 1450 m datum rather than to its measured TOP_SAND of 1590 m, and a displayed depth belongs to a chosen datum rather than to the earth.
