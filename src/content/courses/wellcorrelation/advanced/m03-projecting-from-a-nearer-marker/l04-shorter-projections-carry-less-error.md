# Shorter projections carry less error

Two predictions now exist for the same missing pick, 1671 m from TOP_A and 1682 m from TOP_SAND, and most interpreters who look at this section will end up preferring the second one. This lesson sets out why, as an argument you can inspect rather than a rule you are asked to accept. It also marks clearly where the argument runs out, because it does run out.

## The claim, stated carefully

The claim is that the from TOP_SAND prediction is generally the more defensible of the two on this section. It is not that 1682 m is correct. Ekene-4 stopped above TOP_B, so there is no measurement to be right or wrong against, and there will not be one unless somebody drills deeper. Preferring an estimate and verifying an estimate are different acts, and only the first is available here.

Four reasons support the claim, and they are independent of each other, which is part of why they add up.

## The projection is shorter

The from TOP_SAND prediction carries a borrowed interval 92 m long. The layer-cake prediction carries one 141 m long.

The borrowed interval is the part that is assumed rather than measured in Ekene-4, so its length is the length of the assumption. Thickness variation in this section is real and it accumulates with distance, since a longer stretch of section contains more places for the thickness to differ from the average. Halving the projected distance does not halve the error in any exact sense, and nobody should claim it does, but a shorter projection exposes the prediction to less of the thing that makes predictions wrong here.

## The anchor is nearer the target

Related but not the same point. TOP_SAND sits closer to TOP_B than TOP_A does, so the geology between anchor and target is a smaller and more local piece of section.

Nearer control is better control in almost every part of subsurface work, and the reason is that geological properties tend to vary more the further apart two points are. Between TOP_SAND and TOP_B you are asking the three wells one question about one stratigraphic interval. Between TOP_A and TOP_B you are asking about that interval plus everything above it, which means the answer is a blend of several stretches, each of which may be behaving differently.

## The thickened part of Ekene-4 is already measured

This is the strongest of the four reasons and it is specific to this well.

The Professional tier measured the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval at 48, 53, 46 and 60 m across the section, a growth range of 14 m. Ekene-4 carries the thickest value in the section at 60 m, against a 46 to 60 m range across the four wells, and the other three sit between 46 and 53 m. That well is not average over that stretch, and the section knows it.

Now look at what each prediction does with that fact. Projecting from TOP_A borrows an average A-to-B interval taken from three wells whose A-to-SAND stretches are 48, 53 and 46 m. That average quietly assumes Ekene-4 behaves like them over the upper stretch as well as the lower one, and Ekene-4 demonstrably does not. Projecting from TOP_SAND starts below the thickened stretch entirely. Ekene-4's extra thickness is not assumed away, it is already inside the 1590 m anchor, because that is where the well actually found TOP_SAND.

So the two predictions are not equally well informed. One of them ignores a measurement that the other one uses.

## The borrowed interval is more consistent

The three TOP_SAND to TOP_B intervals are 87, 92 and 97 m, a spread of 10 m. The three TOP_A to TOP_B intervals are 133, 140 and 150 m, a spread of 17 m.

The wells agree more closely about the shorter interval. That agreement is the only direct evidence available that the interval is stable enough to borrow, so more of it is better. This reason matters because it is the one that does not follow from distance. A shorter interval is not automatically a more consistent one, and had the SAND-to-B intervals spread 25 m while the A-to-B intervals spread 5 m, the shortness of the projection would have counted for much less. Here the two point the same way, which is why the argument is comfortable rather than finely balanced.

## Where the argument stops

Four things it does not establish.

It does not establish that 1682 m is right. Nobody drilled it. Both numbers remain estimates, and the more defensible of two estimates is still an estimate.

It does not establish that shorter is always better. The reasoning depended on the specific spreads measured here and on Ekene-4's specific anomaly. A nearer marker that was poorly picked, or one carried by fewer wells, could easily produce a worse prediction than a distant marker with clean control.

It does not make the other prediction wrong. The layer-cake estimate was built correctly from all the available data by a defensible method, and discarding it would throw away the comparison the next module is built on.

And it does not remove the uncertainty. Preferring one estimate narrows nothing by itself. The gap between the two is still there, still 11 m wide, and it is about to turn out to be the most useful thing either method produced.

## Exercise

Suppose Ekene-4's TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval had come out at 47 m rather than 60 m, near the middle of the other wells rather than at the thick end, with every other pick in the section unchanged. Say which of the four reasons above would still stand, which would weaken, and whether you would expect the two predictions to agree more closely or less closely than they do.

Self-check: the first, second and fourth reasons would all still stand, since the projected distances would be unchanged at 92 m against 141 m, TOP_SAND would still be the nearer anchor, and the input spreads of 10 m against 17 m are measured in the other three wells and do not depend on Ekene-4 at all. The third reason is the one that weakens, because it rests entirely on Ekene-4 being anomalously thick over the upper stretch, and a well sitting near the middle of the other values gives the layer-cake route much less to be wrong about. You would expect the two predictions to agree more closely, since the gap between them is driven by the difference between Ekene-4's own upper interval and the average upper interval of the contributing wells, and that difference is what the change would shrink.
