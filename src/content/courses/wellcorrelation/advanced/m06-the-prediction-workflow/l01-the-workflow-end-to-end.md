# The workflow end to end

Everything this tier taught fits into one ordered procedure. This lesson puts it in order, and then puts the whole ladder behind it in order too, because the Expert step only makes sense as the third move of three.

## The Expert path in order

**Step one: find the gap and confirm it is real.** Ekene-4 has TOP_A at 1530 m, TOP_SAND at 1590 m and BASE_SAND at 1615 m, and no TOP_B. The reason is that the well reached total depth above that surface. A missing top because the hole stopped short is a different situation from a missing top because the surface is absent or the log quality was poor, and only the first is predictable by projection. Establish which one you have before doing arithmetic.

**Step two: identify the wells that carry the target.** TOP_B is picked in Ekene-1 at 1640 m, Ekene-2 at 1662 m and Ekene-3 at 1628 m. Three wells, not four. Every interval in the rest of the procedure comes from those three and the count travels with every number you report.

**Step three: choose the anchor markers.** An anchor is a marker the target well actually has, and Ekene-4 has two candidates, TOP_A at 1530 m and TOP_SAND at 1590 m. Both are used, and using both is what produces the range. Projecting from a marker the target well lacks is the error this step exists to prevent.

**Step four: compute the mean interval to the target, for each anchor.** From TOP_A the intervals are 140, 150 and 133 m, so the mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval is 423 divided by 3, which is 141 m. From TOP_SAND they are 92, 97 and 87 m, so the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval is 276 divided by 3, which is 92 m. Both means are three-well means.

**Step five: project each mean down from its anchor.** The layer-cake estimate is 1530 plus 141, which is 1671 m. The from-TOP_SAND estimate is 1590 plus 92, which is 1682 m. Each is a single addition and each is exact.

**Step six: take the spread.** The spread is 1682 minus 1671, which is 11 m. It is the measured disagreement between two defensible methods on the same three wells, and it is the uncertainty on the prediction.

**Step seven: read the relief separately.** TOP_B relief across the three wells that carry it is 1662 minus 1628, which is 34 m. This is a structural reading, it is not derived from the predictions, and it is not the error bar on them.

**Step eight: report.** The predicted depth or range, the method, the anchor marker, the wells the interval came from, the spread, and an explicit label that this is a prediction. Ekene-4's TOP_B slot stays blank in the tops table.

Eight steps, and only steps four, five and six involve arithmetic. The rest is deciding what you are allowed to use and saying what you did.

## The ladder behind it

Three tiers work the same four wells, the same tops table and the same engine. What changes each time is the question.

**The Associate tier read the section.** It taught what a formation top is, how to pick one consistently on logs, how to name it so it means the same thing in every well, and how to read structure across a correlation panel. It taught measured depth against true vertical depth, structural relief, zones between tops, and correlation lines that stop short where a well does not carry a surface. At the end of that tier you could look at the Ekene section and describe it correctly.

**The Professional tier flattened it and measured growth.** It taught the datum, the one-subtraction shift of datum minus the well's flattening pick, and displayed depth against measured depth. On Ekene the shifts from a TOP_A datum of 1450 m are minus 50, minus 62, minus 45 and minus 80 m, every one negative because every TOP_A is deeper than the datum. It taught that intervals survive flattening, since both ends of an interval take the same shift, which is why the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval reads 48, 53, 46 and 60 m before and after. And it taught what that reveals: a growth range of 14 m, a displayed span of 150 m, and the standing fact that 3 of the 4 wells carry all four tops.

**This tier turned that growth into a prediction with an honest range.** The growth measured below is exactly the reason the two estimates here disagree. Surfaces that are not parallel give different answers depending on which one you anchor to, and 11 m is a readout of how far from parallel this section is. If the section were a layer cake, both estimates would land on the same depth and the spread would be zero.

That is the whole progression in three sentences. Read the section. Flatten it and measure how it grows. Use the growth to predict what a well did not reach, and say how much the prediction can be trusted.

## Where the tiers meet

Two joins are worth seeing rather than taking on trust.

The first is that the Professional tier's refusal to invent a pick is what makes this tier honest. That tier taught you to leave Ekene-4's TOP_B blank, stop the correlation line after the third well, and keep the well count at 3. Nothing here overrides that. A prediction is produced, reported and used, and the blank stays blank.

The second is that the Associate tier's relief comes back as the thing you must not confuse with the spread. Relief was a structural reading there and it is a structural reading here, unchanged at 34 m over three wells for TOP_B. The tier that taught you the number is not the tier that taught you where it does not belong.

## Exercise

Write out the eight steps of the Expert path from memory, marking which three involve arithmetic. Then answer in two sentences: which step would you have to redo if a fourth well were added that carries TOP_B, and which step would be unaffected if Ekene-4's TOP_A pick were revised by 2 m.

As a self check: the eight steps are confirm the gap is a total-depth gap, identify the three wells carrying TOP_B, choose anchor markers Ekene-4 actually has, compute the mean interval for each anchor, project each mean from its anchor, take the spread, read the relief separately, and report with all six reporting elements. Steps four, five and six are the arithmetic. A fourth well carrying TOP_B forces step four to be redone, since both means become four-well means and steps five and six follow from them, and it also changes the relief in step seven. A revision to Ekene-4's TOP_A moves the layer-cake estimate in step five but leaves step four untouched, because Ekene-4 contributes no interval to either mean.
