# The top of the ladder

This is the last lesson of the Well Correlation ladder. There is no Expert tier above the Expert tier, so this lesson closes the app rather than pointing at the next rung. It is worth spending a few hundred words on what you can now do, what correlation can and cannot tell you, and where these skills go next.

## What three tiers have given you

**From the Associate tier, you can read a section.** You can pick a formation top on logs, name it so it means the same surface in every well, and keep that naming consistent across a project. You can tell measured depth from true vertical depth. You can read structural relief off a panel, define zones between tops, draw correlation lines and stop them where a well does not carry a surface.

**From the Professional tier, you can flatten a section and measure what structure was hiding.** You can choose a datum, compute the shift as datum minus the well's flattening pick, and apply it. On Ekene, flattening on TOP_A at 1450 m gives shifts of minus 50, minus 62, minus 45 and minus 80 m, and Ekene-2's TOP_SAND displays at 1503 m. You know that intervals survive flattening because both ends take the same shift, so the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval is 48, 53, 46 and 60 m either way. You can read the growth range of 14 m, the displayed span of 150 m, and the count of 3 of 4 wells carrying all four tops, and you label every depth as measured or displayed.

**From this tier, you can predict a pick nobody logged and say how much to trust it.** You can identify the wells that carry a target surface, choose an anchor the target well actually has, compute a mean interval over the right divisor, project it, and do the same from a second anchor. On Ekene that produces 1671 m from TOP_A and 1682 m from TOP_SAND, a spread of 11 m, and a report that gives the range with the method, the anchors, the three wells and an explicit prediction label. You keep the 34 m structural relief of TOP_B separate from the 11 m spread, and you leave Ekene-4's TOP_B blank in the tops table.

That is a complete skill set for the discipline. There is no fourth move.

## What correlation can tell you

Correlation answers questions about surfaces and the section between them, at the wells. It tells you which surface in one well is the same surface in another. It tells you how deep a surface sits and how much it varies across the wells you have. It tells you how thick an interval is in each well and how that thickness changes, which is the only direct evidence you have about how the section accumulated. And where a well stops short, it tells you what a missing pick most likely was and how far apart two honest methods land.

Those answers are the raw material for almost everything above this course. They are also strong answers, because every one of them is anchored on a measurement somebody made in a hole.

## What correlation cannot tell you

It cannot tell you anything about the rock between wells. Every number in this course is defined at a well location. The section drawn between two wells is a drawing, and the correlation line joining two picks is a statement of correlation rather than a measurement of the surface halfway along. Wells are far apart and the drawing is a smooth interpolation of a surface that is not smooth.

It cannot see a fault it was not told about. Growth of 14 m across four wells is consistent with a section thickening across a growth fault, and it is consistent with several other things. Correlation measures the thickening. It does not identify the mechanism.

It cannot narrow a prediction without more data. The 11 m spread on Ekene-4 is not a provisional figure awaiting better arithmetic. Deepening the well, a seismic tie or a nearby well would settle it, and until one of those arrives the range stands as reported.

And it cannot make a predicted depth into a pick. That boundary held through all three tiers and it is the single habit most worth carrying out of this course.

## Where these skills go next

Two places, and they take different things from you.

**Mapping takes the picks.** A mapper contours a surface from control points, and your correlated tops are those points. What they need from you is the depth, the surface name and the confidence, which is why the labelling discipline matters more than it looks. A predicted TOP_B handed over as a measured one becomes a control point that pulls a contour toward a depth nobody drilled, and the map will look no different from a correct one. The mapping course also uses relief the way this course defined it, as the spread of a surface across its control, so the 34 m over three wells goes upward exactly as written.

**Volumetrics takes the surfaces.** Gross rock volume is built between a top surface and a base surface or a contact, so the intervals you measured are what a resource number rests on. The growth you found is why a constant thickness assumption is wrong on this section, and an evaluator who books a volume from a single average thickness has thrown away the 14 m range you measured.

Below you, Well Data Manager is where the wells, the deviations and the tops table came from in the first place. A tops table that was never quality-controlled at that level cannot be rescued at this one, which is worth knowing when a correlation refuses to work.

## Exercise

Write a short paragraph for a colleague joining the project, saying what you can supply from a well correlation study, what you cannot, and what you would need to turn the Ekene-4 TOP_B range into a pick. Name the numbers you would hand to a mapper and the numbers you would hand to an evaluator.

As a self check: you can supply correlated tops with consistent naming, the depth of each surface at each well, structural relief per surface with the well count, interval thicknesses per well and their variation across the section, and a predicted depth with a range where a well stopped short. You cannot supply anything about the rock between wells, the mechanism behind a thickness change, or a narrower prediction range without new data. Turning the range into a pick requires deepening Ekene-4 through TOP_B, and a seismic tie or a nearby penetration would only add independent evidence rather than a pick. A mapper receives the measured picks by surface and well, the relief of each surface with its well count including TOP_B at 34 m over three wells, and the Ekene-4 TOP_B range of 1671 m to 1682 m labelled as a prediction. An evaluator receives the interval thicknesses, the growth range of 14 m and the same prediction label.
