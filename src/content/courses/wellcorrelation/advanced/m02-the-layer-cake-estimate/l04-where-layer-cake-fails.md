# Where layer-cake fails

The estimate of 1671 m is defensible, reproducible and honestly derived. It also rests on an assumption this section has already contradicted, and the contradiction is not abstract. It is specific to Ekene-4, it is measured, and it points in a known direction. This lesson states it plainly, because a prediction whose weakness you can describe is worth more than one whose weakness you cannot.

## The fact that undermines it

Ekene-4 carries the thickest TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval on the section.

The four values are 48 m in Ekene-1, 53 m in Ekene-2, 46 m in Ekene-3 and 60 m in Ekene-4. The range runs from 46 to 60 m and Ekene-4 sits at the top of it. The Professional tier measured that as a growth range of 14 m and treated it as the signal it is.

Now put that beside the three wells the layer-cake estimate borrowed from. Their TOP_A to TOP_SAND intervals are 48, 53 and 46 m. Every one of them is thinner than Ekene-4's 60 m. Ekene-4 carries 7 m more section between those two surfaces than the thickest of the three wells that contributed to the mean, since 60 minus 53 is 7.

So the section at Ekene-4 is already running thick above the target, and it is running thick compared with every well the borrowed interval came from. That is not a subtle statistical concern. It is a measured departure in the only interval where all four wells can be compared directly.

## Why the mean cannot know it

The mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval of 141 m was computed from Ekene-1, Ekene-2 and Ekene-3 alone. Not one metre of it came from Ekene-4, because Ekene-4 has no TOP_B and therefore cannot contribute to that column at all.

That is the structural weakness of the method rather than a fixable oversight. The layer-cake estimate is built from wells other than the one you are predicting into, so it is blind to anything specific about that well. It would return the same 141 m whether Ekene-4 were the thickest well on the section or the thinnest. The one piece of local evidence you have about how Ekene-4's section behaves, its 60 m TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval, is information the calculation had no way to use.

Add the addition to that and the consequence is direct. The prediction is Ekene-4's own TOP_A plus a borrowed interval, so the borrowed interval carries the whole error, one metre for one metre. If the true TOP_A to TOP_B distance in Ekene-4 differs from 141 m, the prediction is off by exactly that difference. Nothing in the method damps it and nothing in the method flags it.

## The part of the interval you cannot check

The 141 m span reaches from TOP_A down past TOP_SAND and BASE_SAND to TOP_B. Split it in your mind at TOP_SAND.

Above TOP_SAND, all four wells report and you know Ekene-4 is the thick one. Below TOP_SAND, only three wells report, because Ekene-4 lacks TOP_B, so nothing can be checked at Ekene-4 for that part of the span at all. The deepest and least constrained part of the transfer is the part where the well you are predicting into contributes no evidence whatever.

Two readings of that are available and the data does not choose between them. The thickening seen above TOP_SAND might continue below it, in which case the borrowed 141 m is too short for this well. Or it might be a local feature confined to the upper interval, in which case 141 m may be closer than it looks. What you can say without argument is that the layer-cake estimate assumed neither of those and considered neither, since it assumed the whole span behaves at Ekene-4 as it does on average elsewhere.

## The smaller weaknesses, still worth quoting

Three further limits ride along with the number, and each belongs in any report that uses it.

The mean rests on three wells, so each one carries about a third of the weight. A revision to any single TOP_B pick moves the prediction by roughly a third of that revision.

The three contributing intervals themselves span 17 m, from 133 m in Ekene-3 to 150 m in Ekene-2, and that spread is a floor rather than a measurement of the section's variability. A fourth well carrying TOP_B could only widen it.

Three points give no spatial control. There is no trend, no direction of thickening and no gradient in a three-well mean, so the estimate applies to Ekene-4 only in the sense that it applies anywhere.

## What to do about it

Nothing yet, and nothing that involves adjusting 1671 m by hand. Nudging a prediction toward what you suspect, with no method behind the nudge, replaces a stated assumption with an unstated one and makes the result harder to check rather than better.

The productive move is to build a second estimate that starts from a different marker, one that Ekene-4 does carry and that sits closer to the target. Comparing the two constructions turns the weakness described in this lesson from a worry into a measured quantity. That is the next module.

For now, report 1671 m as the layer-cake estimate, with the assumption named and this limitation attached. A number that arrives with its own strongest objection already written down is doing its job.

The first sentence names the assumption, the second names the specific measured fact about Ekene-4 that argues against it. Then say why adjusting the 1671 m yourself, without a second method, would make the report weaker rather than stronger.

Self-check: the assumption is that the TOP_A to TOP_B interval in Ekene-4 matches its average of 141 m in the three wells that carry both surfaces. The fact against it is that Ekene-4 carries the thickest TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval on the section at 60 m against a 46 to 60 m range, and that all three contributing wells are thinner than it there, so the well is atypical in the one interval all four wells can be compared on.
