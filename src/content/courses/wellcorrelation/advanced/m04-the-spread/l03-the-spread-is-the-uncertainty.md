# The spread is the uncertainty

This is the lesson the tier exists for. Everything before it was arithmetic you could have been taught in an afternoon. What follows is the idea that makes the arithmetic worth doing.

The 11 m gap between 1671 m and 1682 m is not a defect in your work. It is the result. It is a measured uncertainty on a pick that nobody logged, and you obtained it from the data rather than assuming it, which is rare enough to be worth a great deal.

## Two ways to put an uncertainty on a number

The common way is to attach one. Somebody quotes a predicted depth and adds a range around it drawn from experience, from a company standard, or from a habit of saying ten metres because ten metres is what people say. That range is a statement about the person, and it does not change when the data change. Give the same interpreter a section with no growth in it at all and the range would come out the same, which tells you it was never measuring anything.

The other way is to derive one. Apply two honest methods to the same data, and the distance between their answers is a quantity the data produced. It moves when the data move. The previous lesson settled that with an exact identity rather than an argument: the spread equals Ekene-4's upper interval of 60 m minus the contributing wells' mean of 49 m. So on a section without growth it would be zero, and on a section with more growth it would be larger, and it responds to a change in any pick that feeds it.

That is the difference between an uncertainty you asserted and one you measured. The 11 m here is the second kind.

## What it is an uncertainty about

Be precise, because vague error bars cause more trouble than none.

The 11 m measures the sensitivity of the predicted TOP_B depth to a choice the interpreter had to make and had no data to settle, namely which marker to project from. By the identity, that sensitivity is exactly the amount by which Ekene-4's section above TOP_SAND departs from the wells the intervals were borrowed from. Both choices were legitimate. The section does not tell you which is right. So the answer is not a single depth, it is a depth that depends on a decision, and 11 m is how much the decision is worth.

That is a real and important component of the uncertainty, and it is not all of it. Three things it does not include.

It does not include pick uncertainty. The TOP_B picks in the three wells were read by an interpreter and could each be a metre or two out. That error rides on top of the 11 m rather than inside it.

It does not include sampling error. Three wells is a small sample, and their mean interval is not the field's mean interval. A fourth well carrying TOP_B could move both predictions.

It does not include the possibility of a surprise. A fault cutting section, a channel, a local truncation between the wells and Ekene-4 could put TOP_B somewhere neither method would reach. No method built from three wells can see that.

So 11 m is a floor rather than a full accounting, and the honest word in front of the range is about.

## How to report it

The report is not 1671 m. It is not 1682 m. It is the range, with the method attached.

Predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4 between about 1671 and 1682 m. Estimated by projecting three well mean intervals from Ekene-4's measured TOP_A of 1530 m and from its measured TOP_SAND of 1590 m, using Ekene-1, Ekene-2 and Ekene-3. The 11 m spread between the two estimates is the sensitivity of the result to the choice of projection marker and reflects growth in the A-to-SAND interval. No TOP_B pick exists in this well and the tops table entry stays blank.

Four elements do the work. The range rather than a point. The method, so a reader can reproduce it or object to it. The cause of the spread, so nobody mistakes it for noise or for a mistake. And the status, so the estimate never quietly becomes a measurement.

## What this buys the people downstream

An interpreter reading that report can act on it in ways a single number would not support.

Somebody planning a deepening of Ekene-4 knows to prepare for TOP_B anywhere in an 11 m window, and knows the window came from the section rather than from caution.

Somebody mapping TOP_B knows that Ekene-4 is not a control point in the sense the other three wells are, and can weight it accordingly or leave it out and see what changes.

Somebody who later drills through TOP_B in Ekene-4 can compare the result with the range and learn something about the method. A single number would only have taught them that the number was wrong by some amount.

And anybody reviewing the work can see the size of the assumption, which is the part that a bare depth hides completely.

## The claim, stated as strongly as it deserves

A prediction with a measured range is a better piece of work than a prediction without one, even though it looks less decisive. Precision that the data cannot support is not a virtue, and a number quoted to the metre in a well where the surface was never reached is claiming a precision nobody earned.

The spread is the honest part of this exercise. The next lesson deals with the temptation to throw it away.

## Exercise

Write the one paragraph report you would put in a well summary for Ekene-4's TOP_B, in your own words, containing all four elements listed above. Then write the single sentence you would say to a colleague who reads it and asks you to just give them the number.

Self-check: a good report states a predicted TOP_B between about 1671 and 1682 m, names the two projections and the three wells they came from, attributes the 11 m spread to growth in the A-to-SAND interval and to the choice of projection marker rather than to error, and records that no pick exists in Ekene-4 so the tops table entry remains blank. The word about should survive, since pick error, the three well sample and the chance of unseen structure all sit outside the 11 m. A good answer to the colleague explains that the range is the result rather than an evasion, that the two numbers came from two defensible methods on the same data, and that any single figure would be hiding a decision the data cannot settle. If they still need one number to work with, the last lesson in this module deals with that request properly.
