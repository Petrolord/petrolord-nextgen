# Which estimate to prefer

The range is the right answer and it will not always be accepted. Sometimes a single depth is genuinely required, by a piece of software that takes one value per well, by a drilling programme that needs a prognosis line, or by a decision that has to be made on a date. This lesson is about how to satisfy that requirement without undoing the work the last three lessons did.

The short version is that you may choose, you must say why, and the range goes with the number wherever it travels.

## The choice, and the grounds for it

If one number is required, report the from TOP_SAND estimate of 1682 m. Four grounds, all of them established earlier and all of them open to challenge.

The projection is shorter. It borrows 92 m of assumed interval against 141 m. The borrowed interval is the only part of the prediction that is assumed rather than measured in Ekene-4, and less of it is exposed to thickness variation.

The anchor is nearer the target. TOP_SAND sits closer to TOP_B than TOP_A does, so the stretch being assumed is a smaller and more local piece of section, and geological properties tend to vary more the further apart two points sit.

The inputs agree more closely. The three SAND-to-B intervals of 87, 92 and 97 m spread 10 m. The three A-to-B intervals of 133, 140 and 150 m spread 17 m. The wells are more consistent about the shorter interval, which is the only direct evidence available that it is stable enough to borrow.

And it starts below the thickened part of Ekene-4's section. This is the strongest ground. Ekene-4 carries 60 m between TOP_A and TOP_SAND, the thickest in the section against a 46 to 60 m range across the four wells. The layer-cake route borrows an average that assumes Ekene-4 behaves like wells averaging 49 m over that stretch, and it does not. The from TOP_SAND route does not have to assume anything about that stretch, because the anchor at 1590 m already records where the well actually found TOP_SAND.

## This is a judgement, and it should be labelled as one

State that plainly rather than letting the four grounds imply more than they carry.

Nobody drilled Ekene-4's TOP_B. There is no measurement for either estimate to be checked against, so neither has been shown to be correct and neither can be. The four grounds argue that one estimate rests on a smaller and better supported assumption than the other. That is a statement about the quality of the reasoning, not about the depth of a surface.

The grounds are also specific to this section. Three of them depend on numbers measured here: the two projection lengths, the two input spreads, and Ekene-4's anomalous upper interval. Change any of those and the argument changes with them. A nearer marker picked badly, or carried by only one other well, could easily support a worse prediction than a distant marker with clean control. Nothing in this lesson licenses a general rule that lower anchors win.

And a reasonable colleague can disagree. Somebody who weighted the layer-cake estimate more heavily, on the grounds that it uses the marker with the most reliable picks or the one that ties best to seismic, would be making an argument of the same kind. The way to settle that conversation is to compare grounds, which is possible only because both of you stated them.

## The range still travels

Preferring 1682 m does not narrow anything. The other method still says 1671 m, that estimate is still defensible, and the 11 m between them is still the measured sensitivity of the answer to a choice the data cannot settle.

So the reported form is a preferred value inside a range, never a value on its own.

Predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4, 1682 m, from projection of the three well mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m from the measured TOP_SAND at 1590 m. Preferred over the 1671 m layer-cake estimate because of the shorter projection, the nearer anchor, the tighter input spread and Ekene-4's anomalously thick A-to-SAND interval. Range about 1671 to 1682 m. Estimated, not picked, and the tops table entry stays blank.

Notice the shape of that range. The preferred value sits at the deep end of it, not in the middle, because the alternative estimate lies 11 m shallower rather than half that either side. Say so rather than tidying it into a symmetric plus or minus, since the asymmetry is a genuine feature of where the two methods land and quoting 1676.5 m plus or minus 5.5 m would reintroduce the midpoint this module refused.

## What would actually settle it

One thing. Deepening Ekene-4 until TOP_B is logged, at which point the surface has a pick, the estimates become a matter of record, and the well joins the three that carry all four tops.

Short of that, evidence could narrow the range without closing it. A seismic horizon tied to TOP_B in the three wells and carried across Ekene-4 would be independent of the well projections. Another well drilled between the existing ones would add a fourth interval to both means and show whether the growth continues in the direction the section suggests. Neither of those makes the prediction a pick.

Until then Ekene-4's TOP_B slot stays blank, the count of wells carrying all four tops stays at 3, and the best available statement about that surface is a preferred depth with a stated method and a stated range. That statement is a complete piece of work, and pretending it is more certain than it is would be the only way left to spoil it.

## Exercise

Your drilling engineer needs one prognosis depth for TOP_B in Ekene-4 for a deepening programme and will not take a range in the depth field. Write the entry you would give, then write the two sentences you would attach to it, and say which single new piece of information would most change your preference between the two estimates.

Self-check: the entry is 1682 m, marked as an estimate rather than a pick. The attached sentences should name the construction, that it projects a three well mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m from Ekene-4's measured TOP_SAND at 1590 m, and give the range of about 1671 to 1682 m together with the reason for preferring the deeper value, so the engineer knows the alternative estimate lies shallower rather than deeper and can plan the section accordingly. The single most influential new information would be a well or a tie showing that Ekene-4's thick A-to-SAND interval is not local to that well, since the fourth and strongest ground rests entirely on that anomaly, and evidence that the whole area thickens the same way would weaken the preference and pull the two estimates closer together.
