# Two methods one well

You have arrived at the position this tier was built to put you in. One well is missing one pick. Two methods have been applied to it. Both used the same three wells, both were carried out correctly, and they do not give the same answer.

This lesson holds that situation still and looks at it, without resolving it. The resolving is what the rest of the module does, and it goes somewhere less obvious than the instinct suggests.

## What is on the table

Ekene-4 reached total depth above TOP_B. It carries TOP_A at 1530 m, TOP_SAND at 1590 m and BASE_SAND at 1615 m, all measured, and no TOP_B. The three other wells all carry TOP_B: Ekene-1 at 1640 m, Ekene-2 at 1662 m and Ekene-3 at 1628 m.

The layer-cake method took the TOP_A to TOP_B intervals of 140, 150 and 133 m, averaged them to 141 m, and added that to Ekene-4's TOP_A. Predicted TOP_B 1671 m.

The from TOP_SAND method took the TOP_SAND to TOP_B intervals of 92, 97 and 87 m, averaged them to 92 m, and added that to Ekene-4's TOP_SAND. Predicted TOP_B 1682 m.

Two predicted depths for one surface in one well.

## What the two methods have in common

More than they differ by, which is what makes the disagreement interesting rather than trivial.

They use the same three wells. Neither method has access to evidence the other lacks. There is no data quality argument to be had, no well that one of them quietly dropped, no extra information sitting in one branch and not the other.

They use the same target. Both are predicting the depth of TOP_B in Ekene-4, the same surface in the same wellbore, in the same measured depth frame.

They use the same arithmetic. Take an interval that can be measured in the wells that carry both ends, average it across those wells, add it to the corresponding measured pick in the well being predicted. One recipe, applied twice.

And they are both defensible. Neither is a shortcut, a rule of thumb or a guess dressed up. If a reviewer asked either one to be justified from first principles, it could be, and the justification would be short.

## What they differ by

One thing. The marker they start from.

That single difference implies everything else about them. Starting from TOP_A means borrowing a 141 m interval and assuming Ekene-4 is average over all of it. Starting from TOP_SAND means borrowing a 92 m interval and assuming Ekene-4 is average only over the lower part, because the upper part of the section is already recorded in the 1590 m anchor.

The previous module argued that the second of those is generally the safer assumption on this section, on four grounds. That argument is still standing and it is not what this module is about. Preferring a method is a separate question from understanding why two good methods disagree, and taking them in that order is a mistake many people make.

## The instinct, and why to distrust it

Faced with 1671 m and 1682 m, most people reach for one of three moves without pausing.

Decide one method is wrong and discard its answer. This one feels rigorous and usually is not, because on this section neither method is wrong. They answer slightly different questions and both answers are valid answers to the question actually asked.

Average them and report the middle. This feels balanced and it destroys something, which is the subject of a later lesson in this module.

Recheck the arithmetic on the assumption that a mistake caused the gap. Worth doing once, and it will find nothing, because the arithmetic is exact. Three subtractions and a division in each branch, on integers, with one of the means dividing exactly and the other coming out at 141 m from 423 divided by 3.

All three moves share the same hidden premise, that a disagreement is a problem. Set that premise aside for the moment. A disagreement between two correct methods is information about the thing being measured, and the next lesson works out precisely what information this one carries.

## What the module will do with it

Four steps. Quantify the disagreement and trace exactly where it comes from. Argue that it is the uncertainty on the prediction rather than a flaw in the work. Refuse the average, with reasons. Then, if a single number is genuinely required, choose one and say honestly what choosing costs.

Notice what is not on that list. Nothing in this module makes the disagreement go away, and nothing in it produces a more precise answer than the two you already have. That is not a failure of the method. Ekene-4's TOP_B was never logged, and no amount of arithmetic on three other wells can make it as well known as a pick.

## Exercise

Write down the two predictions with everything a reader would need to reproduce or challenge them: the anchor depth, the borrowed interval, the wells the interval came from, and the status of the result. Then, before reading on, write one sentence predicting what you think the difference between the two numbers is caused by.

Self-check: the layer-cake prediction is Ekene-4's measured TOP_A of 1530 m plus the three well mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval of 141 m, from Ekene-1, Ekene-2 and Ekene-3, giving an estimated TOP_B of 1671 m. The from TOP_SAND prediction is Ekene-4's measured TOP_SAND of 1590 m plus the three well mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m from the same three wells, giving an estimated TOP_B of 1682 m. Both are estimates rather than picks, and Ekene-4's TOP_B slot in the tops table stays blank in either case.
