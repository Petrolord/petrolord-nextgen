# What would settle it

The prediction is a range of 1671 m to 1682 m and no amount of further arithmetic will narrow it. That is worth saying plainly, because the instinct after producing a range is to look for a cleverer method that gives a tighter one. There is no such method here. The 11 m came from working the same three wells two defensible ways, and a third construction on the same three wells would give a third number inside or near the same band rather than a reason to discard the band.

Only data settles it. Three things would count as data, and each of them adds something different.

## Deepening Ekene-4

Drilling Ekene-4 past its current total depth and logging through TOP_B settles the question completely. The predicted range stops being a prediction and becomes a pick. Ekene-4's TOP_B enters the tops table, the count of wells carrying all four tops goes from 3 to 4, the correlation line runs across the whole section, and the TOP_B relief is recomputed over four wells rather than three.

That last consequence is the one people forget. The relief of TOP_B is currently 34 m over three wells, from 1628 m in Ekene-3 to 1662 m in Ekene-2. Both estimates for Ekene-4 are deeper than 1662 m, so if the true pick lands anywhere in the predicted range the relief grows to somewhere between 43 m and 54 m over four wells. A drilled result does not only answer the question you asked. It revises the numbers you had already reported.

Deepening is also the only one of the three that is a decision rather than an observation. It costs rig time and it puts a hole below the current shoe, so the range you reported is part of the input to that decision. Saying the target lies between 1671 m and 1682 m gives the drilling engineer 11 m of section to plan for. Saying it lies at 1676 m gives them a number that is very likely to be wrong by several metres with nothing to warn them.

## A seismic tie

A seismic reflector tied to TOP_B in the three wells that carry it, then followed to the Ekene-4 location, gives an independent estimate that does not depend on any interval assumption. It is independent in the way that matters: the two predictions you already have both assume something about how the section behaves between markers, and they disagree because that assumption is doing work. Seismic measures a travel time to the surface itself at the well location.

What it adds is a third value that is not built from the same three intervals, so if it falls inside 1671 m to 1682 m it strengthens the range, and if it falls outside it tells you that the interval assumption behind both estimates is failing in Ekene-4. Either outcome is informative in a way that a third interval method would not be.

What it does not do is remove uncertainty. A seismic depth carries the uncertainty of the velocity model used to convert time to depth, and near a well that uncertainty can be several metres at this depth even after a good tie. So a seismic tie replaces one range with a different range rather than producing a pick. It is evidence and it is not a log.

## A nearby well

A new well between Ekene-4 and the others that penetrates TOP_B adds a fourth carrier to the intervals. The mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval would then be a four-well mean rather than the current three-well mean of 141 m, and the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B likewise rather than 92 m. Both predictions for Ekene-4 would move, and the spread between them would move with them.

Whether the spread narrows depends entirely on where the new well sits. A well whose intervals resemble the existing three tightens the means and probably tightens the spread a little. A well with intervals unlike the others widens it, and that is a real result rather than a disappointment. It says the section grows more than three wells could show, and the honest response is to report a wider range than you did before.

The value of a nearby well is highest when it sits close to Ekene-4, because the intervals you are projecting are being carried across a distance and a shorter carry is a better one. A well on the far side of the section adds a data point and very little relevance.

## Until then the range stands

Be honest about the position you are in. You have three wells, an interval that varies, and a target well that reached total depth above the surface of interest. The best available answer is a range of 1671 m to 1682 m with the method, the anchor markers, the well count and the 11 m spread stated. That is not a placeholder waiting for better arithmetic and it does not improve by being written more confidently.

There is a professional temptation here that is worth naming. A range invites the question "so what number do I use", and the pressure to supply one is real. You can supply one. Lead with the from-TOP_SAND estimate at 1682 m, because it is the shorter projection at 92 m against 141 m and it starts below the part of the section that thickens most in Ekene-4. What you cannot do is drop the range when you supply the preferred value, because the range is the only honest part of the answer.

## Exercise

For each of the three settling options, say what it would change about the six numbers this tier produces, and rank the three by how much they would reduce the uncertainty on Ekene-4's TOP_B. Then answer in one sentence: if the seismic tie came back at 1690 m, what would you conclude.

As a self check: deepening Ekene-4 replaces the prediction with a pick, moves the well count with all four tops from 3 to 4, and revises the TOP_B relief upward from 34 m over three wells; a seismic tie adds an independent depth estimate at the Ekene-4 location carrying its own velocity uncertainty and changes none of the six numbers, since none of them is computed from seismic; and a nearby well that penetrates TOP_B changes both mean intervals from three-well means of 141 m and 92 m to four-well means, which moves both predictions and the 11 m spread with them. Deepening reduces the uncertainty most because it removes it, the nearby well is next if it sits close to Ekene-4, and the seismic tie is third because it substitutes one range for another. A seismic tie at 1690 m falls outside the predicted range of 1671 m to 1682 m, which says the interval assumption behind both estimates is failing in Ekene-4 and the reported range should be widened rather than defended.
