# Relief is not the error bar

Two numbers now sit side by side on the panel and they are both measured in metres, both attached to TOP_B, and both produced from the same three wells. One of them is 34 m and the other is 11 m. They are different quantities with different meanings, and putting the wrong one on a prediction is the classic mistake at this tier. It is worth a whole lesson because it is not a careless slip. It is a reasonable-sounding move made by people who understand both numbers separately.

## The two quantities

**Relief of 34 m.** TOP_B is picked at 1640 m in Ekene-1, 1662 m in Ekene-2 and 1628 m in Ekene-3. The deepest minus the shallowest is 1662 minus 1628, which is 34 m. This is how much the TOP_B surface itself moves structurally across the section. It is a property of the geology in three wells that were drilled. It exists whether or not anybody ever predicts anything, and it would still be 34 m if Ekene-4 had never been spudded.

**Spread of 11 m.** Projecting the mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval of 141 m down from Ekene-4's TOP_A at 1530 m gives 1671 m. Projecting the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m down from Ekene-4's TOP_SAND at 1590 m gives 1682 m. The difference is 1682 minus 1671, which is 11 m. This is how much two defensible methods disagree about one depth in one well. It is a property of the estimate rather than of the rock, and it exists only because a prediction was attempted.

Read those two paragraphs again and notice that they do not describe the same thing in different words. One counts surfaces across wells. The other counts methods on a single well.

## Why the swap happens

The 34 m arrives first and it looks like an uncertainty. It is a range in metres, it is attached to the surface being predicted, and it feels appropriately cautious, since a larger range seems like the safer error. That instinct is what makes the mistake durable.

There is also a half-argument behind it that sounds respectable. TOP_B moves around by 34 m across this section, so surely the missing TOP_B could be anywhere in that band. The trouble with that argument is that it discards every piece of information the prediction used. You are not guessing where TOP_B might be somewhere on the section. You are estimating where it is beneath one specific well whose TOP_A and TOP_SAND you have measured, using intervals from three wells that carry both. The prediction is anchored. Relief is not.

The prediction is also not confined to the relief band, which is the fastest way to see that the swap is wrong. Both estimates, 1671 m and 1682 m, are deeper than the deepest measured TOP_B on the section at 1662 m. A 34 m error bar centred anywhere in the relief range would not even contain the answers the method produced. The two numbers do not describe overlapping things.

## What each one is for

Quote the relief when somebody asks about the structure of TOP_B. How much does the surface rise and fall across the wells that have it. The answer is 34 m over three wells, and it goes to the person mapping the surface or judging closure.

Quote the spread when somebody asks how confident you are in the Ekene-4 prediction. The answer is that two methods on the same three wells land 11 m apart, so the predicted TOP_B lies between about 1671 m and 1682 m. That goes to the person deciding whether to deepen a well or where to set a casing point.

Neither number can be substituted for the other and neither is a conservative version of the other. Handing 34 m to the drilling engineer as the uncertainty on Ekene-4's TOP_B is wrong in a specific way: it is triple the measured disagreement, it is not centred on either estimate, and it was computed from a question nobody asked.

## The spread has a cause the relief does not share

The 11 m exists because the section grows. The Professional tier measured that growth directly, with the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval running 48, 53, 46 and 60 m across the four wells for a range of 14 m. Surfaces that are not parallel give different answers depending on which one you anchor to, and the size of the disagreement is a readout of how far from parallel they are. If every interval in the section were constant, the two estimates would agree exactly and the spread would be zero. The relief would still be 34 m, because a layer-cake section can sit at any tilt.

Module 4 gave that disagreement an exact meaning, and it settles the argument here. The three carriers' A-to-SAND intervals are 48, 53 and 46 m, so their mean is 147 divided by 3, which is 49 m, and that same 49 m is 141 minus 92, the difference of the two graded means. Ekene-4's own A-to-SAND is 60 m, and 60 minus 49 is 11 m exactly. The spread is not a rough consequence of growth. It measures how far the target well departs from the wells its intervals came from. Relief carries no such meaning, because it answers a different question rather than offering a rival estimate of the same one.

That is the cleanest test of whether the two ideas have separated in your head. Imagine a perfect layer cake with the same structural dip. Relief stays. Spread goes to zero. They are independent.

## Exercise

A colleague sends you a note reading: predicted TOP_B in Ekene-4 is 1676 m plus or minus 34 m. Identify every problem with that sentence, then rewrite it correctly. Say in one sentence what the 34 m actually is and who should receive it.

As a self check: three things are wrong. The 34 m is the structural relief of TOP_B across the three wells that carry it and not the uncertainty on any prediction, so the error bar is the wrong quantity and it is roughly three times the measured disagreement. The 1676 m is the average of the two estimates, which discards the information that the two methods disagreed and lands on a depth neither method produced. And the sentence does not say which method, which anchor marker or which wells were used. A correct version reads that Ekene-4's TOP_B is predicted to lie between 1671 m and 1682 m, from the mean TOP_A to TOP_B interval of 141 m projected from TOP_A at 1530 m and the mean TOP_SAND to TOP_B interval of 92 m projected from TOP_SAND at 1590 m, both taken from the three wells that carry TOP_B, with a spread of 11 m between the two estimates and the value reported as a prediction rather than a pick. The 34 m is the structural relief of TOP_B and it belongs to whoever is mapping that surface or assessing its closure.
