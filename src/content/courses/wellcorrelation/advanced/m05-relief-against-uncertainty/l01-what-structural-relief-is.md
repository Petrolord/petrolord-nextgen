# What structural relief is

Relief is the simplest number in correlation and one of the easiest to misuse. It is the raw spread of one surface across the wells that carry it: the deepest measured pick of that surface minus the shallowest measured pick of that surface. One surface, one pick per well, one subtraction. Nothing is averaged, nothing is projected, and nothing is assumed.

You met relief at the Associate tier as a way of reading structure off a section, and you met it again at the Professional tier as the contrast against growth. It returns here for a third and final reason. At this tier you are producing a predicted depth with a range attached, and relief is the number most likely to be picked up by mistake and quoted as that range. Before you can keep the two apart, the definition has to be exact.

## The four Ekene surfaces

The teaching section has four wells and four named surfaces, and every relief in it is a subtraction you can do in your head.

| surface | shallowest | deepest | relief | wells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOP_A | 1495 m in Ekene-3 | 1530 m in Ekene-4 | 35 m | 4 |
| TOP_SAND | 1541 m in Ekene-3 | 1590 m in Ekene-4 | 49 m | 4 |
| BASE_SAND | 1570 m in Ekene-3 | 1615 m in Ekene-4 | 45 m | 4 |
| TOP_B | 1628 m in Ekene-3 | 1662 m in Ekene-2 | 34 m | 3 |

The first three come straight from the Professional tier and they are unchanged, because relief is defined on measured depths and nothing this tier does touches a measured depth. TOP_A has 35 m of relief, TOP_SAND has 49 m and BASE_SAND has 45 m.

## TOP_B is the one that matters here

TOP_B relief across the three wells that carry it is 1662 minus 1628, which is 34 m. That is the graded relief in the Expert capstone, and two features of it deserve stating out loud every time you write it down.

The first is the denominator. Ekene-4 reached total depth above TOP_B, so the surface is picked in three wells and not four. Relief is defined over the wells that carry the surface, so the honest way to write this number is 34 m over three wells. Write it bare and a reader will assume four, which is the assumption the whole tier is built to prevent.

The second is that the deep end belongs to Ekene-2. For TOP_A, TOP_SAND and BASE_SAND the deepest pick is in Ekene-4, which makes it tempting to treat Ekene-4 as the structurally low well and read the section that way. TOP_B cannot support that reading, because Ekene-4 does not have a TOP_B to be deep. Its deepest measured TOP_B is Ekene-2 at 1662 m. So the 34 m describes the structure of three wells, and it is silent about the fourth by construction.

That silence is the point. If TOP_B had been drilled in Ekene-4, the relief of the surface would almost certainly be a different number. The predicted depths this tier produces, 1671 m and 1682 m, are both deeper than 1662 m, so an Ekene-4 TOP_B at either value would push the relief well past 34 m. You may notice that and you may not report it, because a predicted depth is not a pick and it does not enter a relief calculation. Relief counts drilled surfaces only.

## What relief tells you

Relief answers a structural question and only that question. How much does this surface rise and fall across the wells I have. A relief of 34 m on TOP_B says the top of the B interval sits over a 34 m range of measured depth in three penetrations, so the surface has real structural expression across the section rather than lying flat.

That reading feeds directly into the work above this course. A mapper contouring TOP_B needs to know the range the control points span before deciding on a contour interval. An evaluator judging whether a structure has closure needs the relief of the mapped surface against the depth of a contact. Relief is the number those readers want, and it is exactly what they will get if you give them the picks.

## What relief does not tell you

Relief says nothing about thickness. It is defined on one surface, so it cannot see how much section accumulated between two surfaces. That is growth, and the Professional tier measured it: the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval reads 48, 53, 46 and 60 m across the four wells, for a growth range of 14 m. The relief of TOP_SAND is 49 m and the growth of the interval above it is 14 m. Both are correct, they describe the same four wells, and they answer different questions.

Relief also says nothing about how well any depth is known. It is a spread across wells, not an error on a value. Every one of the depths behind a relief is a pick somebody made on a log in a well that exists. The number carries no uncertainty statement of its own and it cannot be turned into one for something else. That is the subject of the next lesson, and it is the mistake this tier grades hardest.

The habit that keeps relief honest is to count the surfaces in the question before answering. One surface named means relief, measured depths, and a well count quoted with it. Two surfaces named means an interval, and variation in it is growth.
