# Onward to Professional

This course taught you to read a section. That is a real skill and it is where everyone starts, but it is the first of three things a correlator does with the same four wells. The tiers above take the identical Ekene section and ask harder questions of it. Knowing what those questions are is useful now, because it tells you what your beginner skills are foundations for.

## Professional: measuring growth

The Professional tier changes the datum and then measures rather than describes.

It flattens on TOP_A at a 1450 m datum. The shifts follow the same rule you already know, datum minus that well's TOP_A depth, and come out at -50, -62, -45 and -80 m across Ekene-1 to Ekene-4. Same arithmetic, different anchor, different question.

The question is what happens in the interval between TOP_A and TOP_SAND. Read across the section and that interval measures 48, 53, 46 and 60 m. Those four numbers are the whole point of the exercise. They are not four samples of one thickness with a bit of noise on them; they have a spread of 14 m, and the interval is systematically thickest at Ekene-4, the well that sat deepest on TOP_SAND. An interval that thickens toward the low side of a structure is the classic signature of growth: the section was accumulating while the structure was forming, so more sediment fell into the part that was subsiding faster.

That is the jump from beginner to Professional. At this tier you would have said the interval varies. At that tier the 14 m spread becomes a measurement of a syn-depositional process, with consequences for where the reservoir is thickest and why.

The Professional tier also asks two housekeeping questions with real teeth. How many wells carry all four tops: three, because Ekene-4 stops before TOP_B, and any analysis wanting the complete column is a three-well analysis. And what the displayed span of the flattened section is: 150 m from the shallowest drawn top to the deepest. The span is a sanity read on the view itself, confirming the panel is showing the whole column and not clipping it.

## Expert: predicting what was never drilled

The Expert tier goes after the gap. Ekene-4 has no TOP_B, and the Expert question is where TOP_B would have been if the well had reached it.

It is answered two independent ways. The layer-cake estimate works down from TOP_A: measure the TOP_A to TOP_B separation in the three wells that have both, average it, and hang that average below Ekene-4's TOP_A. The interval estimate works down from TOP_SAND instead, using the sand-to-TOP_B separation from the same three wells applied to Ekene-4's own sand pick.

Two methods, two answers, and they do not agree. The beginner instinct is to treat the disagreement as an error and split the difference. The Expert tier does the opposite. The spread between the two estimates is the uncertainty on the prediction, and it is the most honest number in the exercise. The methods disagree because they lean on different reference surfaces, and in a section with growth in it those surfaces are not parallel. Averaging the two estimates throws away the one thing that told you how much to trust either.

That principle travels well beyond this section. A single prediction with no spread around it is not more accurate than two that disagree; it is just less informative about its own reliability.

## The progression

Three sentences hold the ladder:

The beginner reads a section. The professional measures growth in it. The expert predicts what a well did not reach.

Each tier uses the same four wells, the same tops table and the same engine. What changes is the question, and every question at every tier still rests on the two pieces of arithmetic from this course. Shift equals datum minus datum-top depth. Displayed equals true plus shift. The growth analysis is those two rules applied on a different datum. The prediction is those two rules plus an assumption about what the missing surface would have done.

## Where this course sits

Well Correlation is one rung of a longer ladder. Beneath it sits Well Data Manager, which is where the wells, the deviation and the tops table come from in the first place. Everything you correlated here arrived through that door, and a tops table that was never quality-controlled at that level cannot be rescued at this one.

Above it sit mapping and volumetrics. Your correlated tops become the control points a mapper contours into surfaces, and the zone thicknesses you read become the gross rock volume that a volumetrics run turns into a resource number. That is why the reporting discipline from lesson one of this module matters so much. A displayed depth handed upward as a true depth does not stay a display error. It becomes a wrong contour, and then a wrong volume.

## Exercise

Compare the two datums you have now seen on the Ekene section: TOP_SAND at 1500 m and TOP_A at 1450 m. Which readings change between them, and which do not?

Self-check: the shifts change, from -48, -65, -41 and -90 to -50, -62, -45 and -80, and therefore every displayed depth changes with them. What does not change is any thickness. The gross sand is 32, 36, 29 and 25 m in both views, and the TOP_A to TOP_SAND interval is 48, 53, 46 and 60 m in both. Thicknesses belong to the wells; displayed depths belong to the view.
