# Reading structure across wells

Relief compresses a surface into a single number. Reading structure does the opposite: it goes well by well, in order, and asks what shape the picks describe. This is where a correlation section stops being a table and starts being a geological argument.

## Rank the surface first

The first move on any structural section is to rank a well-covered surface from shallowest to deepest. TOP_SAND is the natural choice for Ekene, because it is the reservoir top and it is present in all four wells.

Ranked shallowest to deepest, TOP_SAND sits at 1541 m in Ekene-3, 1548 m in Ekene-1, 1565 m in Ekene-2 and 1590 m in Ekene-4. The steps between consecutive wells in that ranking are 7 m from Ekene-3 to Ekene-1, then 17 m from Ekene-1 to Ekene-2, then 25 m from Ekene-2 to Ekene-4. Those three steps sum to 49 m, which is the TOP_SAND relief from the previous lesson, and that agreement is a useful arithmetic check on the ranking.

## The shape this describes

Read in well-number order, the TOP_SAND picks run 1548, 1565, 1541, 1590. That sequence goes down, then up, then down again, so the section is plainly not a simple one-way dip. Numbering wells is a drilling accident, not a structural statement, and a panel drawn in well-number order will look ragged even when the structure underneath is perfectly orderly.

The structural reading is cleaner. Ekene-3 is the high point of the field on this surface, sitting 7 m shallower than Ekene-1. So the section shallows gently from Ekene-1 across to Ekene-3, reaching a crest there, and then falls away strongly into Ekene-4, which lies 49 m below the crest and 42 m below Ekene-1. The asymmetry is the point: a gentle 7 m rise on one side and a 49 m drop on the other. This is a flank steepening away from a structural high, not a uniformly tilted slab.

Ekene-2 sits between Ekene-1 and Ekene-4 in the ranking at 1565 m, 24 m below the crest, which places it partway down the same falling flank.

## Every surface agrees

A single surface can rank any way at all through pick error alone. What makes a structural picture credible is that the other surfaces rank the same way.

Check TOP_A. Ranked shallowest to deepest it reads 1495 m in Ekene-3, 1500 m in Ekene-1, 1512 m in Ekene-2 and 1530 m in Ekene-4. That is the same well sequence as TOP_SAND: Ekene-3, Ekene-1, Ekene-2, Ekene-4.

BASE_SAND does it again at 1570, 1580, 1601 and 1615 m for the same four wells in the same order. TOP_B does it a third time across the three wells that carry it, at 1628 m in Ekene-3, 1640 m in Ekene-1 and 1662 m in Ekene-2, with Ekene-4 absent.

Four surfaces, four identical orderings, spanning 167 m of section. That is what a consistent structural picture looks like, and it is the strongest quality-control signal a beginner-level section offers. Each surface was picked independently on log character, yet they all place Ekene-3 highest and Ekene-4 lowest. Independent picks agreeing on the same shape is evidence that the picks are right and that the structure is real.

The inverse is equally useful. If TOP_SAND had ranked Ekene-3 highest while BASE_SAND ranked Ekene-2 highest, one of those two picks would be wrong, or a fault would sit between the wells, or the two surfaces would not be the same geological objects you thought they were. Disagreement between surfaces is a flag to go back to the logs, not a result to report.

Notice also that the steps grow with depth on the falling flank. From the crest at Ekene-3 to Ekene-4, TOP_A drops 35 m, TOP_SAND drops 49 m and BASE_SAND drops 45 m. Deeper surfaces have generally taken more relief, which is the growth signature the previous lesson identified.

## What the horizontal axis is not

One warning before you draw conclusions about dip. The horizontal axis of the correlation panel is well order, not distance. Wells are spaced evenly across the panel regardless of how far apart they actually sit on the map, and their left-to-right position reflects the order they were placed in the section, not their geographic positions.

That means the section shows the sequence of the structure and not its true gradient. You can say with confidence that Ekene-3 is structurally highest, that Ekene-4 is lowest, and that the surface falls by 49 m between them. You cannot read a dip angle off the panel, and you cannot say the flank into Ekene-4 is steeper than the rise into Ekene-3 unless you also know the map distances involved. If Ekene-4 happens to be ten kilometres away and Ekene-3 only five hundred metres, the apparent steepness on the panel is an artefact of even spacing.

Converting sequence into true dip needs well coordinates, which is a mapping task rather than a correlation one. Report ranking and relief off the section, and take gradient from a map.
