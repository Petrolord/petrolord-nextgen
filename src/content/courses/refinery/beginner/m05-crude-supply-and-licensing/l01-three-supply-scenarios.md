# Three supply scenarios

A small refinery lives or dies on whether crude arrives. The screen does not model a supply chain. It offers three named futures for crude supply, and each one sets two inputs at once.

{{panel:refinery-screen-explorer}}

## The scenarios as the engine exports them

| id | name | utilisation | crude premium ($/bbl) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| firm | Firm supply | 0.9200 | 0.0000 |
| tight | Tight supply | 0.7500 | 3.0000 |
| disrupted | Disrupted supply | 0.5000 | 6.0000 |

Each carries a note in the engine's words:

- Firm: "A term contract that is honoured, at the market price."
- Tight: "Cargoes are available but contested: the plant runs below nameplate and pays up for barrels."
- Disrupted: "Interruptions are routine. Half the nameplate and a hard premium, which is where most of these projects are actually decided."

## Two inputs in one choice

Every scenario sets a utilisation and a crude premium together. That pairing is the point. When crude is scarce, a plant suffers in two ways: it runs fewer barrels, because cargoes do not arrive, and it pays more for the barrels it does get, because it is competing for them. A screen that let you move only one of the two would understate what scarcity does.

The utilisation replaces the plant's own, whatever utilisation was typed for the plant. The premium is added to the crude cost before the streams are built. So picking tight supply for OKORDIA changes the utilisation to 0.7500 and the crude cost from 76.0000 to 79.0000 in one move.

## Named futures carry no probability

The scenarios are named futures and carry no probability. The engine does not say firm supply is likely, or disrupted supply rare. It says what each would mean for the plant, and leaves the judgement of which to plan on to the people who know the crude source, its contracts and its route to the plant.

That is deliberate. A probability attached to a supply scenario would invite a probability-weighted answer, and that averaged answer would describe a plant that never exists: one running part-firm and part-disrupted every year. The screen keeps the three apart so a reader sees each outcome whole. Probability-weighted valuation belongs to the Economics courses.

## Reading the disrupted note

The disrupted note makes a claim worth taking seriously: that this is where most of these projects are actually decided. A project that only works under firm supply depends on the one scenario it cannot control. Reading all three is how the screen shows whether a plant survives its supply risk or merely hopes it away. The next lesson reads OKORDIA under each.

## The mistake

Picking the firm scenario because it is the first on the list, or because it is the base case in a sponsor's presentation. The scenarios are a set. A screen reported under firm supply alone has left out the two futures in which crude is hard to get, and those are the futures a lender or a partner will ask about first. Report all three, side by side, every time.
