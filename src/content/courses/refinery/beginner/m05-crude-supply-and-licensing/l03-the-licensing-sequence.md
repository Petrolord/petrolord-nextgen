# The licensing sequence

A refinery project needs permission as well as money. The Studio carries a licensing tracker with three stages in a fixed order. It is a process aid: it tracks where a project has got to, and the regulator's current requirements govern.

{{panel:refinery-screen-explorer}}

## The three stages

LICENSING_STAGES, in order:

| stage | id | name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | lte | Licence to Establish |
| 2 | ltc | Licence to Construct |
| 3 | lto | Licence to Operate |

Each stage carries the typical evidence the tracker lists for it.

Licence to Establish: feasibility study; site title or lease; evidence of funding; environmental impact assessment scoping.

Licence to Construct: detailed engineering design; approved environmental impact assessment; construction HSE plan; financing close.

Licence to Operate: mechanical completion and commissioning records; pre-startup safety review; operating and emergency procedures; product quality assurance.

## How the evidence builds

Read the evidence lists in order and they trace the project's life. The first stage asks whether the project is real: a study, a site, money in prospect, and the scope of the environmental work. The second asks whether it can be built: the design finished, the environmental assessment approved, a safety plan for construction and the financing closed. The third asks whether it can run safely: the plant built and commissioned, reviewed before startup, with procedures written and product quality assured.

The feasibility study sits in the first stage's evidence. That is where this tier's screen belongs. The margin per barrel, the capital, the throughput and the supply scenarios are the kind of figures a feasibility study carries into a Licence to Establish application.

## A process aid

The tracker records which stages are complete and names the next one. It does not know the regulator's forms, fees, timelines or the evidence a particular application needs this year. The typical evidence it lists is typical. The regulator's current requirements govern, and a project team reads them directly.

That limit is stated by the app itself, and it is the right one. A tracker that claimed to know the current rules would be out of date the first time they changed, and nobody would notice until an application was returned.

## Why sequence matters to the screen

The stages line up with the screen's own years. Establishing comes before capital is committed. Construction is when the capex rows of the streams are spent. Operation is when the producing years begin. A screen whose construction years begin before the Licence to Construct is in hand is describing a project that cannot legally be built on that timetable.

## The mistake

Reading the tracker as a compliance record. It records what the team has ticked. Whether the regulator agrees is a separate question, answered by the regulator.
