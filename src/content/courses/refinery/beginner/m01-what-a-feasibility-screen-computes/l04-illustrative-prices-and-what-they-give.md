# Illustrative prices and what they give

Every price in this course is invented. The plants are invented, the yields are invented, and the grade names that appear in later tiers are labels on invented numbers. That is deliberate, and it changes how you should read every figure the screen prints.

## OKORDIA's price table

OKORDIA carries one illustrative price for each product, in US dollars a barrel:

| product | price |
| --- | --- |
| lpg | 52.0000 |
| gasoline | 104.0000 |
| naphtha | 74.0000 |
| kerosene | 97.0000 |
| diesel | 101.0000 |
| fuelOil | 55.0000 |

Its crude costs 76.0000 a barrel under firm supply. None of these is a market quotation for any date, and none should be quoted as one. They are chosen so the arithmetic of the screen is easy to follow and the three configurations give visibly different answers.

## What a price gives

A price on its own is not a value. The screen multiplies each product's yield by its price, per barrel of crude, and adds the results. For hydroskimming that gives a gross value per barrel of crude of 83.7900; for topping, 76.9400; for conversion, 90.6300. Those three figures are what the price table is for. They are the top line of the margin, and they are what the crude cost and the variable operating cost are taken away from.

So when you change a price on the panel, watch the gross value, and then watch the gross margin per barrel. A price is an input. The gross value is what the input gives.

## Why invented numbers teach better

A real price table is out of date the day it is printed and argued over the day after. An invented table cannot be argued over, so the argument moves where it belongs: to the method. When you have learned how a slate becomes a value, you can type your own prices into the same boxes and the method carries across unchanged.

The same rule governs names. A crude grade name in this course is a label. It tells you which row of invented yields you are looking at. It says nothing about what the real crude of that name yields or costs.

## What to do with your own prices

The screen is built to take your numbers. When you use it for a real project, every price box needs a source and a date, and the screen will not supply either. A blank price box is not refused; the product is named as unpriced and adds nothing, which the third module shows. That makes a forgotten price quiet, so check the unpriced list before you read the gross value.
