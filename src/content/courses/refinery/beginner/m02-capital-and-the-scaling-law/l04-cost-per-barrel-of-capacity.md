# Cost per barrel of capacity

A total capital figure is hard to compare across plant sizes. Divided by capacity, it becomes a cost per barrel a day of capacity, and that is the figure people quote when they compare refineries of different sizes.

## The per bpd columns

scaleComparison prints a per bpd figure beside each cost:

| 5000 | 12800.00 | 12800.00 |
| 10000 | 11942.82 | 9700.59 |
| 20000 | 11143.05 | 7351.67 |
| 30000 | 10700.27 | 6251.00 |

OKORDIA's own screen, at the reference size, prints a capital of 64000000.00 and a capital per bpd of 12800.00, which is the 5000 bpd row.

## Where the economy of scale shows

A total cost column mixes two things in every row: the size of the plant and the economy of scale. The economy of scale is hard to see there. It becomes visible per bpd. Read each per bpd column from the smallest plant to the largest and you see how each law spreads its cost over more capacity.

Now read the exponent of 1 case. Scaled in proportion to capacity to 10000 bpd, the quotation gives 128000000.00, and the per bpd figure is 12800.00: the same per bpd figure the reference row prints. That is what an exponent of 1 means. With no economy of scale, every barrel a day of capacity costs what it cost at the reference size, whatever the size.

Against that benchmark, both laws print per bpd figures that change with capacity. The stick-built law, with its lower exponent, carries the stronger economy of scale, and its per bpd figure at 30000 bpd is 6251.00. The modular law at the same size prints 10700.27.

## Why per bpd is the screening currency

Per bpd figures let a screen be checked against the outside world without a full estimate. If a published or quoted cost per barrel a day for a similar plant sits far from the screen's figure at the same size, one of the two is using a reference point or an exponent the other would not accept. That is a prompt to look at the inputs, and it takes one division to set up.

Per bpd figures also make the crossover easy to see: at 5000 bpd both columns print 12800.00, and on either side of it they part.

## What per bpd leaves out

A cost per barrel of capacity is a cost per barrel the plant could run on a day it ran at nameplate. It is not a cost per barrel refined. The plant in this tier runs at a utilisation below 1 for a set number of on-stream days, which the fourth module turns into an annual throughput. A capital figure spread over that throughput tells a different story from one spread over nameplate. Keep the two ideas apart: capacity is what the plant was built for, and throughput is what it does.

## The mistake

Comparing a per bpd figure from one law with a per bpd figure from the other and calling the gap an economy of scale. At 10000 bpd the modular law prints 11942.82 and the stick-built law 9700.59. Both come from one quotation. The gap between them is the gap between two exponents, and that is an assumption.
