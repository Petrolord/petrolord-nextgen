# Where this course hands over

This course has one sentence at its centre. A refinery is judged on its margin per barrel of crude: the screen prices that barrel before any capital is spent, the plan finds it with every barrel run through the crude unit, the schedule dates it, and the actuals are read against it line by line on what each gap did to margin. This last lesson says where the course stops, and which course picks up each thread it leaves.

## What this course owns

Across three tiers the course has taught four readings of one barrel.

The Associate tier read the feasibility screen: capital from a reference point by a scaling law, the product slate and its value per barrel of crude, throughput and the gross margin per barrel, supply scenarios and the licensing sequence.

The Professional tier read the plan and the schedule: a configuration of crudes, units and products, the crude unit carrying every barrel, the plan's margin, each stream's marginal value and the schedule cascaded from a fixed period start.

This tier read the actuals and the investment case: one event shape in three ledgers, the volume, price and unexplained split, every line signed on margin, the units against plan, and an expansion valued through the screening engine with its construction-year tax loss carried forward.

## Where the linear program lives

The plan in this course is solved as a linear program, and the stream values are read from it. The `crude` course, Crude Assay & Blending, owns linear programming: what an LP is, what makes a constraint binding, what a shadow price means and what an infeasible answer says. It also owns every output of the crude assay and product blending engines. A learner who wants to know why a stream is worth what the plan says should take that question there.

## Where measurement and logistics live

The `supply` course, Terminals, Depots & Fuel Supply, owns what happens to a barrel once it is measured and moved: strapping, the volume correction factor, stock reconciliation, the Erlang C queue at a loading bay, landed cost and pump price. This course reads movements as quantities and values in a ledger. How a quantity is measured in a tank, and how a tank's stock is reconciled, belong to that course.

## Where the investment decision lives

The Economics courses own NPV, IRR, Monte Carlo and decision trees. This course read the feasibility NPV the screening engine returns as the screen's answer and graded neither it nor the IRR. The question of how to weigh a project under uncertainty, how to choose a discount rate and how to compare one investment against others is theirs. The Economics module also owns a fuller capital allowance model, the question held item H2 leaves open.

## What to carry forward

Three habits from this tier travel on. Sign a figure by what it does to the result before adding it to another. Keep money that moved with no barrels, and movements with no plan, where a reader can see them. And read a held item as a limit of the tool, stated plainly, never as a result.

## Exercise

Name the course that owns each of the following, and say in one sentence why it sits there: the shadow price of a binding constraint, the stock reconciliation of a tank, the IRR a project returns, and a capital allowance schedule starting at commissioning. Then restate the course's one sentence and name the tier that read each of its four steps.
