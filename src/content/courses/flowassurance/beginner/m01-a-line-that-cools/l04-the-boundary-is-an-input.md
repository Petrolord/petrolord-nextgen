# The boundary is an input

Every verdict this course produces is a comparison against a number that came out of a laboratory, and no part of either engine computed it.

{{panel:pd-thermal-explorer}}

## Both headers say so

`flowlineThermal` states that hydrate and wax boundaries "are fluid properties, they come from a lab or a compositional flash, and the consumer supplies them". `hydrateInhibition` states that it "does NOT compute where the hydrate boundary is in the first place".

That is not a limitation being confessed. It is a division of labour. Where hydrates form depends on gas composition, water chemistry and pressure, and a heat leak calculator has none of those and should not pretend to. What it can do is tell you what temperature arrives, to as many figures as you like, and then hand the comparison back to you.

## One line can have two boundaries

Take TEACHING LINE AKASO SPUR, a construct built for this course rather than a published case: a 9.562 in bore buried line, 195.00 degF inlet against a 45.00 degF seabed, 60000.0 ft long. Its hydrate boundary is 71.00 degF while it flows and 78.00 degF once the line packs up after a shutdown.

Both numbers describe the same fluid. They differ because the pressure differs, and neither of them was computed by anything in this module. They arrived as inputs, and the shutdown one is the harder of the two to meet on a line that has already stopped moving and is losing what heat it has left to the same seabed.

## What that does to a margin

With heat loss only, that teaching line arrives at 89.316029952695 degF, which is 18.3160299527 degF outside the 71.00 degF flowing boundary. Against the 78.00 degF packed boundary the same arrival clears by less, on identical thermal arithmetic.

Nothing about the pipe moved between those two readings. The verdict moved because the finish line moved, and the finish line is an input.

## The mistake

Quoting a margin without quoting the boundary it was measured from. A margin of 18.3160299527 degF sounds like a result. It is a subtraction, and the number subtracted is a laboratory value with its own uncertainty, its own pressure and its own water chemistry attached to it.

If the boundary turns out to be wrong by more than the margin, everything computed downstream of it was arithmetically perfect and operationally useless.
