# Onward

A stack of resistances is a statement about a pipe. It is not yet a statement about a line, because nothing in it knows how fast the fluid is moving or how long it has been moving.

## What the stack earns

A U from a drawing, with every share readable and the arithmetic closing: on the published pipe 105.9799311355 bare, 1.3348791131 insulated and 0.7132000377 Btu/(hr ft2 degF) buried at 4.0 ft, all referred to the 6.065 in bore, with shares that sum to 100.00000000 percent.

It also earns a habit. A U is 0.713200037662 at one reference and 0.501513997498 at another on identical physics, with a total resistance of 0.883057962117 unchanged between them, so the diameter is part of the answer and travels with it.

## Where it hands over

Rate, time and temperature. A coefficient says how hard heat leaves per square foot per degF. It does not say what temperature arrives at the far end, how far along the line the fluid gives up most of its excess, or how many hours a shut in line has before it is cold. Those need a mass rate, a heat capacity, a length and a clock, and the whole of that is the next tier.

Past it lies the boundary itself and the chemistry that moves it, which is where a margin in degF becomes a decision about injecting something and a rate in bbl/d to inject it against.

## The limit worth carrying

`ok: true` is a statement about the call, not about the answer. A cooldown handed one good mass and one NaN drops the bad term, returns `ok = true` with no note, and is short by 72.534444 percent. An overall coefficient handed to a consumer with the wrong reference diameter is dimensionally consistent and raises no complaint anywhere.

Neither of those is caught by anything in the module. Both are caught by a reader who counts terms, checks both masses, and keeps a diameter with its coefficient.

## What you take with you

The boundary is an input. The stack adds in one direction and the biggest term runs it. A share belongs to a build. A U belongs to a diameter. A mass belongs to a heat capacity, and the layers with no mass slot are still in the line.
