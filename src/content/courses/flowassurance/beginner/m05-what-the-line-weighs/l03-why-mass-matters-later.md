# Why mass matters later

A mass per foot answers no question on its own. It becomes an answer when a specific heat turns it into a heat capacity and a conductance divides that heat capacity into a time.

{{panel:pd-thermal-explorer}}

## The two masses become one heat capacity

The published cooldown case carries 11.0344753586 lbm/ft of contents at Cp 0.50 and 18.9921056882 lbm/ft of steel at Cp 0.11, in Btu/(lb degF).

| Term | Heat capacity per foot, Btu/(ft degF) |
| --- | --- |
| Contents | 5.5172376793 |
| Steel shell | 2.0891316257 |
| Total M Cp | 7.6063693050 |

The contents carry 72.534444 percent of that total.

## The ranking reverses

The steel mass on this pipe is 1.72116073 times the contents mass. The contents still hold nearly three quarters of the heat. Cp 0.50 against Cp 0.11 is a wider gap than the masses opened, so ordering the two terms by weight puts them in the wrong order.

That is the whole reason a mass per foot is worth computing carefully and worth nothing when quoted alone. Two masses and two specific heats give one number, and only that number goes any further.

## What divides into it

On the published insulated build the conductance per foot is U times the reference perimeter, which comes to 2.119538792399 Btu/(hr ft degF). Divide the heat capacity by it and the units collapse to hours: 7.6063693050 over 2.119538792399 is 3.588690771912 hr.

That is a time constant, and after it there is only a logarithm. It is not a duration anybody asked for and it does not depend on where the line starts or where it is going. A shut-in line falls towards ambient on that clock, and everything a later tier asks about a shutdown is that number multiplied by a log of two temperature differences. Get the masses wrong and the clock is wrong by exactly the same factor, at every temperature, for the whole event.

## The fluid decides which term matters

Teaching line AKASO SPUR is a gas line, and its contents carry 27.263524 percent of the heat capacity the two slots see, against 72.534444 percent on the published liquid filled case. On a gas line the steel is the heat store and the fluid is almost nothing. On an oil line it is the other way. AKASO SPUR is a TEACHING LINE, not a published case.

## The mistake

Quoting mass as though it were heat capacity, or skipping the pipe because the fluid is the product. Both errors survive review, because 18.9921056882 lbm/ft and 11.0344753586 lbm/ft are correct numbers, and a report can carry both of them and still hand the next step a heat capacity that is short. Nothing downstream asks where a heat capacity came from.
