# Onward

A line in operation is one exponential, and the professional reading of it is four numbers and a caveat. Be precise about which the engines earn and which they never touch.

## What they earn

A relaxation length exactly linear in mass rate and heat capacity and exactly inverse in U and diameter: 28308.04630582 ft golden on the published insulated build, doubling with the rate by 2.0000000000 and rising with Cp by 1.2000000000.

An arrival that needs no marching. Golden arrivals of 156.177944028181 degF, 95.094251896906 degF and 43.357693533435 degF at golden ntu of 0.186519406637, 0.932597033183 and 3.730388132730, and the same closed form at 2 stations as at 501, a difference of 0.0000e+0 degF.

An inverse that round trips clean, 0.0000e+0 degF back to the target it was given. And a cooldown with a time constant under it: golden 4.662725032604 hr to the target and a golden constant of 3.588690908413 hr.

## Where they refuse well

`uForArrivalTemp` answers two impossible targets in two different sentences: a line cannot arrive above ambient no matter how well it is insulated, and the fluid already enters below the target, so insulation is not the problem. `cooldownTime` handles a target below ambient properly too, returning Infinity hours, a time constant that is still meaningful, an empty station list and a written note.

## Where they hand over

The hydrate boundary, which neither engine computes and both headers say so. Every margin and every no-touch time in this course is measured against a laboratory number somebody supplied.

Then the mass. `cooldownTime` has two slots and no slot for a coating, and on a teaching gas line folding the insulation and weight coat in multiplies the no-touch time by 3.5480816986, worth 23.2173871518 hr. Then the model itself: one U for the whole length, one ambient, and no pressure term in any published case.

## The limit worth remembering

`ok: true` is not a verdict on the answer. A 3.0 ft trench entered as 0.3 ft on the published pipe drops the ground term and returns U 79.035960 percent high, `ok: true`, no note. A cooldown asked to fall to a target above its start returns -4.6959175559 hr with `ok: true` and 25 stations that warm by 24.2422513458 degF.

## What you have finished

Whether a line arrives warm enough, how long it has once it stops, what coefficient a target arrival would need, and what every one of those is conditional on.

Write the four numbers a professional reading produces and, beside each, the input that moves it most.
