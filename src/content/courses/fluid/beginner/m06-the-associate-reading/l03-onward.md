# Onward

What this tier gave you, and what the next two do with it.

## What you can do now

Take an oil described by gravity, gas gravity, temperature and one of either its bubble point or its solution gas, and produce a complete black-oil description: Rs, Bo, viscosity, z, Bg, gas viscosity, Bw and water viscosity, at any pressure.

More importantly, you can say for each of those numbers which correlation produced it, what that correlation was fitted to, whether the conditions are inside its stated range, and how far a second published correlation would sit from it.

That second skill is the one worth having. The first is arithmetic anybody can look up.

## What this tier deliberately did not do

**It did not measure anything.** Every number came from a correlation or a design decision. The word measured has not applied to a single quantity in this tier.

**It did not use a composition.** The black-oil description treats the fluid as two pseudo-components and never asks what molecules are in it.

**It did not check any correlation against a real fluid.** Three correlations disagreeing tells you they disagree. It cannot tell you which one is closer, because there was nothing to be closer to.

Each of those is what a later tier is for.

## What the Professional tier does

Reads a real study. Good Oil Co. Well No. 4, Core Laboratories report RFL 88001, is a published reservoir fluid study on a Texas well, reproduced in McCain and in Whitson and Brule, and the engine uses it as a gate.

It contains what a laboratory actually does: a constant composition expansion, a differential liberation, separator tests, and a reported composition down to a C7+ fraction. The tier is about reading that report and understanding what each experiment measured and what it did not.

Then it runs an untuned equation of state against it, and the gaps that appear are documented biases rather than mysteries.

## What the Expert tier does

Builds the equation of state and then tunes it.

Four bounded knobs on the heavy pseudo-component, regressed against four laboratory targets at once. Three targets improve and one gets worse, and the tier is about why that is the honest shape of the fit and how to report it.

It also has a module that grades nothing, on the quantities the engine labels `screening`. Teaching a number and certifying somebody can produce it are different acts, and the second implies the number is worth producing.

## The habit to carry

Ask what tier a number is on before asking whether it is right.

That question has run through all five courses in this series in different forms. The decline curve course asked which points a fit was made to. The material balance course asked which representation of an aquifer a number came from. The simulation course asked which section of a deck a keyword lived in and which convention a volume was clipped under.

Here it has a published answer for every quantity on the screen, which is unusual and worth using while you have it. The habit is what transfers.

## The misconception to avoid

"Correlations are for screening and real work uses an equation of state." Real work uses whichever is better constrained for the question. An equation of state that has not been tuned to your fluid can be further from the truth than a correlation applied inside its range, and the Professional tier measures exactly that on a real study. The tool is not the point; the evidence behind it is.
