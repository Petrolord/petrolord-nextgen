# When it is not

The cases where a correlation will not do, and what to reach for instead.

## The four

**When the decision turns on the difference.** A mobility ratio of 0.5 and one of 1.2 are different floods. If a correlation's uncertainty spans the decision boundary, the correlation has not answered the question.

**When the composition is the answer.** What arrives at the plant, whether a condensate drops liquid in the reservoir, how a gas injection scheme will behave. Black-oil correlations have no composition in them and cannot be asked.

**When the fluid is unusual.** Volatile oils, gas condensates, high CO2 or H2S content, waxy crudes. The correlations were fitted to ordinary black oils, and the fluid's being outside that population is often exactly what makes it interesting.

**When the fluid is being changed.** Gas injection, miscible flooding, anything where the composition in place will not be the composition you started with. The black-oil assumption is precisely that this does not happen.

## The pattern

Three of those four are about composition, and the fourth is about precision.

That is the honest summary of when to leave the black-oil world: when the molecules matter, or when the answer needs to be better than a few percent.

## What to reach for

**A measurement**, first. A single constant composition expansion gives a bubble point, and a bubble point is the most consequential single number in a fluid description.

**A tuned equation of state**, when composition matters. Untuned is not enough, as this tier measured. Tuned to the fluid's own measurements it becomes the strongest description available, and it can then answer compositional questions no correlation can.

**A regional correlation**, if your organisation has one fitted to local fluids. Often better than any published correlation and rarely written up.

## The order that follows

The cheapest useful thing is a measured bubble point. The next is a separator test, because it defines what a stock tank barrel is and it fixes the level of everything. After that a full study, and after that a tuned model on top of it.

Each step buys something specific. Buying the last one without the ones before it is what produces a tuned model with nothing to tune against.

## Where this tier leaves you

With one measured fluid, one untuned model, and quantified gaps between them: six percent on saturation pressure, three percent on gas-oil ratio, nine API on stock tank gravity, and a formation volume factor the model declined to report.

That is the state of the art before tuning, measured rather than asserted. The Expert tier closes it, and finds out what closing it costs.

## The misconception to avoid

"An equation of state is the general tool, so use it when in doubt." An untuned equation of state on an unmeasured fluid is a construction resting on correlated pseudo-component properties, and this tier measured it doing worse than a separator test on the gravity. The general tool is not the reliable tool. What makes a description reliable is the measurement behind it, whatever machinery sits on top.
