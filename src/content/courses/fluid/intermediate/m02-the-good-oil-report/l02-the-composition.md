# The composition

Eleven numbers that say what the fluid is, and one of them is doing a third of the work.

{{panel:fluid-study-explorer}}

## What is reported

A compositional analysis lists the mole fraction of each component in the wellstream. For Good Oil Well No. 4:

| component | mol fraction |
|---|---|
| CO2 | 0.0091 |
| N2 | 0.0016 |
| C1 | 0.3647 |
| C2 | 0.0967 |
| C3 | 0.0695 |
| iC4 | 0.0144 |
| nC4 | 0.0393 |
| iC5 | 0.0144 |
| nC5 | 0.0141 |
| C6 | 0.0433 |
| C7+ | 0.3329 |

Eleven entries, summing to one.

## What the entries are

Nine of them are identifiable substances with published critical properties: methane, ethane, propane, the two butanes, the two pentanes, hexane, plus carbon dioxide and nitrogen. Those are looked up rather than characterized.

One is a lump. C7+ is everything with seven or more carbon atoms, which in a real crude is hundreds of distinct compounds, reported as a single entry with a molecular weight of 218 and a specific gravity of 0.8515.

## The one that matters

C7+ is 0.3329 of the fluid by mole, and by mass it is far more than that, because its molecular weight is 218 against methane's 16.

So a third of the molecules and most of the mass of this fluid are described by two numbers.

Everything about how the model behaves at the heavy end, which means the liquid density, the stock tank gravity and much of the saturation pressure, comes from what those two numbers are turned into. The next module is entirely about that turning.

## Why C1 and C7+ together tell you the fluid type

Methane at 0.3647 and C7+ at 0.3329 is a black oil. A large heavy fraction and a moderate methane fraction means a liquid that stays liquid over most of the pressure range.

A gas condensate would have methane above 0.7 and C7+ below 0.05. A volatile oil sits between, with methane near 0.6 and C7+ around 0.1.

Reading those two numbers first is the quickest way to know what kind of fluid a report describes, before reading anything else.

## Where the composition comes from

The wellstream composition is not measured directly. It is RECOMBINED: the separator gas and the separator liquid are analysed separately, usually by chromatography for the gas and by distillation for the liquid, and the two are recombined in the ratio the separator test measured.

That means the reported composition depends on the separator test being right, and a recombination at the wrong gas-oil ratio produces a composition that is systematically wrong in the light end.

It is one more reason a study is a set of connected experiments rather than a list of independent numbers.

## The sum, and what it hides

The mole fractions sum to one, which is a useful arithmetic check and tells you nothing about quality. A composition can sum perfectly and still be wrong, if the recombination ratio was wrong or if the C7+ properties were measured on a partly weathered sample.

## The misconception to avoid

"A compositional analysis is a complete description of the fluid." It is a complete description of the LIGHT end and a two-number summary of the heavy end. For most reservoir fluids the heavy end is where the modelling difficulty lives, so the analysis is most detailed exactly where the fluid is simplest.
