# The numbers come from elsewhere

The three length changes this tier is designed against, and the course that computed them.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The three cases

The Casing and Tubing Design course ends on three tubing load cases for its string, and computes a total length change for each.

Production heating lengthens the string, by a little under nine tenths of a metre. Injection cooling shortens it, by about one point eight metres. Stimulation shortens it much further, by about three point three five metres.

## The shape of that set

One positive and two negative, and the largest magnitude by a wide margin is a contraction.

That asymmetry is not a peculiarity of one well. Producing a well warms it relative to the static profile by a moderate amount, and pumping a cold fluid down it at rate cools it by a great deal more. Contraction usually dominates.

## What that course concluded

It sized a seal arrangement with one and a half metres of stroke and found that two of the three cases stroked out of it. Only the production heating case was carried.

That is a correct conclusion about a seal stroke and it is not the end of the story, because a stroke is not the only way to absorb movement.

## What this course does with them

Takes all three, treats the largest positive and the largest negative as the design extremes, and finds a polished bore length and a landing depth that carry every case.

The swing is the distance between the extremes, a little over four point two metres. That is the number a bore has to accommodate, plus margin at both ends.

## Why borrowing is better than recomputing

Because a second implementation of a thermal and pressure length change calculation, in a course about geometry, would be a second thing to validate and a second thing to disagree with the first.

The engine that computed those three numbers has its own goldens and its own oracle. Taking its output and treating it as data is the same discipline that makes the casing program an input here rather than something this course invents.
