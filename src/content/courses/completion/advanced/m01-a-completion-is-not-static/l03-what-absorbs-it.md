# What absorbs it

Three ways a completion deals with length change, and what each costs.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## Let the steel take it

Anchor the tubing to the packer so it cannot move, and the length change becomes a force. A string that wants to lengthen and cannot goes into compression. One that wants to shorten and cannot goes into tension.

The cost is stress. Those forces are large, and the Casing and Tubing Design course exists partly to check that the string survives them. The compression case also buckles, which adds bending stress and can cause wear against the casing.

The benefit is simplicity: no moving seals, nothing to leak, nothing to space out.

## Give it a stroke

A seal assembly that can slide a limited distance in a bore, with the movement stopped at both ends by shoulders. The string moves, the seals slide, and no force builds until the stroke is used up.

The cost is that the stroke is finite. When a case exceeds it the seals reach the shoulder and the string starts loading up again, and if it keeps going the seals pull out of the bore entirely.

## Give it a long bore

A polished bore receptacle is the same idea with the stroke made long. The seal assembly is inserted some distance into a smooth bore, and it can move up or down inside that bore freely.

The cost is length and money: a longer bore is a longer piece of precision machined pipe. The benefit is that with enough length, every case is carried and there is a choice about where to land.

## What this tier studies

The third one, because it is the one with a design decision in it.

The anchored case has no geometry to decide. The limited stroke case has a yes or no answer. The polished bore case has a landing depth, and choosing it well is the difference between a completion that survives every case and one that does not.

## The connection

The three approaches are not alternatives on a single well so much as different points on a spectrum of how much movement there is to absorb. A well with tens of centimetres of movement can be anchored. A well with metres of it needs a bore.
