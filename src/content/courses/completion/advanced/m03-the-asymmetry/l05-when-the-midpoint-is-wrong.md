# When the midpoint is wrong

Three situations where the balanced landing is not what you want.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## When the failures are unequal in severity

The contraction failure loses well integrity. The elongation failure transfers load into the string.

A design that weights those equally is not weighting them correctly. If both cases pass with margin, and there is a choice about where in the band to sit, sitting towards the deep end buys extra protection against the worse failure at the cost of protection against the milder one.

That is a deliberate asymmetry and it is defensible.

## When one case is much better known than the other

A production heating length change is computed from a flowing temperature profile that will be measured within weeks of first production. A stimulation cooling case is computed from a job that may happen in five years, with a fluid and a rate nobody has decided yet.

The second number is far less certain than the first. Leaving more room against the less certain case is buying insurance where the risk actually is.

## When a case will not happen

If the well will never be stimulated, the stimulation case should not size the bore.

That sounds obvious and it is routinely got wrong in the other direction, by carrying every conceivable operation into the design case list and ending up with a bore nobody will pay for. The design case list is a decision about what the well will actually see and it should be made explicitly.

The converse error is worse: leaving a case out that later happens.

## What all three have in common

They are reasons to move within the band, not reasons to ignore it. The band is where both cases pass with margin, and none of the three arguments is a reason to land outside it.

If an argument pushes you outside the band, the answer is a longer bore rather than a different landing.

## The order of operations

Compute the band. Confirm it exists. Then choose within it, using the three considerations above.

Choosing first and checking afterwards is how a landing outside the band gets defended with a good argument.

## Exercise

Give the three reasons to land off the middle of the band.

For each, say which direction it pushes the landing.
