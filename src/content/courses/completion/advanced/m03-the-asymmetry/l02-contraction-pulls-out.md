# Contraction pulls out

The other direction, its failure mode, and why it is the more serious of the two.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The direction

Cool the string and it shortens. The top is fixed, so the bottom comes up. The seal assembly moves towards the top of the receptacle and out of it.

## Which travel that consumes

The insertion depth itself. Seals landed three metres into the bore have exactly three metres before they reach the top.

That is the cleanest statement in this tier: available travel for contraction is the insertion depth, full stop.

## What happens at the end of it

The seals come out of the polished bore. There is no shoulder to stop them and nothing to load up against.

The well loses its tubing to annulus seal. Produced fluid at tubing pressure is now in the annulus, which is contained by the casing rather than by the tubing, and the casing was very likely not designed for it.

## Why that is worse than shouldering out

The elongation failure is a transition to a different mechanical state, with a load that the string may well survive. It is a design problem and often a survivable one.

The contraction failure is a loss of well integrity. It is a containment failure, it happens at the moment of maximum cooling which is during a stimulation with pumps running, and it does not announce itself until pressure appears where it should not be.

## And the case is usually larger

The three borrowed cases in this course have a largest elongation of under nine tenths of a metre and a largest contraction of about three point three five.

So the direction with the more serious failure mode also has the larger movement, by a factor of nearly four. Both of those point the same way: contraction is what a space out is designed against.

## The published job again

Two point eight metres of contraction against three metres available. Two tenths of a metre remaining.

The design landed the seals almost exactly in the middle of the bore, which would be right if the two cases were symmetric. They are not, and the near miss is on the dangerous side.

## Exercise

State which travel contraction consumes and what happens when it runs out.
