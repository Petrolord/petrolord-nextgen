# Landing at the midpoint

The rule of thumb, when it is right, and what it is actually a rule about.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The rule of thumb

Land the seals in the middle of the polished bore.

It is widely used, it is easy to execute, and it is right under one condition.

## When it is right

When the two length changes are equal and opposite.

Then the two directions need the same room, the budget splits evenly, and the middle of the bore gives each direction exactly half. The remaining travels are equal and the design is balanced.

## When it is wrong

Whenever the cases are unequal, which is nearly always.

With a contraction case four times the elongation case, splitting the bore evenly gives each direction the same room for very different demands. The contraction side runs out first, and the elongation side finishes with a large surplus that did nobody any good.

## What the right rule is

Land in the middle of the band of acceptable landings, not in the middle of the bore.

Those are different points. The band is the set of insertion depths where both cases pass with margin, and its middle is the landing that is equally far from both failures.

The next module computes it. For the published case it sits deeper than the middle of the bore, because the contraction case is the larger one.

## Why the rule of thumb persists

Because it is right often enough and because it needs no calculation. If the cases are within a factor of two of each other and the bore is generously long, the middle of the bore is inside the band and the completion is fine.

It fails exactly when the bore is short relative to the swing, which is when it matters most.

## The general form

A midpoint rule is a good default when the constraints are symmetric and a bad one otherwise, and it is worth knowing which of those you have before applying it.

That is not a statement about completions.
