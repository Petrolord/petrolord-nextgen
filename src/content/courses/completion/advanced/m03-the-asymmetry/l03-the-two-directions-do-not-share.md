# The two directions do not share

A worked demonstration that the budget identity has real consequences.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The claim

There is no landing that improves both directions. Every metre gained in one is lost in the other, exactly.

## The demonstration

Take the published bore of six point one metres and sweep the insertion from zero to the full length. At each point, record the remaining travel after the elongation case and after the contraction case.

Plot them together and you get two straight lines with equal and opposite slopes. Where one rises the other falls, at the same rate, and they cross.

## Reading the crossing

At the crossing point, both cases have the same remaining travel. That is not the same as both having the same available travel, because the two cases use different amounts.

The crossing sits deeper than the midpoint of the bore whenever the contraction case is larger than the elongation case, because the contraction direction needs more room and the crossing is where the two are equally comfortable rather than equally supplied.

## What the crossing is good for

It is the landing that maximises the smaller of the two remaining travels. If you had to pick one landing with no other information, that is a defensible choice.

It is not necessarily the choice a design makes, because the two failure modes are not equally serious. A designer who weights the contraction failure more heavily lands deeper than the crossing.

## The line that does not move

Plot the sum of the two available travels on the same axes and it is a horizontal line at the bore length.

That flat line is the whole tier in one picture. Everything else on the chart moves; that does not.

## The mistake this rules out

Somebody looking at a warning on the contraction case and proposing to land deeper, then checking the elongation case and finding it still passes, and concluding the problem is solved for free.

It is not free. The elongation case lost exactly what the contraction case gained. It may still pass, in which case the move was right, and the reason it was right is that there was slack in the elongation direction and not that the move cost nothing.
