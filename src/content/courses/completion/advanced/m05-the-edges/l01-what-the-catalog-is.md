# What the catalog is

The provenance of every number in this course, restated at the end where it can be judged.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## Three sources

The casing and tubing dimensions come from published API tables: outside diameters, weights and nominal bores that any handbook carries. Those are real numbers and they are checkable.

The drift arithmetic comes from the API specification: four deduction values and two size boundaries. Also real, also checkable.

The equipment dimensions are representative values, flagged as approximate on every row, and they are not any vendor's product.

## Which results depend on which

The drift table depends only on the first two, so every drift in this course is a real number.

The stack up depends on component lengths, which are illustrative. The depths are therefore illustrative, and the arithmetic connecting them is exact.

The clearances depend on both: a real casing drift less an approximate equipment diameter. The magnitudes move with a real catalog and the structure does not.

The through bore depends on approximate nipple and valve bores. Same statement.

The space out depends on none of the catalog at all. It depends on a bore length, a landing and two length changes, all of which are inputs.

## What that means for what you take away

The space out results are unconditional. The identity, the band, the minimum bore length: those are arithmetic and they hold for any numbers.

The clearance and through bore results are conditional on the catalog for their magnitudes and unconditional for their structure. The packer being the tightest row is a structural fact about packers in liners. Four point seven millimetres is not a number to quote.

## The honest way to say it

This course teaches a method on a well built example. The method transfers exactly. The numbers do not transfer at all, and every equipment row says so.

## What would change with real data

Digits. Not one conclusion in this course would reverse, because every conclusion rests on an ordering that real data preserves: big bodies are bigger than pipe, nipple bores are smaller than tubing bores, liners are smaller than the casing they hang in.
