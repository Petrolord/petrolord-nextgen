# The story so far

What the Expert tier established.

## Five modules

A completion moves. Four effects change its length, and this course takes the resulting length changes as inputs from elsewhere rather than computing them.

A polished bore receptacle absorbs the movement, and the insertion depth of the seal assembly splits a fixed budget: the travel available upward and the travel available downward always sum to the length of the bore.

The two directions are asymmetric in three ways. Contraction usually moves further, its failure loses well integrity rather than transferring load, and it is usually the less certain of the two predictions.

From the two conditions comes a band of acceptable landings, whose middle is the balanced landing and whose width is the execution tolerance. The shortest usable bore is the whole swing plus twice the margin, and every metre beyond that buys a metre of band.

And the edges: what the catalog is, what the engine will not decide, what the physics does not include, and what the certificate does not claim.

## The three results worth keeping

The budget identity, which rules out treating the two directions independently.

The band, which replaces the midpoint rule and shows that the published job lands three tenths of a metre outside its own.

And the minimum bore length, which is the swing plus twice the margin, derived two ways.

## The join with the other course

Casing and Tubing Design produced three length changes and found that two of the three stroked out of a one and a half metre seal stroke. This tier took the same three numbers and found the bore and the landing that carry all three.

Neither course could reach that conclusion alone.

## What you can claim

That you can size a polished bore, compute the band, choose a landing within it with a stated reason, and say what the completion does at both extremes.
