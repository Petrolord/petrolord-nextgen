# Onward

Where this leaves you, and what to read next.

## The three tiers together

The Associate tier established what a completion is made of and how to read a tally, with drift as the number that matters for fit.

The Professional tier asked whether the string goes in and what gets through it, and made the casing program a first class object. It also spent a module on a real defect in the engine, which is the most transferable thing in the course.

The Expert tier made the completion move and turned the landing of a seal assembly into a design decision with a computable answer.

## The one sentence

A completion is a geometry problem with a path in it: what gets down depends on everything above the destination, what gets through depends on everything above the target, and what stays put depends on where you landed it between two extremes.

## Where to go in the academy

Casing and Tubing Design, if you have not taken it, because this course consumes three of its outputs and its seven load cases are what put the completion under load.

Perforation and Sand Control, which designs what sits below the packer, and Stimulation Design, which designs the job that produces the largest contraction case in this tier.

Well Integrity and P and A, which asks what the whole assembly looks like after twenty years of the thermal cycling this tier treated as two extremes.

## Where to go on a real well

Get a real equipment catalog and rerun everything in this course on it. Every calculation runs unchanged and every number moves.

Then get a drift run from the well itself and compare it against the casing program. The gap between the two is the thing this course keeps flagging and cannot compute.

## The habit worth keeping

When a tool gives you a single worst case, check it against the list once by hand.
