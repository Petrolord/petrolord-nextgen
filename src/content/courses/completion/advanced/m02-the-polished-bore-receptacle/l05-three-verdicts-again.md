# Three verdicts again

The two refusals in the space-out calculation, and why the margin here is not the same kind of thing as the margin in the clearance check.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The refusals

An insertion depth greater than the receptacle length is refused. You cannot land the seals past the bottom of the bore; the input describes nothing.

A receptacle length of zero or less is refused. There is no bore.

Both are input errors rather than design outcomes, which is the same distinction the stack up made in the Associate tier.

## What is not refused

An insertion depth of zero, which means the seals are at the very top of the bore. That is a legitimate if useless landing, and the calculation reports it as having no travel available downward.

A length change larger than the bore. That is a real design that fails, and it is computed and reported as failing.

## The margin here

Half a metre in the published job. It protects against a different set of unknowns from the clearance margin.

The clearance margin protected against the bore being smaller than the catalog said. This one protects against the length change being larger than computed, and against the landing being other than intended.

Both of those are substantial uncertainties. A thermal length change depends on a flowing temperature profile that is itself a prediction, and the landing depends on measured pipe lengths.

## Why it is absolute rather than proportional

Same reasoning as before, and it is stronger here. The landing error does not scale with anything; it is a matter of centimetres of measurement error accumulating over a few hundred joints.

A proportional margin would give a short bore almost no protection and a long bore more than it needs.

## What a warning actually means

That the design works on the numbers and has less room than you asked for. It is not a prediction of failure.

On the published job the contraction case has twenty centimetres left after a two point eight metre movement. That will very likely be fine. It is also less than half the margin the design set for itself, and the design should either accept that explicitly or move the landing.
