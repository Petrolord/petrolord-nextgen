# The budget is fixed

The central identity of this tier, and everything that follows from it.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The identity

Available travel upward plus available travel downward equals the receptacle length. Always, at every insertion depth.

Land shallow and there is little room above and a lot below. Land deep and the reverse. The sum does not move.

## Why it is exactly true

Because the seals are somewhere in the bore, and the distance from them to the top plus the distance from them to the bottom is the length of the bore. There is nothing more to it than that.

It is worth stating anyway, because it is easy to think of a deeper landing as safer, as though pushing the seals further in bought security. It buys security against one failure and spends exactly the same amount against the other.

## What it rules out

Any approach that treats the two directions independently. You cannot solve for the elongation case, then solve for the contraction case, and land at both.

You cannot improve both by moving. Every millimetre gained in one direction is a millimetre lost in the other, and the exchange rate is exactly one.

## What it leaves

A single scalar decision with a genuine trade in it. That is the best kind of design problem, because it has an answer.

Given the two extremes the well will see and the margin you want against each, there is a range of insertion depths that satisfy both, and it either exists or it does not. If it exists, there is a natural point in the middle of it. If it does not, the bore is too short and no landing will do.

## The consequence for sizing

Since the sum is fixed at the bore length, and the two requirements are the two length changes plus their margins, the bore has to be at least the sum of those requirements.

That is the shortest usable bore, and it drops straight out of the identity with no further argument.

## Checking it

Sweep the insertion depth in the panel and watch the two available figures. They move in opposite directions and their sum is constant to the last digit.
