# Available, used, remaining

Three quantities per case, and the arithmetic that connects them.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## Available

The travel the seals have in the direction the case moves them. For an elongation that is the distance to the top of the bore. For a contraction it is the insertion depth.

Available depends on the landing and on the bore length, and not at all on the case.

## Used

The magnitude of the length change for the case. It depends on the case and not at all on the landing.

## Remaining

Available less used. What is left when the movement is complete.

That is the number the verdict is based on, and it is the only one of the three that depends on both the hardware and the operation.

## The verdicts

Remaining below zero fails: the movement exceeds the travel, and the seals either come out of the top or shoulder at the bottom.

Remaining above zero but below the margin warns: the completion survives with less room than the design wanted.

Above the margin passes.

That is the same three verdict structure as the clearance check in the Professional tier, and deliberately so. It is the same kind of question: a quantity against a limit, with a stated tolerance in between.

## The published job

A six point one metre bore, landed three metres in, with a margin of half a metre.

The elongation case uses one point two metres out of three point one available and has one point nine remaining, which passes comfortably.

The contraction case uses two point eight out of three available and has zero point two remaining, which is above zero and below the margin. It warns.

## Reading that pair

One comfortable pass and one warning, from a single landing. The warning is on the contraction case, which is the larger of the two length changes, and the landing gave that direction slightly less travel than the other.

That is the wrong way round and the next module says by how much.
