# The published landing is short

The job in the golden file lands outside its own band, and the calculation says by how much.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The landing

Three metres into a six point one metre bore.

## Where the band starts

Three point three metres.

## The shortfall

Three tenths of a metre. The published landing is thirty centimetres shallower than the shallowest landing that satisfies both conditions with the stated margin.

## What that produces

Exactly the warning that appears in the golden file. The contraction case has two tenths of a metre remaining, against a margin of half a metre, and it is reported as a warning rather than a pass.

Those two numbers are the same fact seen from two sides. The remaining travel falls short of the margin by three tenths, and the landing falls short of the band by three tenths.

## Is the job wrong

No, and this is worth being careful about. The completion works. The contraction case has twenty centimetres of travel left and the seals stay in the bore.

What it does not have is the margin the design asked for. The design set a half metre margin and then landed somewhere that delivers two tenths, which means the margin was stated and not enforced.

## Why this is the useful case to publish

A job that passed everything comfortably would teach the arithmetic and nothing else. This one shows what a near miss looks like in the output, and it shows that a warning is a specific, quantifiable distance from where you meant to be.

Thirty centimetres, in this case. On a rig floor that is one pup joint.

## The fix

Add three tenths of a metre of pipe above the seal assembly and the landing moves into the band. Add eighty five centimetres and it lands in the middle of it.

Neither is expensive and neither is hard. What is hard is knowing to do it, which requires computing the band rather than landing in the middle of the bore.

## Exercise

State the published landing, the lower edge of the band and the shortfall.

Confirm that the shortfall equals the amount by which the contraction remaining falls short of the margin, and explain why those two are the same number.

Then say what would have to be added to the string to land in the middle of the band.
