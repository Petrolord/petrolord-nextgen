# The band on the published job

The numbers, for a six point one metre bore with the published cases.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The inputs

A bore of six point one metres. An elongation case of one point two metres. A contraction case of two point eight metres. A margin of half a metre.

## The edges

The contraction requirement is two point eight plus half a metre, which is three point three. That is the shallowest acceptable landing.

The elongation requirement is one point two plus half a metre, which is one point seven. Subtract that from the bore and you get four point four, which is the deepest acceptable landing.

## The band

From three point three to four point four metres of insertion. One point one metres wide.

Its middle is three point eight five metres.

## The check on the width

The width should equal the bore less the sum of the two requirements: six point one less three point three less one point seven, which is one point one. It does.

That check is worth running every time, because it catches a sign error in either edge immediately.

## What the width means here

One point one metres of tolerance on the landing. That is comfortable. A space out achieved with pup joints to the nearest foot lands well inside it, and a measurement error of a few centimetres over the string is irrelevant.

So this is a well designed bore in the sense that matters most operationally: the execution does not have to be precise.

## The midpoint of the bore

Three point zero five metres, which is below the lower edge of the band.

That is the striking result. The rule of thumb landing, in the middle of the bore, is outside the band. It fails the contraction condition, by a quarter of a metre.

The published job lands at three metres, which is even shallower.
