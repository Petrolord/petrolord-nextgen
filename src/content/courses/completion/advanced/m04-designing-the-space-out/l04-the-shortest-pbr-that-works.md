# The shortest PBR that works

The sizing result, derived twice, and what it costs to be wrong about it.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## The result

The shortest polished bore that leaves any acceptable landing is the whole swing plus twice the margin.

The swing is the elongation case plus the magnitude of the contraction case, which is the total distance the seals travel between the two extremes. Twice the margin is one margin at each end.

## Derived from the band

The band width is the bore less the two requirements. Set the width to zero and the bore equals the sum of the requirements, which is elongation plus margin plus contraction plus margin.

## Derived from the physics

The seals have to be inside the bore at the most contracted state with a margin, and inside it at the most elongated state with a margin. Between those two states they travel the swing. So the bore must contain the swing, plus a margin at each end.

Two derivations, one line each, same answer. That is a good sign that the result is structural rather than an artefact of how the band was defined.

## The numbers here

For the published cases, one point two plus two point eight is four metres of swing, plus one metre of total margin, giving five metres.

The job uses six point one, which is one point one metres longer than it needs to be. That surplus is exactly the band width, which is the second thing the identity tells you: every metre of bore beyond the minimum buys one metre of landing tolerance.

## For the three borrowed cases

The largest elongation is under nine tenths of a metre and the largest contraction is about three point three five, giving a swing of about four point two four. With a half metre margin at each end, the shortest usable bore is about five point two four metres.

So the published six point one metre bore would carry all three of those cases, with a band about eighty six centimetres wide.

## What being wrong costs

Order a bore shorter than the minimum and there is no landing that works. The error is discovered on the rig, with the string in the hole and a piece of hardware that cannot be lengthened.

Order one longer than necessary and you have paid for precision machined pipe you did not need, and gained execution tolerance you may well be glad of.

The asymmetry of those two outcomes is why bores are specified generously.
