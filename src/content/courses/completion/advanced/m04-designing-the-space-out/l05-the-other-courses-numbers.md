# The other course's numbers

Closing the loop with Casing and Tubing Design, on the three cases it ended on.

{{panel:cd-spaceout-explorer}}

## What that course found

Three tubing load cases, three length changes, and a seal arrangement with one and a half metres of stroke.

Production heating lengthens the string by a little under nine tenths of a metre, which the stroke carries. Injection cooling shortens it by about one point eight metres and stimulation by about three point three five, and neither of those is carried. Two of the three strokes out.

That is a correct result about that seal arrangement, and it is where a course about tubing design properly stops.

## What this course does with it

Takes the same three numbers and asks a different question. Not whether a stroke of one and a half metres is enough, but how long a polished bore would have to be and where the seals would have to land.

The swing across the three cases is about four point two four metres. With half a metre of margin at each end the shortest usable bore is about five point two four.

## The published bore carries all three

Six point one metres is longer than five point two four, so the band is not empty. It runs from about three point eight five metres of insertion to about four point seven, a width of about eighty six centimetres, with a middle a little over four point two seven metres.

Land there and all three cases pass with margin. The two that stroked out of a one and a half metre stroke are comfortably carried.

## Why that is not a contradiction

The other course was not wrong. A one and a half metre stroke really is insufficient for those cases.

The two courses are answering different questions about the same string, and the answers compose: given the length changes, given a long enough bore, here is where to land.

Neither course could produce that conclusion alone. One has the thermal and pressure model and no landing calculation. The other has the landing calculation and takes the length changes as data.

## The band is narrower here

Eighty six centimetres against the one point one metres of the published two case job, because the swing is larger. Still comfortable, and worth noting: adding a third, more extreme case narrows the band without closing it.

## Exercise

Write down the three borrowed cases and compute the swing.

Compute the shortest usable bore and the band in a six point one metre bore, and compare the band width against the two case job.

Then explain in two sentences why the two courses reach different conclusions about the same three numbers without either being wrong.
