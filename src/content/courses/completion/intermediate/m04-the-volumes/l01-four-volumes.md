# Four volumes

Two from the string alone, two from the string against the casing.

{{panel:cd-clearance-explorer}}

## The four

Capacity, which is the fluid inside the string. Displacement, which is the outside envelope of the string. The annulus above the packer, which is the casing bore less the string outside, integrated from the hanger to the packer. And the volume below the packer, which is the full casing bore from the packer to total depth.

The first two came from the beginner tier and need no casing. The last two need the exposed profile.

## The published numbers

Capacity is about eleven point eight cubic metres and displacement about twenty six point eight. The annulus above the packer is about sixty seven point nine, and below the packer about seven point seven.

The annulus is the big one by a wide margin. It is more than five times the capacity of the string, which surprises people who think of the annulus as a gap.

## Why the annulus is so large

Because the casing bore is much larger than the tubing outside diameter, and area goes as the square. A nine and five eighths inch casing bore is about two hundred and twenty millimetres and the tubing outside is one hundred and fourteen. The annular area is about four times the tubing outside area and about five times the tubing bore area.

Over two and a half kilometres that adds up to a lot of packer fluid, and packer fluid is not free.

## Below the packer

The volume below the packer is the full casing bore, not an annulus, because below the packer the string is a tail with a perforated joint and the fluid path is the whole liner.

That is a modelling choice worth being explicit about. The tail is physically there and it does occupy volume. This calculation reports the gross volume of the liner below the packer, which is the number a kill volume or a displacement calculation wants.

## What each is used for

Capacity for displacing the tubing. Displacement for pit gain while running. The annulus for ordering packer fluid and for annulus pressure calculations. The volume below the packer for kill volumes and for understanding what is in communication with the reservoir.

Four numbers, four different conversations, one geometry.
