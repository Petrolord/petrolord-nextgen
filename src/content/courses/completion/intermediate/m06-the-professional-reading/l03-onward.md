# Onward

What the Expert tier adds, and where else this leads.

## The completion moves

Everything in the first two tiers treated the string as a rigid object at fixed depths. The Expert tier removes that assumption.

Producing a hot well heats the tubing and it lengthens. Injecting cold water or stimulating cools it and it shortens. The length changes are metres, not millimetres, on a string of this length.

## What absorbs it

If the string is anchored at the packer, the length change turns into a force, and the Casing and Tubing Design course computes that force and asks whether the string survives it.

If the string is free to move, it needs somewhere to move to. A polished bore receptacle is a smooth bore that a seal assembly slides in, and the length change is taken up by the seals sliding rather than by the steel stretching.

## The central result

The insertion depth of the seals splits a fixed budget. What is left above them is what elongation can use, what is left below is what contraction can use, and the two always sum to the length of the bore.

From that follows everything: a band of landings that carry both extremes, a shortest usable bore equal to the whole swing plus twice the margin, and a clean way to see that the published job in this course is landed a little too shallow.

## The link to the other course

Casing and Tubing Design ends on three tubing cases with three computed length changes: one that lengthens the string and two that shorten it. Those three numbers are exactly what a space out is designed against, and two of the three stroked out of the seal arrangement that course used.

The Expert tier takes those three numbers and finds the bore and the landing that carry all three.

## Where to go after this course

Perforation and Sand Control designs what sits below the packer. Stimulation designs the job that causes the largest contraction case. Well Integrity asks what the whole assembly looks like after twenty years of thermal cycling.
