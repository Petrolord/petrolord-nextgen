# Working the capstone

The six graded values in this tier, and how to get each one right.

{{panel:cd-clearance-explorer}}

## The conditions

Same capstone well and string as the Associate tier: fourteen components of two and seven eighths inch equipment, three casing strings including a liner, and a total depth that is not the one used in the lessons.

Note the liner weight again. It is heavier than the one in the lessons, which makes its drift smaller, which is what makes this capstone tighter than the published case.

## The six values

Two clearances, two bores and two volumes.

The worst clearance is the tightest row within the most severe status present, which is the corrected ranking from module five. Compute every row, then select. Do not take the first row and do not take the largest body without checking its depth.

The side pocket mandrel clearance is a named row. Find it, find its bottom depth, find the governing drift to that depth, and subtract.

The minimum through bore is the running minimum over all fourteen components. The through bore at the packer is the running minimum down to and including the packer, which is a different and larger number, because something below the packer is tighter.

The two volumes are the annulus above the packer and the volume below it. Both need the exposed profile, and the second uses the full bore rather than an annular area.

## Where the marks get lost

Taking the governing drift at a component's own depth instead of the smallest above it. In this well the liner top is above the packer, so this error affects several rows and not all of them.

Building the exposed profile from two strings instead of three. There is a surface string in this capstone and it does not appear in the lessons.

Using the annular area below the packer. It is the full bore below the packer, for the reason module four gave.

## Checks you can run

The worst clearance must be no larger than the side pocket mandrel clearance, since the mandrel is one of the rows. The through bore at the packer must be no smaller than the minimum through bore. And the annulus volume must be much larger than anything the string itself holds.

## Exercise
