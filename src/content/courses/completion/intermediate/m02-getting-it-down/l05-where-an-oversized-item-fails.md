# Where an oversized item fails

The same component, run to two depths, with two different verdicts.

{{panel:cd-clearance-explorer}}

## The experiment

Take a safety valve of six point nine four inches outside diameter, which is larger than anything in the published string, and run it on tubing to two depths in the same well.

First to about two thousand five hundred metres, below the liner top. Then to two thousand metres, above it.

## The two answers

At the deeper depth it fails. The governing drift is the liner, and the valve is wider than it by about twenty two millimetres. It will not enter the liner.

At the shallower depth it passes. The governing drift is the nine and five eighths inch casing, and the valve clears it.

## What that proves about the model

Two things, and both are worth having demonstrated rather than argued.

That the clearance check reads the path rather than the component. The component was identical in both runs; only the depth changed, and the verdict flipped.

And that a failure is reported as a failure and not as a refusal. An oversized component is a legitimate input describing a real design that does not work, and the engine computes it and reports it. Contrast with a component whose bore is larger than its body, which describes nothing physical and is refused.

## Where the fail is located

The engine reports the failing row with its governing drift and the label of the string that set it. In this case the label is the liner, which tells the reader immediately where the problem is.

That is more useful than the depth of the component. The component is at two thousand five hundred metres; the problem is at twenty four hundred, where the liner top is. Naming the string points at the liner top.

## The worst row

With one failure and one pass in that two row string, the worst row is the failure. That is the easy case for ranking.

The hard case is a string where every row passes, and what the engine should call the worst then is the subject of a later module in this tier. It is worth noticing now that the two questions are different: which row fails, and which row is closest to failing.
