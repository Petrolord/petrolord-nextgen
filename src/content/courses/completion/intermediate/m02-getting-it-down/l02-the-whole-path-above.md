# The whole path above

The consequence of using the governing drift rather than the local bore, worked on two components in the same string.

{{panel:cd-clearance-explorer}}

## Two similar components, two different constraints

The published string carries a safety valve near the top and a side pocket mandrel near the bottom. Both are five and three quarter inches on the outside. They are the same size.

The safety valve is checked against the nine and five eighths inch casing and clears by about seventy millimetres. The side pocket mandrel is checked against the liner and clears by under eight.

Same component diameter, clearances differing by a factor of nine. Nothing about the components explains it. The difference is entirely in where they stop.

## The safety valve never meets the liner

The safety valve's bottom is at about one hundred and fifty three metres. The liner top is at twenty four hundred. The valve is never anywhere near it.

So checking the valve against the liner would be checking it against a pipe it does not encounter, and the answer would be wrong in the conservative direction, which is still wrong. A design conversation about a valve that does not fit, when it does, wastes real money.

## The mandrel meets everything

The side pocket mandrel's bottom is at about two thousand four hundred and fifty two metres, which is below the liner top. So it has to pass the casing, both weights of it, and then the liner.

The governing drift at its depth is the liner, because the liner is the smallest thing on the way. It is checked against that, and it clears by a small margin.

## The general statement

A component's constraint is set by the tightest thing between surface and where it stops. Move the component deeper and the constraint can only get tighter or stay the same. Move it shallower and it can only loosen or stay the same.

That monotonicity comes straight from the governing drift being a running minimum, and it is the reason the same equipment can be entirely appropriate at one depth and impossible two hundred metres lower.

## What this changes about design

It means the depth at which equipment is placed is a fit decision as well as an operational one. A side pocket mandrel does not have to be immediately above the packer. Moving it above the liner top would multiply its clearance by nine, at the cost of gas lift injection being higher in the string.

That trade is real and it is made on wells.
