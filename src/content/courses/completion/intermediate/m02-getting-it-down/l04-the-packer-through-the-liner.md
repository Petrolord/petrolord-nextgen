# The packer through the liner

The tightest row in the published string, why it is tight, and what the design has left to give.

{{panel:cd-clearance-explorer}}

## The row

The production packer is five and seven eighths inches on the outside, it lands at two thousand six hundred point five metres, and it is checked against the liner drift.

Its clearance is about four point seven millimetres. Every other row in the string clears by more, and the loosest clears by more than twenty times as much.

## Why the packer is the tight one

Two reasons compound. A packer is sized to the casing it seals in rather than to the tubing it is made up into, so its body is close to the casing bore by design. And it sits below the liner top, so the casing it is checked against is the smallest string in the well.

Neither of those is avoidable. A packer that cleared the liner easily would not seal in it.

## What it clears by, in context

Four point seven millimetres diametral is about two point three radial if centred. In a liner, running a packer on tubing, it will not be centred.

Against the three millimetre margin it has one point seven to spare. Against a real well with any scale in the liner, it has considerably less.

## The design has two levers and neither is free

Move the packer above the liner top, which multiplies the clearance by more than thirteen and puts the seal above the reservoir interval where it does not belong.

Or use a slimmer packer, which usually means a lower differential rating or a different setting mechanism.

The well takes the tight clearance because the alternatives are worse, which is the usual answer in completion design.

## The lesson beyond this well

When a string has one row much tighter than the others, that row is the design. Everything else on the sheet is passing comfortably and contributes nothing to the decision.

Reporting a completion as passing, with thirteen green rows, hides that. Reporting it as passing with a tightest row of under five millimetres in the liner tells the reader what they need to know.
