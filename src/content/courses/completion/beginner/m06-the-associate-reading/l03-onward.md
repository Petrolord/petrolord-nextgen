# Onward

Where the next two tiers go, and what else in the academy connects to this.

## The Professional tier

It asks the question this tier could not: will the string go in the hole, and what will get through it afterwards.

That means the casing program becomes a first class object rather than a lookup. You will build an exposed profile from overlapping strings, take the innermost bore wherever they overlap, and compute a governing drift to any depth as the smallest bore anywhere above it.

Then the two checks. Run in clearance, which compares every component against that governing drift and reports three verdicts. And the through bore, which is a running minimum from the top and names the component that sets it.

It also spends a module on something unusual: a real defect this course found in the engine it teaches, why nothing caught it, and what the fix asserts.

## The Expert tier

It makes the completion move. Heating lengthens the string and cooling shortens it, and a polished bore receptacle absorbs the movement if the seals are landed in the right place.

The central result is that insertion depth splits a fixed budget: every metre gained against elongation is lost against contraction. From that follows a band of acceptable landings, a shortest usable bore, and a clean answer to the question of how long a polished bore has to be.

## Elsewhere in the academy

Casing and Tubing Design sizes the casing this course fits a string through, and its three tubing length changes are what the Expert tier lands a seal assembly against.

Cementing places the cement around the casing whose bore this course inherits. Perforation and Sand Control decides what is below the packer. Well Integrity asks what happens to all of it over twenty years.

## What to do next

Pass the Associate capstone, then start the Professional tier at its first module rather than jumping to the clearance check. The exposed program module is short and everything after it depends on getting the profile right.
