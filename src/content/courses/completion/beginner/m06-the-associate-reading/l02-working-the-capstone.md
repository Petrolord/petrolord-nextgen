# Working the capstone

How to approach the six graded values, and what each one is testing.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## A different string and a different well

The capstone is not the published completion. It is a fourteen component string of two and seven eighths inch equipment in a three string program with a liner, and every number in it differs from the lessons.

Read the conditions carefully before computing anything. In particular, note the liner weight, which is not the one used in the lessons, and note that there are three casing strings rather than two.

## The six values

Two depths, two drifts and two volumes.

The bottom of the string is the hanger depth plus the sum of the fourteen lengths. Check it by the telescoping identity: it must equal the last component's bottom depth.

The packer bottom depth comes out of the same tally. Find the packer row and read its bottom.

The two drifts are the liner and the surface casing. Both are the nominal bore less a deduction, and the deduction depends on the outside diameter alone. One of the two sits in a different class from the other, and the class boundary lesson is the one to reread before you decide which.

The two volumes are the string capacity and the string displacement. Both are sums over the fourteen components of an area times a length, using the inside diameter for one and the outside for the other.

## Where the marks get lost

The most common loss is the drift class on the surface casing. Look at its outside diameter and place it in the table carefully, remembering that the ranges are inclusive at the top.

The second most common is a volume computed with a diameter that was not converted from inches. Every catalog value in this course is quoted in inches and every engine value is in metres, and the conversion is one inch to zero point zero two five four metres exactly.

The third is reading the packer depth as its top rather than its bottom.

## Checking yourself

Three checks are available without the answer. The bottom depth must equal the sum of the lengths. The displacement must exceed the capacity. And both drifts must be smaller than their nominal bores by an amount that appears in the four value table.

If any of the three fails, the error is in your arithmetic and not in the question.

## Exercise

Before opening the capstone, write down the three self checks above.

Work each of the six values and apply the checks.

Then say which of the six you are least confident in, and what you would need to be sure.
