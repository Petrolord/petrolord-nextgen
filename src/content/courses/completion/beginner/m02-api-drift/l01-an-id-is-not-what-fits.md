# An inside diameter is not what fits

Why the published bore of a piece of casing is not the size of the largest thing that will pass through it.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## The nominal bore is an average

A joint of casing is quoted with an outside diameter and a weight per foot, and from those two a nominal inside diameter follows. Nine and five eighths inch casing at fifty three and a half pounds per foot has a nominal bore of eight point five three five inches.

That number is what the pipe would have if the wall thickness were exactly uniform, exactly on specification and exactly concentric. No joint of pipe is any of those three things.

## What actually varies

Wall thickness carries a manufacturing tolerance. The bore is not perfectly round; it is slightly oval, and the ovality is not in the same direction along the joint. The pipe itself is not perfectly straight, so a rigid tool passing through sees an effective bore smaller than the local one. And the coupling has its own bore, which is not the pipe bore.

Every one of those makes the largest thing that will pass smaller than the nominal number. None of them can be measured on every joint in a string.

## The guarantee instead

Rather than quote a nominal bore and hope, the API specification quotes a drift: a diameter that is guaranteed to pass. It is defined by a physical test with a mandrel of a stated size and length, and every joint is drifted at the mill.

So a joint of casing carries two inside diameters. The nominal bore, which is what you use for volume, and the drift, which is what you use for fit. They are different numbers and they answer different questions, and using the wrong one is the most common mistake in this whole subject.

## How much smaller

For that nine and five eighths inch casing the drift is eight and three eighths of an inch and a bit, which is a fifth of an inch below the nominal bore. In metres the nominal is about zero point two one seven and the drift is about zero point two one three.

Four millimetres does not sound like much. In this course there is a component whose clearance against that drift is under five millimetres, and there is a capstone where the same component clears by less than two and a half. Four millimetres is the whole margin.
