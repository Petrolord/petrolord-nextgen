# The drift mandrel

The physical test behind the number, and why knowing it explains the shape of the arithmetic.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## The test

A drift mandrel is a cylinder of specified diameter and specified length. It is passed through every joint of pipe at the mill, under its own weight, and the joint passes if the mandrel goes through.

That is the whole definition. There is no calculation in it. The drift diameter of a joint is the diameter of the largest standard mandrel that went through.

## Why the mandrel has a length

The length is not incidental. A short mandrel would pass through a bent joint that a long one would jam in, so the length is what makes the test say something about straightness rather than only about bore.

For casing the mandrel is twelve inches long for most sizes. For tubing it is forty two inches. Tubing gets the longer mandrel because tubing is run in longer unsupported strings and because the tools that go through it are longer.

## Why the deduction is a step function

Now the shape of the arithmetic makes sense. The drift is the nominal bore less a fixed deduction, and the deduction depends only on the outside diameter of the pipe, because the mandrel sizes are standardised by pipe size.

It does not depend on the weight, because the mandrel does not know the weight. It does not depend on the grade, because the mandrel does not know the grade. It does not scale continuously with the bore, because there is not a continuum of mandrels; there are a handful of standard ones and the specification assigns them by size range.

## The consequence for the table

So every row in the catalog at a given outside diameter loses the same amount. All the nine and five eighths inch rows give up five thirty seconds of an inch, whether they weigh forty pounds per foot or fifty three and a half. Their drifts differ only because their nominal bores differ.

That is why a drift is never quoted on its own. Saying the drift is eight and a bit inches is meaningless without the weight, because the weight is what fixed the bore that the deduction came off.
