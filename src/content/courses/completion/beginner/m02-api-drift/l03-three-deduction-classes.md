# Three deduction classes

The lookup table, its four entries, and how the twenty eight tubulars in this course fall into them.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## The four entries

Tubing gives up three thirty seconds of an inch. Casing up to and including eight and five eighths inches gives up one eighth. Casing above eight and five eighths and up to and including thirteen and three eighths gives up five thirty seconds. Casing above thirteen and three eighths gives up three sixteenths.

In metres those are about two point three eight, three point one eight, three point nine seven and four point seven six millimetres.

## Three classes of casing, one of tubing

The title of this lesson says three because there are three casing classes. The fourth entry is the tubing rule, which is a separate specification with a separate mandrel length, and it happens to fall between the first two casing values.

That coincidence is worth noticing and worth not relying on. Tubing at three thirty seconds gives up less than any casing size does, even though tubing is the smallest pipe in the well. The reason is the mandrel programme, not the pipe.

## How the catalog falls out

The twenty eight tubulars this course carries divide as three rows in the largest class, nine in the five thirty seconds class, ten in the one eighth class and six tubing rows.

The panel will sort them for you. Do the sorting once by hand anyway, because the boundaries are where the mistakes happen.

## Reading the ranges correctly

The ranges are inclusive at the top. Eight and five eighths inch casing itself is in the one eighth class, not in the class above. Thirteen and three eighths inch casing itself is in the five thirty seconds class, not in the class above.

That is a specification detail, and it is exactly the sort of detail a reimplementation gets wrong. Two of the three sizes named in the table are common casing sizes, so the error would show up on real wells rather than on edge cases.

## What the classes are not

They are not a strength classification, a service classification or a connection classification. Two rows in the same deduction class may have nothing else in common. A twenty inch conductor and a sixteen inch intermediate string share a deduction and share nothing else.

The class is a fact about which mandrel the mill used. Nothing more is implied.
