# Landing nipples and seat bores

Why the smallest bore in a completion is usually a deliberate one.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## A nipple is a restriction on purpose

Every other narrow bore in a completion is an unwanted consequence of something. A nipple is different: the restriction is the point.

A landing nipple has a machined seat of a precise diameter, and a lock mandrel run on wireline expands into it and holds. That gives a known depth at which a plug can be set, a gauge hung or a choke placed, and it gives a seal bore for the plug to seal against.

## X and XN

The two profiles in this course are the selective and the no go.

An X nipple has a seat bore and a locking recess, and tools sized for it can be run past a shallower one to a deeper one, because there is nothing below the seat to stop them. That is what selective means.

An XN nipple has a no go shoulder immediately below the seat, machined smaller than the seat itself. Anything larger than the no go bore stops there. It is normally the deepest nipple in the string, and it is what a plug is set in when the well is to be shut in from below.

## The numbers in this string

For three and a half inch tubing the X seat bore is two and three quarter inches and the XN no go bore is two point six three five inches.

Both are below the tubing bore of two point nine nine two. The no go is the smaller of the two by a little over an eighth of an inch, and it is the smallest bore anywhere in the published completion.

## The trade

Every nipple you put in the string is access you gain at a depth and access you lose below it. A no go nipple is the strongest version of that trade: it guarantees a tool stops, which is the whole point, and it guarantees the same tool cannot go deeper, which is the same fact stated as a cost.

That is why the no go is the deepest one. Putting it high in the string would deny access to everything under it.

## Why this matters for the next module

The through bore of a completion is a running minimum from the top, and in this string the minimum is set by the no go nipple, which sits near the very bottom. That is not an accident of the design; it is what a no go nipple is for.

A course that treated the smallest bore as a problem to be engineered away would have missed the point of the component that causes it.
