# The body OD is the coupling OD

The default rule that governs most of the catalog, and the reason it is the default.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## The rule

Most completion equipment is machined to the same outside diameter as the coupling of the tubing it is made up into.

Three and a half inch external upset tubing has a coupling outside diameter of four and a half inches. So the flow coupling, the blast joint, the landing nipples and the wireline entry guide are all four and a half inches on the outside, even though the pipe body is three and a half.

## Why that is the sensible default

Because the coupling is going to be there anyway. Every thirty feet of string carries one, so the largest thing that has to pass the casing is already the coupling, whatever the equipment does.

Making a nipple as wide as the coupling costs nothing in runability and buys wall thickness, which buys a machined profile without weakening the pipe. Making it narrower would buy nothing, because the coupling above it still has to go down.

## Where the rule breaks

Four types in the three and a half inch kit are wider than the coupling: the safety valve, the side pocket mandrel, the sliding sleeve and the expansion joint.

Each has a physical reason. A safety valve has to house a flapper and a hydraulic piston around a full bore. A side pocket mandrel has to carry a valve in a pocket beside the bore, so it is deliberately eccentric. A sliding sleeve has to have an outer sleeve and ports. An expansion joint has to have one tube sliding inside another.

## And where it breaks the other way

One type in the kit is narrower than the coupling: the perforated joint, which is a plain pipe body with holes in it and no upset at all, at three and a half inches.

That makes it the easiest thing in the string to run and the one that never appears in a fit discussion.

## Why the rule is worth knowing

Because it tells you where to look. If most of the kit is at the coupling diameter, then the fit problem is entirely in the four exceptions, and a size question about a completion is really a question about four components.

That is the argument the size matrix in the next tier makes quantitatively, and it comes out the same way every time.
