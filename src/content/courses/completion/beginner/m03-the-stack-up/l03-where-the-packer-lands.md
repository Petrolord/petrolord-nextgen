# Where the packer lands

The most consequential depth in the tally, what depends on it, and what happens when it moves.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## Four things read it

The packer depth is not just another row. Four separate calculations depend on it.

The clearance check reads it to find which casing bore the packer body has to pass through. The annulus volume above the packer is integrated down to it. The volume below the packer is integrated from it. And the through bore at the packer is what a tool has to pass to get to anything below.

## Where it lands in the published string

At two thousand six hundred point five metres, which is the bottom of the ninth component. The string continues below it: two metres of tubing, a no go nipple, a perforated joint and an entry guide, four more components in a little under six metres.

Those four are the tail of the completion, below the seal, in the reservoir interval.

## What sets it

The reservoir does. The packer goes above the top perforation with enough distance to be in competent casing and away from the perforated interval, and that distance is a completion engineering choice rather than a geometric one.

What this course contributes is the constraint from the other direction: the packer body has to get down to that depth. In the published string it is the tightest component on the whole sheet, and the depth it lands at is exactly the depth that decides which casing string it has to pass through.

## The consequence of moving it

Move the packer up by two hundred metres in the published well and it no longer has to pass the liner at all. It would then be checked against the nine and five eighths inch casing, and its clearance would go from under five millimetres to over sixty.

Nothing about the packer changed. The constraint on it changed, because the geometry above it changed. That is worth holding onto, because it is the central point of the whole clearance module in the next tier.

## And the constraint the reservoir imposes

You cannot use that freedom. The packer has to be above the perforations and below the liner top, and in this well that leaves it inside the liner. The tight clearance is not a design mistake; it is the consequence of a reservoir depth and a casing program that were both fixed before the completion was designed.
