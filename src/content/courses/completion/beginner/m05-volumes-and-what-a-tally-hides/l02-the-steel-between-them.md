# The steel between them

The difference of the two string volumes is a physical object, and it is worth computing on its own.

{{panel:cd-string-explorer}}

## The subtraction

Displacement less capacity is the volume of steel in the string. For the published completion that is a little under fifteen cubic metres.

It is the only number in this module that you can weigh. Multiply it by the density of steel and you have the weight of the string in air, which is a number the rig cares about a great deal.

## Why it is a good check

The steel volume has to be positive for every component and therefore for the string. If a tally has a swapped diameter pair somewhere, the steel volume of that component is negative, and while the total may still come out positive, the component level number is unmistakable.

That is a second line of defence behind the input refusal. The engine will not accept a bore larger than the body, so the case should not arise, and the steel volume is a way of checking that the refusal did its job.

## What it tells you about the string

Fifteen cubic metres of steel in two and a half kilometres of string is about six litres per metre. That is a heavy wall for the size, and it comes from the external upset tubing, which is thicker than plain end pipe.

Compare against the capacity, which is about four and a half litres per metre. There is more steel in this string than there is fluid inside it, which surprises people who have only ever thought about tubing as a conduit.

## What it does not tell you

It does not tell you the weight in the well, which is the weight in air less the buoyancy, and buoyancy depends on the fluid the string is run in. That calculation belongs to the Casing and Tubing Design course.

It does not tell you the load on the hanger, which also involves the annulus fluid, the packer and any set down or pick up. And it does not tell you whether the string will buckle, which needs pressures and temperatures.

## The useful habit

Compute all three volumes, not two. Capacity, displacement and the difference. The third is free, it is a physical quantity, and it is the one that catches an arithmetic error in either of the other two.
