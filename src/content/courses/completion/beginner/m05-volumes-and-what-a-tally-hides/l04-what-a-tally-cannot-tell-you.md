# What a tally cannot tell you

The limits of the beginner tier, stated honestly, before the next tier extends them.

## What you can now do

From a component list and a casing program you can build a stack up, compute a drift for any tubular in the catalog, compute the capacity and displacement of a string, and read a tally well enough to predict what the fit checks will say.

That is a real skill and it covers a large fraction of what a completion tally is used for day to day.

## What you cannot do yet

You cannot say whether the string will go in the hole. That needs the clearance check, which compares each component against the casing above it, and that is the next tier.

You cannot say what will get through it afterwards. That needs the through bore, which is a running minimum rather than a sum.

You cannot say how much packer fluid the annulus takes. That needs the casing program integrated against the string, and it needs to know where the packer is.

## What the tally structurally cannot tell you at all

Three things, and no amount of extra tiers will change them.

It cannot tell you whether the string can be run in a deviated well, because there is no survey and no friction model. Geometry says it fits; mechanics says whether it goes.

It cannot tell you whether the completion will produce, because there is no reservoir in it.

And it cannot tell you whether the equipment is rated for the well, because there is no pressure, temperature or fluid composition in it. A component that fits perfectly may be entirely wrong for the service.

## The honest summary

A tally establishes that a completion is geometrically possible. Everything else about whether it is a good completion comes from elsewhere.

That is not a weak claim. Geometric possibility is a hard constraint that binds before anything else, and a great many completion problems are geometric problems that were discovered late.
