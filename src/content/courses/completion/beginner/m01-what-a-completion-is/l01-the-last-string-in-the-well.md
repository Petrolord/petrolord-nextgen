# The last string in the well

What a completion is, what it is made of, and why its design problem is a geometry problem before it is anything else.

## The string that stays

Drilling puts a hole in the ground and casing lines it. A completion is what goes inside that casing and stays there for the producing life of the well: a string of tubing with equipment made up into it at chosen depths, hung off at surface and sealed into the casing near the reservoir.

Everything above it is temporary. The drill string comes out, the work string comes out, the running tools come out. The completion is the only thing in the hole when the well is handed over.

## What it has to do

Four things, and the course cares about the first three in geometric terms.

It has to carry the produced fluid from the reservoir to surface inside a conduit of known size. It has to isolate the annulus above the reservoir so that pressure is contained by the tubing rather than by the casing. It has to allow tools to be run inside it later, on wireline or coiled tubing, without dressing the well over. And it has to be removable, or at least recoverable, when the well is worked over.

## Why the design problem is geometry

Read those four again and notice how much of each is a question about diameters. A conduit of known size is an inside diameter. Isolating the annulus is a packer that has to seal against a casing bore. Running tools later is a through bore. Recovering the string is clearance in the other direction.

That is why this course starts with the drift of a piece of casing and not with reservoir inflow. Before you can ask whether a completion produces well, you have to establish that it can be got into the hole at all, that it can be got through afterwards, and that it can be got out.

## What the well already fixed

The casing program is finished before this course starts. Its sizes, weights and setting depths were decided by pressure containment, by hole stability and by cost, none of which had the completion in mind. The completion designer inherits those bores and works inside them.

That inheritance is the whole tension in the subject. Every fit question in this course is a question about a decision somebody else already made.

## Exercise

Write down the four things a completion has to do, in your own words.

For each one, say which single diameter in the well it depends on most: a tubing inside diameter, a tubing outside diameter, a casing inside diameter, or a casing outside diameter.

Then say which of the four you could still get wrong even if every diameter in the well were correct.
