# Onward

The portfolio engine chooses whole projects under a limit and reports how widely their risked value could fall, and the AFE engine tracks the spending once a project is authorised. Be clear about what that earns and where it hands over.

## What it earns

It turns an inventory into a defensible set. OKONO at 450.0000 funds OK-1 + OK-3 + OK-4 for 291.0000, beating the ranking by EMV per million USD, and the frontier shows OK-3 dropped at 600.0000 for OK-1 + OK-2 + OK-4 + OK-5 at 402.7500. The seeded simulation turns each set into a loss probability anyone can reproduce: 0.123600 and a Low case P90 of -18.3574 at 450.0000, seed 20260829, 10000 iterations.

It also turns an AFE into a dated report. OFON-1 as of 2027-08-15 forecasts 27600000 by one rule, earns 15231500, and runs at a CPI of 1.009377 and an SPI of 0.872063, with each partner billed by working interest and the operator carrying 25.0000 percent. Every one of those numbers carries its date.

## Where it hands over

The portfolio engine takes each NPV as typed. It does not shape a development, choose a well count, phase capex or say when first production arrives. The AFE engine takes the budget as authorised and the plan as a straight line, and it never asks whether the budget was right. Neither engine says whether a project should exist at all. Those questions belong to field development planning, the next course in the Economics path, where a concept becomes the project whose NPV, capex and phasing engines like these take as inputs.

## The limit worth remembering

A portfolio number without its seed, iterations and correlation is not a number, and neither is an AFE number without its as-of date, its rule and its actuals total. OKONO's 450.0000 set reads P(loss) from 0.119100 to 0.128500 across three seeds, and OFON-1's SPI reads 1.141290 at 2027-06-30 and 0.872063 at 2027-08-15 on the same lines. Each is right for its condition, and none is right without it.

## What you have finished
