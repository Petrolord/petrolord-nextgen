# What an extra million buys

Raising a budget buys whatever the next frontier point adds, and only once the new money reaches it. On OKONO, going from 450.0000 to 600.0000 million USD lifts the best risked EMV from 291.0000 to 402.7500 in four steps whose ratios run 0.733333, 0.533333, 1.191667 and 0.633333.

| 15 | 600.0000 | 402.7500 | 38.0000 | 60.0000 | 0.633333 |

These are the points of the frontier run to a limit of 600.0000; its points 0 to 11 are the same as the frontier run to 450.0000.

## One more million buys nothing

At a budget of exactly 450.0000 the best value is 291.0000. One more million leaves it at 291.0000, and so does every million until the budget reaches 480.0000, where the value steps to 313.0000. The ratio 0.733333 is 22.0000 over 30.0000, an average across a whole step. It is not a price per million that can be bought a million at a time, because no project can be funded in part. Every extra million inside a step buys 0.0000.

## Each step changes the set

Step 12 funds OK-2, OK-4 and OK-5 at 480.0000 for 313.0000, dropping both OK-1 and OK-3 from the 450.0000 answer. Prove it from the inventory: capex of 180.0000, 240.0000 and 60.0000 sums to 480.0000, and risked EMV of 115.0000, 160.0000 and 38.0000 sums to 313.0000. Step 13 funds OK-1, OK-3, OK-4 and OK-5 at 510.0000 for 329.0000, which is the 450.0000 set with OK-5 added, and OK-2 is out again. Step 14 funds OK-1, OK-2 and OK-4 at 540.0000 for 364.7500. Step 15 adds OK-5 to that set, 364.7500 plus 38.0000, which is 402.7500 at 600.0000.

Step 14 gains 35.7500 at 1.191667, the same figures as step 8. It is the same trade: OK-3 and OK-5 out, OK-2 in, for 30.0000 more capex.

## The mistake

The expensive mistake is to extrapolate the last step. At 450.0000 the step just taken bought 0.108333 per extra million USD, and a reader who projects that forward argues that more budget is barely worth having. The next step buys 0.733333, and step 14 buys 1.191667. On a frontier of whole projects the ratios do not fall steadily, so the value of more money is read from the points ahead, never from the step behind. The honest figure for this budget change is the difference between two last points: 402.7500 at 600.0000 against 291.0000 at 450.0000.

The opposite mistake asks for one more million because 0.733333 looks attractive. That million buys 0.0000; the argument needs the full 30.0000 to 480.0000.

A third mistake compares a step ratio with a project's own. Step 12's 0.733333 is close to OK-1's 0.747917, and OK-1 is the project step 12 drops.

## What it refuses

The frontier assumes the inventory is fixed. It cannot fund part of a project, phase a capex across years, or say what an extra million does to the spread. It prices a budget change only between the points it has solved.
