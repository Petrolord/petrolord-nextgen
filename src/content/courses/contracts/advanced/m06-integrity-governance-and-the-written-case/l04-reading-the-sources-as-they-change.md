# Reading the sources as they change

Every rule this course teaches comes from a dated text, and texts change. An expert contract manager reads each source with its edition and date, and checks it again before relying on it.

## This course's own dates

The sources for this course were checked on 27 September 2026, and the course is due for review by 27 September 2027. Each lesson names the edition it read: the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010); the Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007); GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026); the Sourcing Playbook (June 2023); the World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025); and the World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024). The UK Procurement Act 2023 was read as enacted, dated 26 October 2023 on its title page; amendments made after enactment are not shown in that copy, so a rule from it is checked against the current text before a decision relies on it.

## Guidelines under an Act

The NCDMB Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) revise the guidelines issued in April 2018 to reflect an industry service level agreement of September 2023 and Presidential Directives of February 2024 (Purpose (ii)). They add the Nigerian Content Compliance Commitment at award (Purpose (vii) and Step 5). NCDMB's Joint Qualification System guideline (Issue D1), read by concept, makes that system the single platform for registration, pre-qualification, compliance certificates and reports (sections 1 and 2).

## Doubts stay doubts

Some printed texts carry problems a reader should see. The printed Public Procurement Act 2007, s.16(12), gives its record retention period as "often years from the date of the award", which looks like a misprint; this course teaches no retention period until an official copy confirms one. The World Bank guidance gives 10% of the contract price only as an illustration of a delay damages limit (p.38), so it is taught as an illustration.

## A habit for every decision

Before relying on a rule: name the text, its edition and the date read; check the issuer's site for a newer edition; and read the section itself. Say which kind of source it is: Nigerian law, UK guidance used as practice, or World Bank practice.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic well services call-off framework with supplier WS-A (synthetic), with a Nigerian content plan and quarterly reporting. The scenario adds its own facts: the framework is being renewed, and the draft contract management procedure cites the NCDMB contracting-process guidelines of April 2018 and says nothing about a compliance commitment at award. Write a short note on what you will check and change.

### A reading

The note says the procedure cites the 2018 guidelines, which the NCDMB 2025 update revises. Check the current guidelines, then add the Nigerian Content Compliance Commitment at award and the Compliance Certificate at completion to the procedure. Check that registration and reports run through the Joint Qualification System as NCDMB's guideline describes. Record each source with its edition and the date read, and diarise a fresh check of each.
