# Fraud and corruption in execution

Fraud and corruption are often pictured at the tender, yet much of the risk sits in execution: change orders, invoices, materials and payments. The contract manager sees those documents first.

## Red flags in execution

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, lists red flags during execution: poor quality materials or workmanship, change orders without contractual justification, unjustified changes in specification or conditions, payments that do not follow the contract, and false or duplicate invoices (p.41).

## What the Nigerian public procurement law makes an offence

The Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) binds federal procuring entities; for an operator it is a published statement of public procurement practice. Its s.58(4) lists offences including collusive pricing, procurement fraud by corrupt acts, bribery or undue influence, splitting tenders to dodge thresholds, bid-rigging, altering procurement documents, using fake documents, and refusing the Bureau access to procurement records. Section 58(6) sets penalties of "(a) debarment from all public procurements for a period not less than 5 calendar years ; and (b) a fine equivalent to 25% of the value of the procurement in issue." Individuals face custodial penalties under s.58(1), (5) and (7).

The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) binds every Ekene contract, and its s.68 makes carrying out a project contrary to the content Act an offence, with a fine of five per cent of the project sum or cancellation of the project.

## Audit and reporting

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), also taught by concept, require Bank-financed contracts to let the Bank inspect all accounts, records and documents on procurement and contract execution and have them audited, and treat impeding those rights as an obstructive practice (Annex IV, para 2.2 e). On Bank-financed contracts the Contract Management Practice guidance says suspected fraud or corruption should be reported promptly to the Bank's Integrity Vice Presidency (p.40). On an Ekene contract the route is Ekene's own policy, and the principle is the same: report promptly, keep the records intact.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic instrument maintenance service with supplier IM-F (synthetic), reimbursed at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost, with a monthly service report. The scenario adds its own facts: in checking two monthly invoices, you find the same technician's hours charged on two sites on the same days, and a materials line priced well above the supplier's own purchase invoice that is attached. Write a short red-flag note and say to whom it goes.

### A reading

The note records the facts: the duplicated hours by technician and date, and the materials line against the attached purchase invoice, since materials are payable at cost. Both match red flags the World Bank guidance lists: false or duplicate invoices and payments that do not follow the contract. Hold the disputed amounts under the contract's payment terms, keep the originals intact, and report through Ekene's integrity route to the contract owner. Any investigation belongs to the people Ekene's policy names.
