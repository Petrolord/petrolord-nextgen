# Governance and assurance of the contract portfolio

Governance decides who is accountable for each contract and how much attention each gets; assurance checks that contracts are run as their terms require.

## Accountability is named

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), the UK commercial functional standard used as published good practice, places a senior business owner above each contract or group of related contracts, owning its justification and accountable for parts of its governance (4.6.5). The contract manager answers to that person for day-to-day delivery (4.6.6). The UK Contract Management Principles put the rule in one line: "Be clear about accountability, roles and responsibilities. Ensure contract ownership, management processes and governance mechanisms are clear with defined roles and responsibilities at appropriate levels of seniority." (principle 2).

In Nigerian public procurement, the Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), which binds federal procuring entities and serves an operator as published practice, fixes personal accountability at s.16(21): "The accounting officer of a procuring entity and any officer to whom responsibility is delegated are responsible and accountable for any actions taken or omitted to be taken either in compliance with or in contravention of this Act."

## Attention follows risk

Principle 4 of the UK Contract Management Principles directs "the most/strongest resource to contracts where the risks and rewards are highest." A portfolio view ranks contracts by risk and value and sets review rhythm and assurance to match.

## Assurance checks the records

GovS 008 says at 5.4.4: "Payments should be audited periodically to ensure they reflect the contract terms and service levels received from suppliers." Outside checks exist too. The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) gives the Board the function to "make auditing procedures and conduct regular audits for the purposes of monitoring and implementing compliances with the provisions of this Act;" (s.70(k)), and s.64 requires operators and contractors to give the Board access to the documentation that substantiates the content they report. Audit as a discipline belongs to the compliance course; this lesson reads what a contract portfolio must be ready for.

## Exercise

The scenario is Ekene's synthetic contract portfolio, EKC-01 to EKC-07: well services (WS-A), vessel charter (MV-B), catering (CF-C), single-source tubulars (TB-D), lump-sum flowline works (FW-E), reimbursable instrument maintenance (IM-F) and a monitoring consultancy (EM-G). The scenario adds its own fact: one senior business owner, three contract managers and no payment audit so far. Write a one-page governance map.

### A reading

The map names the senior business owner and assigns each contract a contract manager, with roles written down as principle 2 advises. It ranks the contracts by risk and value: the well services framework, the single-source tubulars agreement and the vessel charter need the strongest resource; the flowline works and reimbursable maintenance need close cost control; catering and the consultancy follow service levels and acceptance. It schedules a periodic payment audit, starting with the reimbursable IM-F contract, as GovS 008 5.4.4 advises, and it keeps each contract's content records ready for the Board's audits under the content Act s.64 and s.70(k).
