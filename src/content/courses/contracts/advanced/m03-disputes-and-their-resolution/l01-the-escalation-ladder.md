# The escalation ladder

A dispute is a claim or other matter the parties have not agreed. Most contracts set a ladder for it: the people closest to the work try first, then more senior people, then a third party who helps or decides, and only at the top arbitration or the courts. Higher rungs usually cost more time and money, so the contract manager's aim is to settle a matter on the lowest rung that can settle it fairly.

## The process is set in advance

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), the UK commercial functional standard used here as published good practice, says:

> "A process for handling and resolving commercial or legal disputes with suppliers should be defined and subject to governance controls in line with organisational policy."

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, adds that an alternative dispute mechanism such as an adjudicator, a dispute review expert or a dispute review board has to be set up on time; trying to create one after a dispute has started tends to fail (p.36).

## The top of the ladder

The World Bank guidance describes a range of techniques, from informal talks, through formal negotiation, to mediation and arbitration; it asks that a dispute be managed actively and at the right level, which is where senior representatives come in, and it treats arbitration and litigation as the last resort (p.35). In Nigerian public contracts, the Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), which binds federal procuring entities and serves an operator as published practice, provides at s.16(26): "All procurement contracts shall contain provisions for arbitral proceedings as the primary forms of dispute resolution."

## A typical ladder

1. The contract manager and the supplier's manager meet, with the record.
2. Senior representatives of each party meet.
3. A third party helps (mediation) or decides (an expert, adjudicator or board), as the contract states.
4. Arbitration or the courts, as the contract states.

The next three lessons take the rungs in turn.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is the synthetic camp catering and facility services contract with supplier CF-C (synthetic), a fixed monthly fee with service levels and service credits. The scenario adds its own facts: CF-C disputes three months of service credits for cleanliness audits, saying the audit method changed without agreement. The contract has a dispute clause naming senior meetings and then arbitration, with nothing between. Write the ladder you will follow now, and one change you will propose for the renewal.

### A reading

Now: the contract manager meets CF-C's manager with the audit records and the contract's audit method, then escalates to senior representatives if needed; arbitration follows only if the contract's lower rungs fail, since the World Bank guidance treats it as the last resort. For the renewal: add a middle rung, such as mediation or an expert for technical questions like audit method, set up in the contract from the start, as the World Bank guidance advises, under a defined process of the kind GovS 008 expects.
