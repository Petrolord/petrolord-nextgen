# Expert determination and adjudication

On the middle rungs of the ladder a third party decides. The names vary with the contract: an expert, an adjudicator, a dispute review expert, a dispute review board, or the Engineer under a works form. What they share is a decision made faster than arbitration by someone who knows the subject, with its force set by the contract.

## What the published practice describes

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, says that depending on the contract, alternative dispute resolution may use an adjudicator, a dispute review expert or a dispute review board. It stresses setting the mechanism up on time, because trying to create it after a dispute starts tends to fail (p.36).

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), also taught by concept, go further for international competitive procurement on Bank-financed contracts: the contract states the applicable law and forum, includes independent dispute resolution through dispute review experts or boards, and normally provides international commercial arbitration in a neutral venue (Annex IX, paras 2.24 to 2.25). For Ekene that Bank rule is a published model, with a decision-maker in the middle of the ladder.

## The Engineer's determination

Under the FIDIC conditions (Second Edition 2017, reprinted 2022), licensed and taught by concept, the Engineer, or the Employer's Representative in design and build forms, makes a fair determination of a claim under the contract. It is the first decision on a claim; the contract then provides what a party does if it disagrees.

## Reading the clause

Because the force of each decision is set by the contract, the contract manager reads four things in the dispute clause:

- who decides, and how that person or board is appointed;
- what questions they may decide (technical, financial, or any matter);
- whether the decision binds at once, binds unless challenged, or only advises;
- what a party must do, and by when, to take the matter further.

Each is read from the signed Ekene contract.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic instrument maintenance service with supplier IM-F (synthetic), with KPIs for planned work completed and repeat failures. The scenario adds its own facts: IM-F and Ekene disagree whether six failures in a quarter count as repeat failures, because the KPI definition does not say whether a failure after a part replacement counts. The contract's dispute clause goes from senior meetings straight to arbitration. Write a short note recommending a mechanism for this question and the clause points the renewal should settle.

### A reading

An expert suits this question: it is technical and turns on records. The current contract has no such rung, so one can be used now only if both parties agree. For the renewal, the World Bank guidance supports setting the mechanism up in the contract from the start. The clause should settle who decides and how they are appointed, which questions they decide, whether the decision binds, and the steps and times to take it further. The KPI definition itself should also be fixed.
