# Delivery models and make or buy

Before a strategy chooses a contract, it asks whether a contract is the answer at all. Some work is better done by the operator's own people, some is better bought, and some is split. The UK texts call this question the delivery model, and their method is openly licensed and worth borrowing as published good practice.

## What a delivery model assessment is

The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), a UK Cabinet Office text, defines it:

> "A delivery model assessment (formerly known as a ‘make versus buy assessment’) is an analytical, evidenced-based approach to reach a recommendation on whether a contracting authority should deliver a service or part of a service in-house, procure from the market or adopt a hybrid solution."

Three outcomes are named: in-house, market, or a hybrid. The word that matters is evidenced: the recommendation rests on written data about cost, capability, risk and the market that someone else can check.

## How long it takes

The UK Delivery model assessments guidance note (May 2021) says the time needed depends on how complex the service is, the data available and the resources given to the work. It offers 3-6 months as an indication for an initial assessment of a service of mid-level complexity (para 2.2.1). On an Ekene renewal the lesson is to start early enough to finish before the contract ends.

## What the contract manager brings

The contract manager holds the evidence the assessment needs: the KPI history, the change register, the invoices, the service reports and the record of every problem. The Playbook's Chapter 12 (p.68) adds a point that shapes any buy decision: "All outsourced services should be built on a robust contractual relationship overseen by an appropriately qualified contract manager with a clear operational understanding of the contract." A market option that the operator cannot staff with such a manager is weaker than it looks on paper.

## The Nigerian dimension

In Nigeria a buy decision also brings the content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) into the contract. Section 10(1)(b) gives Nigerians "first consideration for training and employment in the work programme for which the plan was submitted", and the contract must carry reporting duties under s.65. An in-house option carries the same employment duties through the operator's own plan.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is the synthetic camp catering and facility services contract with supplier CF-C (synthetic), for a fixed monthly fee with service levels and service credits. The scenario adds its own facts: camp maintenance requests have missed their service level for two quarters, and the maintenance engineer proposes taking camp maintenance in-house. Write a short list of the evidence you would put in front of a delivery model assessment, and name the three outcomes it could reach.

### A reading

The evidence: service level records and service credits applied; the causes of the misses; the cost of the maintenance element inside the fixed fee; the operator's own staff, skills and tools; and the market for camp maintenance. The three outcomes, as the Sourcing Playbook names them, are in-house, market, or a hybrid (for example catering bought and maintenance done in-house). Whichever is chosen, the content Act's employment and reporting duties follow the work.
