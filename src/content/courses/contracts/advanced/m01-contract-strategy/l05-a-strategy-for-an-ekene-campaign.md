# A strategy for an Ekene campaign

This lesson joins the module's decisions into one written strategy for a campaign that needs several contracts at once. Its test: could a contract manager run each contract from it on the first day?

## Start from what is known

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught by concept as published practice, match contract type to knowledge: lump sum where scope can be clearly specified and tied to milestones, unit price where the work is well defined and the quantities are uncertain, and reimbursable cost for emergency repairs with open records (Annex VIII, paras 3.3, 3.7 and 3.13). A campaign strategy begins by stating, contract by contract, what is known and what is uncertain.

## Write the management into each contract

The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), a UK Cabinet Office text used as published good practice, says that how a contract will be managed "shall be reflected in the contractual agreement" (Chapter 12, p.68). Its Chapter 7 (p.48) recommends the UK Model Services Contract as a starting point and lists what users should tailor: performance indicators and service credits, insurance and parent company guarantees, invoicing and indexation, pricing, benchmarking, financial distress, and the governance and contract management structure.

The World Bank Regulations add that a contract management plan sets key performance indicators and milestone events (Annex I, para 3.7).

## Carry the Nigerian content duties

The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), s.6, provides that "all subsequent oil and gas arrangements, agreements, contracts or memoranda of understanding relating to any operation or transaction in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall be in conformity with the provisions of this Act." The NCDMB Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) add a Nigerian Content Compliance Commitment at award, recording the content targets the operator and contractor agree for execution (Purpose (vii) and Step 5). The strategy names where each contract's commitments are measured and reported.

## One page per contract

For each contract: the scope and its uncertainty; the price basis and the reason; the KPIs and review rhythm; the tailored clauses; the content commitments; and the interfaces with the other contracts.

## Exercise

The scenario is a synthetic Ekene workover campaign on four wells, with three synthetic contracts: EKC-01, the well services call-off framework with WS-A (schedule of rates, KPIs for mobilisation, supplier-caused non-productive time and safety reporting); EKC-02, the vessel time charter with MV-B (day rate, availability as the service level); and EKC-04, the tubulars frame agreement with TB-D (unit prices, single qualified source for one connection). The scenario adds its own fact: the order of wells may change once the first well is opened. Write the one-page strategy outline for the campaign.

### A reading

A strong outline keeps the three price bases, since each matches what is known. It names the interfaces: well order changes flow to call-offs, vessel days and tubular orders together, so one change route serves all three. It flags TB-D as the single-source risk. It lists each contract's KPIs, review rhythm and tailored clauses, and, under the content Act s.6 and the NCDMB guidelines, the content commitments each contract carries and who reports them.
