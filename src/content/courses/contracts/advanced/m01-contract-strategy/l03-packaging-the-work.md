# Packaging the work

Packaging decides how the work is cut into contracts: one large contract, several smaller ones, or a framework with call-offs. The cut sets the interfaces a contract manager will police, and which rules apply.

## Packaging is a named part of the strategy

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught by concept as published practice, list the contracting strategy among the contents of a procurement strategy, and they describe it as how the work is packaged and which contract types are used (Annex V, para 3.19(e)).

## Two questions that shape each package

The World Bank's PPSD guidance (Fourth Edition, August 2025) offers a supply positioning model that places each purchase by its supply risk and its value (p.17). It usually counts a contract as high cost when its estimate reaches 1 percent of the project's total estimated procurement cost (Figure IV and Table IV). The figure belongs to Bank-financed projects; the idea travels: a package that joins a scarce, high-risk item to routine work carries that risk into the whole contract.

The UK Contract Management Principles give the management side: "Adopt a differentiated approach based on risk; distinguish between tactical and strategic contracts, direct the most/strongest resource to contracts where the risks and rewards are highest." (principle 4). Each package should be one the operator can resource at the level its risk needs.

## Packages and the content Act

The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) attaches duties to contract size. Section 34 provides that "all projects or contracts whose total budget exceeds $100 million (USD) shall contain a "Labour Clause" mandating the use of a minimum percentage of Nigerian labour in specific cadres as may be stipulated by the Board." A packaging decision changes which contracts cross that line, and a strategy note should say so openly.

## Packaging is honest or it is an offence

The Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) binds federal procuring entities; for an operator it is a published statement of public procurement practice. Its s.58(4) lists splitting tenders to dodge thresholds among its offences. For any Ekene package: cut by interfaces, risk and management capacity, and record the reason.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic well services call-off framework with supplier WS-A (synthetic), priced from a schedule of rates. EKC-04 is the synthetic casing and tubulars frame agreement with supplier TB-D (synthetic), the only supplier qualified for one premium connection. The scenario adds its own facts: a planner proposes folding the tubulars supply into the next well services framework so that one supplier answers for the whole well. Write a short note giving your recommendation and its reasons.

### A reading

The sources favour keeping the packages apart. Joining them carries TB-D's single-source supply risk into the whole well services contract, as the PPSD supply positioning idea warns, and makes WS-A answer for an item it cannot source elsewhere. Separate packages let each get the resource its risk needs, as principle 4 advises. The note should also record whether either package crosses the content Act s.34 labour clause line, and it should state the reason for the cut in writing.
