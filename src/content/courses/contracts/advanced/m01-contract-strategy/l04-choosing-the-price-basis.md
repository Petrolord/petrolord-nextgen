# Choosing the price basis for the risk

The price basis is where the strategy allocates cost risk, and each basis fits a different state of knowledge about the scope. Computing the cost of each basis under uncertainty belongs to the procurement course; this lesson reads the choice from published practice.

## What the World Bank texts describe

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) are taught by concept. Under a lump-sum contract the contractor performs a defined scope for a fixed amount (Annex VIII, para 3.2). A unit price contract pays the quantities actually delivered at stated unit prices (para 3.6). A performance-based contract ties payment to measured outputs of quality, quantity and reliability (para 3.4).

The Regulations match the type to what is known (paras 3.3, 3.7 and 3.13): lump sum where the scope can be clearly specified and tied to milestones; unit price where the work is well defined and only its quantities are uncertain; reimbursable cost for emergency repairs, with open records and incentives that limit cost.

## What the UK pricing guidance adds

The UK Risk Allocation and Pricing Approaches guidance note (January 2026), openly licensed UK good practice, ties a fixed price to a fixed scope. Where scope floats or is still unknown, a fixed price does not fit, and where base prices cannot be estimated at all, cost plus may be the only option (Appendix II, p.34). Cost plus pays allowable costs plus a margin, suits novel work, and demands open book transparency and real effort to police allowable costs (p.38).

Between the two sits a guaranteed maximum price with a target cost: savings below target are shared, overruns above target are shared equally up to a cap, and the UK model services contract sets that cap 10% above the target price (p.43). On inflation the note says: "For contracts with considerable inflation uncertainty, using indexation is advisable." (para 6.1.6). The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) records that price adjustment clauses are established practice for contracts running more than about 18 months (p.24).

## What each basis asks of the contract manager

- Lump sum: firm control of scope, since every scope change is priced.
- Unit price: measurement of quantities actually delivered.
- Reimbursable or cost plus: audit of allowable costs and hours.
- Target cost: an agreed record of actual cost.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic flowline replacement works contract with supplier FW-E (synthetic), a lump sum paid against milestones. The scenario adds its own facts: the next phase replaces buried flowlines whose condition is unknown until they are dug up, and the work will run over two years. Write a recommendation on the price basis for the next phase, with two reasons and one condition.

### A reading

A lump sum fits badly: the UK pricing guidance ties fixed price to fixed scope, and this scope is unknown until excavation. A unit price for the length replaced, or a target cost arrangement, fits better: the World Bank Regulations match unit price to well defined work with uncertain quantities. A price adjustment or indexation clause suits a contract of this length under both the UK note and the World Bank guidance. The condition: the contract manager must be resourced to measure quantities or record actual cost.
