# Lessons learned that change the next contract

A lesson is learned only when it changes something: a clause, a KPI, a price basis, a management practice in the next contract.

## The sources ask for lessons at close

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught by concept as published practice, require an evaluation of how the contract was carried out, made at completion, to assess performance and, where it applies, to draw lessons for later contracts (Annex XI, para 2.4). The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) asks for the changes made during the contract to be evaluated for their effect on cost, schedule and performance and kept as lessons for the future (p.30), and for the KPIs to be used in the post-contract review with the lessons recorded (p.94).

The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), a UK text, gives the lesson its time: "Sufficient time should be allocated to knowledge transfer and lessons learned analysis. Did the KPIs drive the required behaviours and outcomes from the contract?" (Chapter 13, p.72).

## Where lessons come from

The best sources are the contract manager's own registers: the change register (which changes were foreseeable), the claims and notices (which could have been prevented), the KPI history (which measures drove behaviour) and the dispute record.

## Lessons that affect the next award

In Nigeria, close-out feeds the next award directly. The NCDMB Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) ask, for a replacement contract, for evidence that the prior contract met its Nigerian content commitments, such as the 1% Nigerian Content Development Fund remittance and the human capital development execution (notes to NC Plan stage). A contract that closes without that evidence delays its successor.

## Writing a lesson that works

A usable lesson names what happened, the evidence, the cause, and the specific change for the next contract with an owner. It then goes where the next strategy will read it: the Sourcing Playbook's point that how a contract will be managed is a key strategic decision, taken early (Chapter 12, p.68), is where the loop closes.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic well services call-off framework with supplier WS-A (synthetic), priced from a schedule of rates, with KPIs including mobilisation on time, and quarterly Nigerian content reporting. The scenario adds its own facts: the framework is closing. The change register shows many call-offs for nitrogen services outside the schedule of rates; mobilisation KPIs were always met because the KPI counted from the call-off date set by WS-A; two quarterly content reports were late. Write three lessons in the form this lesson describes.

### A reading

One: nitrogen services were ordered often outside the rates; evidence, the change register; change, add them to the next schedule of rates; owner, the contract strategy lead. Two: the mobilisation KPI never failed because the supplier set the start date; evidence, the KPI history; change, count from Ekene's request date; owner, the contract manager. Three: content reports were late; evidence, the report log; change, a reporting date and remedy in the contract, and evidence gathered now so the NCDMB replacement-contract check is met; owner, the contract manager.
