# Evaluating the supplier at close

At close the contract manager writes the last assessment of the supplier's performance. It draws on the whole life of the contract and will be read when the supplier bids again, so it must be fair and evidenced.

## The KPIs are the spine

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, asks for the KPIs to be used in the review after the contract ends, and for the lessons to be recorded for future operations (p.94). The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), a UK text, asks the question that tests them:

> "Sufficient time should be allocated to knowledge transfer and lessons learned analysis. Did the KPIs drive the required behaviours and outcomes from the contract?"

## A UK model of the final assessment

For UK public contracts, the Procurement Act 2023 KPI guidance (April 2026 edition), used here as published good practice, has the authority "at least once in every twelve month period during the lifetime of the contract and on termination, assess the supplier’s performance against those KPIs" (para 14). Published assessments use five ratings set by regulation: Good (meeting or exceeding the KPI), Approaching target, Requires improvement (below the KPI), Inadequate (significantly below the KPI) and Other (para 15). The UK Contract Performance Notices guidance adds that when a contract ends, the final KPI notice is published before the contract termination notice, because the termination notice closes the contract's record (para 21).

None of these UK duties binds an Ekene contract. The model is still useful: a final assessment on termination, against the stated KPIs, with a small fixed set of ratings, completed before the file is closed.

## Hard and soft measures

The UK Contract Management Principles advise: "Use a balanced scorecard to measure ‘hard’ data such as KPI performance alongside ‘soft’ measures e.g. customer satisfaction and relationship management, with a focus on achievement of outcomes." (principle 7).

## Fairness

A close evaluation separates what the supplier controlled from what Ekene caused. Where Ekene's own late permits or payments drove a missed KPI, the evaluation says so, with the record.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic instrument maintenance service with supplier IM-F (synthetic), with KPIs for planned work completed and repeat failures, and a monthly service report. The scenario adds its own facts: the contract ends this month. Over its life, planned work completion met its KPI in most quarters and missed it in two quarters when Ekene's permit system was down; performance on repeat failures fell far short of its KPI throughout the last year; the operations team rates IM-F's technicians highly. Write the close evaluation in four short lines.

### A reading

Planned work completed: met in most quarters; the two misses coincided with Ekene's permit system outage and are recorded as Ekene-caused. Repeat failures: far short of the KPI in the final year, which on the UK scale would read as Inadequate (significantly below the KPI). Soft measures: technicians rated highly by operations, recorded as the balanced scorecard of principle 7 advises. Lesson: did the repeat failure KPI drive the right behaviour, as the Sourcing Playbook asks, and should its definition or remedy change in the next contract?
