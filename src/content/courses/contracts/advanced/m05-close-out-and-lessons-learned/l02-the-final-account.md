# The final account and releasing securities

The final account is the last statement of what the buyer owes the supplier under the contract: the original price, every approved change and settled claim, the deductions, and what has already been paid. The contract manager checks it line by line.

## Correct errors before the final payment

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, says payment errors found before the final payment is made should be corrected (p.21). A final account is built from the records: the change register, the claim assessments, the payment certificates, the service credits applied and the retention held.

## Retention and securities

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), also taught by concept, allow a reasonable retention on works contracts that is released once the contractor has met its obligations (Annex IX, para 2.14). At start-up, the World Bank guidance has the employer check that any performance security matches the contract in amount, validity and wording (pp.13 to 14). At the end, the contract says when each security is returned, and every condition is checked before release.

## The final price against benchmarks

Among its value for money checks, the World Bank Regulations have the borrower confirm that the final price compares well with comparable benchmarks (Annex XI, para 3.3(f)). The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance asks for the changes made during the contract to be evaluated for their effect on cost, schedule and performance (p.30). For Ekene, the final account explains the final price against the award price, change by change.

## The Nigerian content remittance

The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act, Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), s.104(2), provides that one per cent of every contract awarded in the upstream sector "shall be deducted at source and paid into the Fund." The NCDMB Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) ask, for a replacement contract, for evidence that the prior contract met its content commitments, such as the 1% Nigerian Content Development Fund remittance (notes to NC Plan stage). The final account keeps that evidence.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic flowline replacement works contract with supplier FW-E (synthetic), a lump sum paid against milestones, with retention, a performance security and a defects period. The scenario adds its own facts: FW-E submits a final account with the lump sum, four variations, one settled delay claim and a request to release all retention and the performance security now. The change register shows three approved variations. The defects period has two months to run. Write your response to the final account.

### A reading

Check each line against the records: the lump sum; the three approved variations from the change register, with the fourth queried because it has no approval; the delay claim at its settled amount; payments already made. Correct any error before the final payment, as the World Bank guidance advises. Release retention and the security only on the conditions the contract states; with the defects period still running, check what the contract ties to its end. Confirm the content Act s.104(2) deduction evidence, and explain the final price against the award price by change.
