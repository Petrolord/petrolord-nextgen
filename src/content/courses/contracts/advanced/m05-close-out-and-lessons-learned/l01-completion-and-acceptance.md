# Completion and acceptance

Close-out starts when the supplier says the work is done. Completion is a claim about the contract, and acceptance is the buyer's decision that the claim is true. The contract manager tests one against the other before anything is released.

## Every way a contract ends

The UK Contract Terminations guidance under the Procurement Act 2023 (April 2026 edition), used as published good practice, gives a broad meaning of termination for its notices: discharge (obligations fulfilled, payments made, disputes settled), expiry, termination by a party, rescission, or being set aside by a court (para 8). Discharge, in that list, needs obligations fulfilled, payments made and disputes settled.

## Testing completion

Acceptance follows the contract's own test: the milestones, the specifications, the acceptance step, the documents to be handed over. Where the contract has a defects period, as EKC-05 does, the contract states when it starts and what the supplier owes during it. The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught by concept as published practice, allow a reasonable retention that is released once the contractor has met its obligations (Annex IX, para 2.14).

## Evaluating as the contract closes

The World Bank Regulations also require an evaluation of how the contract was carried out, made at completion, to assess performance and, where it applies, to draw lessons for later contracts (Annex XI, para 2.4).

## Nigerian content at completion

The NCDMB Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) provide that a Nigerian Content Compliance Certificate is issued by NCDMB's Monitoring and Evaluation Directorate once the contract is performed and the commitments recorded at award are met (Purpose (vii) and Step 5). Completion on an Ekene contract therefore includes the content commitments, and the evidence for them.

## Recording the close

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) says: "On contract closure, information systems should be updated, staff and facilities (if any) reassigned and the contract documentation archived in accordance with the organisation’s information retention policy and procedures." (5.4.7).

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic flowline replacement works contract with supplier FW-E (synthetic): a lump sum paid against milestones, a retention held from each payment until completion, a performance security, a defects period, and a Nigerian content plan covering fabrication and labour. The scenario adds its own facts: FW-E writes that the last flowline is in service and asks for completion to be confirmed. Write your completion checklist.

### A reading

The checklist: the final milestone tested against the specification; handover documents received; open changes and claims settled or listed; the defects period start recorded; retention and performance security released only as the contract provides; Nigerian content evidence for fabrication and labour gathered for the compliance certificate under the NCDMB guidelines; and the completion evaluation opened, as the World Bank Regulations require for Bank-financed contracts and good practice suggests for Ekene.
