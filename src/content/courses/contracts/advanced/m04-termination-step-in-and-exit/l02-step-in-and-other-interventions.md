# Step-in and other interventions

Between a formal notice and termination lies a range of stronger interventions. The Professional tier taught the first remedies; this lesson reads the heavier ones and the order in which a contract manager reaches for them.

## The tools the contract may hold

The UK Contract Management Principles, published by the Crown Commercial Service as good practice, name three: "Understand and use contractual options such as appointment of Remedial Advisor, Rectification Plans and Step In rights." (principle 3). The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), a UK text, lists protections against supply chain risk: "These include step-in rights, the approval of key sub-contractors and assignment and novation provisions." (Chapter 5, p.41).

The Principles call them contractual options: each exists on an Ekene contract only if that contract grants it, and works on the terms the contract sets.

## The ladder of remedies

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, lists typical employer remedies under a works contract in rising order: a notice to correct a default, withholding payment, calling the performance security, delay damages for late completion, and termination when a contractual termination event occurs (Figure XII, p.37).

On delay damages the same guidance explains that they are deducted as a percentage or an amount for late completion, usually with an aggregate limit; it gives 10% of the contract price as an illustration of such a limit. Reaching the limit usually permits termination, though continuing may still be the better option (p.38). The 10% is an illustration from World Bank guidance. An Ekene contract states its own rate and limit, if it has any.

## Choosing the intervention

The choice turns on three questions: what does the contract allow; which intervention restores the service fastest; and which keeps the record fair to both parties. GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) says corrective action is taken "within the terms of the contract" (5.4.3). The contract manager therefore uses only the interventions the contract grants.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is the synthetic platform supply vessel time charter with supplier MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown, and availability as its service level. The scenario adds its own facts: the vessel has had three breakdowns in two months, each put off-hire; MV-B's technical superintendent has not produced a repair plan; the charter contains a rectification plan clause and no step-in right. Write the interventions you will use, in order, with the contract basis for each.

### A reading

First, off-hire continues for each breakdown, as the charter provides. Second, a formal notice requiring a rectification plan under the charter's clause, with a date. Third, if the plan is not produced or not delivered, the charter's further remedies, read from its terms. Step-in is not available, because the charter does not grant it; the UK Contract Management Principles treat these tools as contractual options. Termination stays last, as GovS 008 and the World Bank guidance advise, and every step is recorded in writing.
