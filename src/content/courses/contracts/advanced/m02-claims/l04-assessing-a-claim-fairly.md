# Assessing a claim fairly

Fair assessment means the claim is valued as the contract and the evidence support, neither inflated to keep the supplier happy nor cut to protect a budget.

## The first question: was it already in scope?

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, puts one question before any pricing: whether the so-called extra work was already in the contract scope and has been labelled extra by mistake (p.52). Only once that is settled are the costs checked for relevance and reasonableness. The same guidance sets an order for pricing the costs: the contract's own unit rates first, then recorded historical costs, then market rates.

## Delay claims

For a claim that work was delayed, the World Bank guidance tests each part (p.52):

- idle labour and equipment: could they have been released or moved to other work;
- unabsorbed site and head office overheads;
- profit, checking whether the contract allows cost only or cost plus profit for this kind of event.

Each line is checked against the contract and the records, and each accepted amount is recorded with the reason.

## A determination under the contract

Under the FIDIC conditions (Second Edition 2017, reprinted 2022), licensed and taught by concept, the Engineer or the Employer's Representative makes a fair determination of a claim under the contract. Whatever the form, the principle holds for an Ekene contract manager: the assessment follows the contract's own terms, it is written down with its reasons, and it is given to the supplier.

## Controlling what is granted

The World Bank guidance expects the contract manager to track and control every increase that is granted (Cost Control, Figure VI, p.20). GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), the UK commercial functional standard used as published good practice, expects an audit trail in a change register and approvals obtained before a change is implemented (5.4.5). A settled claim that changes the contract price or time belongs in that register, with the approval that authorised it.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic flowline replacement works contract with supplier FW-E (synthetic), a lump sum paid against milestones. The scenario adds its own facts: Ekene accepts that it gave FW-E late access to the manifold. FW-E claims the full day cost of its crew and equipment for every day of the delay, site overheads, head office overheads and a profit margin. Its daily reports show the welding crew was moved to another client's job after the first two days. Write your assessment note, line by line.

### A reading

Entitlement is accepted for the late access. Idle labour and equipment are allowed only for the days they could not be released or redeployed: the daily reports show the welding crew moved after two days, so the crew's cost after that is disallowed. Site overheads are tested against records for the delay period. Head office overheads need evidence that they went unabsorbed. Profit depends on whether the contract allows cost plus profit for this event. Each accepted line is valued at contract rates first. The result is recorded in the change register with its approval.
