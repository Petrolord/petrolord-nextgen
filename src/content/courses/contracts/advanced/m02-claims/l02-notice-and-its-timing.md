# Notice and its timing

A claim usually starts with a notice: a written statement that an event has happened and that the supplier may ask for time or money because of it. Many contracts attach a period to the notice and another to the full submission, so the contract manager watches the clock on both sides.

## Why timing matters

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, stresses that a contractor gives notice of a claim and submits it on time, because a late notice or a late submission can carry consequences under the contract (p.51). What those consequences are is written in the contract itself. The contract manager reads the clause the parties signed and states it in the register.

The FIDIC conditions (Second Edition 2017, reprinted 2022), licensed and taught by concept, follow the same pattern: a notice first, then detailed particulars, then a determination by the Engineer or the Employer's Representative. The periods are in the particular contract and are read from it.

## Timing runs both ways

The same World Bank guidance says good practice prevents many claims, and one of its items is answering contractor notices on time (p.50). The contract manager therefore logs every notice received with its date, the clause it cites and the date a reply is due.

## The record of notices

The World Bank guidance lists what to record: how the supplier performed and delivered, every communication and notice, the dates, and who was involved (Figure XIV, p.38). A notice register built on that list lets anyone check later whether a notice was given, when, and whether it was answered.

## When a claim goes to mediation

If a claim later becomes a dispute and the parties mediate in Nigeria, the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023 (Nigeria) provides at s.71(1):

> "When the mediation proceedings commence, the running of the limitation period regarding the claim that is the subject matter of the mediation is suspended."

That statutory rule sits beside the contract's own notice periods and leaves them in place.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is the synthetic platform supply vessel time charter with supplier MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown. The scenario adds its own facts: the charter requires MV-B to give written notice of any event for which it will seek extra payment within a period stated in the charter. Ekene held the vessel at the field for weather over a weekend; MV-B mentioned it by phone on the Monday and sent a written notice five weeks later. Write the notice register entry and a short note on what you will check before replying.

### A reading

The register entry records the event, the phone call with its date and caller, the written notice with its date and the clause it cites, and the reply due date. Before replying, the contract manager reads the charter's notice clause to see whether the phone call counts as notice, whether the written notice falls inside the stated period, and what the charter says follows from a late notice. The World Bank guidance supports treating the timing seriously and recording every communication; the reply itself should go out promptly.
