# What a claim is

In this course a claim is a request for more time or money under the contract, and a dispute is a claim or other matter the parties have not agreed. The two words describe stages: most claims are settled without ever becoming disputes, and the way a contract manager handles a claim decides which way it goes.

## Claims live inside the contract

Many international works contracts use the FIDIC conditions (Second Edition 2017, reprinted 2022), which are licensed and taught here by concept only. In their approach, the conditions define what counts as a claim, set a procedure of notice followed by detailed particulars, and give the Engineer, or the Employer's Representative in design and build forms, the task of making a fair determination under the contract. The contract manager reads the signed contract for the procedure it actually holds.

## What a claim must state

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept as published practice, says a claim should state the breach of contract or the other legal basis it relies on (p.51). A letter that only says "costs went up" states none, and the reply asks for it.

## Claim, change and dispute

Three things arrive on a contract manager's desk looking alike:

- a change request, where one party proposes to alter the work, which is handled by the change control route the Professional tier teaches;
- a claim, where the supplier says the contract already entitles it to more time or money for something that happened;
- a dispute, where a claim or other matter has been answered and the parties still disagree.

Sorting them early matters, because each follows a different route in the contract.

## Most claims can be prevented

The same World Bank guidance says good practice prevents many claims: understand the contract, pay on time, define scope and specifications properly, give timely access to the site, and answer contractor notices on time (p.50). Each is a duty of the buyer's side, and each is something a contract manager controls.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic flowline replacement works contract with supplier FW-E (synthetic), a lump sum paid against milestones. The scenario adds its own facts: FW-E writes that its crew could not start on the agreed day because Ekene gave access to the manifold late, and asks for more money. The letter names no clause. Write a short note classifying the letter and saying what you will reply.

### A reading

The letter is a claim: it asks for more money for something that happened, which the supplier says Ekene caused. It becomes a dispute only if it is answered and the parties still disagree. The reply should ask FW-E to state the contractual or other legal basis it relies on, as the World Bank guidance expects, and to support the cost. The contract manager should also check Ekene's own record of site access, since late access is one of the buyer-side causes the guidance names.
