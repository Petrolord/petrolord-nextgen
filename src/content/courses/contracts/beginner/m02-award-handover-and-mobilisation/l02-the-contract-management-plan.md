# The contract management plan

A contract says what each party must do. A contract management plan says how the buyer will see that it is done: who watches what, how often, with which records. It is the contract manager's working document.

## Who writes it and who approves it

The UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) sets the plan out at 5.4.2:

> "A contract management plan should be established that defines the roles and the responsibilities of each party, and should be reviewed periodically throughout the life of the contract."

The same paragraph says "The plan should be produced by the contract manager (see 4.6.6) and be approved by the senior business owner (see 4.6.5)." So the manager writes it and the owner signs it off. The owner is accountable for the outcome, and the plan shows how it is protected.

## When it is written

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) expect the plan to be drafted while the contract is being created and finished by the time it is signed (Annex XI, para 2.3). For the contracts a borrower's procurement strategy identifies, the plan sets key performance indicators and milestone events (Annex I, para 3.7). This course reads these Regulations by concept as published practice.

## What it covers

Annex XI, para 3.1 of the World Bank Regulations lists what such a plan typically summarises. In this course's words:

- the risks identified and how each is mitigated;
- key contacts, roles and the authorisations each party has set up;
- communication and reporting;
- local labour monitoring;
- the key terms;
- milestones and the critical path, with payment procedures;
- key deliverables, kept up to date as change orders are agreed;
- the KPIs and how each is measured;
- variation and change control;
- record keeping.

On an Ekene contract, local labour monitoring has a legal edge: it feeds the Nigerian content reporting that module six teaches.

## A living document

Because GovS 008 asks for periodic review, the plan changes when the contract does. A new delegation, a new indicator or an agreed change order each means an update, dated, with the reason.

## Exercise

EKC-04 is a synthetic casing and tubulars frame agreement. Supplier TB-D (synthetic) supplies casing and tubing at stated unit prices per item, with delivery lead times stated per order. TB-D is the only supplier qualified for one premium connection the Ekene well designs use.

Write the headings of a one-page contract management plan for EKC-04, with one line under each saying what you will record there. Say who approves the plan.

### A reading

A good plan uses the headings above and fills them for this contract. Risks: TB-D is the only qualified source for the premium connection, so a late order has no quick substitute, and the plan names how orders are placed early. Contacts and authorisations: who may place an order and up to what value. Payment: unit prices checked against each delivery. Deliverables and milestones: the stated lead time per order, tracked. Records: orders, delivery notes and inspection results. The contract manager writes the plan and the contract owner approves it, the split GovS 008 at 5.4.2 describes for UK practice.
