# The price basis and what it asks of you

Every contract says how the price is worked out. That clause decides what the contract manager must check before paying: a milestone, a quantity, an hour, a day, or an output. Choosing a price basis, and costing each basis under uncertainty, belongs to the procurement course, Procurement, Tendering & Contracting. This lesson reads the basis a signed contract already has.

## The common bases

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught here by concept, describe three bases in Annex VIII:

- **Lump sum** (para 3.2): the contractor performs the defined scope for a fixed contract amount.
- **Unit price** (para 3.6): the contract states estimated quantities and a unit price for each item, and pays the quantities actually delivered at those prices.
- **Performance-based** (para 3.4): payment is tied to measured outputs that meet functional needs of quality, quantity and reliability.

The UK Risk Allocation and Pricing Approaches guidance note (January 2026) adds, as UK practice, that a fixed price fits a fixed scope, and that where base prices cannot be estimated at all a cost plus approach may be the only option (Appendix II).

## Who carries which cost

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) expects the contract manager to know in advance which costs, possible extra costs included, sit with the contractor and which sit elsewhere. Under a lump sum, most cost risk inside the scope sits with the contractor. Under hourly rates plus materials at cost, the buyer carries the quantity, so the manager's checks move to hours and evidence.

## Inflation and indexation

The UK pricing guidance says at para 6.1.6: "For contracts with considerable inflation uncertainty, using indexation is advisable." The World Bank guidance records that price adjustment clauses are established practice for contracts running more than about 18 months. If a contract carries an index clause, the manager applies it exactly as written, with the index and dates it names.

## Currency

Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), s.16(27), provides that "The values in procurement documents shall be stated in Nigerian currency". It binds federal procuring entities, and for an operator it is published public practice. Read the currency clause in each Ekene contract.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is a synthetic instrument maintenance service. Supplier IM-F (synthetic) is reimbursed at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost, with a monthly service report.

Write three checks you would make on every IM-F invoice because of its price basis.

### A reading

Hours: the hours charged match the service report and the job records, at the stated rate for each grade. Materials: each item at cost is backed by the supplier's own purchase invoice, since a cost is only at cost when it is evidenced. Scope: the work charged is planned or corrective maintenance within the contract, because under this basis the buyer carries the quantity risk the World Bank guidance asks a manager to understand in advance.
