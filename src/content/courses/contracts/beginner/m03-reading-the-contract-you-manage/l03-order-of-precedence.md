# The order of precedence between documents

A contract is rarely one document. It is an agreement with conditions, schedules, a specification, a price list, and sometimes the supplier's own proposal bound in. When two of them differ, the manager needs to know which one wins.

## The clause that decides

Most contracts carry an order of precedence clause: a ranked list saying which document prevails where they conflict. The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught here by concept, lists at a kick-off meeting a walk-through of the contract documents so that everyone understands which document takes priority over which (p.82, item 3).

## Standard forms and what was signed

Many contracts are built on published forms. The course teaches three families by concept only, since each is licensed:

- **FIDIC conditions.** Many international works contracts, including those built on World Bank standard documents, use FIDIC general conditions with project specific particular conditions. A reader works through the agreement, the particular conditions and the general conditions in the order the contract itself sets, and checks the role the contract gives to the Engineer or the Employer's Representative.
- **LOGIC standard contracts.** Industry forms used on the UK continental shelf for offshore services, construction and supply. A manager reads the edition and any agreed amendments in the signed contract before relying on a clause.
- **AIPN model contracts.** Models such as the model joint operating and farmout agreements, which many upstream parties adapt. Joint operating agreements are taught in the joint ventures course.

The common lesson: a standard form is a starting point. The version the parties signed, with its elections and amendments, is the contract.

## Above every document: the content Act

On an Ekene contract one text sits above the whole ranked list. The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), s.6, provides:

> "all subsequent oil and gas arrangements, agreements, contracts or memoranda of understanding relating to any operation or transaction in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall be in conformity with the provisions of this Act."

No precedence clause can rank a schedule above the law: s.6 requires the contract to conform.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is a synthetic time charter of one platform supply vessel from supplier MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown, with a crew complement that names positions for Nigerian seafarers and trainees. In this scenario, MV-B's technical proposal, bound into the charter as an appendix, shows a smaller crew than the crew complement in the charter's own schedule, and MV-B mobilises the smaller crew.

Write a short note to MV-B setting out which document you rely on and why.

### A reading

The note cites the charter's order of precedence clause. If it ranks the charter's schedules above the bound-in proposal, the crew complement schedule governs and MV-B must supply the named positions; if the clause is unclear, the note says so and the question goes to the contract owner. The note asks MV-B to confirm when the full complement will be aboard. Because the named positions for Nigerian seafarers and trainees serve the charter's Nigerian content duties, the note also recalls that the charter must conform to the content Act under s.6.
