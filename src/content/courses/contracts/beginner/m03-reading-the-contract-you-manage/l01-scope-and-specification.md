# Scope and specification

The scope says what the supplier must deliver. The specification says to what standard. Almost every question a contract manager is asked starts as a scope question: is this in the contract, or is it extra?

## Read the words that were signed

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) reminds its reader that a contract is managed according to its own terms. For scope, that means the scope of work or terms of reference as signed, with any agreed amendments, read beside the rest of the contract. A manager answers a scope question from the document.

## Fixed scope and fixed price go together

The UK Risk Allocation and Pricing Approaches guidance note (January 2026) ties a fixed price to a fixed scope (Appendix II). Where the scope floats or is still unknown, a fixed price does not fit. This course teaches the guidance as UK practice. The lesson for a manager reading a lump-sum or fixed-fee contract is direct: the price covers the scope as written, so the exact edges of that scope carry money.

## What a scope usually contains

A scope section usually states what is to be delivered, where and when, the standards and specifications that apply, what the buyer provides, and how delivery is checked or accepted. The buyer's own duties are part of the scope too.

## Warranties sit beside the scope

Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), s.16(28), requires every procurement contract to carry warranties of durability of goods, requisite skill in service provision, and genuine materials and inputs in execution. That Act binds federal procuring entities; for an operator it is a published statement of public practice. It shows that reading the scope includes reading the warranty clause that sets the standard of what is delivered.

## When the answer is no

If the requested work is outside the scope, it is a change. The contract's change clause says how a change is requested, priced and approved, and the Professional tier teaches that process. At Associate level the job is to recognise the question and route it.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is a synthetic camp catering and facility services contract. Supplier CF-C (synthetic) provides catering, housekeeping and camp maintenance at the Ekene shore base for a fixed monthly fee, with service levels for meal service, cleanliness audits and response to maintenance requests. In this scenario, the base manager asks CF-C to deep-clean a newly opened second accommodation block, and CF-C asks who will pay.

Write a four-line note to the file setting out how you will answer.

### A reading

First, read the scope as signed: does housekeeping cover a named list of buildings, a floor area, or the base as it stands at any time? Second, check any amendment since award. Third, if the block is outside the written scope, the work is a change, and the fixed monthly fee does not cover it, since a fixed fee pays for the scope as written. Fourth, tell the base manager that the request goes through the contract's change clause before CF-C starts, and record the request and your answer.
