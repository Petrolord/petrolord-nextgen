# What a key performance indicator measures

A key performance indicator, or KPI, turns a loose question about whether the supplier is doing well into a question with an answer. This lesson sets out what a KPI is, and what separates a KPI from other things people measure.

## A definition from law

The UK Procurement Act 2023, as enacted, defines the term at s.52(4):

> "A “key performance indicator” is a factor or measure against which a supplier’s performance of a contract can be assessed during the life-cycle of the contract."

The same section, at s.52(1), sets a duty for UK contracting authorities: "Before entering into a public contract with an estimated value of more than £5 million, a contracting authority must set at least three key performance indicators in respect of the contract." The UK Act binds UK contracting authorities only. This course uses it as a clear, published statement of practice, and no Ekene contract is governed by it.

## What a KPI is for

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught here by concept, set KPIs where they are needed to confirm that the contractor performs satisfactorily and meets the requirements of the contract (Annex XI, para 2.4). For the contracts a borrower's strategy identifies, the contract management plan sets the KPIs and milestone events (Annex I, para 3.7). So a KPI is tied to the contract's requirements and written into the plan.

## A KPI and a management measure

This course uses the word KPI in one sense only: an indicator the contract states. A team may also track things the contract does not state, such as how quickly the supplier answers email. Those are management measures. They can be useful, but they carry no contractual remedy, and a supplier cannot be held to a target it never agreed.

The difference matters most when something goes wrong. A missed KPI can lead to the steps the contract provides. A missed management measure can lead to a conversation.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is a synthetic well services call-off framework. Supplier WS-A (synthetic) provides coiled tubing, nitrogen and pumping services by call-off. The framework states KPIs for mobilisation on time, non-productive time caused by the supplier, and safety reporting, with a quarterly performance review.

In this scenario, the drilling superintendent wants WS-A marked down at the quarterly review because its crews arrived without the superintendent's preferred paperwork format, and because WS-A's invoices arrived late twice.

Write a short reply saying which of these points the review can score as KPIs and what you will do with the rest.

### A reading

Neither point is one of the three stated KPIs, so neither can be scored as a KPI at the review. The paperwork format is a management measure: raise it with WS-A as a request, and if Ekene needs it enforced, it has to be written into the framework through the change clause. Late invoices affect Ekene's own records, and are raised with WS-A as a management point. The review scores mobilisation on time, non-productive time caused by WS-A and safety reporting, on the evidence for each.
