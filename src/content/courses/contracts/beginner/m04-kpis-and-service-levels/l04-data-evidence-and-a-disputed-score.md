# Data, evidence and a disputed score

A KPI score is only as good as the data behind it. When a supplier disagrees with a score, the conversation that follows is about evidence: what was recorded, by whom, and when.

## What to record as you go

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught here by concept, lists what a manager should record: how the supplier performed and delivered, every communication and notice, the dates, and who was involved (Figure XIV).

For each indicator, the evidence should match its definition. If the contract measures availability from a daily log, the daily log is the evidence; an email written a month later is a poor substitute.

## A regular assessment

The UK Cabinet Office guidance on KPIs under the Procurement Act 2023 (April 2026 PDF) explains the duty the UK Act's s.71 sets: to assess a supplier's performance against its KPIs "at least once in every twelve month period during the lifetime of the contract and on termination" (para 14), with published ratings set by regulation: Good (meeting or exceeding the KPI), Approaching target, Requires improvement (below the KPI), Inadequate (significantly below the KPI), and Other (para 15). That duty binds UK contracting authorities. The practice it models is simple: assess on a fixed cycle, against the stated indicator, with a stated rating, so a disagreement is caught early.

## When a score is contested

A contested score is handled in steps. First, go back to the indicator's definition in the contract. Second, compare both sides' data for the period. Third, find where they differ: a different start time, a different reading of what counts, a missing record. Fourth, agree the score, or record precisely what is not agreed and why.

In this course's words, a dispute is a claim or other matter the parties have not agreed. A contested score that the evidence settles never becomes a dispute. The Expert tier teaches formal dispute resolution. At Associate level the aim is a clean record, so that if a matter does go further, the facts are already written down.

## The cause behind the number

The World Bank guidance expects the underlying cause of a missed KPI to be found and dealt with through an action plan.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is a synthetic time charter of one platform supply vessel from supplier MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown, with the vessel's availability as its service level. In this scenario, Ekene's log records the vessel as unavailable for a period after an engine fault. MV-B says the vessel was waiting at the port for a berth for part of that period and should count as available.

Write a list of the evidence you would gather, and one line saying how you will record the outcome.

### A reading

Gather the charter's definition of availability and of off-hire; Ekene's daily log with its times; the vessel's own engine and deck logs; the port's berth records for the period; and the messages exchanged at the time. Compare the times against the definition. The outcome line records the agreed availability for the period with its evidence or, if the parties still differ, exactly which hours are not agreed and why.
