# Plans and reports a contract carries

Nigerian content is planned before work starts and reported while and after it happens. The operator holds most of the duties under the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), but the information comes from the contracts. This lesson follows it from the supplier to the Board.

## The plan

Section 7 provides that "before carrying out any project in the Nigerian oil and gas industry, an operator shall submit a Nigerian Content Plan ("the Plan") to the Board demonstrating compliance with the Nigerian content requirements of this Act." The contract's own Nigerian content plan, where it has one, carries the operator's plan down to the supplier's work.

The Board's Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update), taught here by concept, add a Nigerian Content Compliance Commitment at award. It records the targets the operator and its contractor agree for execution, and a Nigerian Content Compliance Certificate is issued once the contract is performed and the commitments are met.

## The contract binds the supplier to report

Section 65 is the link between the contract and the report:

> "The operator shall ensure that its partners, contractors and subcontractors are contractually bound to report Nigerian content information to the operator and, if so requested by the Board, directly to the Board"

Section 66 adds that operators "shall effectively communicate its Nigerian content policies and procedures to its contractors and subcontractors and to monitor and enforce their compliance." Monitoring and enforcing is contract management work.

## The reports

The operator's reports draw on every contract. Under s.24(1), the operator lists quarterly "all contracts, subcontracts and purchase orders exceeding $1,000,000 (USD) or such other limit as the Board may determine, awarded in the previous quarter." Under s.29, it reports employment and training quarterly. Under s.60:

> "Within sixty days of the beginning of each year, each operator shall submit to the Board their annual Nigerian Content Performance Report covering all its projects and activities for the year under review."

Behind every report sits s.64: operators and contractors must give the Board or its agent access to "all documentation and information required for substantiating the Nigerian content reported." A figure a supplier reports must be one it can prove.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is a synthetic well services call-off framework. Supplier WS-A (synthetic) works under a Nigerian content plan with quarterly reporting. In this scenario, WS-A's quarterly Nigerian content report is late, and when it arrives its person-hours figure has no supporting timesheets.

Write a short note to WS-A and a line for the contract file.

### A reading

The note cites the framework's reporting clause, which exists because the content Act, s.65, requires the operator to bind contractors to report, and s.66, under which the operator monitors and enforces compliance. It asks WS-A for the supporting timesheets by a stated date, since s.64 requires contractors to give the Board access to the documents that substantiate what is reported. The file line records the late report, the missing support, the request and its date, so the operator's own reports rest on figures that can be proved.
