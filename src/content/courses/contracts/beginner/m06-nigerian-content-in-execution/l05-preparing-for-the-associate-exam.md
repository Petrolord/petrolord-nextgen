# Preparing for the Associate exam

The Associate tier has followed a contract from award to steady running. This lesson reviews the tier and explains the exam.

## How the exam works

Each module has a bank of scenario questions, and the tier ends with a final exam of scenario questions set on the synthetic Ekene contracts. Every question is multiple choice, and each keyed answer rests on a cited source. The Associate certificate is issued when you pass the final exam at the academy's pass mark.

## The tier in six ideas

1. **A contract is managed by its own terms.** The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) says so. Under the UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 4.6.6, the contract manager runs it day to day.
2. **Handover and the plan set the contract up.** Resource before award, a handover from sourcing, a contract management plan written by the manager and approved by the owner, and a kick-off that confirms roles and authority.
3. **Read before you act.** Scope, price basis, order of precedence and the working clauses tell you what to check and what counts as a change.
4. **Measure what the contract states.** A KPI is an indicator the contract states; anything else is a management measure. Service credits are applied as written, and GovS 008 at 5.4.4 asks for payments to reflect the service received.
5. **Money moves on evidence, and records outlast people.** Invoices are checked, certificates rest on inspection, instructions are written by someone with authority, and every step is dated and filed.
6. **Nigerian content runs through execution.** The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) binds contractors (s.2), requires contracts to conform (s.6) and requires the operator to bind contractors to report (s.65).

## How to answer a scenario question

Find the contract, its price basis and what has changed, then ask which kind of text governs: the contract, the content Act, or practice from a UK or World Bank text. Then pick the answer that follows the source, and be wary of an option that states a figure with no citation.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is a synthetic camp catering and facility services contract. Supplier CF-C (synthetic) is paid a fixed monthly fee, with stated service levels and service credits, and a Nigerian content reporting clause. In this scenario, in one month: a maintenance response level is missed; the base manager verbally asks CF-C to run an extra catering service for visitors; and CF-C's Nigerian content return arrives without supporting records.

Write three lines, one for each event, saying what you will do and on what source.

### A reading

The missed response level: apply the service credit the contract states, with the evidence, and ask CF-C for the cause and a plan, as World Bank practice expects. The extra service: tell CF-C in writing that, if it is outside the scope the fixed fee pays for, it must come through the change clause from someone with authority, as GovS 008 at 5.4.5 describes for UK practice. The return: ask for the supporting records, since the content Act, s.64, requires contractors to give the Board access to the documents behind what is reported.
