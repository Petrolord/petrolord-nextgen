# The content Act and the Board

Every Ekene contract is performed inside one Nigerian statute: the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010). This module reads what it asks of a contract in execution. Nigerian content at the tender, under s.14 and s.16, belongs to the procurement course.

## Who it binds

The content Act, s.2, reaches well beyond the operator:

> "All regulatory authorities, operators, contractors, subcontractors, alliance partners and other entities involved in any project, operation, activity or transaction in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall consider Nigerian content as an important element of their overall project development and management philosophy for project execution."

Section 6 requires later contracts in the industry to "be in conformity with the provisions of this Act." So the supplier on an Ekene contract is bound as well as Ekene, and the contract itself must fit the content Act.

## What Nigerian content means

The content Act defines it at s.106:

> ""Nigerian Content" means the quantum of composite value added to or created in the Nigerian economy by a systematic development of capacity and capabilities through the deliberate utilization of Nigerian human, material resources and services in the Nigerian oil and gas industry;"

The reports in this module measure exactly that.

## The Board

In this course, the Board is the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board. The content Act gives it a monitoring role in execution. Section 62 provides that "The Board shall undertake regular assessment and verification of the Nigerian Content Performance Report filed by all operators", and s.70(k) lists among its functions to "make auditing procedures and conduct regular audits for the purposes of monitoring and implementing compliances with the provisions of this Act;". The Board's Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) set out the roles of operators, service providers and the Board at each stage of tendering and contract execution.

## The consequence

Section 68 states the sanction:

> "An operator, contractor or sub-contractor who carries out any project contrary to the provisions of this Act, commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine of five per cent of the project sum for each project in which the offence is committed or cancellation of the project."

## Exercise

EKC-03 is a synthetic camp catering and facility services contract. Supplier CF-C (synthetic) provides catering, housekeeping and camp maintenance at the Ekene shore base. In this scenario, CF-C's manager writes that the content Act is a matter for Ekene as operator, and that CF-C need not supply the Nigerian content information the contract asks for.

Write a short reply citing the content Act by section.

### A reading

The reply says that s.2 applies to contractors and subcontractors as well as operators, that s.6 requires the contract to conform to the content Act, that s.65 is why the contract binds CF-C to report, and that s.68 makes a contractor who carries out a project contrary to the content Act liable to a fine of five per cent of the project sum or cancellation of the project. It asks CF-C to supply the information by the date the contract sets, and it is filed.
