# Nigerians employed and trained on the job

People are the part of Nigerian content a contract manager can see every day: who is on site, who is on the vessel, who is being trained. The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) sets the duties; the contract carries them to the supplier.

## First consideration for employment and training

The content Act, s.28(1), provides:

> "Nigerians shall be given the first consideration for employment and training in any project executed by any operator or project promoter in the Nigerian oil and gas industry."

Section 35 goes further for some grades: "All operators and companies operating in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall employ only Nigerians in their junior and intermediate cadre or any other corresponding grades designated by the operator or company."

## Training where skills are short

Section 30 addresses the gap between the duty and the labour market:

> "Where Nigerians are not employed because of their lack of training, the operator shall ensure, to the satisfaction of the Board, that every reasonable effort is made within a reasonable time to supply such training locally or elsewhere"

Under s.29, every plan carries an Employment and Training Plan covering the hiring and training needs of the operator and its major contractors, expected skill shortages, project specific training and forecast spend, and the operator reports to the Board quarterly on employment and training against it, including new hires, where they lived when hired, and their employment status.

## Labour clauses in large contracts

Section 34 requires that "all projects or contracts whose total budget exceeds $100 million (USD) shall contain a "Labour Clause" mandating the use of a minimum percentage of Nigerian labour in specific cadres as may be stipulated by the Board." The percentage is left to the Board to stipulate, and this course states none.

## What the manager watches

The manager compares the supplier's actual crew or workforce with the positions the contract names, checks that trainee positions are filled and that training happens as planned, and collects the information the operator needs for its quarterly employment and training report. Expatriate positions and succession plans are taught at the Professional tier.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is a synthetic time charter of one platform supply vessel from supplier MV-B (synthetic). The charter states a crew complement with named positions for Nigerian seafarers and trainees. In this scenario, the crew list for this month shows one named trainee position empty, and a second Nigerian position filled by a foreign seafarer.

Write a short note to MV-B and a line for the operator's quarterly employment and training return.

### A reading

The note cites the crew complement in the charter and the content Act, s.28(1), and asks MV-B to fill the trainee position and to explain the substitution, with the date by which both will be put right. If MV-B says no qualified Nigerian seafarer was available, the note asks what training is under way, since s.30 expects every reasonable effort to supply training. The return line records the positions filled and unfilled this quarter, with the reason, as s.29 requires the operator to report.
