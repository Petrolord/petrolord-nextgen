# First consideration in practice

The phrase "first consideration" runs through the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010). How first consideration is weighed when bids are evaluated belongs to the procurement course. This lesson looks at what it means once the contract is running and the supplier is buying, hiring and fabricating.

## What the content Act says

Section 10(1) sets out first consideration for the work programme a Nigerian Content Plan covers:

> "(a) first consideration shall be given to services provided from within Nigeria and to goods manufactured in Nigeria; and (b) Nigerians shall be given first consideration for training and employment in the work programme for which the plan was submitted."

Section 12 requires the plan to set out "how the operator and their contractors will give first consideration to Nigerian goods and services". The duty reaches contractors: s.11(3) provides that "All operators, alliance partners and contractors shall comply with the minimum Nigerian content for particular project item, service or product specification set out in the schedule to this Act". The schedule's minimums are read in the content Act itself; this lesson states none of them.

## Where it shows in execution

A contract in execution keeps making small procurement decisions: a supplier's sub-order, a replacement part, a new hire, a fabrication job. Each of these is a place where first consideration either happens or does not. The contract manager sees them through the supplier's reports and through requests for approval.

Two sections are specific enough to act on directly. Section 53 provides:

> "all operators, project promoters, contractors and any other entity engaged in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall carry out all fabrication and welding activities in the country."

And s.50 provides that "No insurance risk in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall be placed offshore without the written approval of the National Insurance Commission which shall ensure that Nigerian local capacity has been fully exhausted."

## What the manager does

The manager reads the contract's Nigerian content plan and clauses, watches the supplier's sub-orders and fabrication plans for anything that moves work abroad, and asks for the reason and the approvals whenever it does. A request is answered in writing, citing the section, and filed.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is a synthetic flowline replacement at the Ekene manifold. Supplier FW-E (synthetic) works under a lump sum with a Nigerian content plan in the contract covering fabrication and labour. In this scenario, FW-E proposes to have a set of spool pieces fabricated and welded at its parent company's yard outside Nigeria, because the yard has spare capacity and would deliver sooner.

Write a short reply to FW-E.

### A reading

The reply cites the content Act, s.53, which requires contractors to carry out all fabrication and welding activities in the country, and the contract's own Nigerian content plan covering fabrication. It says Ekene cannot agree to the proposal as made, asks FW-E to confirm an in-country fabrication plan that meets the schedule, and invites it to raise any schedule concern with evidence. The reply is filed, and the contract owner is told, since the proposal touches both schedule and a legal duty.
