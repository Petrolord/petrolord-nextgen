# Correspondence and instructions in writing

Much of a contract is managed in conversation: on site, on the phone, in a meeting. Anything that changes what the supplier must do, or what the buyer must pay, belongs in writing, from someone with the authority to give it.

## Why writing matters

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught here by concept, lists every communication and notice among the things a manager records, with the dates and who was involved. A spoken instruction leaves no such record. When a question is asked months later, the file shows whichever version was written down.

Contracts usually say how notices must be given, and the manager follows that clause every time.

## Authority behind an instruction

An instruction binds only when the person giving it holds the authority. The World Bank guidance asks the manager to confirm at the start that each party has its authorisations and delegations in place, since a contracting decision is valid and enforceable only when its maker holds the authority. For changes it asks for clear roles, with named people holding delegated authority to act on a change request or to escalate it.

A field supervisor who asks a supplier for extra work may be well meaning and still lack the authority to order it.

## Changes go through change control

The UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) says at 5.4.5 that "Contract changes shall be justified and controlled", and expects approvals to be obtained before a change is implemented, with an audit trail in a change register. That is UK practice. At Associate level the job is to recognise a change request and send it through the contract's change clause. How it is assessed is taught at the Professional tier.

## A written instruction for defects

Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), which binds federal procuring entities and is published practice for an operator, gives one example of a written instruction at s.53(3): the Bureau, on the procuring entity's advice or after a review or an investigation, may issue a variation order requiring a contractor, at its own expense, to repair or replace work done with inferior or defective materials. Even an instruction to put defects right is written.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is a synthetic instrument maintenance service. Supplier IM-F (synthetic) carries out planned and corrective maintenance of field instruments at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost. In this scenario, a production supervisor has asked IM-F's technician, in person, to start maintaining a set of analysers that are not on the contract's instrument list. The technician has emailed you asking whether IM-F will be paid.

Write a reply to IM-F and a short note to the supervisor.

### A reading

The reply thanks IM-F, confirms the analysers are outside the instrument list, and asks it to stop that work until a written instruction is issued under the change clause by someone with authority. It asks for a record of any hours already spent. The note to the supervisor explains that requests for work outside the contract go through the contract manager, and that the request will now be raised as a change. Both are filed with their dates.
