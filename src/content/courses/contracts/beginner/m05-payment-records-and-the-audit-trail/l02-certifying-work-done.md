# Certifying work done

On works and many service contracts, payment follows a certificate: a signed statement by the buyer's side that a stated piece of work has been done to the contract's standard. Money moves on it.

## Why the certificate matters

Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) shows how central the certificate is in Nigerian public practice. It binds federal procuring entities, and for an operator it is a published statement of that practice. Section 35(2) provides:

> "Once a mobilization fee has been paid to any supplier or contractor, no further payment shall be made to the supplier or contractor without an interim performance certificate issued in accordance with the contract agreement."

Under s.37(2) of the same Act, the period after which a payment counts as delayed runs from the submission of the invoice and the valuation certificate. A certificate therefore starts the payment clock as well as authorising the payment.

## Payment as progress is made

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025), taught here by concept, provide that a works contract may, where appropriate, allow a mobilisation advance, advances against plant and materials the contractor brings, regular payments as progress is made, and a reasonable retention released once the contractor has met its obligations (Annex IX, para 2.14). Each is paid or released against evidence the certificate confirms.

## What a certificate should rest on

Before signing, the manager confirms four things:

- the work described is the work the contract or the milestone defines;
- it has been inspected or tested as the contract requires, and the results are on file;
- the quantity or percentage certified matches the measurement records;
- the deductions the contract provides, such as retention, have been applied.

A certificate signed on the supplier's say-so, without those records, is an unsupported payment. If the work is partly done and the contract pays only on completion of the milestone, the certificate waits.

## Certifying honestly both ways

Certifying less than was done is as wrong as certifying more. The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) stresses that an employer must meet its own obligations, timely payment included. The manager certifies what the evidence supports, promptly.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is a synthetic flowline replacement at the Ekene manifold. Supplier FW-E (synthetic) is paid a lump sum against milestones, with a retention held from each payment until completion. In this scenario, FW-E reports that the milestone for installing the new flowlines is complete and asks for its certificate. Your site inspector confirms the lines are laid, but the pressure test the contract ties to the milestone has not yet been done.

Write a short reply to FW-E and a line for the contract file.

### A reading

The reply says the milestone as the contract defines it includes the pressure test, so the certificate will be issued once the test is done and its results are accepted, and asks FW-E to propose a test date. It confirms the retention will be applied when the certificate is issued. The file line records the report, the inspection, the missing test, the reason the certificate is held, and the date. Once the test passes, the manager certifies promptly.
