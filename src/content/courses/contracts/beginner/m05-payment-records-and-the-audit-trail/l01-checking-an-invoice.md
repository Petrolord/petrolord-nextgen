# Checking an invoice against the contract

An invoice is the supplier's statement of what it believes it is owed. The contract manager's job is to check that statement against the contract and the records, promptly, and to pay what is due on time.

## What to check

Every invoice check asks the same questions, in the terms the contract's price basis sets:

- Was the work or supply ordered, and is it within the contract?
- Was it delivered or performed, and is there a record that says so?
- Are the rates, prices and quantities the ones the contract states?
- Have any service credits, retentions or other deductions the contract provides been applied?
- Has anything on it been invoiced before?

The UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) says at 5.4.4 that payments "should be audited periodically to ensure they reflect the contract terms and service levels received from suppliers." That is UK practice, and a fair description of what every check aims at.

## Warning signs

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught here by concept, lists red flags of fraud and corruption during execution. Among them are payments that do not follow the contract, and false or duplicate invoices. The guidance also says payment errors found before the final payment should be corrected.

## Paying on time is the buyer's duty

Checking is not a reason to delay. Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) binds federal procuring entities, and for an operator it is a published statement of public practice. Its s.37(2) provides:

> "Any payment due after more than sixty days from the date of the submission of the invoice, valuation certificate and confirmation or authentication by the Ministry, Extra-Ministerial Office, Government Agency, Parastatal or Corporation shall be deem a delayed payment."

Section 37(3) and (4) add that delayed payments attract interest at the rate the contract specifies, and that every contract must include terms on interest for late payment. In the UK, Cabinet Office guidance on electronic invoicing and payment under the Procurement Act 2023 (April 2026 PDF) describes an implied term that a valid, undisputed invoice is paid within 30 days (para 7). Each Ekene contract states its own payment period, and the manager works to it.

## Exercise

EKC-07 is a synthetic environmental monitoring consultancy. Supplier EM-G (synthetic) is paid a time-based fee against approved timesheets. In this scenario, EM-G's invoice for the quarter charges for more days than the timesheets you approved, and one line repeats a charge from last quarter's invoice.

Write a short note to EM-G and a line for the contract file.

### A reading

The note says you will pay the days covered by approved timesheets, at the contract rate, within the contract's payment period, and that the extra days and the repeated line are held pending evidence. It asks EM-G for the missing timesheets, if any exist, and a corrected invoice. The file line records the invoice, the days approved, the items held and why, and the date of the note. The undisputed part is paid on time.
