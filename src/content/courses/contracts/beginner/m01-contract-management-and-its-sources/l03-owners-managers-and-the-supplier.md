# Owners, managers and the supplier

A contract has at least two parties and, inside each party, several people with different jobs. This lesson names the roles and the authority each one holds.

## The contract owner and the contract manager

This course uses two words for the buyer's side. The contract owner is accountable for the outcome the contract buys. The contract manager runs the contract day to day. Each contract names its own roles.

The UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) calls the owner a senior business owner (4.6.5). That person owns the justification for the contract, is accountable for parts of its governance and management, and keeps commercial and other specialists involved across the whole life cycle. At 4.6.6 the contract manager is accountable to the senior business owner for day-to-day delivery and for the contract management plan. The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) treats appointing a contract manager for each contract as good practice.

## Clear roles are a principle

The UK Contract Management Principles (Crown Commercial Service, undated, page updated 12 December 2025) put it as principle 2:

> "Be clear about accountability, roles and responsibilities. Ensure contract ownership, management processes and governance mechanisms are clear with defined roles and responsibilities at appropriate levels of seniority."

## Authority to decide

The World Bank guidance asks the contract manager to confirm at the start that each party has its authorisations and delegations in place, because a contracting decision is valid and enforceable only when the person taking it holds the authority to take it.

Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) puts it at s.16(21): the accounting officer of a procuring entity and any officer with delegated responsibility "are responsible and accountable for any actions taken or omitted to be taken either in compliance with or in contravention of this Act." That Act binds federal procuring entities. For an operator such as Ekene it is a published statement of public practice, and the lesson it teaches carries over: a delegation is personal, and so is the accountability.

The supplier has its own manager and its own limits of authority, and the kick-off meeting in module two is where the two sides exchange those names.

## Exercise

EKC-07 is a synthetic environmental monitoring consultancy. Supplier EM-G (synthetic) samples and reports quarterly, is paid a time-based fee against approved timesheets, and has a named key person and a report acceptance step.

Write a roles line for the contract file naming who, on the Ekene side, approves timesheets, who accepts the quarterly report, and who is told if EM-G proposes to replace its key person. Say what you would check before any of them acts.

### A reading

A good answer names the contract manager for timesheet approval, and names whoever the contract gives acceptance to for the report, with the contract manager recording the decision. The key person change goes to the contract owner, since it touches the outcome the contract buys. Before anyone acts, the manager checks that each person holds a written delegation for that decision, the check the World Bank guidance asks for at the start.
