# A practice course and its written work

This is a practice course. It has no calculator and computes nothing, because contract management is mostly judgement applied to written terms and records. Its practicals are written scenario work, and each tier's certificate is issued when you pass that tier's final exam. The Expert final exam is a written case. There is no capstone in this course.

## Why written work

A contract manager's output is written: a note to a supplier, a line in a register, a checklist before a payment, a decision with its reason. The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) ask for contract management that is planned, carried out, monitored and evaluated systematically (Annex XI, para 2.1). Each of those verbs leaves a document behind, so the exercises ask you to produce one.

## The synthetic Ekene contracts

Every exercise is set on one of seven synthetic contracts held by Ekene, an operator invented for this course. The contracts, suppliers and any figures in a scenario are synthetic teaching data.

- EKC-01, a well services call-off framework with WS-A, priced from a schedule of rates.
- EKC-02, a platform supply vessel time charter with MV-B, on a day rate with off-hire.
- EKC-03, camp catering and facility services with CF-C, for a fixed monthly fee with service credits.
- EKC-04, a casing and tubulars frame agreement with TB-D, at unit prices.
- EKC-05, flowline replacement works with FW-E, a lump sum paid against milestones.
- EKC-06, instrument maintenance with IM-F, at hourly rates plus materials at cost.
- EKC-07, environmental monitoring with EM-G, on a time-based fee.

A scenario may add facts of its own, such as a late delivery or a missed service level. Those facts belong to the scenario. Legal rules and figures come only from the named texts, with their citations.

## How an exercise works

Each lesson ends with a short task. Write your answer before reading on. A section called A reading then sets out the answer the cited sources support. Where yours differs, find the source that settles it.

The World Bank Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) reminds its reader that a contract is managed according to its own terms. So the first move in almost every exercise is the same: find the clause.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is a synthetic camp catering and facility services contract. Supplier CF-C (synthetic) provides catering, housekeeping and camp maintenance at the Ekene shore base for a fixed monthly fee, with service levels for meal service, cleanliness audits and response to maintenance requests, and service credits deducted when a level is missed.

The camp manager tells you that breakfast was late on three days this month and asks whether CF-C will be paid less. Write two sentences: what you will read first, and what you will say to the camp manager today.

### A reading

You read the contract first: the service level for meal service and the service credit it states, since the contract is managed on its own terms. Today you tell the camp manager that any deduction depends on what the contract states and on the evidence of the late breakfasts, and you ask for that evidence in writing. Module four of this tier teaches service credits in full.
