# The contract lifecycle from award to close

Every contract passes through the same broad stages after it is awarded: handover and mobilisation, steady running, and close. Knowing which stage a contract is in tells a contract manager which duties are live this week.

## Before award: the decision that shapes the rest

This course starts at award. Tender evaluation and the award decision belong to the procurement course, Procurement, Tendering & Contracting. The UK Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), Chapter 12, says:

> "How a contract will be managed is a key strategic decision that needs adequate consideration early in the procurement process and shall be reflected in the contractual agreement."

The contract a manager inherits already carries the reporting, indicators and remedies written into it.

## Mobilisation

The Playbook treats the time between award and the contract start date as a key phase in setting the contract up for success (Chapter 12). Module two covers it: handover, the contract management plan, the kick-off meeting and the first deliveries.

## Steady running

The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) describe contract management as planned, carried out, monitored and evaluated systematically (Annex XI, para 2.1). In practice: indicators read on a cycle, invoices checked, instructions in writing and records kept, as modules three to five teach. The UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) expects the contract management plan to be "reviewed periodically throughout the life of the contract" (5.4.2).

## Close

The World Bank Regulations require an evaluation of how the contract was carried out, made at completion, to assess performance and draw lessons for later contracts (Annex XI, para 2.4). GovS 008 at 5.4.7 adds the practical side:

> "On contract closure, information systems should be updated, staff and facilities (if any) reassigned and the contract documentation archived in accordance with the organisation’s information retention policy and procedures."

The Expert tier teaches close-out in full. The Associate habit is simpler: the records kept during steady running are what close-out reads.

## Nigerian content across the stages

Nigerian content duties run through every stage of an Ekene contract. Under the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), s.60, each operator files an annual Nigerian Content Performance Report early in the following year, so a contract's content record is read long after the work is done.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is a synthetic flowline replacement at the Ekene manifold by supplier FW-E (synthetic): a lump sum paid against milestones, a retention held from each payment until completion, a performance security, a defects period, and a Nigerian content plan covering fabrication and labour.

Place each of these four items in a lifecycle stage: checking the performance security, certifying the second milestone, recording labour hours for the content plan, archiving the contract file.

### A reading

The performance security is a mobilisation check. Certifying the second milestone belongs to steady running. Recording labour hours also belongs to steady running, and it feeds the operator's annual report under the content Act, s.60, so it stays live until that report is filed. Archiving the file is close, done under the organisation's retention policy, as GovS 008 at 5.4.7 describes for UK practice.
