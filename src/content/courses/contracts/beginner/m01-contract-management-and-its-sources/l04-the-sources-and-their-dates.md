# The sources and the dates they were checked

Everything this course teaches comes from a named text read on a stated date. This lesson shows the kinds of text and how each is used.

## Three kinds of text

**Nigerian law.** The Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act; Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) binds operators, contractors and sub-contractors in the industry. Its s.6 requires every later contract in the Nigerian oil and gas industry to "be in conformity with the provisions of this Act", so every Ekene contract sits inside it. Nigeria's Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007) binds federal procuring entities; for an operator it is a published statement of public practice. The Board's Guidelines for NCDMB Approvals of Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Contracting Processes (2025 update) set out who does what at each stage of tendering and execution.

**UK law and government guidance.** The UK Procurement Act 2023 as enacted, the UK Government Functional Standard GovS 008: Commercial (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026) and the UK Sourcing Playbook (June 2023) bind UK contracting authorities. The course teaches them as openly licensed good practice and names them as UK texts.

**World Bank texts.** The World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (Seventh Edition, September 2025) and its Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) bind borrowers on Bank-financed contracts. Their licence is non-commercial, so the course teaches them by concept.

No Ekene scenario is governed by UK law or by the World Bank Regulations. A lesson may say what that practice recommends and ask what it would mean for Ekene.

## Quoted or by concept

Public texts whose licence allows it are quoted word for word, with a citation. Licensed texts are explained in the course's own words: the World Bank texts, the AIPN and LOGIC model contracts, FIDIC conditions, ISO 44001, CIPS study material and Kraljic's 1983 article.

## The dates

Every text was read on or before 27 September 2026, and the course is due for review by 27 September 2027. A reader after the review date checks the current edition before relying on a figure.

## Doubts taught as doubts

The printed copy of the Public Procurement Act 2007 gives its record retention period at s.16(12) in garbled words, so this course teaches no retention period. Where a text can be read two ways, the course names the doubt and leaves it open.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is a synthetic time charter of one platform supply vessel from supplier MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown. A colleague drafts a note to MV-B saying the World Bank Regulations require it to report performance monthly.

Write two sentences correcting the note, naming which kind of text binds the charter and how a World Bank text may still be used.

### A reading

The charter sits under the content Act, which by s.6 requires Ekene's contracts to conform to it, and under its own terms. The World Bank Regulations bind borrowers on Bank-financed contracts and do not bind MV-B. The note can say that World Bank practice expects a contract management plan to cover communication and reporting, and then point to the reporting duty the charter itself states.
