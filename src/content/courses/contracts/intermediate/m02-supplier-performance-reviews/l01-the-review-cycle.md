# The review cycle

The Associate tier measured KPIs and service levels month by month. This module steps back to the review that looks at those measures together: how often it happens, what it covers and what it leaves behind.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The contract sets the rhythm

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, reminds its reader that a contract is managed by its own terms. The review cycle is one of those terms. EKC-01, the synthetic Ekene well services framework with WS-A, states a quarterly performance review, so the Ekene contract team holds one every quarter whatever the season's workload.

The World Bank Procurement Regulations (Seventh Edition, September 2025), also by concept, ask for contract management that is planned, carried out, monitored and evaluated systematically. A review calendar written at the start of the contract is the simplest proof of that system.

## A UK floor for public contracts

UK law sets a minimum for its own public contracts. The Procurement Act 2023 as enacted, s.71(2), opens:

> At least once in every period of twelve months during the life-cycle of the contract and on termination of the contract the contracting authority must

and the UK guidance on Key Performance Indicators (April 2026 PDF), para 14, completes the duty: "at least once in every twelve month period during the lifetime of the contract and on termination, assess the supplier’s performance against those KPIs". This binds UK contracting authorities. For Ekene it is published practice: a yearly assessment, and one at the end, is a sensible least.

## Three layers of review

A contract team can organise its reviews in three layers, even when the contract names only one:

* **routine checks**, monthly or per order, where the contract manager reads the service report and the KPI results;
* **the contractual review**, at the cycle the contract states, where both parties review performance against every KPI and agree actions;
* **a yearly look**, where the contract owner asks whether the contract is still buying what the operation needs.

## What a review leaves behind

A review that leaves no record did not happen for any later purpose. Each one should leave the scorecard as agreed, the actions with owners and dates, and any disagreement noted with both views. Module 6 shows why that record matters when performance becomes poor.

## Exercise

EKC-01 (synthetic) states KPIs for mobilisation on time, non-productive time caused by the supplier, and safety reporting, a quarterly performance review, and a Nigerian content plan with quarterly reporting. The contract starts on 1 January.

Write the review calendar for the first year as a short note to WS-A: when each review falls, what it covers, and what each side brings.

### A reading

Four reviews, one after each quarter, each covering all three KPIs and the quarter's Nigerian content report. WS-A brings its own KPI data and the content report; Ekene brings its records of mobilisation times and non-productive time. The note adds a yearly look by the contract owner after the fourth review, in line with the World Bank's call for systematic evaluation.
