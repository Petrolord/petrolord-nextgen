# The scorecard and its evidence

A scorecard is the page a review is built around. It is only as strong as the evidence behind each line. This lesson builds a scorecard and ties each line to a record both parties can check.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## Hard and soft measures

The UK Contract Management Principles (Crown Commercial Service, publication page updated December 2025), principle 7, put the idea plainly:

> Use a balanced scorecard to measure ‘hard’ data such as KPI performance alongside ‘soft’ measures e.g. customer satisfaction and relationship management, with a focus on achievement of outcomes.

The course's vocabulary draws a line through that scorecard. A KPI is a measure the contract states. A measure the contract does not state is a management measure: useful in a review, and carrying no contractual remedy. A scorecard should show which lines are which.

## What a KPI is

The UK Procurement Act 2023 as enacted, s.52(4), defines the term:

> A “key performance indicator” is a factor or measure against which a supplier’s performance of a contract can be assessed during the life-cycle of the contract.

UK practice also warns against too many. The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023), Chapter 5, says that "having too many KPIs (i.e. more than 10 to 15 per service) will lead to overcomplicated contracts and ambiguity with suppliers." These are UK texts, read here as practice.

## Ratings with plain meanings

The UK guidance on Key Performance Indicators (April 2026 PDF), para 15, describes five ratings set by regulation for published KPI assessments: Good (meeting or exceeding the KPI), Approaching target, Requires improvement (below the KPI), Inadequate (significantly below the KPI) and Other. An Ekene scorecard is under no duty to use them, yet a rating with a written meaning is harder to argue about than a colour.

## Evidence for every line

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), by concept, lists what a contract manager should record: how the supplier performed and delivered, every communication and notice, the dates, and who was involved. A scorecard line should point to that record. When the supplier's figure and Ekene's figure differ, the review looks at both records before it rates the line.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic Ekene instrument maintenance service by IM-F, with KPIs for planned work completed and repeat failures, and a monthly service report. The Ekene operations team also wants to score IM-F on how quickly its technicians answer the phone at night.

Draft three scorecard lines: the two KPIs and the night-call measure. For each, give the source of the evidence and say whether it is a KPI or a management measure.

### A reading

Planned work completed is a KPI; its evidence is the monthly service report checked against Ekene's own maintenance work orders. Repeat failures is a KPI; its evidence is the failure history of each instrument, drawn from Ekene's records and IM-F's report. The night-call measure is a management measure, since the contract does not state it. It can be discussed at the review, and it carries no contractual remedy; if Ekene wants one, the route is a change to the contract, which module 3 covers.
