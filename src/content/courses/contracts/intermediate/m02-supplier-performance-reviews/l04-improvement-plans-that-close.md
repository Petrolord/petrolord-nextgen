# Improvement plans that close

This lesson looks at what makes an improvement plan close: a cause, actions that address it, a measure that shows success, and a date on which someone checks.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## Where the plan comes from

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.3, names the tool: where performance does not or is unlikely to meet the contract, corrective action is taken within the terms of the contract, "such as through formal notice or a performance improvement plan." The UK Contract Management Principles (publication page updated December 2025), principle 3, ask contract managers to "Understand and use contractual options such as appointment of Remedial Advisor, Rectification Plans and Step In rights." Both are UK texts, read here as practice. On an Ekene contract the first question is whether the contract itself provides a rectification plan procedure; if it does, that procedure is followed.

## Five parts of a plan that closes

1. **The failure**, stated as the KPI or service level missed and the evidence.
2. **The cause**, found before the actions are written, as the World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024) expects.
3. **The actions**, each with an owner and a date.
4. **The closure test**: the measure that shows the failure is fixed, and for how long it must hold.
5. **The checkpoint**: the date and meeting at which the contract manager applies the test.

The closure test is the part most plans lack. "Improve cleanliness" cannot close. "Pass the monthly cleanliness audit in each of the next three months" can.

## A proper opportunity to improve

The UK guidance on Contract Performance Notices (Crown copyright 2024), para 28, says of a public contract:

> When the contract performance notice is used to publish information about a supplier not performing a public contract to the authority’s satisfaction, the supplier must have been given proper opportunity to improve performance, and must have failed to do so before the notice is published.

The same guidance, para 36, suggests the date that counts may be when a notice was issued saying improvement had not happened, or that an agreed rectification plan had not been carried out. An Ekene contract is under no UK duty to publish, yet the logic holds: a plan with a closure test and a checkpoint shows the supplier had a fair chance.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is the synthetic Ekene camp catering and facility services contract with CF-C, with service levels (cleanliness audits among them) and service credits deducted when a level is missed. The scenario adds its own facts: CF-C failed the cleanliness audit in three months out of four, and the cause found is too few housekeeping staff on the night shift.

Write parts 3, 4 and 5 of CF-C's improvement plan.

### A reading

Actions: CF-C adds housekeeping staff to the night shift and names a supervisor, with dates. Closure test: the cleanliness audit passed in each of a stated run of consecutive months, the number agreed and written in the plan. Checkpoint: the monthly service meeting after that run ends. The service credits for the missed months still apply as the contract states; the plan runs alongside them.
