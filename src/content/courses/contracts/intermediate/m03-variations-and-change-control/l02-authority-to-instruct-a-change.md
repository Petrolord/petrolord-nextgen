# Authority to instruct a change

A change instructed by the wrong person is a problem waiting to be found. The supplier did the work in good faith, the operator's records show no approval, and both sides now argue about who pays. This lesson is about who may instruct a change, and how the contract manager keeps it that way.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## Authority makes the decision valid

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, asks the contract manager to confirm at the start that each party has put its authorisations and delegations in place. Its reason is plain: a contracting decision holds only when the person who takes it holds the authority to take it.

The same guidance asks for clear roles in change management, with named people holding delegated authority to act on a change request, or to escalate it when there are problems.

## Approval comes first

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.5, expects approvals to be obtained before a change is implemented, with an audit trail in a change register. It is a UK text, read here as practice. The order matters. An approval given after the work is done is a ratification of a fact, and it gives the approver no real choice.

## Accountability for what was done

For federal procuring entities in Nigeria, the Public Procurement Act 2007 (Act No. 14, Official Gazette No. 65, Vol. 94, 19 June 2007), s.16(21), is direct:

> The accounting officer of a procuring entity and any officer to whom responsibility is delegated are responsible and accountable for any actions taken or omitted to be taken either in compliance with or in contravention of this Act.

An operator is outside that Act's reach, but the principle it states is sound practice anywhere: a delegation carries accountability with it.

## A delegation table

Each Ekene contract should carry a short table in its contract management plan: who may instruct a change, up to what value, and who must be consulted. The values are the operator's own choice, set in its policy. Field staff are told plainly what they may and may not instruct. A supplier is told who can bind the operator, so it can refuse an instruction from anyone else.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic Ekene instrument maintenance service by IM-F, reimbursed at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost. The scenario adds its own facts: an Ekene production supervisor, who holds no delegated authority under the contract, tells IM-F's technician at the well pad to install and maintain six extra pressure gauges. IM-F does the work and invoices it.

Write a note to IM-F and a note to the production supervisor.

### A reading

To IM-F: the instruction did not come from a person with delegated authority under the contract, so the invoice cannot be paid as instructed work until the change is assessed and approved by someone who holds that authority; IM-F should refer any future instruction to the named contract manager. To the supervisor: field instructions that add work are a change, and they go to the contract manager, who has the authority and keeps the register. Whether the gauges are paid then depends on the assessment in the next lesson.
