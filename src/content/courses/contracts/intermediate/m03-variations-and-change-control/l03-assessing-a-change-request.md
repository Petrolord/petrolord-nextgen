# Assessing a change request

Once a change is recognised and reaches the person with authority, it is assessed. This lesson gives the order of that assessment: scope first, then justification, then price, then approval.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## What the request should carry

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, expects a supplier's request for a change order or variation to come with its contractual justification, an analysis, method statements, and evidence of where the extra cost comes from and why it is reasonable. It also expects the contract manager to track and control every increase that is granted.

## Step 1: is it in scope?

The same guidance puts the scope question first: was the "extra" work already in the contract, labelled as extra? If it was, the assessment ends there.

## Step 2: is it justified?

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.5, a UK text read here as practice, expects the cost of a change to be justified against the business case, the change to be value for money and lawful, and approval to come before implementation. On an Ekene contract the justification is the operational need.

## Step 3: is the price reasonable?

The World Bank guidance gives an order for testing cost. Use the contract's own unit rates first. Where the contract has none, use recorded historical costs. Only then look to market rates. A schedule of rates, like the one in EKC-01, is therefore the first place to look.

## Step 4: does it shift the balance?

The UK guidance on Contract Modifications (Crown copyright 2024), para 23, gives examples of changes likely to tilt the economic balance toward the supplier: a change lifting the supplier's profit from 8% to 16%, a transfer of valuable intellectual property to the supplier, or repeated additions adding up to a large volume of extra work. The figures are the guidance's own illustration; the question of who gains suits any change.

## Step 5: approve, record, instruct

Approval by the person with delegated authority, a line in the change register (lesson 5), then a written instruction to the supplier. The order matters: the instruction follows the approval.

Management of change as an engineering and safety discipline belongs to the risk, change and learning course. This lesson is about the contract side of a change.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic Ekene well services call-off framework with WS-A, priced from a schedule of rates. The scenario adds its own facts: WS-A asks to add a second nitrogen unit to a planned job, at a day rate it quotes, and sends a one-line email. The schedule of rates holds a day rate for a nitrogen unit.

Write the contract manager's reply.

### A reading

The reply thanks WS-A and asks for what the World Bank guidance expects: the reason the second unit is needed (the justification), a method statement, and the cost build-up. It says the schedule of rates already prices a nitrogen unit, so that rate applies and the quoted rate does not. It confirms that no work proceeds until the change is approved by the named authority and instructed in writing.
