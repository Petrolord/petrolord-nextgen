# Segmenting the Ekene suppliers

This lesson puts the module together. It sorts all seven synthetic Ekene contracts, using the portfolio idea for the item bought and the UK tiering factors for the contract, and writes each result as a register line with its reason. Every contract, supplier and fact below is synthetic teaching data.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The rules the register follows

Three published statements shape the register. GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 6.3: "Suppliers should be segmented according to their commercial and business impact." The UK Contract Management Principles, principle 4, direct the strongest resource where risks and rewards are highest. The Sourcing Playbook (June 2023) lists the tiering factors: impact of service failure, service continuity, speed and ease of switching, and value. All three are UK texts, read here as practice Ekene may adopt. The portfolio idea (Kraljic, Harvard Business Review, 1983, by concept) supplies the four groups: strategic, bottleneck, leverage and non-critical.

## The seven contracts

| contract | supplier | what it buys | the deciding facts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EKC-01 | WS-A | well services by call-off | wells stop without it; KPIs on non-productive time |
| EKC-02 | MV-B | one supply vessel on time charter | daily reliance offshore; availability is the service level |
| EKC-03 | CF-C | camp catering and facility services | many capable suppliers; service credits already in place |
| EKC-04 | TB-D | casing and tubing | the only supplier qualified for one premium connection |
| EKC-05 | FW-E | flowline replacement works | one-off lump sum; retention, security, defects period |
| EKC-06 | IM-F | instrument maintenance | repeat failures affect production and safety systems |
| EKC-07 | EM-G | quarterly environmental monitoring | a named key person; quarterly rhythm |

## A reasoned reading

**EKC-01 and EKC-02** read as strategic. Failure stops operations, switching takes time, and both hold people and equipment the operation relies on daily.

**EKC-04** reads as strategic or bottleneck. The single qualified source is the deciding fact; the team's view of its spend settles which. Either way it needs a continuity plan, which module 4 builds.

**EKC-06** reads as leverage or strategic, depending on how far repeat failures touch safety-critical instruments. The register line should say which instruments the team had in mind.

**EKC-05** is a one-off works contract. Its risk is high while the work runs and falls away at completion, so its tier is set for the life of the works and reviewed at handover.

**EKC-03 and EKC-07** read as non-critical or leverage. Both can be managed firmly by their own terms, with EKC-07's key person noted as its one switching difficulty.

## What changes because of the segment

A segment decides effort. It leaves entitlement where the contract put it. WS-A's strategic label earns a senior owner at its quarterly review; it earns no relief from its KPIs. CF-C's non-critical label means lighter relationship work, and its service credits still apply in full, because a contract is managed by its own terms.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is planned and corrective maintenance of field instruments by IM-F (synthetic), reimbursed at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost, with KPIs for planned work completed and repeat failures and a monthly service report. The scenario adds a fact: some of the instruments IM-F maintains are on the Ekene gas detection system.

Write the register line for EKC-06: the portfolio group, the tier, one sentence on each UK tiering factor, and the reason in one sentence.

### A reading

The gas detection instruments raise the impact of a service failure and make continuity essential, so EKC-06 reads as strategic and critical even though its hourly rates are modest. Switching is possible, since other maintenance suppliers exist, though a new supplier needs time to learn the system. The reason line names the safety instruments as the deciding fact, so the next reviewer can test it.
