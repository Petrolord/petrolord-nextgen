# Tiering contracts by risk and value

The portfolio grid sorts what is bought. A contract team also needs to sort the contracts it holds, because the same supplier can hold a critical contract and a trivial one. UK government practice does this with a tiering tool, and its four factors travel well to an oil and gas contract register.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The four factors of the UK tiering tool

The Sourcing Playbook (UK Cabinet Office, June 2023) names the tool in a definitions note:

> These can be identified using the Cabinet Office Contract Tiering Tool, which considers factors such as the potential impact of service failure; service continuity; the speed and ease of switching suppliers; and the contract value.

Value is the last of four. The first three ask what happens when a supplier stops, so a modest contract can rank high.

## Gold and Silver

The UK guidance note on assessing and monitoring the economic and financial standing of suppliers (2026 edition) takes the tiers a step further. Its key suppliers are identified with the tiering tool and include every supplier of a Gold (critical) or Silver (important) contract. Their financial standing is reviewed at least once a year, and more often for critical suppliers or those with more than a low risk of failure. Module 4 returns to that review.

## Risk and reward decide the effort

The UK Contract Management Principles (publication page updated December 2025), principle 4, direct the most and strongest resource to contracts where the risks and rewards are highest. The tier is the written form of that choice: which contracts get a senior owner at reviews, whose finances are watched, and which need a continuity plan.

All of these are UK texts that bind UK contracting authorities. For Ekene they are published practice: the operator may adopt the four factors in its own register, and the course asks what they would mean on an Ekene contract.

## Writing the tier down

A tier line carries the contract, the tier, a sentence on each factor and the date it was set, and is reviewed when the facts change.

## Exercise

Tier two synthetic Ekene contracts on the four factors of the UK tiering tool, one line per factor, and give each a tier name of your choosing (for example critical, important or routine).

* EKC-02, a platform supply vessel on time charter from MV-B (synthetic), paid a day rate with off-hire for breakdown, with the vessel's availability as its service level.
* EKC-07, quarterly environmental sampling and reporting by EM-G (synthetic), with a named key person and a report acceptance step.

### A reading

EKC-02 scores high on impact of failure and on service continuity, since the offshore operation relies on the vessel every day, and switching to another vessel takes time; it reads as critical whatever its value. EKC-07 is quarterly, so a short failure can be recovered before the next round, and another consultancy could be engaged, though the named key person adds some switching difficulty. It reads as important or routine, and the line should say which factor decided it.
