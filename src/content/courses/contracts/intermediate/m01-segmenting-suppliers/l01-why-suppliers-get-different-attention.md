# Why suppliers get different attention

The Associate tier followed one contract from award to steady running. The Professional tier asks a wider question: how a contract team looks after its suppliers over the whole life of their contracts. It starts with a plain fact about time. A contract team has a fixed number of hours, and the seven synthetic Ekene contracts do not all deserve the same share of them.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The principle in the UK standards

The UK Contract Management Principles (Crown Commercial Service, undated, publication page updated December 2025) state the idea in one line, as principle 4:

> Adopt a differentiated approach based on risk; distinguish between tactical and strategic contracts, direct the most/strongest resource to contracts where the risks and rewards are highest.

GovS 008, the UK government's commercial functional standard (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), says the same of suppliers at 6.3: "Suppliers should be segmented according to their commercial and business impact." Both are UK texts. They bind UK government bodies, and this course reads them as published good practice that an operator such as Ekene may choose to follow.

## Some suppliers need very little

GovS 008, at 6.2, accepts that some suppliers need no relationship management at all. Its example is the low cost, low risk purchase bought from a catalogue or a common framework. The point for a contract manager is permission as much as instruction: time spent on a supplier who cannot hurt the operation is time taken from one who can.

## Two disciplines that meet here

Professional bodies such as CIPS teach contract management and supplier relationship management as two linked disciplines (the course describes CIPS material by concept only). Contract management protects what the signed contract promises. Supplier relationship management looks for more value with the few suppliers that matter most. Segmentation is where the two meet, because it decides which suppliers get only the first and which get both.

## What this module does

The next four lessons give three ways to sort suppliers: by profit impact and supply risk, by the risk and value of each contract, and by the relationship each segment needs. The last lesson sorts all seven Ekene suppliers. None of it changes a contract's terms.

## Exercise

Two synthetic Ekene contracts come up at the same planning meeting. EKC-03 is camp catering and facility services from CF-C (synthetic) for a fixed monthly fee, with service levels and service credits. EKC-04 is a casing and tubulars frame agreement with TB-D (synthetic), and TB-D is the only supplier qualified for one premium connection the Ekene well designs use.

The contract team can give one of them a monthly relationship meeting with a senior person present. Write two sentences: which contract gets it, and why.

### A reading

EKC-04 gets the meeting. Principle 4 of the UK Contract Management Principles directs the strongest resource to where the risks and rewards are highest, and a single qualified source for a connection the well designs depend on carries a supply risk that CF-C's catering does not. EKC-03 still needs its service levels checked and its service credits applied every month, which is contract management, and that work continues.
