# Resolution planning for critical contracts

A continuity plan asks what happens if a supplier stumbles. Resolution planning asks a harder question: what happens if the supplier's whole business fails? UK government practice has built a routine around that question, and this lesson reads it as published practice for the most critical Ekene contracts.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The UK requirement

The Sourcing Playbook (UK Cabinet Office, June 2023), Chapter 11, says: "All new critical service contracts require resolution planning information from suppliers." GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.6, adds: "For critical public services organisations shall have resolution plans."

## Which contracts

The UK Resolution Planning guidance note (May 2021), paras 1.4.1 to 1.4.4, applies the guidance to new critical service contracts, to other outsourced service contracts worth more than £10m per year, and to critical construction contracts. Such contracts should require the supplier to provide corporate resolution planning information and an insolvency continuity plan.

The Playbook also defines a group the UK watches closely:

> Public sector dependent suppliers are supplier groups with over £50 million pa revenue of which over 50% is derived from public sector work.

These thresholds belong to UK public contracts. An Ekene contract has no such figure, and the course sets none. The Ekene equivalent of "critical" is the tier the contract team gave the contract in module 1.

## What the information is for

Read plainly, the two documents give the buyer in advance what it would need on the day a supplier fails: how the supplier's group is put together, and how the service would continue through an insolvency. Without them, the operator learns both on the day of the failure.

## Asking for it on an Ekene contract

An Ekene contract can require this information only if its terms do. For an existing contract without such terms, the contract manager can still ask, and record the answer. For a future contract, the request belongs in the contract strategy, which the Expert tier teaches.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is the synthetic Ekene platform supply vessel time charter with MV-B, paid a day rate, with the vessel's availability as its service level. The contract team tiered EKC-02 as critical in module 1. The scenario adds its own fact: MV-B belongs to a group whose vessels are owned by one company and whose crews are employed by another.

List four pieces of information the contract manager asks MV-B to provide, and say what each is for.

### A reading

Which group company is the charter party, so Ekene knows whose failure ends the charter. Which company owns the vessel, since the vessel is what Ekene needs to keep. Which company employs the crew, including the Nigerian seafarers and trainees the charter names, since crew continuity decides whether the vessel can sail. And how MV-B would keep the vessel on hire through an insolvency of any of those companies. All four follow the UK practice of resolution planning information and an insolvency continuity plan, requested by Ekene as good practice.
