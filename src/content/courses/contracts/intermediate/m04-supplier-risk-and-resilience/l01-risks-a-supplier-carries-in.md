# Risks a supplier carries into your operation

When a supplier signs, its weaknesses become the operator's exposure. A vessel owner in financial trouble, a consultancy that depends on one person, a pipe mill with one qualified product line: each can stop an Ekene operation without breaching a single clause. This module teaches how a contract team sees those risks and prepares for them.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## Kinds of supplier risk

The synthetic Ekene contracts show four kinds:

* **delivery risk**: the supplier cannot perform as the contract requires, through lack of people, equipment or capacity;
* **financial risk**: the supplier's finances weaken until it cannot keep performing;
* **dependency risk**: the operator relies on a single source or a single key person;
* **compliance risk**: the supplier's conduct exposes the operator under Nigerian law.

The last kind has a sharp edge in Nigeria. The content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), s.68, provides that an operator, contractor or sub-contractor who carries out a project contrary to that Act "commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine of five per cent of the project sum for each project in which the offence is committed or cancellation of the project." A contractor's failure can become the project's problem.

## Where the contract helps

UK practice lists the contractual tools. The Sourcing Playbook (UK Cabinet Office, June 2023), Chapter 5, on protecting against supply chain risk, says: "These include step-in rights, the approval of key sub-contractors and assignment and novation provisions." The Playbook binds UK government bodies; for Ekene it is published practice. Whether an Ekene contract holds these tools is read from its own terms, and the contract manager should know before a crisis which ones it has.

## Keeping the risks in view

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, expects the contract manager to review the risk register regularly while the contract runs, drawing on the parties where that helps. Supplier risks belong in that register, with an owner and a review date. Building and running risk registers as a discipline belongs to the risk, change and learning course; this module covers the supplier's part in them.

## Exercise

EKC-07 is the synthetic Ekene environmental monitoring consultancy: quarterly sampling and reporting by EM-G, paid a time-based fee against approved timesheets, with a named key person and a report acceptance step.

List three supplier risks EKC-07 carries into the Ekene operation, one of each of three different kinds, and for each write one line on what the contract manager watches.

### A reading

Dependency: the named key person could leave or fall ill, so the contract manager watches for changes in EM-G's team and knows what the contract says about replacing a key person. Delivery: a missed sampling round cannot be repeated for the same quarter, so the contract manager checks the sampling plan before each round. Compliance: EM-G's work is part of a project in the Nigerian oil and gas industry, so its Nigerian content reporting under the contract is checked like any other supplier's, with the content Act s.68 in mind.
