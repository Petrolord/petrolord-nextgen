# The Nigerian content plan

The Associate tier met Nigerian content as duties that run through execution. This module reads those duties in depth, section by section, from the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Development Act 2010 (the content Act: Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010). The content Act binds operators, contractors and sub-contractors in the Nigerian oil and gas industry, and every Ekene contract sits inside it. The Board is the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board (NCDMB).

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The plan comes before the work

The content Act, s.7, requires the plan before any project starts:

> In the bidding for any licence, permit or interest and before carrying out any project in the Nigerian oil and gas industry, an operator shall submit a Nigerian Content Plan ("the Plan") to the Board demonstrating compliance with the Nigerian content requirements of this Act.

Under ss.8 and 9 the Board reviews the plan and, if satisfied, issues a Certificate of Authorization for the project. Where the Board runs a public review of a plan, the review is completed and the certificate issued or denied within 30 days from the start of the review.

How the plan shapes the tender and the award (the content Act s.14 and s.16) is taught in the procurement course. This module starts where the contract does.

## What the plan promises

The content Act, s.10(1), sets two first considerations the plan must carry: "(a) first consideration shall be given to services provided from within Nigeria and to goods manufactured in Nigeria; and (b) Nigerians shall be given first consideration for training and employment in the work programme for which the plan was submitted."

## The contract carries the plan

The content Act, s.6, requires that later contracts in the industry "shall be in conformity with the provisions of this Act." On EKC-05, the flowline works, the contract itself holds a Nigerian content plan covering fabrication and labour. That plan is a term the contract manager manages like any other.

NCDMB's guidelines for its approvals of contracting processes (2025 update) add a Nigerian Content Compliance Commitment at award, recording the targets the operator and its contractor agree for execution. A Nigerian Content Compliance Certificate follows once the contract is performed and the commitments are met.

## Exercise

EKC-05 is the synthetic Ekene flowline replacement works by FW-E, a lump sum against milestones, with a Nigerian content plan in the contract covering fabrication and labour. The scenario adds its own fact: FW-E proposes to have the spool pieces fabricated and welded at its parent company's yard outside Nigeria, to save time.

Write the contract manager's reply to FW-E.

### A reading

The reply declines. The content Act, s.53, requires that "all operators, project promoters, contractors and any other entity engaged in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall carry out all fabrication and welding activities in the country." The proposal would also depart from the fabrication part of the contract's own Nigerian content plan and from s.10(1)(a). The reply asks FW-E for a schedule that meets the programme with fabrication in Nigeria, and records the exchange.
