# Performance reports and monitoring

Nigerian content is reported, and the reports are checked. The operator answers to the Board (NCDMB) for what its contractors achieved, so the contractor's reporting under each Ekene contract feeds the operator's own. This lesson traces that chain under the content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010).

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The operator's reports

The content Act, s.60:

> Within sixty days of the beginning of each year, each operator shall submit to the Board their annual Nigerian Content Performance Report covering all its projects and activities for the year under review.

Under s.61 that report sets out Nigerian content by category of spend on current and cumulative cost bases, employment as hours or days worked by Nigerian and foreign workers, and procurement as quantity and tonnage of locally made and foreign materials. Every one of those figures is built from contract records.

The operator also reports during the year. The content Act, s.24(1): "The operator shall submit to the Board, within 30 days at the end of each quarter, a listing of all contracts, subcontracts and purchase orders exceeding $1,000,000 (USD) or such other limit as the Board may determine, awarded in the previous quarter."

## The contractor's duty to report

The content Act, s.65, makes the link:

> The operator shall ensure that its partners, contractors and subcontractors are contractually bound to report Nigerian content information to the operator and, if so requested by the Board, directly to the Board

So the reporting duty is a contract term, and the contract manager enforces it like any other: on time, complete, and in the form the operator needs.

## The Board checks

The content Act, s.62: "The Board shall undertake regular assessment and verification of the Nigerian Content Performance Report filed by all operators". And s.64: "all operators and contractors shall provide the Board or its designated agent with access to their facilities and all documentation and information required for substantiating the Nigerian content reported."

A figure reported without its evidence is a figure that may not survive verification. The contract manager keeps, or makes sure the contractor keeps, the records behind each figure.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic Ekene well services call-off framework with WS-A, with a Nigerian content plan and quarterly reporting. The scenario adds its own facts: WS-A's report for the last quarter arrived three weeks after the date the framework sets, and it gives the number of Nigerian staff but no hours worked by Nigerian and foreign workers.

Write a note to WS-A.

### A reading

The note records the late date against the framework's reporting date. It asks for the missing hours worked by Nigerian and foreign workers, since the operator's annual report under the content Act, s.61, sets out employment in hours or days worked, and a head count cannot supply that. It reminds WS-A that its reporting is a contract term under s.65, and that it must keep the records behind the figures, since s.64 gives the Board access to them. The late report goes on the agenda of the next quarterly review.
