# Employment and expatriate positions

Who works on an Ekene contract is governed by the content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) as well as the contract. This lesson reads its employment sections and its rules for expatriate positions.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## First consideration for Nigerians

The content Act, s.28(1):

> Nigerians shall be given the first consideration for employment and training in any project executed by any operator or project promoter in the Nigerian oil and gas industry.

And s.35 goes further for the lower grades: "All operators and companies operating in the Nigerian oil and gas industry shall employ only Nigerians in their junior and intermediate cadre or any other corresponding grades designated by the operator or company."

## Large contracts carry a labour clause

The content Act, s.34, requires that "all projects or contracts whose total budget exceeds $100 million (USD) shall contain a "Labour Clause" mandating the use of a minimum percentage of Nigerian labour in specific cadres as may be stipulated by the Board." The percentage is for the Board (NCDMB) to stipulate; the content Act does not print it, and neither does this course.

## Expatriate positions

* **Succession.** The content Act, s.31(1): "the operator shall submit to the Board a succession plan for any position not held by Nigerians and the plan shall provide for Nigerians to understudy each incumbent expatriate for a maximum period of four years and at the end of the four year period the position shall become Nigerianised."
* **A ceiling on management positions.** The content Act, s.32: "For each of its operations, an operator or project promoter may retain a maximum of five per cent of management positions as may be approved by the Board as expatriate positions to take care of investor interests."
* **The Board first.** The content Act, s.33(1): "the operators shall make application to, and receive the approval of, the Board before making any application for expatriate quota to the Ministry of Internal Affairs or any other agency or Ministry of the Federal Government."

The duties in ss.31 to 33 are placed on the operator. The contract manager's part is to read a contractor's proposed staffing change against them: if it moves a position from a Nigerian to an expatriate, or delays a planned succession, it goes to the contract owner and the operator's Nigerian content team before any answer is given.

## Exercise

EKC-02 is the synthetic Ekene platform supply vessel time charter with MV-B. The charter states a crew complement with named positions for Nigerian seafarers and trainees. The scenario adds its own facts: MV-B proposes to replace a Nigerian second officer, who has resigned, with an expatriate officer from its fleet, and to fill a trainee position later in the year.

Write the contract manager's reply to MV-B.

### A reading

The reply holds MV-B to the crew complement the charter states, including its named positions for Nigerians and trainees, and asks for a Nigerian replacement for the second officer, citing the first consideration in the content Act, s.28(1). If MV-B says no qualified Nigerian is available, the reply asks for the evidence and says any expatriate appointment needs the operator's review against ss.31 to 33 first. The trainee position is filled as the charter states.
