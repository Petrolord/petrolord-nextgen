# Training and capacity building

Nigerian content is about more than who is on the payroll today. The content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010) asks operators and their contractors to build capacity: to train Nigerians for the work, and to transfer technology to Nigerian people and companies. This lesson reads those duties as a contract manager meets them.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## What Nigerian content means

The content Act, s.106, defines the term:

> "Nigerian Content" means the quantum of composite value added to or created in the Nigerian economy by a systematic development of capacity and capabilities through the deliberate utilization of Nigerian human, material resources and services in the Nigerian oil and gas industry;

The words "systematic development of capacity" are why training is part of every Nigerian content plan.

## The Employment and Training Plan

Under the content Act, s.29, every Nigerian content plan carries an Employment and Training Plan. It covers the hiring and training needs of the operator and its major contractors, the skill shortages expected, project specific training, and the forecast spend. The operator reports to the Board quarterly on employment and training against that plan, including new hires, where they lived when hired, and their employment status.

A major contractor's training commitments are therefore part of what the operator reports. The contract manager needs the contractor's training records in a form the operator's quarterly report can use.

## When the skills are missing

The content Act, s.30:

> Where Nigerians are not employed because of their lack of training, the operator shall ensure, to the satisfaction of the Board, that every reasonable effort is made within a reasonable time to supply such training locally or elsewhere

A contractor who says no qualified Nigerian is available has raised a training duty. The contract manager records the claim, and asks what training will close the gap and when.

## Technology transfer

The content Act, s.44: "The operator shall submit to the Board annually a plan, satisfactory to the Board, setting out a programme of planned initiatives aimed at promoting the effective transfer of technologies from the operator and alliance partners to Nigerian individuals and companies." Contracts that bring specialised equipment or methods to Ekene are a natural place for the operator's initiatives to run.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic Ekene instrument maintenance service by IM-F, reimbursed at stated hourly rates plus materials at cost. The scenario adds its own facts: IM-F brings a specialist from abroad for every visit to calibrate the gas analysers, saying no Nigerian technician in its team is qualified, and IM-F has no training plan for the task.

Write a note to IM-F asking for what the operator needs.

### A reading

The note records IM-F's statement that no Nigerian technician is qualified. Citing the content Act, s.30, it asks IM-F for a training plan for Nigerian technicians on analyser calibration: who is trained, how, by when, and at what cost to whom under the contract. It asks for training records in a form Ekene can use in its quarterly employment and training report under s.29, and notes the operator's own annual technology transfer plan under s.44 as a place the initiative could sit.
