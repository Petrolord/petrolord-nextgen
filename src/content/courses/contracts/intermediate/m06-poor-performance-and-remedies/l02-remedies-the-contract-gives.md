# Remedies the contract gives

Remedies are what the contract lets the operator do when a failure continues or has already cost something. This lesson surveys them, read from the contract's own terms.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## Remedies and their triggers

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, gives an example list of employer remedies under a works contract, each beside the event that may trigger it:

1. a notice to correct a default, when the contractor defaults;
2. withholding payment, when work or obligations are not performed as the contract requires;
3. calling the performance security, when the contractor breaches its obligations;
4. calling the environmental and social performance security, for a breach of those obligations;
5. delay damages, for late completion;
6. termination, when a termination event the contract specifies occurs;
7. on major works, referring sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment concerns to the dispute review board.

The list sets no order of severity. The guidance calls termination the ultimate remedy for a default, taught at the Expert tier. This course advises proportion: the least remedy that will work. GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.7, a UK text read here as practice, calls early termination "a last resort only enacted after other provisions for the delivery of the contract, including contractual remedies for improving performance, have been exhausted."

## Remedies in the Ekene contracts

Each synthetic Ekene contract carries its own:

* **service credits** on EKC-03: stated deductions from CF-C's monthly fee for a missed service level;
* **off-hire** on EKC-02: MV-B is not paid the day rate while the vessel is broken down;
* **retention and performance security** on EKC-05: held against FW-E's completion and performance.

The UK Contract Management Principles (publication page updated December 2025), principle 3, add further contractual options: "appointment of Remedial Advisor, Rectification Plans and Step In rights." An Ekene contract holds these only if its terms do.

## Delay damages, and one doubt

The World Bank guidance describes delay damages as a percentage or amount deducted for late completion, usually with an aggregate limit, and gives 10% of the contract price as an illustration of that limit. Once the limit is reached the contract usually allows termination, though continuing with the contractor may be the better option. The 10% is an illustration; an Ekene contract's limit is whatever it states.

## The operator's own performance

The same guidance tells of an employer whose own late payments forced a contractor to suspend work, so it could not rely on the contractor's default. Before using a remedy, check Ekene has met its own obligations, timely payment first.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is the synthetic Ekene camp catering and facility services contract with CF-C, for a fixed monthly fee, with service levels and service credits. The scenario adds its own facts: CF-C missed the meal service level twice this month, and Ekene paid its last two invoices late.

Recommend, in a note to the contract owner, this month's remedy.

### A reading

The note recommends applying the service credits for the two missed levels, as the contract states: the contract's own remedy for a missed level, and the least that works. It flags that Ekene's late payments should be put right now, before Ekene relies on CF-C's failure.
