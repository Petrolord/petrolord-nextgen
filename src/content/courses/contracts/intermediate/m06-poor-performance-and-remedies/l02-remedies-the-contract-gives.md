# Remedies the contract gives

A formal notice asks the supplier to put a failure right. Remedies are what the contract lets the operator do when the failure continues or has already cost something. This lesson surveys them, and reads each one from the contract's own terms.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## A rising order

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, lists the usual employer remedies under a works contract in rising order:

1. a notice to correct a default;
2. withholding payment;
3. calling the performance security;
4. delay damages for late completion;
5. termination, when a contractual termination event occurs.

The order is a guide to proportion: the operator uses the least remedy that will work. Termination sits at the top, and the Expert tier teaches it. GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.7, a UK text read here as practice, calls early termination "a last resort only enacted after other provisions for the delivery of the contract, including contractual remedies for improving performance, have been exhausted."

## Remedies in the Ekene contracts

Each synthetic Ekene contract carries its own:

* **service credits** on EKC-03: stated deductions from CF-C's monthly fee when a service level is missed, applied as the contract states;
* **off-hire** on EKC-02: MV-B is not paid the day rate while the vessel is broken down;
* **retention and performance security** on EKC-05: held against FW-E's completion and performance.

The UK Contract Management Principles (publication page updated December 2025), principle 3, add further contractual options: "appointment of Remedial Advisor, Rectification Plans and Step In rights." An Ekene contract holds these only if its terms include them.

## Delay damages, and one doubt

The World Bank guidance describes delay damages as a percentage or amount deducted for late completion, usually with an aggregate limit. It gives 10% of the contract price as an illustration of that limit. The figure is an illustration in guidance, and no rule; an Ekene contract's limit, if it has one, is whatever the contract states.

## The operator's own performance

The same guidance tells of an employer whose own late payments forced a contractor to suspend work, which left the employer unable to rely on the contractor's default. Before using a remedy, the contract manager checks that Ekene has met its own obligations, timely payment first among them.

## Exercise

EKC-03 is the synthetic Ekene camp catering and facility services contract with CF-C, for a fixed monthly fee, with service levels and service credits. The scenario adds its own facts: CF-C missed the meal service level twice this month, and Ekene paid CF-C's last two invoices late.

Write a note to the contract owner recommending the remedy for this month.

### A reading

The note recommends applying the service credits for the two missed levels, as the contract states, since that is the remedy the contract itself provides for a missed service level and the least that works. It flags that Ekene's own late payments should be put right now, so that Ekene has met its obligations before relying on CF-C's failure. If the misses continue, an improvement plan follows; stronger remedies wait.
