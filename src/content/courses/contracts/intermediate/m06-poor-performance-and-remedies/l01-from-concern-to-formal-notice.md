# From concern to formal notice

Poor performance can start as a concern at a site meeting, grow into a pattern in the scorecard, survive an improvement plan, and come to need a formal notice. This module follows that path, starting with the move from a spoken concern to a written one.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The standard

GovS 008 (version 2.2, issued 1 April 2026), at 5.4.3, a UK text read here as practice:

> Where performance is unlikely to, or does not meet the requirements of the contract, preventative and/or corrective action should be taken, within the terms of the contract, to remediate the situation, such as through formal notice or a performance improvement plan.

"Within the terms of the contract" is the key phrase: a formal notice follows the contract's notice clause.

## Why spoken warnings fail

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, tells of an employer who moved against two slow road contractors after a year and found that every earlier warning had been given verbally. With nothing in writing, the employer's next step was delayed by months. The same guidance lists what the record should hold: how the supplier performed, every communication and notice, the dates, and who was involved.

## The steps

1. **Raise the concern** at the regular meeting, and minute it.
2. **Put it in writing** to the supplier's named representative: the facts, the contract requirement, and a request for the cause and a plan.
3. **Agree an improvement plan** with a closure test and a checkpoint, as module 2 taught.
4. **Issue a formal notice** under the contract's clause if the plan fails its checkpoint, or at once if the failure is serious.

For UK public contracts, the UK guidance on Contract Performance Notices (Crown copyright 2024), para 28, requires a proper opportunity to improve before poor performance is published. The steps above give that opportunity and prove it.

## What a formal notice says

A notice names the contract and the clause it is given under, states the facts and the evidence, states the requirement that has not been met, says what the supplier must do and by when (as the contract allows), and says what the contract provides if it does not. It is signed by the person with authority to give it.

## Exercise

EKC-07 is the synthetic Ekene environmental monitoring consultancy by EM-G, with a named key person and a report acceptance step. The scenario adds its own facts: for two quarters the named key person did not attend the sampling rounds, and a junior member of EM-G's staff did the work; the contract manager raised this by telephone each time.

Write the first written step.

### A reading

The first step is a letter to EM-G's named representative, from the contract manager. It states the facts with dates, cites the contract term naming the key person, records that the matter was raised by telephone on the stated dates, and asks EM-G for the cause and a plan to restore the key person's involvement by a stated date. The World Bank guidance also describes a client asking a consultant to correct or replace staff under the contract; the letter can ask for either, as the contract allows.
