# Preparing for the Professional exam

This is a practice course: its practicals are the written scenarios closing each lesson, and it has no capstone. The Professional certificate is issued when you pass this tier's final exam at the academy's existing pass mark. This lesson gathers the tier and shows how to read a question.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The exam

The Professional final exam holds 42 scenario questions, each set on a synthetic Ekene contract and each marked automatically as multiple choice. Every keyed answer rests on a cited text.

## The tier in six lines

1. **Segmenting suppliers.** Effort follows risk and reward (UK Contract Management Principles, updated December 2025, principle 4); suppliers are segmented by commercial and business impact (GovS 008, version 2.2 issued 1 April 2026, at 6.3).
2. **Performance reviews.** A balanced scorecard with evidence for each line; causes found before actions; improvement plans with a closure test and a checkpoint.
3. **Change control.** Scope first, then justification, price and approval before implementation, with an audit trail in a change register (GovS 008, 5.4.5).
4. **Supplier risk.** Key suppliers' finances watched, warning signs recorded, continuity and resolution plans written before they are needed.
5. **Nigerian content in depth.** The plan, employment, training and reporting under the content Act (Act No. 2 of 2010, commenced 22 April 2010), cited by section.
6. **Poor performance.** From spoken concern to written record to formal notice, then the remedies the contract gives, in proportion.

## Reading a question

* **Which kind of text applies?** The content Act governs every Ekene contract. UK texts (GovS 008, the Sourcing Playbook of June 2023, the Procurement Act 2023 as enacted and its guidance) and World Bank texts (such as the Contract Management Practice guidance, Second Edition, 2024) are published practice for Ekene. An option that says a UK or World Bank rule binds an Ekene contract is wrong.
* **What does the contract say?** An option that reaches for a remedy the contract does not hold is usually wrong.
* **What comes first?** Many questions turn on order: cause before action, approval before implementation, a written record before a formal notice, the least remedy that works.

## Exercise

EKC-01 is the synthetic Ekene well services call-off framework with WS-A, with KPIs, a quarterly performance review and a Nigerian content plan with quarterly reporting. The scenario adds its own facts: in the same week, WS-A misses its non-productive time KPI for the second quarter running after an improvement plan; a field engineer has instructed WS-A to add a pumping unit to a job; and WS-A's Nigerian content report is late.

Write a triage list for the contract manager: the three matters in the order you would act, each with its first step.

### A reading

One order that the sources support: first, the unauthorised instruction, because work may be under way without approval (GovS 008, 5.4.5); the first step is to tell WS-A who holds authority and to assess the change. Second, the failed improvement plan: record the failure with its date and prepare a formal notice under the contract (GovS 008, 5.4.3). Third, the late report: ask for it under the reporting term the content Act, s.65, requires. Another order can be defended if its reasons are written.
