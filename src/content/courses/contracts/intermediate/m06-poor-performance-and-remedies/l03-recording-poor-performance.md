# Recording poor performance

A record of poor performance outlives the contract. UK law turns part of it into published notices; this lesson reads the UK scheme as practice and asks what an Ekene record should hold.

Every text in this lesson was read on 27 September 2026.

## The UK scheme, in outline

Under the UK Procurement Act 2023 as enacted, contracting authorities publish contract performance notices, explained in the UK guidance on Contract Performance Notices (Crown copyright 2024):

* **Breach**: under para 26, a breach triggers a performance notice only when it has led to partial termination, an award of damages or a settlement agreement; a breach that ends in full termination is reported in the termination notice.
* **Failure to improve**: under para 28, the supplier must first have had a proper opportunity to improve, and failed.
* **Timing**: para 34 says "The contract performance notice must be published within 30 days of the day on which section 71(5) first applies in relation to a particular breach or failure to perform."
* **Consequences**: under para 10, a published notice creates a discretionary exclusion ground in later procurements, for a supplier whose notice was published in the preceding 5 years and whose circumstances are continuing or likely to recur.

All of this binds UK contracting authorities only. No Ekene contract publishes such notices.

## What the scheme teaches an operator

Three habits carry across to Ekene:

1. **A clear trigger.** A record says what happened, against which requirement, and what followed (a credit, a notice, a plan).
2. **A clear date.** Para 36 of the same guidance suggests the date that counts for failure to improve may be when a notice said improvement had not happened, or that an agreed rectification plan had not been carried out. An Ekene record dates its decisions the same way.
3. **A fair process first.** The record shows the supplier was told, given a chance, and heard.

## Where the record lives

The World Bank's Contract Management Practice guidance (Second Edition, 2024), taught by concept, lists what to record: how the supplier performed and delivered, every communication and notice, the dates, and who was involved. On an Ekene contract it sits in the contract file, with the scorecards, minutes, plans and notices. Whether and how it informs a later award is a question for the procurement course.

## Exercise

EKC-06 is the synthetic Ekene instrument maintenance service by IM-F, with a KPI for repeat failures and a monthly service report. The scenario adds its own facts: repeat failures exceeded the KPI for four months; an improvement plan agreed in month two set a closure test for month four; the test failed; the contract manager wrote to IM-F on the fifth of the next month saying so.

Write the entry for the contract file's performance record.

### A reading

The entry names the KPI and the months it was missed, with the evidence (the monthly service reports and Ekene's failure history). It records the improvement plan, its closure test and the checkpoint date, and states that the test failed. It dates the failure to improve by the contract manager's letter on the fifth, following the logic of para 36 of the UK guidance. It records IM-F's response, or that none came.
