# Onward to Expert

You can now read this deck and say where every number came from. The Expert tier stops reading and starts building.

## The gap

Everything so far has been about a deck that already exists. Even the reconciliation was an audit: the model was built, and this tier asked whether it added up.

Building one is different work. A trajectory has to become a completion list. A production database has to become a schedule. A free parameter has to be tuned and the tuning has to be reported. And the result has to survive a validator that refuses what it cannot accept.

## What the Expert tier builds

**Trajectories into connections.** A deviated well is a path in three dimensions, and its completions are wherever that path crosses a cell. That is an intersection problem, and the answer is a list with a direction flag per cell rather than a k1 and a k2. Ekene's side-track is the worked example.

**A history schedule.** Thirty six months of monthly volumes become thirty six pairs of rate blocks and dates, and the arithmetic has to reproduce the ledger exactly when it is put back.

**The calibration decision.** This tier told you the regional mean was tuned. The Expert tier does the tuning, decides what to match, and writes the sentence that reports it.

**Validation.** Seven deliberately broken specs, one rule each, and what each refusal means. Then the harder question of what a validator cannot catch, which is most of what goes wrong.

**Deck security.** A deck is an input file that a program executes decisions from, and some keywords in the wider format are genuinely dangerous. Knowing which is part of running anybody else's deck.

## And a module that grades nothing

The Expert tier ends with a module on reading results: what a SUMMARY file holds, what the field and well vectors mean, what the published SPE1 reference shows, and how history-match vectors are meant to be read.

None of it is graded, for the same reason nothing in this course is graded on a simulated number: the engines this course is built on emit decks and do geometry, and they do not solve flow equations. A course should grade what it can check, and teach what a practitioner needs whether or not it can be checked.

## What changes about the work

This tier compared the deck against its sources. The next tier makes decisions that go INTO a deck, and every one of them is a judgement somebody will have to defend later.

That is the difference between auditing and authoring, and it is why the Expert tier's central lesson is about reporting rather than about arithmetic.

## Before you go on

Two things from this tier the Expert tier assumes.

**The reconciliation, and that it was arranged.** The Expert tier's calibration module picks that up directly and expects you to know what was matched and what it cost.

**The cell-centre rule.** Every volumetric statement in the next tier depends on knowing that Eclipse decides a cell's fluid by its centre depth, and that the alternative convention gives a different and equally defensible number.
