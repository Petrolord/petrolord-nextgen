# The BaseB ledger

The BaseB column reads plus 5, plus 45, plus 37, plus 36, and this lesson derives where those numbers come from with an identity exact enough to grade. The column is not a set of four accidents; it is a ledger of zone B disagreements, and the clamp you studied two tiers ago is standing in the middle of it.

{{panel:em-tie-explorer}}

## The identity

For a vertical well, subtract the TopB tie from the BaseB tie:

$$r_{BaseB} - r_{TopB} = (\mathrm{TVDSS}_{BaseB} - \mathrm{TVDSS}_{TopB}) - (z_{BaseB} - z_{TopB})$$

The first bracket is the zone B interval the WELL logged, in vertical rock. The second is the zone B thickness the MODEL carries at the well. So:

$$r_{BaseB} - r_{TopB} = \mathrm{zone\ B}_{\mathrm{well}} - \mathrm{zone\ B}_{\mathrm{model}}$$

The difference of two residuals measures neither surface alone; it measures the model's zone against the well's zone. Every quantity in it is already in the table.

## The ledger, checked

Each well logs 30 m of zone B. The model's zone B at the wellheads, from the clamped surfaces: at W1, TopB 1538 and BaseB 1565, thickness 27 m. At W3, TopB 1598 and BaseB 1598: thickness ZERO, the clamp's pinch-out. At W4, 1596 and 1596: zero again.

W1: $r_{BaseB} - r_{TopB} = 5 - 2 = 3 = 30 - 27$. Exact.
W3: $37 - 7 = 30 = 30 - 0$. Exact.
W4: $36 - 6 = 30 = 30 - 0$. Exact.

The huge BaseB residuals at the vertical eastern wells are the number 30 wearing a disguise: each is that well's TopB residual plus the full 30 m of zone B the model erased there. Nothing about trajectories, nothing about sampling subtleties; the 180 clamped nodes of the Associate tier, meeting wells that logged the zone those nodes deleted.

In the panel, select W3 and look at the section's east side: the TopB and BaseB curves are one line at the well, and the two dashed bars at the well differ by exactly the zone the model does not have.

## What the ledger says about the model

Here the tie table stops being arithmetic and becomes geology. The clamp fixed 180 BaseB nodes because the resampled BaseB rose above TopB there; the Associate tier taught that a clamp count is information. The ledger now says the wells CONTRADICT the pinch-out: where the model says zone B is absent, wells found 30 m of it. Both cannot be right.

Possible resolutions, in rising order of cost: the BaseB source surface is simply wrong in the east and should be remapped with these wells honoured; the pinch-out is real but located wrong, and the boundary needs moving; the wells' BaseB picks are miscorrelated in the east. The tie table cannot choose, but it has converted a vague "big residuals in the east" into a precise, falsifiable statement: the model's zone B pinch-out is contradicted by 30 m of logged section at two wells. That sentence is what a Professional hands to the mapping team.

## W2's row under the ledger

W2's version has trajectory in both terms, so the identity is not integer-clean, but the structure persists: $45.02816332199586 - 8.318351595797822 = 36.70981172619804$, and the model's zone B at W2's BaseB landing (TopB sampled there minus BaseB sampled there) accounts for the gap to the roughly 42.4 m of vertical zone B rock W2 crossed. The pinch-out region begins between W2's TopB landing and its BaseB landing, which is why its ledger entry sits between W1's 3 and the eastern wells' 30.

## Worked example

Predict a hypothetical W5 at (1150, 2150), vertical, KB 0, picking TopB at 1540 and BaseB at 1572. The model's zone B near W1's corner is about 27 m. Ledger: well zone 32 m, model zone about 27, so $r_{BaseB} - r_{TopB} \approx +5$. If its TopB residual resembled W1's plus 2, its BaseB residual would sit near plus 7. The point of the exercise is the direction of inference: the ledger predicts the RELATION between two residuals from zone thicknesses, before either residual is computed.
