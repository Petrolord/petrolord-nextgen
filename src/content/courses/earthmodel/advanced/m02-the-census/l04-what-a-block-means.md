# What a block means

Before the data enters, one conceptual lesson: what has actually been claimed when a model is cut into blocks, and what has not. The bookkeeping is easy to over-read, and Expert-level judgement is mostly knowing the edges of your own model's claims.

## What the labels claim

The block labels claim exactly one thing: for the purpose of PROPERTY POPULATION AND ACCOUNTING, nodes with different labels belong to different statistical populations. Data from one block will not inform estimates in the other; volumes will be reported per label. That is the whole claim. It is a strong claim, and the right default at a mapped fault, because faults juxtapose rocks with different histories and can seal flow entirely.

## What the labels do not claim

They do not claim the fault seals. Sealing is a flow property, decided by juxtaposition and fault rock, and nothing in this tier's geometry knows it. A model cut for accounting can later be declared connected for flow; the blocks remain useful as reporting compartments either way. The reverse error is worse: treating an accounting cut as proof of two independent accumulations, with two contacts free to differ. On this model, whether block 1 could hold a different fluid contact from block 0 is a question the earth model cannot answer, and, by the division of labour, contacts are not even this course's property: they belong to ReservoirCalc, where exactly this two-blocks-two-contacts question is that ladder's Professional tier.

They do not claim throw. The polygon is a map trace with no displacement attached. A real fault offsets the surfaces themselves, and an earth model that honoured it would show TopA stepping across the trace. The golden surfaces were built smooth and unfaulted; the model is a fault-bounded ACCOUNTING on continuous geometry, a deliberate simplification the fixture documents. Reading structural throw off this model returns zero, and that zero is an artefact of construction, not an observation.

They do not claim the boundary's position is known to 25 m. The polygon is an interpretation product. Every census and volume downstream inherits its positional uncertainty, and the honest way to carry that is a sensitivity: relabel with the boundary moved by its uncertainty and report the volume swing. On this model, moving the southern panel's eastern edge one cell west, from 1575 to 1525, strips column 11 out of the nine southern rows: nine nodes cross from block 1 to block 0, carrying roughly 9 times 2500 m2 times the local 36 m of zone A, about 0.8 million m3, from one block's books to the other's. The census makes that arithmetic instant, which is a large part of its value.

## Blocks and the tie table

The Professional tier's open finding, the eastern BaseB contradiction, sits entirely in block 0: W3 and W4, the contradicting wells, are east of the fault. Cutting the model did not resolve that contradiction, but it did QUARANTINE it: block 1's geometry is uncontested by any well, and statistics computed in block 1 stand on cleaner ground than the field-wide numbers did. This is a general and quietly powerful use of blocks: they localise doubt.

## Worked example

Answer a review question in the model's own terms: "does the model say the fault seals?" The defensible reply, in three sentences: the model treats the fault as a statistical and accounting boundary, so no data crosses it in population and volumes report per block; it takes no position on hydraulic sealing, which is not derivable from a map trace; if sealing matters to a decision, the decision needs fault-rock or pressure evidence from outside this model, and the block framework is ready to carry whichever answer arrives.
