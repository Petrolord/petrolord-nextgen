# A hundred percent is arithmetic

{{panel:fc-inhibitor-integrity-explorer}}

Type a corrosion inhibitor efficiency of 100 percent and the engine does not
argue with you. At 100 percent efficiency and 95.000000 percent availability
the effective protection is 95.000000 percent, with no clamp applied and no
hidden ceiling of its own. The effective protection comes out as exactly the
availability, which is what the time average has to give when the chemical
removes everything while it is on.

Then the engine adds a note, and the note is the lesson:

> an efficiency of 100 percent is the arithmetic of the number typed in, not a prediction: no inhibitor removes all metal loss while it is on.

## What that sentence is doing

It draws the line between a calculation and a claim. The calculation is correct.
Take a figure of 100 percent, apply it for the fraction of the time the
programme is available, and the protection is the availability. Nothing in that
chain is wrong and the engine will not refuse it.

What the engine will not do is let the result stand as a statement about a
chemical. No corrosion inhibitor takes the metal loss to zero while it is on
the wall, so a typed 100 percent is an input a user has chosen and not a
property anything measured. The note says which of the two the reader is
looking at, in the engine's own words, on the same screen as the number.

## The habit this teaches

Every screen in this suite carries inputs a user typed and outputs a model
produced, and they look identical once rendered in the same table. The question
to ask of any figure is where it came from, and here the page answers it: the
effective protection is derived and the efficiency was typed.

## The ceiling that does exist

There is a clamp, and it sits above 100 percent rather than at it. An efficiency
of 120.000000 percent with an availability of 90.000000 percent gives an
effective protection of 90.000000 percent, and the engine names the clamp it
applied:

> the inhibitor efficiency was clamped from 120 to 100 percent

So the arithmetic accepts a typed 100 percent and reports it plainly, while
anything above 100 percent is moved to 100 percent and the move is declared.
The two behaviours are consistent. A value that is arithmetically meaningful is
computed and annotated, and a value that is arithmetically impossible is
corrected in the open.

## What it means for a graded field

Because the corrosion inhibitor arithmetic is arithmetic over typed
percentages, a capstone can grade it and you can check it. That is only true
while the clamps and the notes are visible. A silent clamp would make a graded field
disagree with a learner who divided correctly.

## Exercise

Record the effective protection at 100 percent efficiency with 95.000000
percent availability, then at 120.000000 percent efficiency with 90.000000
percent availability. State the relationship the engine's note draws between
the first of those figures and the availability you typed, and record what the
engine says it did to the second one.
