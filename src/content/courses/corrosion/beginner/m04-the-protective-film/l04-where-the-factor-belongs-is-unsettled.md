# Where the factor belongs is unsettled

{{panel:fc-rate-explorer}}

Here is a question this module cannot answer about its own arithmetic, and it says so in its list of held items. The protective film multiplier has to be applied somewhere in the chain. This engine forms the series combination of the reaction term and the transport term first, and then multiplies the combined rate by the film factor. Multiplying the reaction term instead, before the two are combined, is a different physical claim.

It is a different claim because the two terms sit in series. A film that slows the surface reaction reduces one of the two resistances. A film applied to the combined rate reduces the throughput of both steps together. Where the reaction is already the slower step the two treatments come close. Where transport controls, they give materially different answers, and transport controls at the studio's own defaults.

## What the engine does and what it declares

The engine multiplies the combined rate, and it states in its held list that which of the two forms the published correlation intends is not established here. That is the honest position. The module is not claiming its order is the published one, and it is not hiding that a reader has a choice to make.

The consequence for this course is direct. Nothing downstream of that factor is graded anywhere in these three tiers. A rate carrying a film credit depends on an unresolved modelling decision as well as on three unsourced constants, so a capstone that graded one would be examining a learner on a question the engine itself declines to settle.

It also changes what a reader should do with a low rate from a hot line. Two defensible arrangements of the same correction exist, this module implements one of them, and they part company wherever the transport step is the slower of the two. A rate quoted without saying which arrangement produced it is a rate quoted without one of its assumptions.

## Why the capstones are still clean

Every capstone scenario in this course sits below its own computed onset, so its film multiplier is exactly one. Multiplying by one before the combination or after it gives the same number to the last bit, so the unresolved question cannot move any graded field. That is an identity rather than a tolerance, which is why it is worth stating as the reason.

Carry the habit rather than the detail. When a calculation applies a correction, ask what it is applied to, and ask whether the answer would change if it were applied one step earlier. Where the answer is no, you have a free choice. Where the answer is yes and nothing in the module says which is intended, you have found a question that a number on the screen is quietly answering for you.
