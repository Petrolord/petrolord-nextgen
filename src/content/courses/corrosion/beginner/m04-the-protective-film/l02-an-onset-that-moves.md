# An onset that moves

{{panel:fc-rate-explorer}}

The temperature at which the protective film starts to be credited is not a fixed number in this engine. It is computed from the carbon dioxide fugacity, and it moves a long way as the fugacity changes. That single fact retires a sentence people carry in from elsewhere, that the film appears at some round temperature.

Walk the fugacity and watch the onset. At a fugacity of 0.050000 bar the computed onset is 132.297731 C. At 0.200000 bar it is 108.978001 C. At 1.000000 bar it is 85.058955 C, at 2.000000 bar 75.655878 C, at 5.000000 bar 63.957912 C, at 20.000000 bar 47.679108 C and at 60.000000 bar 35.853960 C. Across those swept fugacities the onset moves by 96.443771 degrees Celsius.

## The shipped case sits below its own onset

At the shipped defaults the carbon dioxide fugacity is 1.344240 bar, the computed onset is 80.984504 C, and the stream runs at 60.000000 C. The multiplier there is 1.000000000000, exactly one, so no film credit is taken on that screen at all.

Two of the engine's worked streams make the same point from opposite sides. One runs at 119.400000 C with a fugacity of 1.467680 bar, so its computed onset is 79.792274 C and its multiplier is 0.206012. The other runs at 58.200000 C with a fugacity of 0.753085 bar, so its onset is 89.053653 C and its multiplier is 1.000000000000. One expression, two streams, two different answers about whether a film exists.

The direction of the movement is the useful part. The onsets above fall as the fugacity rises, so a stream at a given temperature can sit below its onset at one pressure and above it at another with nothing else changed. Two screenings of one line at two operating pressures can therefore differ in whether any film credit is in the number, and the printed rate alone will not tell you which case you have.

## Why a fixed number would be wrong

A help page quoting a single onset temperature would be right at one fugacity and wrong at every other. The panel beside this lesson therefore prints the computed onset for the conditions you set rather than a constant, and you should expect the figure to move as you change the pressure or the composition. Record it beside the rate whenever you copy a screening down, because it is the number that says whether the film is in the answer.

What the published correlation turns at is a separate question and it is held for literature. So are the three constants of the expression that produces this onset. The onset the engine computes is teachable because it is measured out of the engine's own behaviour by bisection and cross checked against a closed form. It is still not a sourced temperature.

## Exercise

Set the fugacity to 0.200000 bar, then to 5.000000 bar, then to 60.000000 bar, and record the computed onset at each against the 108.978001 C, 63.957912 C and 35.853960 C above. Take the smallest and the largest of the seven onsets in this lesson and form their difference yourself, then compare it with the 96.443771 degrees Celsius the digest reports. Now hold a temperature of 60.000000 C and say at which of those fugacities a film credit is being taken.
