# Why offshore supply is planned

{{panel:marine-voyage-calculator}}

An offshore installation lives on what the supply vessels bring. Containers, tool baskets, casing, diesel, water, mud, brine, cement and barite all leave a shore base on a vessel's deck or in its tanks. This course treats offshore supply as a set of stated routes, vessels, cargoes, rates and rules that can be written down and computed. This lesson says what a voyage plan answers and what this tier asks of you.

## Four questions a voyage plan answers

A planner facing one voyage asks four things. How long will the vessel be away? How much fuel will it burn, and at what cost? Will the cargo fit? And which limit is closest to being reached? The course's engine answers each from inputs the planner states, and prints the rule it applied beside every answer.

## Why the plan is written down

Each figure rests on inputs: the route and its legs, the speed, the hours in port and at each installation, a weather allowance and the activities it slows, a fuel burn for each activity, a fuel price, and the capacities of the deck and the tanks. Change one input and a figure moves. A plan that names its inputs can be checked by a colleague and rerun when the weather or the cargo changes. A figure with no inputs behind it can only be believed or doubted.

So the engine holds no domain figure of its own: no built-in speed, no standard fuel burn, no typical deck size. Every such figure is an input, and a call that leaves one out is refused by name, as the last lesson of this module shows.

## What this tier covers

The Associate tier is about voyages, capacity and the binding constraint. You will plan milk runs and dedicated voyages, their hours by activity, the weather factor, the fuel and its cost, and the capacity of the usable deck, the deck load, the deadweight and each tank. You will read every constraint's utilisation and name the binding one. The Professional tier builds these voyages into a fleet and a packed deck, and the Expert tier queues the shore base.

## What the engine leaves to people

A voyage plan sets no departure time, forecasts no weather and prices no vessel hire. Contracting a vessel belongs to the procurement course, and the stock the cargo replenishes belongs to the materials course.
