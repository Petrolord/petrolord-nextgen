# Sources and the dates they were read

{{panel:marine-voyage-calculator}}

Every rule the engine applies either comes from a published text or is a convention the engine states as its own. This lesson names the six texts the course draws on, with the edition of each, its licence, what the course takes from it and the date it was read. Every one of them was read on 2026-09-27.

## Three ways to use a text

The licence decides how a text may be used, so the course treats each kind differently. A text with no licence printed is cited for its figures and formulas, by table, equation and example, and none of its prose is reproduced. A text under a share-alike licence is cited, and its wording is never pasted in. A text under publisher copyright is taught by its ideas alone, in the course's own words. The two CC BY 4.0 papers would allow quotation with credit, and the course still cites them by table and equation, so every explanation you read is written for this course.

## What this tier reads

At the Associate tier two texts do most of the work. Skoko et al. print fuel burns by activity for a PSV and an AHTS, a fuel price of USD 870 a tonne, the distance each vessel covers in a day at its economic speed, and the optimal days by activity of a month's work with the fuel cost that follows (their Tables 1, 4, 5 and 7). The engine reproduces the published daily figures and the AHTS month exactly; module three runs those checks. Aas, Halskau and Wallace describe offshore supply in ideas the engine adopts: deck cargo measured in square metres with no stacking, bulk carried in segregated tanks, an economical speed, and weather that limits both sailing and loading at the installation.

The other four texts belong to later tiers. The queueing notes, the handbook and the arXiv paper drive the shore base at Expert, and the article on first-fit decreasing drives the deck plan at Professional.

## Texts that were looked for

No openly readable offshore paper was found that prints a fleet-sizing example with its inputs and a vessel count. Several papers were paywalled or blocked where they were sought, and industry guidance was not used. The course says so plainly and validates what it can through the published parts it could read.

## Why dates matter

A lecture note is revised, a handbook stays in draft and a web article changes whenever someone edits it. A figure checked against one edition may not match the next. Naming the edition and the date read lets anyone repeat the check against the same text.
