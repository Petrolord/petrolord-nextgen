# The voyage in days

{{panel:marine-voyage-calculator}}

Hours suit a single voyage, but a planner who has to fit voyages into a week thinks in days. The engine gives both: every voyage's total hours, and its days, which are the hours over 24. This lesson reads the Ekene voyages in days and shows what the days are for.

## Days are hours over 24

The conversion has no allowance and no rounding in it. The milk run's 62.072727 hours are 2.586364 days. A voyage's days include every activity the voyage spends time on, after the weather factor: sailing, port and field.

| voyage | total hours | days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| milk-run | 62.072727 | 2.586364 |
| EKA | 32.727273 | 1.363636 |
| EKJ | 36.436364 | 1.518182 |
| EKB | 32.945455 | 1.372727 |
| EKF | 38.727273 | 1.613636 |

When a route has several voyages, as a dedicated route does, the engine adds them. The four dedicated voyages take 5.868182 days in all, and the Total days tile shows that sum. The milk run is a single voyage, so its total is its own 2.586364 days.

## Reading the two totals side by side

Serving the four installations once each takes 2.586364 days on the milk run and 5.868182 days on dedicated voyages. The dedicated route sails further in all and pays the port time four times, and those two effects account for the gap. That comparison is only fair because both plans state the same vessel, the same speed, the same port and field hours and the same weather. Change any one on one side and the comparison says something else.

## A day is a planning unit

A day in this course is a planning measure: 24 hours of a voyage's time. It says nothing about the clock. The engine sets no departure time, no night holding at an installation that does not work cargo in the dark, and no sailing plan by day. Those belong to the planner's own schedule, and the engine leaves them out. Where a plan needs them, it adds them to the stated hours: an installation that takes cargo only in daylight can be given longer field hours, stated and named in the plan.

## Where the days go next

At this tier the days of a voyage are a result to read and compare. The Professional tier takes them up: a period's demand sets a number of voyages, and those voyages times their days become the vessel-days a fleet must supply.
