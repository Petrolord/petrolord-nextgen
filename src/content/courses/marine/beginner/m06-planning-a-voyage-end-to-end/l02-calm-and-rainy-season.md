# Calm weather and the rainy season

{{panel:marine-voyage-calculator}}

A supply plan made in the dry season can fail in the wet one. The cargo is the same, but the voyages run longer and burn more fuel. This lesson compares the Ekene PSV milk run in calm weather and with the rainy-season allowance, and separates what the weather moves from what it leaves alone.

## Two seasons, one voyage

The calm plan states a weather factor of 1 on every activity. The rainy-season plan states 1.2 on sailing and field time.

The rainy season adds hours and fuel, and the fuel bill rises by 2829.872727. The binding constraint and its utilisation do not move at all.

## What weather changes, and what it leaves

Weather changes time, and fuel follows time. It leaves the cargo where it is. The deck area, the deck load, the deadweight and every tank carry the same load against the same capacity in both seasons, so every utilisation is the same. A voyage that fits in calm weather fits in the rainy season; it just takes longer and costs more.

That separation is useful. A planner can settle the cargo question once, with the capacities, and then ask the time and fuel questions for each season with its own stated factor. It also guards against a common slip: blaming the weather for a voyage that was overloaded from the start. If a voyage is infeasible, the reasons name a capacity, and no weather factor, calm or rough, will change that verdict.

## The seasonal factor is a choice

The 1.2 is the Ekene planner's stated rainy-season allowance, and it applies to sailing and field time only. A planner with a vessel's own records from past wet seasons might state another factor, or name port time as well. Each choice moves the hours and the fuel, and each belongs beside the figures it produces. The course asks that a plan quoting a rainy-season fuel bill name the factor and the activities in the same sentence.

## Where the season matters more

At this tier the season changes a voyage's days and its fuel bill. When voyages are added up over a week at the Professional tier, each voyage's extra days are counted once for every voyage the week needs, so the season's allowance grows with the number of voyages.
