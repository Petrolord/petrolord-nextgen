# Published figures that do not reproduce

{{panel:marine-base-calculator}}

Every source this course leans on was read on 2026-09-27, and every figure it quotes from one was run through the engine. Most reproduce exactly. A few do not, or carry a quirk, and the course shows each as it found it.

## A total the rounded days do not give

Skoko, Lusic, Sanchez-Varela and Boko (J. Mar. Sci. Eng. 12(2), 263, 1 February 2024, CC BY 4.0) print, in Tables 5 and 7, the optimal month of an AHTS and of a PSV with its fuel cost. The AHTS row reproduces exactly: the engine returns 80847.360000 against the printed USD 80,847.36. The PSV row does not. Its Table 5 days, rounded to two decimals, give 186214.104000 through the engine, 59.996000 short of the printed USD 186,274.10. The rounded days in the table cannot rebuild the total printed beside them, so the course uses the AHTS row and leaves the PSV total out.

## A slip in a printed table

Adan and Resing print a mean wait of 1.53 at five servers in Table 5.1, where the formula gives 1.524986, which rounds to 1.52. Every other figure in Tables 5.1 and 5.2 is the engine's rounded. The course names the printed figure a slip and quotes the engine's.

## A handbook in draft

Iversen's Teletraffic Engineering Handbook was read in its ITU-D draft of 20 June 2001. Its Example 12.3.1 is in seconds; the golden inputs read the second as the working hour, which changes no figure, since every time in the call is in the same unit. Both mean waits reproduce at three decimals.

## An approximation read at second hand

Cosmetatos (1975) was not read directly. The M/D/c formula is taken as Liu, Pantelidis, Tam and Chow print it in eq. (2) of arXiv 2102.05851v2 (CC BY 4.0), and the engine labels it an approximation.

## A living page

The Wikipedia article on first-fit decreasing (CC BY-SA 4.0) changes over time, so the course cites revision 1317275412 of 17 October 2025 and its examples as that revision prints them. Johnson's 1973 thesis and Dosa's 2007 paper were not read directly.

## A usable share stated two ways

Skoko et al. treat 85% of a vessel's carrying capacity as usable; the Ekene fixture states a usable deck fraction of 0.75. Neither is a default: the usable fraction is a required input on every call.

## What the course does with a quirk

It names the edition and the date read, shows both figures, and quotes the engine's. It does not tune an input until a printed figure appears. A publisher's text, Aas, Halskau and Wallace (2009), is taught by concept and quoted nowhere.
