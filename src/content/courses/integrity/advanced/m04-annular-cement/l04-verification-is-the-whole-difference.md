# Verification is the whole difference

The rule you met on the Associate tier and the rule you just met on this one are the same rule, wearing different clothes.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## Two statements of one principle

On the Associate tier, an element that has not been verified degrades its envelope. It gets the same verdict as an element known to be damaged, because not knowing is treated as a deficiency and not as a neutral state.

Here, cement that has not been logged needs 100 m MD where logged cement needs 30 m MD. More than three times the length, for the same material, because nobody looked.

Both rules refuse the assumption that an untested thing is probably fine. That assumption is the commonest way a well ends up categorised better than it is.

## Where they differ, and why

The two rules differ in how they let you respond.

| | Unverified element | Unlogged annular cement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The penalty | The envelope is degraded | The requirement rises to 100 m MD |
| Can you buy it back with more? | No. Extra verified elements do not repair it | Yes. 100 m of cement passes unlogged |
| The clean route | Test it | Log it |

The envelope rollup is a chain and the worst status present decides the whole, so no quantity of good links mends a link nobody has checked. Length is a continuous margin, so the standard will trade metres for evidence.

## What that trade actually costs

Five of the nine swept annular lengths pass with a log and fail without one, and the digest prices the log at 70 m of cement. That is slurry, rig time, and a longer interval you may not have room for above the source.

The point is not that logging is cheap. It is that the standard has already decided evidence is worth 70 m, so a skipped log should be skipped deliberately, with the cement taken to 100 m, rather than skipped in the hope that 40 m is accepted.

## The habit to leave with

Whenever a rule in this engine changes its answer without the well changing, look for a verification flag. `foundation` on a plug, `verifiedByLog` on annular cement, element status on an envelope. Each one is a claim about evidence, each one moves a threshold, and each one is entered by a person.
