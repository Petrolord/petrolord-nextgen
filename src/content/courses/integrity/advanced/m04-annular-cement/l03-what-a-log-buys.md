# What a log buys

The engine takes one boolean. Deciding what goes in it is the whole of the engineering.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## One flag, no quality field

`verifiedByLog` is true or false. There is no bond index input, no coverage percentage, no tool type, no vintage. The engine does not grade your log. It asks whether you have one and applies the corresponding requirement.

So every judgement about whether the log demonstrates a barrier happens before the call, in a human head, and the flag records the conclusion. Set it true on a poor log and the engine drops the requirement from 100 m MD to 30 m MD and reports a pass.

## What a cement evaluation log does demonstrate

That there is material behind the pipe over a measured interval, that it is acoustically consistent with set cement, and that it is bonded to the casing. Modern tools add an azimuthal picture, so you can see whether the cement goes all the way round or channels down one side.

That is exactly what the length rule is short of otherwise. It tells you where the top of cement really is rather than where the job ticket says it should be, and whether the interval is continuous.

## What it does not demonstrate

It is not a pressure test. It shows a condition, not a performance. Nothing in a log proves the annulus will hold a differential.

It reads the casing to cement interface far better than the cement to formation interface, so a barrier can leak on the outside while looking bonded on the inside. It says nothing about a microannulus that opens later when the well cools. And it is a snapshot of a well that has since carried on working.

## The pairing that follows

This is why a logged annular barrier is quoted alongside a positive test of the plug in front of it. The published programme says it in every barrier step: verify by tag and pressure test. The log tells you the annulus is filled, the test tells you the barrier in the bore holds, and neither substitutes for the other.

## Before you set the flag

Was the interval logged with a tool suited to this casing and fluid? Is the cement continuous over the full length you are claiming, not merely present somewhere in it? Does the coverage go all the way round?

If the answer to any of those is no, the honest flag is false and the requirement is 100 m MD.
