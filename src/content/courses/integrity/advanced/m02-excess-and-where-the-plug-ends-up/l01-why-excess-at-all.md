# Why excess at all

Nobody mixes the theoretical volume and expects a plug. The question is what the extra is for, and where it goes.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## Three reasons the theoretical volume is never enough

The first is hole enlargement. An open hole is not the bit diameter. Washouts, breakouts and swelling sections all mean the real capacity per metre is larger than the nominal one, sometimes by a lot, and a caliper is the only honest source for how much.

The second is contamination. The leading and trailing edges of the slurry meet spacer and mud, and some length at each end is never going to set as design cement. Excess buys back the length you are going to lose to those interfaces.

The third is losses. Slurry left in surface lines and in the string, and slurry that goes into a thief zone, are volume you paid for and will never see in the plug.

## How the engine applies it

Excess is a single multiplier on the design volume. The slurry volume is the hole capacity times the plug length times one plus the excess. Nothing else in the design changes.

On the published fixture, a 150 m plug at 20 per cent excess calls for 6.596 m3 of slurry. The same plug at zero excess calls for 5.497 m3.

Excess is required to be zero or positive. A negative excess is refused, because a job pumped short of its own theoretical volume is not a design decision the arithmetic should help you express.

## The extra cement goes up, not down

This is the part people miss. The plug base is fixed by where you set the stinger shoe, so the extra volume has nowhere to go except upward. Excess makes the plug longer at the top, it does not make it deeper.

| Excess | Slurry, m3 | Settled top, m MD | Settled top above the design top, m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 5.497 | 1850 | 0 |
| 0.2 | 6.596 | 1820 | 30 |
| 0.5 | 8.245 | 1775 | 75 |
| 1 | 10.993 | 1700 | 150 |

At 100 per cent excess the plug is twice the designed length, all of the growth above the design top. If a rule check needs the plug to sit clear of something shallower, excess is what pushes you into it.

## Exercise

For each of those two cases, write down where the base and the top ended up, and say which of them moved.
