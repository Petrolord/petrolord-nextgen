# A pump chart is not a plug top

Two depths, both correct, one of them wrong to write on the tour report.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## Which one goes in the record

At the end of displacement on the published fixture the pump chart supports a top of 1801.562 m MD. Hours later the plug is at 1820 m MD, and 1820 m MD is what the well has.

The engine keeps both and names them differently for exactly this reason. The as-pumped top is a record of a condition that lasted until you started tripping. The plugged top is the plug. Everything downstream wants the second one.

That includes every rule check. A permanent barrier has a minimum length and has to extend a set distance above the source of inflow, and both of those are measured on the settled plug. Record the as-pumped top and you will be checking the rules against a plug that stopped existing when the stinger cleared the cement, and the error runs the wrong way, making the plug look longer and shallower than it is.

## Tag it anyway

The settled top is a calculation, and calculations assume the hole is the size you told them. Hole enlargement is the one input excess exists to cover and the one input you never know exactly. So the calculated settled top is a prediction, and the tag is the measurement.

Tag depth, hole condition and the calculated settled top all belong in the record together. The gap between the last two is the best hole enlargement estimate that job will ever produce, and it is worth carrying into the next plug in the same hole section.

## The settle rate is linear

The drop from the as-pumped top to the settled top is proportional to slurry volume. On the published geometry it runs at 2.795 m per m3 of slurry, which is a fixed property of that hole and that stinger.

| Excess | Settled top, m MD | Drop from as-pumped, m |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1850 | 15.365 |
| 0.2 | 1820 | 18.438 |
| 0.5 | 1775 | 23.047 |
| 1 | 1700 | 30.730 |

Double the slurry and you double the drop, from 15.365 m at zero excess to 30.730 m at 100 per cent. Change the geometry and the rate changes with it, so it is a per-well number, not a rule of thumb to carry between wells.
