# The as-pumped top

There is a depth you can read off the pump chart at the end of displacement. It is a real depth, and it is not the plug top.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## What the balanced height is measuring

While the stinger is in the hole, the slurry is not in the hole. It is in two connected spaces: the annulus outside the stinger, and the bore inside it. The height the slurry reaches is its volume divided by the capacity of those two spaces added together.

The engine calls that the balanced height, and subtracting it from the plug base gives the as-pumped top. On the published fixture the balanced height is 198.438 m and the as-pumped top is 1801.562 m MD.

That is a genuine measurement of a genuine condition. If you could see into the well at the moment displacement stopped, the top of the cement in the annulus would be at 1801.562 m MD, and so would the top of the cement inside the string.

## It is already taller than the plug

Look at the zero excess row. A 150 m plug at zero excess stands 165.365 m tall as pumped, and its as-pumped top is 1834.635 m MD, which is 15.365 m shallower than the 1850 m design top.

| Excess | Balanced height, m | As-pumped top, m MD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 165.365 | 1834.635 |
| 0.1 | 181.901 | 1818.099 |
| 0.2 | 198.438 | 1801.562 |
| 0.5 | 248.047 | 1751.953 |
| 1 | 330.730 | 1669.270 |

The reason is the capacity ratio from Module 1. The annulus and the bore together come to 0.9071 of the full hole capacity, so the same volume has to stand taller in them than it would in the open hole. No excess is needed to produce that, and no excess can remove it.

## When it cannot balance at all

Push the volume far enough and the balanced height exceeds the plug base depth, which would put the top of the slurry above surface. The engine will not pretend that is a plug. It returns a warning that the balanced slurry column exceeds the stinger depth and the plug cannot balance. Shallow plugs with generous excess are where you will meet it.
