# Pulling the stinger

The plug top moves while you are tripping out, and it moves even on a job with no excess at all. This lesson is about why.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## The slurry changes container

Nothing is added and nothing is lost when you pull the stinger. The same volume of slurry is in the well before and after. What changes is the shape of the space it is allowed to occupy.

With the stinger in the hole, the slurry stands in two narrow spaces at once. It is in the annulus, which is the hole with the stinger steel taken out of it, and it is in the stinger bore. Add those two capacities together and on the published fixture they come to 0.9071 of the full hole capacity.

Pull the stinger and both of those spaces stop existing. The steel that was blocking part of the hole comes out, and the bore that was carrying the rest of the slurry comes out with it. What is left is one open space of the full hole capacity.

A fixed volume in a larger capacity is a shorter column. The base does not move, because the base is the bottom of the slurry and it is sitting where it was. So the top comes down.

## The engine says it in one line

The settled top is the plug base minus the slurry volume divided by the hole capacity. That is the only place the hole capacity is used on its own, and it is why the settled top does not care what the stinger looked like.

| Excess | As-pumped top, m MD | Settled top, m MD | Drop, m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1834.635 | 1850 | 15.365 |
| 0.1 | 1818.099 | 1835 | 16.901 |
| 0.2 | 1801.562 | 1820 | 18.438 |
| 0.5 | 1751.953 | 1775 | 23.047 |

## This is geometry, not over-pumping

Read the first row again. Zero excess, and the top still drops 15.365 m. There is no extra cement in that job to explain it.

The drop grows with excess only because the drop is proportional to the volume, and excess is a multiplier on the volume. At 100 per cent excess the slurry doubles and the drop doubles too, to 30.730 m. The mechanism is unchanged.

Anyone who reads the drop as an excess effect will respond by tightening the excess, and will be surprised when the gap between the pump chart and the tag refuses to close.
