# Onward

Where P&A work goes once you are past the arithmetic in this tier.

## Cement evaluation as a discipline

The 30 m credit for a verified annulus rests on a log, and this course treated the log as a yes or no. In practice it is a specialism. Bond, ultrasonic and azimuthal tools each measure something different, none measures a hydraulic seal directly, and interpreting them across changing casing, fluid and cement conditions is where the judgement sits. Learn what a cement log can and cannot tell you.

## Rock to rock barriers

This engine reasons about plugs inside a hole. Where the annulus behind casing is not competent, the barrier must be restored across the full cross section of the well, by section milling or by perforating, washing and cementing in place. That is how the annular isolation module five never tested actually gets built.

## Jurisdiction

The rules used here follow commonly cited NORSOK D-010 conventions, and the header is explicit that they are "defaults overridable; the standard document governs". Requirements vary between jurisdictions and change over time, in barrier lengths, in verification and in what may be left in the ground. Work from the governing document for the well in front of you, and treat any default in a tool as a starting point.

## The cost question

Nothing in this engine costs anything. The takeoff counts plugs and cement volume and names the plugs it could not compute. There is no rig time, no logging cost, no milling cost, and no comparison between a longer plug and the verification run that would let you set a shorter one.

That gap is deliberate, and it is where most real abandonment decisions are made. A programme that passes cheaply and one that passes expensively look identical here. Putting a number on the difference is the natural next step.
