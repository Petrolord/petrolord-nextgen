# Working the capstone

A method for taking a well you have never seen and producing a defensible reading of it, in an order that does not double back.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## Geometry before rules

Start with placement, one plug at a time, for every plug that has a geometry. Compute the four capacities: the hole, the annulus outside the stinger, the inside of the stinger, and the slurry volume the plug interval demands with its excess. From those you get the balanced height, the displacement, and the as-pumped top.

Take the second step deliberately, because it is the one people skip. The settled top is not the as-pumped top. Once the stinger comes out, the slurry redistributes from annulus plus stinger bore into the full hole and the top falls. Work with the settled top when you talk about where the cement finished, and remember that at zero excess it lands on the design top exactly.

## Then the rules, one plug at a time

Check each plug against the length rule with its own foundation. Open hole and unsupported is 100 m. On a mechanical or tagged foundation it is 50 m. A surface plug is 50 m. Do not carry one plug's foundation across to another.

Then check the source rules zone by zone rather than plug by plug. For each flowing zone, ask which plugs cover the source and clear it by 50 m, and which sit entirely above the source top and pass the length rule alone. The same plug can answer differently for two zones.

Check the annular cement separately, and both ways: 30 m with a log, 100 m without. Whether the log changes the answer tells you where a logging run would earn its keep.

## Read the verdict last

Only when you have the placements and the per zone lists should you read the programme result. Zone rows first, then the surface plug row, then the overall pass.

Two habits will keep you out of trouble.

A zone with no primary cannot pass, however many secondaries it has. If `primaryQualifying` is empty, stop looking for a rescue elsewhere in the well and find the plug that has to cover the source.

A margin reported as negative is a shortfall in metres, not a failure code. A margin of -2 m and a margin of -70 m both read as a failing check, and they are entirely different problems. Quote the metres when you report it.

## Exercise

1. Rehearse the whole sequence on the published case first, so the order is automatic before the conditions change.
2. For every failing check you meet, write the shortfall in metres beside it rather than the word failed.
3. For each zone, name the plug you would add or lengthen, and say which check you expect it to satisfy.
