# What balanced means

A plug that is not balanced starts moving the moment you stop pumping. Balance is the whole reason the number on the pump chart means anything at all.

{{panel:wi-pa-explorer}}

## Level columns, and nothing to drive a U-tube

You pump cement down a stinger and it turns the corner at the shoe and comes back up the annulus. For a while the slurry inside the string and the slurry outside it stand at different heights. Two columns of the same fluid at different heights, connected at the bottom, are a U-tube, and a U-tube moves.

Displacement stops at the one point where the inside column and the annulus column stand level. At that moment there is no head difference across the shoe, so there is nothing to push slurry one way or the other while you pull out. That is what balanced means. It is a statement about heights, not about volumes.

The engine states it as one line: the balanced height is the slurry volume divided by the sum of the annulus capacity and the inside capacity. On the published fixture that height is 198.438 m, and it is 198.438 m on both sides of the pipe at once.

## The spacer has to obey the same rule

The slurry is not the only fluid in the picture. There is spacer ahead of the cement, in the annulus, and spacer behind it, inside the string. Those two columns also sit across the shoe from each other, so they also have to stand level or the balance you just achieved is undone by the fluid above it.

Equal heights in two different geometries mean unequal volumes. On the published fixture, 1 m3 of spacer ahead is balanced by 0.386 m3 behind, and both of those stand 41.709 m tall.

## What balance does not promise

Balance says the plug will not U-tube. It does not say the plug is where you designed it, it does not say the cement is uncontaminated, and it does not survive the stinger being pulled unchanged. Module 2 is about that last point, and it is the one that catches people.

## Exercise

Open the panel on the published fixture and read the balanced height. Confirm that it is a single number, not one for the annulus and one for the string.

Change the spacer ahead volume and watch the spacer behind volume follow it. Work out, before you look, whether behind should be larger or smaller than ahead, and say why in terms of the two flow areas.
