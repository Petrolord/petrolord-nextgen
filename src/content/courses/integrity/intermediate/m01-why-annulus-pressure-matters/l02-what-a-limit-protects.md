# What a limit protects

The limit is not a property of the annulus. It is a property of the weakest thing standing around it.

{{panel:wi-annulus-explorer}}

## Pressure pushes both ways

Put pressure in an annulus and it acts on every wall of that annulus at once.

The wall outside it feels the pressure from the inside, so the outer casing is loaded in burst. The wall inside it feels the pressure from the outside, so the inner string is loaded in collapse.

The pipe that yields first is whichever has least room left, so an annulus limit is never a single rating you can look up. It is a comparison across everything bounding the volume.

## Inside it and outside it

The candidate list on the published well is exactly that mixture:

| element | role | limit, Pa |
|---|---|---|
| 9-5/8 production casing burst | outer-casing-burst | 40000000 |
| 7 in production liner burst | inner-casing-burst | 35000000 |
| 4-1/2 tubing collapse | inner-tubing-collapse | 25000000 |

Two of those are walls of pipe with the annulus on one side, and they are loaded in opposite senses. The strongest rating on the list belongs to the outer casing, and it is not the element that governs. The governing element on this well is the 4-1/2 tubing collapse.

## The elements that are not steel

Two more roles exist in the engine, and both matter on real wells.

A shoe formation is a bounding element: pressure in an annulus that is open to a formation can break it down and broach, and the fracture strength at the shoe is then the limit, no matter what the casing is rated to.

A wellhead or tree rating is the other. It sits at the top of the annulus, at zero vertical depth, so it feels the surface pressure with no help from any fluid column.

## What a limit does not protect

It does not protect against a leak. It protects against the consequence of one.

An annulus sitting below its limit is not thereby healthy. It tells you that if the pressure you are reading holds, no bounding element is being overloaded today. Whether that pressure should be there at all is the barrier question from the Associate tier, and it is a separate question with a separate answer.
