# What a negative MAASP obliges

A MAASP of zero is not a tight operating limit. It is the engine telling you the well has stopped being a design problem.

{{panel:wi-annulus-explorer}}

## It is not a number to design to

Everywhere else in this tier, the output is something you work against. A MAASP of 20585228.21103133 Pa on the published fixture says how much you may put on the annulus, and the sensible response is to stay under it.

Zero with the flag raised does not say that. It says the factored rating of the weakest bounding element is already exceeded by what is standing in the hole. There is no envelope to plan inside, because the load that used it up is not one you are choosing to apply. It is the fluid column. Treating that as a limit to respect, and carrying on, is the misreading the flag exists to prevent.

## It is a finding

Say it in the form the engine means it: the well as configured cannot hold what is already in it.

Three words in that sentence do the work.

**Configured.** The finding is about a state, not an event. Densities, depths, ratings and factors, all of them recorded. Change one and the finding may go away.

**Already.** The exceedance predates any operation. Nothing was applied.

**Cannot hold.** This is a statement about the element, in the differential form module 4 gave you. Not about a leak, not about a rate.

## What it does not tell you

This tier's answer is deliberately narrow. A negative row does not say the element has failed, or is leaking, or when it might. It does not say how the well arrived here, and it does not distinguish a fluid that got heavier from a far side that got lighter, because the row only sees the difference.

Nor does it rank the responses. Lightening the annulus, restoring the column on the far side, or re-rating the element on evidence all change an input, and all three sit outside what this tier computes.

What the finding does oblige is that somebody looks. A row past its rating with the well merely sitting there is not an operating restriction to be logged and left. It is a reason to go back to the inputs and establish which of them is wrong, or which of them has to change.

## Exercise

Produce the clamped case, then find the smallest single change to the annulus density that clears the flag, and the smallest change to the far side density that does the same.

Write both as a one line finding in the form above, naming the element.

Then list, without numbers, what you would need to know about the well before choosing between the two.
