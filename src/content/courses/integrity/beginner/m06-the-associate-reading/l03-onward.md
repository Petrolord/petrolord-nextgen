# Onward

This tier asked whether the barriers hold. The next one asks what they hold against.

## From status to pressure

Everything you have done so far is categorical. An element is verified or it is not, an envelope is intact or it is not, the well is one of four colours, and nothing in the tier carried a unit.

The Professional tier puts limits on the same elements. The casing that was a verified element becomes a casing with a burst rating at a depth. The wellhead becomes a rated pressure. The formation at the shoe becomes a strength. Then you can ask the question this tier could not: how much pressure may this well carry at surface before one of those elements stops being a barrier?

## Annulus pressure

The question is asked one annulus at a time. The allowable surface pressure is the most the weakest element on that annulus will tolerate, and the engine names the element that governs, because the number alone tells you nothing about how to raise it.

Sustained casing pressure, an annulus that builds back up after you bleed it, is the operational reason the subject exists.

## Why a rating is a differential

Here is the idea that reorganises everything. An element does not feel the pressure at surface. It feels the difference between the two sides of itself, at its own depth.

So the allowable surface pressure is the element's factored limit, less the head of the annulus fluid, plus back whatever head the fluid on the far side supplies. Change the fluid in the annulus and the answer moves. Change the fluid behind the casing and it moves again, with nothing having happened to the steel.

That is why a rating printed on a casing tally is not an operating limit.
