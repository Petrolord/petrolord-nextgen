# What a category obliges

The colour is not the finding. The action it commits you to is the finding.

{{panel:wi-envelope-explorer}}

## Green

Both envelopes intact, everything verified. The well operates normally.

The obligation is not nothing. Green is a statement about evidence, and evidence expires. Every element carries a test interval, and the day one falls due without a fresh result the element becomes not verified, the envelope becomes degraded and the well becomes yellow. Green is a thing you maintain, and the work that maintains it is the periodic verification programme.

## Yellow

Degradation somewhere, no failure anywhere. Both envelopes still contain the source.

The well may usually keep producing, and that is what makes yellow the dangerous colour. It is the state a well can sit in for years while a repair is deferred. The obligation is to write down what is degraded, why continued operation is acceptable, what will restore it and by when, and then to hold that date. A yellow well with no restoration plan and no expiry on the plan is being managed by hope.

Note the trap from module three. Yellow does not distinguish an element somebody inspected and found worn from an element nobody has looked at. Both read as degradation, so read the element list, not just the colour.

## Orange

One envelope has failed, or there is only one where two are required. Either way you have a single barrier between the reservoir and the environment, and no spare.

The obligation steps up sharply. Restore the second envelope, or bring the well to a condition where one barrier is sufficient, which usually means killing or securing it so that flow potential genuinely goes away. Orange is a countdown, and continued production on a single barrier is a decision that belongs well above the well site.

## Red

A failed envelope with the other degraded, failed or missing. Nothing you can stand behind.

The obligation is immediate action to regain containment: shut in, secure, and intervene. There is no version of red in which the correct answer is to schedule something.

## Who sets the deadlines

The engine gives you the colour. It does not give you the hours or the days, because those come from the operator's own well integrity management system and from the regulator, and they differ by jurisdiction and by well.
