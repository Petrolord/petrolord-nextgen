# The kinds

Fourteen element kinds is a list nobody remembers. Six groups is a list you can rebuild from first principles.

{{panel:wi-envelope-explorer}}

## The list, grouped by what the thing actually does

**Rock and the seal to it.** In-situ formation, casing cement. The formation itself is a barrier element when it is tight enough to hold, and cement is what bonds the steel to it. These two are the deepest and the least inspectable.

**Steel that contains.** Casing, completion string. Long pressure vessels. They hold because their walls have a rating and the rating has not been eaten away.

**Joints between steel and steel.** Wellhead, tubing hanger, production packer. Everywhere one piece of pipe hands pressure to another there is a seal, and a seal is an element in its own right.

**Things that shut.** Downhole safety valve, Christmas tree, BOP. These are barriers only in the closed position, which is why they are the ones that get function tested rather than merely inspected.

**Fluid that holds pressure down.** Annulus fluid column, kill-weight fluid column. A column of the right density is a barrier. It is also the barrier most easily destroyed by an operation nobody thought of as a barrier operation.

**Things you set to abandon.** Cement plug, mechanical plug. The permanent versions of everything above, and the whole subject of the second engine.

## Why the grouping is worth the effort

Two elements in the same group tend to fail the same way and to be verified the same way. You pressure test a seal. You function test a valve. You log or tag a cement plug. You calculate and monitor a fluid column.

That matters more than it sounds, because independence is about failure mechanisms. Two elements drawn from the same group are weaker evidence of independence than two drawn from different ones. A primary envelope that leans on a valve and a secondary that leans on a different valve of the same design shares more than the drawing suggests.

## The kind does not choose the envelope

Nothing in the list says which envelope a kind belongs to. Casing cement appears twice in the published roster, once in the primary envelope as the production cement and once in the secondary as the intermediate cement. Casing appears twice as well, as the production casing below the packer in one envelope and as the production casing in the other.

Same kind, different job, different element. The next lesson is about who decides.
