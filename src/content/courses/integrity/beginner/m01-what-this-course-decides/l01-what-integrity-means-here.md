# What integrity means here

Every other question about a well can wait. This one has to be answered before the well is allowed to exist.

## Containment is the whole subject

Well integrity is the discipline of keeping what is in the well inside it. Not productivity, not efficiency, not general condition. Containment.

A well is a hole that connects a pressurised formation to the surface, or to the seabed, or to a shallower formation that would rather not have hydrocarbons in it. Left to itself, pressure moves fluid up that hole. Everything you build into the well to stop that movement is an integrity measure, and integrity is the running answer to one question: is the stopping still working?

That question has a direction. It is always asked about a specific source of pressure and a specific place the fluid must not reach. An answer that does not name both is not an answer.

## At every stage of the well's life

A well passes through drilling, completion, production, workover, suspension and permanent abandonment. The fluids change, the pressures change, the hardware in the hole changes, and the crew changes.

The requirement does not change. The engine this course teaches uses one barrier vocabulary for a well being drilled, a well on production and a well being plugged for good. Its list of element kinds puts the cement plug and the mechanical plug alongside the BOP and the Christmas tree, because a permanent plug is doing the job the BOP was doing: standing between a pressure source and somewhere it must not go.

That shared vocabulary is why abandonment is the second half of this engine and not a separate subject. Abandonment is not the end of integrity. It is integrity with no one left on site to watch it.

## Integrity is a status, not a feeling

The engine will not accept an opinion about a well. It accepts elements, each with a recorded status, and it rolls those up.

The published teaching well carries ten barrier elements, five in each envelope. Nine are recorded as verified and one, the downhole safety valve, is recorded as degraded. That single entry is enough to move the whole well off a clean bill of health, and the reason is the subject of this whole course.

Notice what the engine did not need in order to say that. No leak rate, no corrosion model, no view about how long the valve has left. Just a status on an element and a rule about how statuses combine.

## Exercise

Write down, for a well you know or can imagine, the pressure source you are containing and the two places the fluid must not reach. Be specific about depth.

Then list every physical thing you believe stands between that source and each of those places. Keep the list. Module 2 will tell you what it is called and what the engine needs to know about each entry.
