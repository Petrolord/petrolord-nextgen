# The carryforward

Whatever the allowance would not release stays in the pool and comes out later. That is why profit oil rises for years on a field whose revenue is falling the whole time.

## The balance over time

The Designer's default project under the "Generic Royalty/Tax" instruments at four cost recovery limits. Each column is what is still owed to the contractor at the close of the year:

| year | pool at 50 percent | pool at 80 percent | pool at 90 percent | pool at 100 percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 412.0076 | 340.6105 | 316.8115 | 293.0124 |
| 2 | 333.9031 | 198.3346 | 153.1450 | 107.9555 |
| 3 | 264.6871 | 71.4398 | 7.0241 | 0.0000 |
| 4 | 203.4615 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| 5 | 143.4591 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| 7 | 43.9427 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |

A 100 percent limit clears the pool in year 3. A 90 percent limit leaves 7.0241 million USD standing at the end of year 3 and clears in year 4. An 80 percent limit leaves 71.4398 and also clears in year 4. A 50 percent limit is still carrying 43.9427 at the end of year 7 and closes the whole 25 year life with 0.9444 unrecovered.

## What the carryforward does to profit oil

Profit oil is revenue after royalty minus cost recovered, so while the pool is deep the allowance eats the profit oil, and the moment the pool clears the profit oil is released:

| year | grossRevenue | costRecovered at 80 percent | profitOil at 80 percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 271.9889 | 190.3922 | 47.5981 |
| 2 | 244.4628 | 171.1239 | 42.7810 |
| 3 | 219.7286 | 153.8100 | 38.4525 |
| 4 | 197.5024 | 96.6215 | 76.1932 |
| 5 | 191.1513 | 23.6264 | 143.6310 |
| 6 | 171.8074 | 22.2311 | 128.1004 |

Gross revenue falls in every one of those rows, from 271.9889 to 171.8074, and profit oil climbs from 47.5981 to 143.6310 before turning down with everything else. Nothing improved. A balance simply ran out. In year 5 cost recovered is 23.6264, which is that year's opex from the engine's own cost column and nothing more, because there is nothing else left to recover. From that point the cost recovery instrument is doing no work at all: it hands back each year's opex and passes the rest through as profit oil.

Once the pool is clear the limit stops mattering: profit oil in year 5 is 143.6310 at an 80 percent limit, at 90 and at 100. The three ledgers converge and stay converged.

## The mistake

The rising profit oil line reads as a story about the field, and it is a story about the pool. A reader who annotates year 4 or year 5 as an operational improvement has credited a well with a carryforward exhausting itself. The check is to read gross revenue beside it: if revenue is falling while profit oil rises, the pool cleared.

The second error is to expect the pool to unwind smoothly. It does not. Cost recovered at an 80 percent limit runs 190.3922, 171.1239, 153.8100 and then drops to 96.6215 and 23.6264, because the year the pool empties is a partial draw and every year after it is just that year's opex.

## What it refuses

The carried balance accrues nothing. There is no uplift, no interest and no indexation on a dollar waiting in the pool, so the contractor is repaid in nominal terms whenever the repayment happens. The pool is also not an asset that survives: it is not transferable, it does not offset tax, and at year 25 whatever remains is written off in silence. The 0.9444 the 50 percent run still carries is never paid to anybody and appears in no other column.
