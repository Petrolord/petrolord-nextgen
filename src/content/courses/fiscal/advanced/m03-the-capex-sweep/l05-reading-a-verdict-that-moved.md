# Reading a verdict that moved

A verdict can change for three quite different reasons, and only one of them means the fiscal answer changed.

{{panel:ec-comparison-explorer}}

## The field moved

The capex verdict on the default project names Angola - Deepwater PSC the most resilient, giving up 88.6123 million USD, and USA - Gulf of Mexico the most exposed at 267.7301. On the teaching field ODIDI the same six templates return a different verdict: Ghana - Deepwater gives up the least at 181.1922 million USD, Angola - Deepwater PSC the most at 252.6075. Angola crossed from best to worst with all four of its instruments unchanged. What changed was the field underneath, so a resilience verdict is a statement about a regime on a project.

## The range moved

The second reason is the swept range itself. The engine counts eight multipliers in whole steps, so the last is exactly 1.5 and the loss runs from a 20 percent underspend to a 50 percent overrun. A sweep that stops short of its label prices a smaller overrun and understates every loss under it, even where the names it prints stay the same. USA - Gulf of Mexico gives up 267.7301 million USD over the range that reaches 1.5. Count the points before quoting an amount.

## Nothing moved at all

The third reason is that there is nothing to rank. On the published comparison built with capex of 20000, all six templates give up 12727.272727 million USD of contractor NPV across the sweep, identical to six decimals, and the verdict says so: no regime can be ranked on resilience to cost overrun, and all six are named as giving up 12,727.3 million USD each, within 0.1 million USD of each other. A pick by strict less-than would keep the first of six equal values and crown a winner by list order, which is why the sentence declines instead.

## The mistake

The mistake is to treat a change in a verdict as a finding. A verdict is only a verdict when the quantities it ranks are separated by more than the precision they are printed to, and these sentences print one decimal place. Two regimes whose losses both print as 12,727.3 million USD have not been ranked. The engine enforces that on both sweeps, at 0.1 million USD for capex and one percentage point for price. Read the ranked quantities anyway, and check that they differ, that the range is the one you meant and that the project is.

## What the verdicts refuse

They report a name and an amount, never a margin. They will say that a pick was a tie, and they will not say how many regimes on the project return a negative NPV. On ODIDI they name "Nigeria - PIA (2021)" best for the contractor at 1.7 million USD, with no single IRR because its NPV is zero at -19.2% and 12.2%. The verdict is correct, the other five regimes lose money at 12 percent, and the winner clears zero by less than two million USD.
