# What resilience measures

The resilience verdict is one subtraction, and everything a reader wants from the word resilience has to survive that subtraction.

{{panel:ec-comparison-explorer}}

## The quantity behind the sentence

The capex verdict names the regime that gives up the least contractor NPV over the swept capex range and the one that gives up the most, and the quantity it ranks is the first swept point minus the last. That is the NPV given up between a 20 percent underspend and a 50 percent overrun, which is the range the axis carries. The engine sweeps eight multipliers counted in whole steps, so the last point is exactly 1.5 and the loss prices the full overrun the label promises.

On the default project, the contractor NPV given up over the swept range, in million USD:

| regime | x1.0 NPV | loss over the swept range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Angola - Deepwater PSC | 211.3787 | 88.6123 |
| Brazil - Concession | 287.2804 | 100.7185 |
| Ghana - Deepwater | 172.7531 | 109.8425 |
| Nigeria - PIA (2021) | 432.0925 | 243.6525 |
| Generic Royalty/Tax | 397.0445 | 244.0782 |
| USA - Gulf of Mexico | 382.0660 | 267.7301 |

## It ranks money and ignores exposure

The loss is an absolute amount in million USD, neither a fraction nor a slope, so a regime with more NPV to lose is penalised for having it. USA - Gulf of Mexico is named the least resilient with 267.7301 million USD given up, and it is also the regime whose contractor NPV at the base multiplier is 382.0660, the third highest on the project. Angola - Deepwater PSC is named the most resilient with 88.6123 given up, on a base NPV of 211.3787. A contractor choosing on this verdict alone would prefer the smaller prize because it shrinks more slowly.

The reason the tiered regimes shrink slowly is structural. Cost recovery, an R factor split and a resource rent tax uplift all move with capex, so part of an overrun is absorbed by the state before it reaches the contractor's line. A concession with a flat royalty and a full profit share has nothing standing between the overrun and the contractor.

## The mistake

The mistake is turning the sentence into a rate. Reading that a regime gives up 88.6123 million USD and calling it robust to an overrun of any size assumes the curve keeps its slope, and it does not: Angola - Deepwater PSC rises from 213.5335 to 218.7874 across the first interval before it starts falling, so the loss over the whole range is smaller than the loss from its peak. The second mistake is comparing the loss across projects. On ODIDI the same six templates give up between 181.1922 and 252.6075 million USD, and the ordering is different, so a resilience ranking belongs to a field and never to a regime alone.

## What it refuses

The verdict reports two endpoints and a name. It will not tell you the shape between them, it will not report a percentage of base NPV, and it does not ask whether the project was viable at either end. What it does say is when two regimes are too close to separate: a lead under 0.1 million USD counts as no lead, and the sentence names every regime inside that spread rather than picking one.
