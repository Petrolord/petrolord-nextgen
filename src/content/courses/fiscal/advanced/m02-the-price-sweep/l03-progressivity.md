# Progressivity

A regime is called progressive when its government take rises with price, and the sweep measures that with two points out of nine.

{{panel:ec-comparison-explorer}}

## The definition and the climb

A regime whose government take rises with price captures the upside and is progressive. A regime whose take falls with price is regressive: a fixed royalty rate and a fixed tax rate on a growing profit hand the government a shrinking fraction once cost recovery has been paid off. The quantity the sweep reports is a climb, the last swept government take minus the first, at full precision. All six templates on the default project:

Brazil - Concession climbs the most at 20.5076 percentage points, on a resource rent tax at 40 percent that takes a growing share of a growing profit. Angola - Deepwater PSC is next at 19.6873: it splits profit oil on the R factor, and a rising price walks the split into a tier that favours the state, which is why the other two R factor templates climb too, by 10.1420 and 7.8960. The two with a flat royalty, a full contractor split and no resource rent tax fall, by 17.2498 and 10.1897.

## Two points do not make a shape

The climb is an endpoint difference and hides everything between the endpoints. Angola - Deepwater PSC climbs 19.6873 percentage points and is not simply rising: it falls from 63.4520 to 59.5510 over the first interval and only then turns, so a climb measured from 40 USD per bbl starts past its own minimum. Ghana - Deepwater reverses twice inside a positive climb, from 76.7284 down to 76.4647 and from 77.2412 down to 77.0702.

## Progressivity is not a property of the regime

The same six templates on the teaching field ODIDI return climbs of 0.1968 for Nigeria - PIA (2021), 1.9971 for Ghana - Deepwater, 7.4752 for Brazil - Concession, -37.6994 for USA - Gulf of Mexico, -8.3874 for Angola - Deepwater PSC and -23.6661 for Generic Royalty/Tax. Angola crosses from 19.6873 on one field to -8.3874 on the other with its four instruments unchanged. What moved is the field, whose decline, deck and cost stack change where cost recovery binds across the range.

## The mistake

Quoting progressivity as a fact about a regime, rather than about a regime on a project, is the error. On ODIDI the verdict names Brazil - Concession, rising 7.5 percentage points. On the default project it names nobody: the steepest climb, 20.5 points for Brazil - Concession, leads the next, 19.7 for Angola - Deepwater PSC, by less than one percentage point, so the sentence declines to rank.

## What it refuses

The climb sees two points. It cannot report the shape, the minimum, the reversals, or what a percentage point is worth. The verdict is stricter: it ranks only where every regime's point is a share, and only when the lead clears one percentage point. On the Suite test project four templates exceed 100 percent at 40 USD per bbl, so it ranks from 50 to 120 and names Brazil - Concession at 11.2181, against a whole-sweep climb of -13.4045.
