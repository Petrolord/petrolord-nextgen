# Progressivity

A regime is called progressive when the government's share rises with price, and the sweep measures that with two points out of nine.

{{panel:ec-comparison-explorer}}

## The definition and the climb

A regime whose share rises with price captures the upside and is progressive. A regime whose share falls with price is regressive, and the flat-royalty concessions in this template set are exactly that: a fixed royalty rate and a fixed tax rate on a growing profit hand the government a shrinking fraction once cost recovery has been paid off. The quantity the sweep reports is a climb, the last swept share minus the first, at full precision. All six templates on the default project:

The three templates whose profit split is tiered on the R factor climb the most, 12.1305, 10.1420 and 7.8960 percentage points, because a rising price raises cumulative revenue against cumulative cost and walks the split into a tier that favours the state. The two templates with a flat royalty, a full contractor split and no resource rent tax fall, by 17.2498 and 10.1897. Brazil - Concession is a concession too, and its resource rent tax at 40 percent gives it a positive climb of 6.0391.

## Two points do not make a shape

The climb is an endpoint difference and hides everything between the endpoints. Brazil - Concession climbs 6.0391 percentage points and is not rising: it falls from 50.0329 to 46.6052 over the first three points and only then turns, so a climb measured from 40 USD per bbl starts on the far side of its own minimum. Ghana - Deepwater reverses twice inside a positive climb, from 76.7284 down to 76.4647 and from 77.2412 down to 77.0702.

## Progressivity is not a property of the regime

The same six templates on the teaching field ODIDI return climbs of 0.1968 for Nigeria - PIA (2021), 1.9971 for Ghana - Deepwater, -6.9358 for Brazil - Concession, -37.6994 for USA - Gulf of Mexico, -14.8244 for Angola - Deepwater PSC and -23.6661 for Generic Royalty/Tax. Angola crosses from 12.1305 on one field to -14.8244 on the other with its four instruments unchanged. What moved is the field: a different decline, a different deck and a different cost stack change where cost recovery still binds across the range.

## The mistake

Quoting progressivity as a fact about a regime, rather than about a regime on a project over a range, is the error. The verdict sentence rounds the climb to one decimal and names a winner: on ODIDI that winner rises 2.0 percentage points across the whole sweep, which is a ranking of near-flat curves.

## What it refuses

The climb sees two points. It cannot report the shape, the minimum, the reversals or the price at which a curve turns, it cannot say how much money a percentage point is worth, and it will not tell you that one of the two points it used was not a share at all.
