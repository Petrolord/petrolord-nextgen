# The effective tax rate in the table

The summary's last column is a ratio of two lifetime totals, one of which has the whole capex programme added back into it, and knowing that changes what the number is allowed to say.

{{panel:ec-comparison-explorer}}

## What the column divides

The summary computes government take divided by government take plus contractor take, where contractor take has TOTAL CAPEX ADDED BACK. The add-back is deliberate: it turns the denominator into something closer to profit before investment, so the ratio reads as a rate on profit rather than a rate on cash.

Total capex on the default project is 500.0000 million USD, spent in year 1. Generic Royalty/Tax on that project returns lifetime government take of 758.7514 million USD and lifetime contractor net cash flow of 986.7327 million USD, and its summary effective tax rate reads 33.7901 percent. Nigeria - PIA (2021) on the same field returns take of 1339.2784 against contractor net cash flow of 406.2057, and reads 59.6432 percent. The same 500.0000 million USD goes back into both denominators, so on this project the column ranks the six templates in the take column's order.

## It is not a tax rate

Government take is royalty plus the government's share of profit oil plus tax, so the column labelled effective tax rate is charged with a great deal that no tax authority calls tax. Angola - Deepwater PSC pays 181.7318 million USD of tax on the default project inside a take of 1200.2886, while its column reads 53.4534 percent. The Angola template's corporate income tax is 25 percent and its resource rent tax 50 percent, and neither of those numbers, nor any combination of them a reader might reach for, is what 53.4534 measures. USA - Gulf of Mexico is clearer still: its only tax is corporate income tax at 21 percent, its column reads 34.0485 percent, and a flat royalty of 18.75 percent contributes 503.7989 million USD of the 764.5528 collected.

## The mistake

The reading to guard against is treating a low number in this column as a mild fiscal outcome. On the published comparison built with capex of 20000:

| rank | regime | npv | govTake | effectiveTaxRate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Brazil - Concession | -15354.6816 | 700.1194 | 13.5916 |
| 4 | USA - Gulf of Mexico | -15701.7744 | 1312.7238 | 25.4842 |
| 6 | Angola - Deepwater PSC | -15900.1132 | 1662.7835 | 32.2800 |

Brazil - Concession shows the gentlest effective tax rate in the whole course, 13.5916 percent, on a project that destroys 15354.6816 million USD of value. The rate is low because 20000 of capex was added back into the denominator and swamped everything else in it. A rate that falls as the project gets worse is not measuring the harshness of the terms. Angola - Deepwater PSC on the same comparison collects 1662.7835 million USD and reads 32.2800 percent, so the column still orders the regimes against each other. What it will not survive is being read as a level.

## What it refuses

The column carries no time value: both totals are undiscounted lifetime sums, while the NPV beside them is discounted. It cannot be compared with a statutory rate, and it cannot be compared with the rate the price sensitivity plots, which divides the same two quantities without the add-back and returns 43.4694 percent where this column returns 33.7901.
