# Two final dispositions

Two of the six comment statuses lead nowhere. From "Closed" and from "Withdrawn" the legal table lists no next status, and every move out of them is refused with the same kind of sentence: "A closed comment is final." and "A withdrawn comment is final."

{{panel:rc-review-explorer}}

## Two ways to finish a comment

The two final statuses are reached by different people for different reasons.

"Closed" is the end of the full loop. The author responded, the reviewer verified the response, and the coordinator closed it out. The comment was raised, answered and accepted, and the record shows each step.

"Withdrawn" is the reviewer taking the comment back. It is reachable from "Open" and from "Rejected", which means the reviewer can withdraw before the author has answered, or after refusing an answer. A withdrawn comment was not answered to anybody's satisfaction. It was judged, by the person who raised it, to be a comment that should not stand.

Both finish the comment. Only one of them records an answer that somebody accepted.

## Resolved is wider than final

The engine's resolved set holds three statuses: "Verified", "Closed" and "Withdrawn". "Verified" is resolved and is still not final, because it has one legal move left, to "Closed". A verified comment needs nobody to judge it any more, so it no longer holds a review open, but the coordinator still has a step to take.

That gives three groups:

- final and resolved: "Closed", "Withdrawn";
- resolved and not final: "Verified";
- neither: "Open", "Responded", "Rejected".

The review closure rule in the next module reads the resolved set. A review can close with verified comments still waiting for the coordinator, because what blocks closure is a comment somebody still has to judge.

## Why final means final

A closed comment reopened would erase the fact that it was once accepted. If a closed comment turns out to be wrong, the honest record is a new comment that says so, raised against the work as it now stands. The final statuses keep the history of each comment as it happened.

The same idea runs through every register in this course. A change that reaches a final stage stays there, and a lesson that is archived stays archived. Finishing is a recorded event, and the engines refuse to undo it quietly.

## Withdrawn is the reviewer's to give

Withdrawing is one of the three moves the reviewer owns, alongside verifying and rejecting. Because it finishes a comment without an accepted answer, it is also the move an author would most like to take on a comment they disagree with. The engine refuses the author that move, and the second module of this tier reads that refusal in full.

These answers were also replayed through the independent oracle for peerReview, so each is two methods agreeing.
