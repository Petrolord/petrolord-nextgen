# Beyond the typewell

Every course in this ladder ran on one well: a clean clastic section with honest hole conditions, a cooperative water leg, and parameters that converge when asked. That was a design choice, not an accident, and the right way to close the course is to be precise about what the typewell deliberately left out, and what you now own that transfers to any well anywhere.

## Why the typewell is ideal on purpose

The typewell holds hole conditions ideal so the parameter logic stays visible. There is no washout dragging the density log, no gas pulling neutron and density apart, no missing water leg, and no ambiguity about formation temperature. Strip those complications away and what remains is the reasoning skeleton: how a parameter is earned, validated, and stress-tested. That skeleton is the hardest thing to learn from field data, because in field data it is buried under the complications. Learn it clean first; add the noise later. That has been this ladder's teaching bet from the first lesson.

## What real studies add

A real evaluation surrounds this skeleton with more evidence, and most of it arrives from outside the log suite:

* **Core calibration.** Plugs from the reservoir give porosity-permeability relationships, laboratory-measured $m$ and $n$ instead of the classic defaults, and Dean-Stark saturations that check the log-derived $S_w$ directly. Where core exists, it outranks every transform in this course.
* **Formation pressure and sampling.** Wireline formation testers give pressure gradients that locate fluid contacts independently of any resistivity argument, and collected water samples give $R_w$ from direct measurement of the produced brine, the strongest rung of the provenance ladder.
* **More logs.** NMR gives porosity partitioned into bound and free fluid; dielectric tools estimate water-filled porosity almost independently of salinity; image logs reveal the thin beds and laminations that standard tools average away.
* **Thin-bed and laminated-sand analysis.** Where sands and shales interleave below tool resolution, the models in this ladder read too pessimistic, and dedicated techniques take over.
* **Multi-well consistency.** A field study interprets every well with one parameter set and one documented set of exceptions, and the field-wide coherence of the result is itself a validation no single well can provide.

None of this replaces what you learned. Each item slots into the same framework: it is another route for triangulation, another validation surface, or another sensitivity to run.

## What transfers unchanged

Three things move from the typewell to real work without modification.

First, the triangulate-validate-stress-test discipline. Whatever the parameter, you will look for independent routes to it, prove it where the answer is known, and measure what it does to the booking. On the typewell that was Arps, SP, and Pickett converging on $R_w$, the water leg reading 0.9991, and the 18.0 versus 16.5 m booking pair. In a field study the routes and surfaces change; the discipline does not.

Second, the provenance ladder. Given, fitted, triangulated: every number in every report you write from now on sits on a rung, and stating the rung is part of stating the number.

Third, the refusal to let one unexamined number drive a booking. The typewell's whole Expert tier was built around a single cautionary arithmetic: an uncorrected lab value, used innocently, erased 1.5 m of real pay. Every well you ever evaluate has at least one number capable of doing that. Your job is to know which one it is and to have examined it.

## Closing the ladder

Take stock of what you now own. From the Associate tier: the complete interpretation loop, from raw curves to flagged samples to a booked zone summary. From the Professional tier: porosity by four methods and the judgment to choose, saturation in shaly rock, and a fitted validation of the Archie parameters. From this tier: formation temperature and the Arps correction, the SP quicklook, triangulation, water-leg validation, and parameter sensitivity carried all the way into booked pay.

That is the full workflow of a working petrophysicist on a conventional clastic well: raw curves in, a defended, sensitivity-tested pay booking out. The capstone will ask you to prove it once more, end to end, and then the ladder is yours.
