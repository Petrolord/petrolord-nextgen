# A bore is an area

A bore is not yet a hydraulic quantity. The area it encloses is, 0.347410 ft2 on the OGBIA line, and so is the velocity the duty makes through it, 2.244621 ft/s.

{{panel:fc-liquid-explorer}}

## From a bore to an area

The bore arrives in inches and the area is wanted in square feet, so the 144 square inches in a square foot enter here. A bore of 7.981000 in encloses 0.347410 ft2, and that single number is what the rest of the chain works with.

The relationship is a square. A bore twice as wide encloses four times the area, which is why bore changes move a velocity so much further than they look as though they should.

## From an area to a velocity

The duty arrives as 12000.000000 bpd, a volume per day. The area is in square feet and the velocity is wanted in feet per second, so the barrel of 5.6145833333333 cubic feet and the 86400.000000 seconds in a day both enter, and the result is 2.244621 ft/s.

Nothing physical has happened yet. This is a rate and a geometry, and it would give the same answer for water, for crude or for anything else that fills the pipe.

## The same duty in four bores

| bore in | velocity ft/s |
| --- | --- |
| 1.939000 | 38.027872 |
| 2.067000 | 33.463911 |
| 6.065000 | 3.886834 |
| 15.000000 | 0.635441 |

One duty, four pipes, and the velocity spans the table. The crude did not change and neither did the rate, so every one of those differences is geometry.

## Why the velocity is where the physics starts

Everything after this point reads the velocity rather than the rate. The Reynolds number is built on it, the friction factor follows from the Reynolds number, the friction loss is proportional to the velocity head, and the erosional check compares the velocity directly against a ceiling.

So a bore is a decision about a velocity, and a velocity is the input that four separate answers depend on. That is the reason a sizing sweep varies the bore and holds everything else still. Change the bore and all four move together.

## The mistake

Comparing two lines by their rates. A line at 12000.000000 bpd through 7.981000 in and a line at the same duty through 2.067000 in have nothing in common hydraulically: one runs at 2.244621 ft/s and the other at 33.463911 ft/s. The rate is the duty, and the velocity is the condition.
