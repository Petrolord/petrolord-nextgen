# A schedule is two numbers

A pipe size is an outside diameter and a wall thickness. The bore is what is left, and the bore is the only one of the three the hydraulics ever sees.

{{panel:fc-liquid-explorer}}

## The same outside diameter in two schedules

| nominal | outside diameter in | wall light | wall heavy | bore light | bore heavy | velocity light ft/s | velocity heavy ft/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 2.375000 | 0.154000 | 0.218000 | 2.067000 | 1.939000 | 33.463911 | 38.027872 |
| 4 | 4.500000 | 0.237000 | 0.337000 | 4.026000 | 3.826000 | 8.820843 | 9.767147 |
| 6 | 6.625000 | 0.280000 | 0.432000 | 6.065000 | 5.761000 | 3.886834 | 4.307863 |
| 8 | 8.625000 | 0.322000 | 0.500000 | 7.981000 | 7.625000 | 2.244621 | 2.459110 |

## The outside diameter does not change

Read the second column down the table and then read it across each row. A 6 in line is 6.625000 in on the outside in either schedule, because that is what the flanges, the supports and the fittings are built to. The heavier schedule spends its extra steel inwards.

So the nominal size is a purchasing and a mechanical fact. The bore is the hydraulic one, and the two are related by a wall thickness that the buyer chooses for reasons of pressure and corrosion rather than of flow.

## A heavier wall is a faster line

Every heavy-schedule bore in the table is smaller than its light-schedule partner, and every heavy-schedule velocity is higher. On the 8 in rows the bore goes from 7.981000 in to 7.625000 in and the velocity from 2.244621 ft/s to 2.459110 ft/s.

That is worth holding onto, because the reason for the heavier wall usually has nothing to do with the flow. A line uprated for pressure or given more corrosion allowance quietly becomes a faster line at the same duty, and its pressure drop and its erosional ratio both move.

## Where the bore comes from

The engine reads the bore from a vendored pipe schedule rather than deriving it. Each row of that schedule carries the nominal size, the schedule, the outside diameter, the wall and the resulting bore, and the hydraulics takes the last of those.

## The nominal size is a name

A 6 in pipe is 6.625000 in on the outside, and neither of its bores is 6.000000 in. The nominal size is a label that fixes the outside diameter and the fittings that go with it, and the bore underneath it follows from the schedule.

So two lines can both be called 6 in and differ hydraulically, at 6.065000 in and 5.761000 in of bore.

## The mistake

Sizing on the nominal size. A 2 in line is neither 2.000000 in nor one fixed bore: it is 2.067000 in in schedule 40 and 1.939000 in in schedule 80, and on this duty those two bores give different velocities and very different pressure drops.
