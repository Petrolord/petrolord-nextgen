# What a recommendation ranks

Twelve bores, two criteria, and a table whose order is neither. Against a velocity ceiling of 6.000000 ft/s as well as the erosional limit, 7 of the 12 bores pass.

## The table order is not the bore order

The sweep is ordered by nominal size and then by schedule. In that order the bores run 2.067000, 1.939000, 3.068000, 4.026000, 3.826000, 6.065000, 5.761000, 7.981000, 7.625000, 10.020000, 11.938000 and 15.000000.

A heavier schedule is a thicker wall and a smaller bore on the same outside diameter, and it sits after the lighter one, so reading down the table crosses back and forth over the bore that a line actually needs. Any code that walks the rows and stops at the first acceptable one has picked a row rather than a bore.

## Two defensible answers to one question

Among the seven that pass both criteria, the first in TABLE order is 6 in schedule 40 at a bore of 6.065000 in. The smallest passing BORE is 6 in schedule 80 at 5.761000 in. On this duty they are different rows. Seven rows pass, so there are five more answers available to anyone ranking on a different rule.

Both are correct answers to the question they were actually asked. The first is the smallest nominal size that works, which is what a buyer wants. The second is the tightest bore that works, which is what a hydraulics engineer might mean by smallest. A recommendation that does not say which of the two it ranked has not said much.

## What the ranking leaves out

Neither criterion knows what the pipe costs, what the yard has in stock, what wall the pressure rating demands, or what corrosion allowance the service needs. A schedule 80 pipe is heavier and dearer than a schedule 40 of the same nominal size, and the sweep that prefers its bore says nothing about that. A ranking is worth what its criteria are worth, and both criteria here were supplied from outside the pressure drop calculation.

## Why the velocity ceiling is separate

The 6.000000 ft/s figure is a practice rather than a result of this method. Applied alongside the erosional limit it removes bores the erosional check alone would have allowed, which is the point of it: a line can be inside the RP 14E ceiling and still be faster than an operator wants to run crude.

## The mistake

Reporting a single recommended bore with no statement of the criteria and their order. The same sweep, on the same duty, defensibly recommends two different rows, and the difference between them is what the word smallest was taken to mean.

## Exercise

Say how many of the twelve bores pass both criteria, and name the two different rows that each have a claim to being the answer. Then say what the table's ordering does to code that stops at the first passing row.
