# Elevation is not friction

A hill is a static column of liquid, and the engine returns it as its own term. Over a 420.000000 ft rise the OGBIA line carries 158.958333 psi of elevation beside an unchanged 25.660631 psi of friction.

{{panel:fc-liquid-explorer}}

## The same line, level and then tilted

| elevation change ft | friction psi | elevation psi | total psi |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.000000 | 25.660631 | 0.000000 | 25.660631 |
| 420.000000 | 25.660631 | 158.958333 | 184.618965 |
| -420.000000 | 25.660631 | -158.958333 | -133.297702 |

Read the friction column first. It is identical on all three rows, because the hill changed nothing about the rate, the bore, the roughness or the viscosity. Then read the elevation column, which is symmetric about zero.

## Two terms with different natures

Friction is what a bigger pipe fixes. Open the bore and the velocity falls, the velocity head falls with its square, and the friction loss collapses.

Elevation is what no pipe fixes. The static column is the density times the height, and a wider pipe holds a wider column of exactly the same height. A designer who responds to an elevation-dominated total by buying a larger bore has spent money on the smaller half of the problem.

## A total can be negative

The downhill row returns -133.297702 psi. The line gains more from the descent than it spends on friction, so the fluid arrives at a higher pressure than it left.

That is a correct result rather than an error state. The published case of 800.0000 bpd over 3000.000000 ft dropping 60.000000 ft does the same thing: 14.550645 psi of friction against -26.000000 psi of elevation for a total of -11.382322 psi.

## Why keeping them apart matters

A single total hides which of the two is in charge. On the uphill row the elevation term is the larger part of 184.618965 psi, and knowing that redirects the design conversation towards pumping, routing or a different profile.

The engine could have returned one number. Returning three means the question of what to do about the answer can be asked properly.

## The published case with a hill in it

The 20000.0000 bpd case runs through 10.020000 in of bore over 52800.000000 ft and climbs 250.000000 ft. It spends 56.140803 psi on friction, 0.153195 psi on its fittings and 97.222222 psi on elevation, for a total of 153.516220 psi.

The elevation term is the largest of the three on that line. That is an ordinary situation on a route with relief in it, and it is the reason the engine declines to blend the terms: the answer to a friction problem and the answer to an elevation problem are different pieces of equipment.

## The mistake

Reading a negative total as a fault. It says the arrival pressure exceeds the departure pressure, which is what a descent does, and the friction term sitting inside it is still positive and still real.
