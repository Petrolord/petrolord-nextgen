# The velocity head

A velocity head is the stream's own motion expressed as a pressure. On the OGBIA line, at a resistance sum of one, it is 0.029634 psi.

## What it is made of

The velocity head is the density times the square of the velocity, divided by twice gc, and then divided by 144 to reach psi. On OGBIA that is 54.500000 lb/ft3 at 2.244621 ft/s, with gc at 32.174000 lbm ft per lbf s2.

Both constants are doing ordinary work. gc reconciles the pound as a mass with the pound as a force, and the 144 takes the answer to psi.

## The square is the important part

The velocity is squared, so the velocity head grows far faster than the velocity does. A line running twice as fast carries four times the velocity head, and every loss built on it follows.

This is the quantity that makes high velocity expensive. The friction loss is the velocity head multiplied by the friction factor and by the length over the bore, and the fittings loss is the velocity head multiplied by a resistance sum, so both of the pipe's two pressure terms are the same square wearing different coefficients.

## One head, and then several

A resistance sum of one costs 0.029634 psi on this line. The OGBIA isometric carries a resistance sum of 4.500000 velocity heads and costs 0.133351 psi.

That is what a resistance sum is: a count of velocity heads. A fitting does not have a pressure drop of its own until a velocity is put through it, and the same elbow on a faster line costs more.

## Why it is a useful unit

Expressing a fitting as a number of velocity heads makes it portable. The count belongs to the shape of the fitting, and the pressure it costs belongs to the stream it is placed in, so one table of counts serves every line.

It also explains why fittings matter more on short lines. The pipe's own loss grows with length while the fittings loss does not, so the same resistance sum is a small correction on a long export line and a large one on a manifold run.

## The mistake

Treating a velocity head as a fixed cost per fitting. It is a cost per fitting at one velocity, and the velocity is set by the bore and the duty, so the same isometric in a smaller pipe costs more without a single fitting being added.
