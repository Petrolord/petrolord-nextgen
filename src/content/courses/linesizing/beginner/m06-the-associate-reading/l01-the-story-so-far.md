# The story so far

The Associate tier sizes one liquid line. Six numbers carry the whole of it, and every one of them has been met: a velocity, a Reynolds number, a friction factor, a friction loss, a total loss and an erosional ceiling.

## The chain, once more

A bore gives an area, the area and the rate give a velocity, the velocity gives a Reynolds number, the Reynolds number and the relative roughness give a friction factor, and the friction factor and the length give the loss. On OGBIA that is 2.244621 ft/s, 48431.2523, 0.0218149625 and 25.660631 psi through 7.981000 in of bore.

## The three losses stay apart

Friction, fittings and elevation are returned separately because they answer different questions. Friction is what a bigger pipe fixes. Elevation is what no pipe fixes. The third term, the fittings, is a count of velocity heads rather than a length.

On the built line the pipe spends 25.660631 psi and the isometric's 4.500000 velocity heads add 0.133351 psi. Put the same line over a 420.000000 ft rise and 158.958333 psi of static column appears beside an unchanged friction term.

## The friction factor has two branches

Below Reynolds 2100 the engine is on the laminar branch. Above 4000 it is on the turbulent one. Between them it labels the answer transitional and computes it on the turbulent branch, and at the OGBIA relative roughness the step across the lower boundary is 60.303955 percent.

## The limit that is not a cost

The erosional ceiling of 13.545709 ft/s comes from the density and a c factor, and the line uses 0.165707 of it. It appears on every row of a bore sweep at the same value, and it is what stops a bore being chosen on pressure drop alone.

## What a bore choice actually is

Twelve bores carry the same duty, from 33.463911 ft/s down to 0.635441 ft/s and from 20803.315944 psi down to 1.236026 psi. Seven of them pass both the erosional limit and a 6.000000 ft/s velocity ceiling, and the first of those in table order and the smallest of them by bore are different rows. Choosing between those two is a question about what the word smallest was taken to mean, and the sweep does not settle it.

## The mistake

Carrying one of these numbers to another line. Every figure above belongs to a complete row: that rate, that bore, that length, that fluid, that wall. The method travels and the numbers do not.
