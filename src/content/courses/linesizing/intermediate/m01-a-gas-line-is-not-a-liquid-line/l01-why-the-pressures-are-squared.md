# Why the pressures are squared

The SOKU gas trunk reaches the studio as 850.000000 psia at the station against 620.000000 psia at the delivery point, through 11.938000 in of bore over 32.000000 miles. The number every transmission form starts from is not the difference of those two pressures. It is the difference of their squares, 338100.0000 psia squared.

## The trunk as the studio reads it

| condition | value |
| --- | --- |
| inlet psia | 850.000000 |
| outlet psia | 620.000000 |
| bore in | 11.938000 |
| length miles | 32.000000 |
| gas gravity | 0.670000 |
| average flowing temperature degR | 535.000000 |
| average compressibility | 0.885000 |
| transmission efficiency | 1.000000 |

Eight conditions, and the first two of them never appear in a gas form on their own. They appear squared.

## Why a square and not a difference

A liquid line subtracts pressures because the fluid that leaves the pipe is the fluid that entered it, at the same density and the same velocity. A gas line cannot do that. The density the friction sees is itself proportional to the pressure, so the gas is heavier and slower where the line is full of pressure and lighter and faster where it has spent it. Integrating that along the pipe is what puts the pressures inside a square, and the whole of the compressible half of this course follows from it.

## The group is the quantity that moves

Because the group is what the form reads, a small change near the inlet and the same change near the outlet are not worth the same rate. The same trunk shows it at two settings. Held at 620.000000 psia the outlet gives a group of 338100.0000 psia squared and Weymouth reads 66104956.1404 scfd. Brought up to 845.000000 psia, five psi below the inlet, the same form on the same pipe reads 10466013.4729 scfd. The pipe did not change and neither did the gas. The group did.

## Absolute, always

Every pressure in this half of the engine is absolute. A gas form squares what it is handed, and squaring a gauge pressure produces a number that is wrong by an amount no later step can recover, because the error is no longer a constant offset once it has been through a square. The trunk is quoted at 850.000000 psia and 620.000000 psia for that reason, and the flowing temperature of 535.000000 degR is absolute for the same reason.

## Two of those conditions are averages

The flowing temperature of 535.000000 degR and the compressibility of 0.885000 are named as averages, and the word is part of the method. One temperature and one compressibility stand in for the whole 32.000000 miles, and the form has no way to represent a line that is hot where it leaves the station and cold where it arrives. Choosing what to average over is therefore part of setting a case up, and two engineers who average differently report different rates from the same pipe.

## The mistake

The mistake is handing a gas form the arithmetic a liquid line taught. Subtracting 620.000000 psia from 850.000000 psia gives a pressure difference, and a pressure difference is not what the form wants. The second mistake is quoting the group as though it were a pressure. It is 338100.0000 psia squared, and its units say so.
