# The diameter exponent

Double the bore and read the engine twice, and the ratio of the two rates says what power of diameter a form carries. It is the single most important number in a gas form, because it is what says how much a bigger pipe buys.

## Measured rather than quoted

| form | rate at 10 in scfd | rate at 20 in scfd | measured exponent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| weymouth | 41217643.2456 | 261715720.9907 | 2.6666666667 |
| panhandleA | 54628394.3526 | 335409354.2407 | 2.6182000000 |
| panhandleB | 56450013.6000 | 326039290.8986 | 2.5300000000 |
| general | 46594890.2141 | 282004596.2750 | 2.5974750199 |

The exponent on each row is the base-two logarithm of the ratio of that row's two engine rates. Nothing was read off a textbook. The form was asked for a rate at 10.000000 in and again at 20.000000 in, and the exponent is what the two answers imply.

## Why doubling is the right experiment

Any two bores would reveal the exponent, and doubling makes the arithmetic legible. The ratio of the two rates across a factor of two in diameter is two raised to the exponent, so the exponent is the base-two logarithm of that ratio and nothing else. Nothing has to be assumed about the constants standing in front of the form either, because they divide out of a ratio. What is measured is a property of the correlation alone.

## Weymouth gains the most from a bigger pipe

Weymouth carries 2.6666666667, the largest of the four, and Panhandle B carries 2.5300000000, the smallest. So the form that responds most strongly to a change of bore is the one that gave the lowest rate on the SOKU trunk, and the form that responds least is the one that gave the highest.

## Where the two Panhandles change places

Read the two rate columns rather than the exponents. At 10.000000 in Panhandle B stands above Panhandle A, 56450013.6000 against 54628394.3526. At 20.000000 in the order has reversed, 335409354.2407 for Panhandle A against 326039290.8986 for Panhandle B. Somewhere between those two bores the higher exponent overtakes the higher starting point. Which of the pair is higher is therefore a question about the bore rather than a fact about the forms.

That is the whole argument for measuring the exponent rather than reading the rates alone. An ordering established on one bore is an ordering on that bore, and this pair of forms crosses within the range of sizes an ordinary trunk is built at.

## What the exponent is worth in a sizing conversation

A designer moving up a size is buying rate at a rate the exponent sets, and the four forms price that purchase differently. This is also why the choice of form and the choice of bore cannot be made independently: the answer to what a larger pipe buys depends on which correlation is being asked.

## The mistake

The mistake is taking an exponent from one form and using it to scale another form's answer. They differ across the four, and on this table the spread in the exponents is enough to reverse an ordering.

The second mistake is quoting an exponent as though it were a property of the pipe. It is a property of the correlation.
