# Onward

The line is sized, the wall is specified and the pig is scheduled. What is left is a short list of things this engine will not do and a shorter list of habits worth carrying out of it.

## What lives somewhere else

A system of lines sharing a header is a network solve and has its own module. A live-oil flowline upstream of separation carries full PVT and belongs elsewhere. The vessel that catches a slug is sized in the separation work. The whole multiphase half, flow regime and slip and holdup, is app code outside this engine, which is why the holdup arrives here as an input.

Each of those is a seam rather than a gap. The work exists. It is simply not in this chain, and a number crossing one of those seams should be named as having crossed it.

## The four held items

The API RP 14E c factors, the efficiency multiplier on all four gas forms, the band from a Reynolds number of 2100 to 4000, and the fully rough friction law Weymouth assumes. Each becomes an answer the day somebody reads a source against it. Until then each is a limit to state.

Beside them sit the goldens, which are synthetic throughout. They arbitrate between two implementations and they do not speak for any measured pipeline.

## The question to ask of any figure

Was it computed, copied or assumed?

A computed figure moves when its duty moves. A copied figure never moves, because it was never a function of anything. An assumed figure moves when somebody changes their mind, and nothing in the answer records that it might.

A swept volume looks computed and is half assumed. A c factor looks like a standard and is copied. A friction factor at a Reynolds number of 3000 looks like the others and came from a correlation that was not written for it.

## The habit that carries

Read what sits beside the number. A regime word, an error string, a stated class, a stated holdup. This engine is unusually good at saying what it did, and nearly every trap in this tier is sprung by taking the number and leaving the qualifier behind. The qualifier is the cheaper half to carry and the half that survives review.
