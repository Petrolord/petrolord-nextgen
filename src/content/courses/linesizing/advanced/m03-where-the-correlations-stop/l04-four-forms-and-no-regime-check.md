# Four forms and no regime check

Each of the four transmission forms was derived for a kind of line under a kind of flow. Not one of them checks whether the line it has been handed is that kind of line.

{{panel:fc-gasline-explorer}}

## The same line, twice

Take the trunk in two very different states and read Weymouth against General Flow.

On the nearly dead line, with five psi of driving pressure across the whole of it, Weymouth gives 10466013.4729 scfd and General Flow gives 11016984.8816 scfd at a friction factor of 0.0126337788. The two forms sit at 0.949989 of each other.

At full duty on the same unchanged pipe those two forms sit at 0.894987 of each other.

The two forms are therefore closer together when the line is barely flowing than when it is working. Neither form says so, and neither reports anything about the condition it was asked to describe.

## What a regime check would have caught

A gas transmission form assumes a flow well into the turbulent region, developed, and steady. A line with five psi across thirty-two miles is barely moving, and whether the published forms still describe it is a question with a real answer. None of the four asks it.

Each form takes the inputs, applies its exponents and returns a rate to four decimals. The rate is the arithmetic done correctly. It is not a statement that the form applies.

## Only one of the four says anything about the pipe

General Flow returns the friction factor it settled on. That single field is the only place in the four forms where a reader can see an assumption being made and test it against the line. Weymouth, Panhandle A and Panhandle B report a rate and nothing else.

So the form with the most visible assumption is also the only one that can be argued with, which is a point in its favour that has nothing to do with accuracy.

## The mistake

The mistake is choosing a form by which answer is most convenient and then defending the choice with the arithmetic. All four are arithmetically correct. The choice between them is an engineering judgement about which set of assumptions fits the line, and it has to be made and recorded as a judgement.

The second mistake is reading agreement between two forms as validation. Two forms agreeing closely on one duty is a fact about those two forms at that duty, and the pair above moves by duty on one unchanged pipe.
