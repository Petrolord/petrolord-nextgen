# Items held for the literature

Four figures in this course are recorded rather than checked. They are not errors and they are not suspect. They are numbers nobody in this package has read a source for, and the honest thing to do with such a number is to teach it as a limit.

{{panel:fc-gasline-explorer}}

## The four

**The API RP 14E c factors.** 100.000000 for continuous service, 125.000000 for intermittent, and 175.000000 for clean inhibited service. The recommended practice says its own figures are conservative, and the third row is labelled operator practice with no publication behind it at all.

**The efficiency multiplier on all four gas forms.** Nothing in this package stands behind any particular value of it, and it multiplies every form linearly, so a change in efficiency moves every rate by the same proportion. The engine does bound it, refusing anything at or below zero and anything above 1.000000, but a bound is not a source.

**The band from a Reynolds number of 2100 to 4000.** The engine computes on the turbulent branch there and labels the answer transitional, and the step across the boundary is 1.603040 times.

**The fully rough friction law Weymouth assumes.** Module 3 measures it out of the engine by asking General Flow what friction factor would make it agree, and nothing in the package sources the law itself.

## How a held item is kept safe

Each of these is taught as a limit and never as an answer. Nothing graded in this course rests on one of them.

The mechanism is that every value which touches a held item states its own assumption alongside. Every graded erosional figure states the c factor it was computed at, and every graded gas figure states its efficiency. That turns a held item from a hidden dependency into a stated condition, and a stated condition is something a reader can disagree with.

## What holding does not mean

A held item is not a wrong number. The c factors are the figures the industry uses and the derived velocities are arithmetically correct. What is missing is the reading that would let anybody say how conservative 100.000000 actually is.

Nor does holding make a result useless. An erosional velocity at a stated c factor is a complete answer to a stated question. It stops being complete the moment the c factor drops out of the sentence.

## The mistake

The mistake is promoting a held figure by repetition. A c factor of 100.000000 quoted in three documents starts to look like a standard, and none of the three read a source.

The second mistake is the opposite, refusing to use a held item at all. These four are the best figures available and the course teaches them. What the course declines to do is let one of them carry a graded conclusion on its own.

## Exercise

Name the four items held for the literature and say what is unchecked about each. Then explain the mechanism that keeps a held item out of a graded answer, and say why a held item is not the same thing as a wrong number.
