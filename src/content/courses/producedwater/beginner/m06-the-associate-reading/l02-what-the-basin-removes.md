# What the basin removes from this water

Step nine of the chain is the first place the equipment and the droplets meet, and the number it produces is the one an operator actually cares about. On the UZERE stream the basin removes 3.284882 percent of the oil.

## Why so little

The basin cuts at 165.003927 micron. The droplets arrive with a median of 26 micron. The cut size is the droplet the device removes half of, so a population whose median sits far below that cut size is a population the basin barely touches. Almost all of the oil volume in this water is in droplets the basin cannot catch in the time the water is inside it.

## The removal is an integral over the distribution

That figure did not come from a table of typical efficiencies. The engine takes the grade efficiency of the device at every bin of the droplet distribution, multiplies it by the oil volume in that bin, and sums. A coarse bin contributes nearly all of its volume, a bin near the cut size contributes about half of it, and a fine bin contributes almost nothing. The result is one number that depends on the device and the water together. That is why the engine insists on a droplet median and a spread before it will report a removal at all, and why a stream described only by its concentration cannot be given an answer here.

## The same device would look different on other water

The best way to see that is to move the cut size and watch what the same water gives up:

| cut micron | removal percent | outlet median micron |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 | 44.236922 | 16.943389 |
| 20 | 60.477315 | 14.126621 |
| 12 | 78.263077 | 10.953502 |
| 6 | 92.922276 | 7.691060 |
| 2 | 99.398720 | 4.880416 |

The basin sits far above the coarsest row of that table, which is why its removal is where it is. Reaching the rows that remove most of the oil means a device with a completely different argument behind its cut size, because the last lesson of the module before this one shows what buying it with plan area alone would cost.

## What a small removal is still worth

A basin removing a few percent of the oil is not a failure of design, and dismissing it misreads the job. Gravity devices are there to take out slugs and coarse carryover, to protect the equipment downstream of them, and to do it with no moving parts and no consumables. The percentage on a steady sample understates that work, because the upsets a basin absorbs are exactly the events that a sample bottle never catches.

What a reader should refuse to do is the opposite mistake: quoting the 3.284882 percent as though it were a property of the basin. It is a property of this basin on this water at this droplet distribution, and a stream with coarser droplets would give a different figure out of the same vessel.
