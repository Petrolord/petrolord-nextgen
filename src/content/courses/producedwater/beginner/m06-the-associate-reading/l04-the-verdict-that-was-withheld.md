# The verdict the engine would not give

The chain is finished. The engine has the flow, both densities, the viscosity, the rise velocity, two cut sizes, two removals, an outlet concentration and an outlet droplet median. The last thing it is asked for is a verdict, and it returns null, because no discharge specification was given.

## This is the point of the whole tier

It would have been easy to write an engine that answered. There is a number on the screen and there are figures in the industry that could have been compared against it, and most software would have painted a colour and moved on. This one has eleven results and no standard to judge them by, so it says so and stops.

## What the engine would need

A verdict needs a specification supplied by the caller, and that caller is the person who knows the licence, the receiving environment and the regulator. Give the engine a positive specification and it will compare the train outlet against it. The comparison is withheld when any stage of the train did not run, or when the figure supplied is not positive, because a verdict on equipment that did not run is a verdict on equipment that is not there.

## Why the engine states none of its own

This module carries no discharge limit anywhere, and a test asserts that its declared constants hold no specification-like key. The reason is the same one behind the six things held for literature. A number a repository cannot justify is a number that will be quoted as though it had been justified, and a limit quoted out of a simulation is the worst place for that to happen, because the person reading it is usually making a compliance decision rather than an engineering one. Limits vary by country, by receiving water, by licence and over time, and none of that is knowledge this engine has.

## How to read a blank verdict

A blank verdict is not a failure and it is not a pass. It is an absence of input, and the correct response is to supply the input rather than to interpret the silence. A studio that painted the missing verdict red would be telling its user that the train failed, when what happened is that nobody said what passing meant.

## What this tier leaves you with

Four habits, and they generalise well past produced water. Read oil in water as a distribution rather than a concentration. Expect a cut size to come out of the equipment geometry and the fluid properties. Know which of the four kinds each figure on a screen belongs to. And treat a refusal, a withheld verdict and a warning as three different acts, none of which is a failure.
