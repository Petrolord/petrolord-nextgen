# Saturation is a pressure ratio

A gas in contact with liquid water picks up water until the water in the gas is in equilibrium with the water in the pool. The method this engine uses to describe that is ideal vapour liquid equilibrium over liquid water, and it comes down to one division.

{{panel:fc-water-explorer}}

## The one division

The mole fraction of water in the gas is the vapour pressure of water over the total pressure. Nothing else enters. On OBIAFU, at 950.000000 psia and 104.000000 degF, the vapour pressure of water is 1.069612 psia. Divide that by the total pressure and the mole fraction of water in the gas is 0.001125908, which is the entire physical content of the step.

The mass then follows from the pound mole, which the next two lessons take apart. At those conditions the content is 53.450380 lb of water per MMscf of gas.

## What each side of the division does

The numerator carries the whole temperature dependence. The vapour pressure of water at 104.000000 degF is 1.069612 psia whatever the gas around it is doing, and it would be the same number at 200.000000 psia or at 1500.000000 psia.

The denominator carries the whole pressure dependence, and it enters only as a divisor. There is no separate pressure effect anywhere in the method. Squeeze the gas and the same amount of water vapour is spread through more molecules of gas, so the fraction falls.

That is worth holding on to, because it is why the answer behaves so simply. Two independent handles, one on top and one underneath, and a single division between them.

## An intensive answer

The 53.450380 lb per MMscf has no idea how much gas there is. It is a property of the gas at those conditions, in the same way a density is. Double the rate and it does not move. Halve the rate and it does not move. It is a number per MMscf, and the per is doing real work.

The rate arrives two lessons from now, and when it does it multiplies a removal rather than this content. Getting that order right is most of what separates a correct dehydration answer from a plausible one.

## What the method assumes

Ideal mixing. The water vapour and the gas are assumed to share a volume without interacting, which is what lets one division carry the whole calculation. That assumption is very good at low pressure and it gets steadily worse as the gas is compressed. The lesson on the honest band reads where it stops being good enough.
