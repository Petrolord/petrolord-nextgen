# One stream, one answer

This module is a reading rather than a new topic. One stream, six answers, and a question about each of them: does this figure know how much gas there is.

{{panel:fc-water-explorer}}

## The stream

OBIAFU arrives at 950.000000 psia and 104.000000 degF carrying 53.450380 lb of water per MMscf, which is a mole fraction of 0.001125908 and has nothing to do with the rate.

A spec of 7.000000 lb per MMscf takes 46.450380 of that out, and only now does the rate matter. At 62.000000 MMscfd it is 2879.9235 lb a day.

The choice of 3.200000 gallons of glycol per pound turns that into 9215.7553 gallons a day, or 6.399830 gpm. Each of those gallons needs 1386.1650 Btu to reach the still and 429.6875 Btu to give its water up, 1815.8525 Btu in all, and the reboiler that does it is 0.697269 MMBtu an hour.

The vessel the gas rises through is 3.192661 ft across, and the glycol loop carries 74.065024 short tons of aromatics a year out of the still overhead on the way.

## Six answers, sorted

Two of them do not know the rate exists. The water content of 53.450380 lb per MMscf and the duty per gallon of 1815.8525 Btu are properties of the gas and of the loop, and they would read the same at any rate whatever.

Three of them are the first two with the rate applied. The water removed a day, the circulation and the reboiler duty an hour all scale with how much gas there is, and none of them is a new piece of physics.

The last one is about a vessel and sits outside the dehydration chain altogether. THE ASSOCIATE READING is those five steps plus this sixth answer, and the sixth is the only one that cares what pressure the gas is at rather than only how wet it is. A contactor is sized by what the gas weighs and how fast it may rise, and neither of those questions came up anywhere in the dehydration chain.

## What the sorting is for

Six figures on a data sheet look alike. They arrive in the same font in the same table, and nothing on the page says that two of them are properties, three are sizes, and one belongs to a different calculation altogether.

Being able to sort them is the practical output of this tier. It tells you which figures to re-derive when the rate changes, which to leave alone, and which one to check against a completely separate method because it never came through the dehydration chain at all.
