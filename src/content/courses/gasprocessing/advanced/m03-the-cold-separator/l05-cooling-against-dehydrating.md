# Cooling against dehydrating

Three units in two courses answer one question, and the question is what to do about the water in a gas. Knowing which of the three you are looking at, and which one the number in front of you came from, is the whole of this lesson.

{{panel:fc-coldend-explorer}}

## The one question

A wet gas at pressure will drop liquid water somewhere, and where it drops it decides whether you get a hydrate, a corrosion problem or a sales gas rejection. Every answer is a way of making sure that somewhere is a place you chose.

## Three answers

Take the water out with a solvent. A glycol contactor absorbs water into lean glycol, and the Associate and Professional tiers of this course build that chain from the line conditions through to the reboiler duty. The gas leaves drier in absolute terms, at the same pressure it arrived at.

Cool the gas and let the water fall out. That is this module, and the cold separator is where it happens. AGBADA carries 33.801656743 lb per MMscf at its inlet and can hold 18.762820770 lb per MMscf at the cold spot, a ratio of 0.555085832, and the difference leaves in the boot.

Leave the water where it is and stop it freezing. Inject an inhibitor so the water that is there cannot form a hydrate at the conditions it will see. That belongs to Flow Assurance along with the hydrate boundary itself.

## What separates them

The first removes water and costs a regeneration duty. The second removes water and costs pressure, which is why a skid that has pressure to spare is attractive and one that does not is a compressor bill. The third removes no water at all and costs chemical.

They also leave the gas in different states. A contactor outlet is dry at line pressure. A cold separator outlet is saturated at a low temperature, so it is only dry as long as nothing downstream gets colder. An inhibited stream is still wet and is relying on a dose being right.

## Which number you are holding

Every figure in this section is the second answer. A water content from this module is a saturation at a state. It is never an absolute dryness, and it is never a hydrate margin. Carry it into a conversation about the other two and it will be read as the wrong quantity.

The seam between the three is a course boundary rather than a gap. Say which answer produced your number and the boundary stops being a trap.
