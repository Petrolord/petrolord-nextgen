# Water the cold gas can still hold

The march delivers a temperature. On its own a temperature sells nothing. What a dew point skid is bought for is the water the colder gas can no longer carry, and that needs the saturation answer read twice.

{{panel:fc-coldend-explorer}}

## Two reads of one routine

The water content routine is the same one the Associate tier builds end to end. Nothing about it changes here. What changes is that it gets asked twice on the same stream, once at the state the gas arrives in and once at the state the cold separator holds it at.

At its inlet, 1180.000000 psia and 96.000000 degF, AGBADA carries 33.801656743 lb of water per MMscf. At 59.683516566 degF and 640.000000 psia the gas can hold 18.762820770 lb per MMscf.

As a ratio, the cold gas holds 0.555085832 of what the warm gas held.

## Can hold, rather than carries

The wording on the second figure matters. The engine returns a saturated water content, which is the most water a gas can hold at that pressure and temperature. Calling it the water the gas carries assumes the separator actually reaches equilibrium and actually takes the liquid out.

In a cold separator with a boot that assumption is close enough to be the design basis, which is why the two figures are read as an inlet load and an outlet load. It is still an assumption rather than a result, and it is worth naming, because there is no stage efficiency and no approach to equilibrium anywhere in this module to soften it.

## The answer knows nothing about the rate

Both figures are per MMscf. The saturation answer is an intensive property of the gas at a state, and the rate of the stream never enters it. A skid handling a large rate and a skid handling a small one at the same pressure and temperature get the same two numbers here.

That is the same distinction the Associate tier draws on the dehydration side, and it holds identically at the cold end. Turning a content into a load is a separate multiplication by a rate, and the moment that multiplication happens the answer stops being a property of the gas and becomes a property of the plant.

## What to record

Record both contents with their two states attached. A water content quoted without its pressure and its temperature is not an answer, because the same gas gives a different figure at every state along the let-down.

## Exercise

Record the inlet pressure and temperature with the water content the gas carries there, and the cold separator pressure and temperature with the water the gas can hold there. Record the ratio the digest prints for the cold content against the warm one. Then say why both figures are quoted per MMscf and what has to happen before either becomes a load in pounds a day.
