# What a theoretical stage is

A contactor is a tall vessel with gas rising through it and solvent running down. The whole absorption half of this module rests on agreeing to read that vessel as a stack of stages rather than as one box, and on knowing exactly how little that reading claims.

## A stage is an accounting device

A theoretical stage is a place where the gas and the liquid leave in equilibrium with each other. Height, tray spacing, contact time and mechanical design enter nowhere in that definition. It is a unit of separation difficulty, and it is the only absorber model this engine has.

The module is open about the absence. It carries no rate-based absorber model and no stage efficiency, so a stage count is an answer about how hard the duty is and never an answer about what to order. The gap between the two is a vendor's work and this course hands it over rather than filling it in.

## The relation that ties three things

The Kremser relation ties the absorption factor, the number of theoretical stages, and the fraction of the solute the column removes. Give it any two and it gives the third. That shape is the reason this module can answer two different questions with one piece of arithmetic: how much a column of a given size removes, and how large a column a given spec demands.

Two of the three are usually yours to set. The absorption factor follows from how much solvent the column has for the duty the gas brings it, and the stage count follows from how much steel is bought. The removal is what comes back.

On the OBIAFU absorber an absorption factor of 1.600000 over 6.000000 stages removes 0.976783371 of what arrives in the gas. That figure is a fraction of the solute present, so it says nothing at all about the rate, the pressure or the vessel. It is the column's efficiency at the duty it was given.

## What it does not carry

Reading the removal as a fraction is what keeps the relation honest. It does not know what is being absorbed, whether the solvent is glycol or amine, or what the equilibrium behind the absorption factor actually is. Those are all folded into the one number the caller supplies. A stage count that comes back fractional is therefore an exact answer to the question asked and an approximate answer to any question about trays.

The rest of this module works that surface in both directions. First the dial itself, then the relation, then the ceiling that appears the moment the absorption factor falls below one.
