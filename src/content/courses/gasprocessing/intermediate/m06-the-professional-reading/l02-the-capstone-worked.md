# The capstone worked

The graded exercise for this tier is a sour gas train. It is marked on the figures produced, and most lost marks come from answering a nearby question rather than the one asked. Four habits carry it.

## Decide which of the three questions is being asked

Every figure in this tier belongs to one of three independent readings of a column: the mole balance, the stage relation and the vessel. Before working anything, decide which one the question is about. A circulation and a stage count are both correct answers about the same piece of steel and only one of them is an answer to the question in front of you.

## Write down the conditions first

Almost every quantity here is conditional on something stated in the question. A removal of 0.976783371 belongs to an absorption factor of 1.600000 at 6.000000 stages, and the same relation at a different factor gives a different number that is equally correct and was not what was asked. The same is true of every circulation, every duty and every diameter in the tier.

Write down, before computing anything: the inlet mole percentages, the spec mole percentages, the gas rate, the amine, the strength, the lean and rich loadings, the duty per gallon, the absorption factor, the stage count, the K value and the liquid. A figure produced from a different set of those is simply a different train.

Pay particular attention to any value the question states that differs from the amine table's own. The property table's strengths, rich limits and duties are defaults, and a question is free to state its own. When it does, the stated value is the one to use and the table figure is a distractor.

## Check the units and the precision

| quantity | how it is written |
| --- | --- |
| gas rate | MMscfd |
| inlet and spec | mol percent |
| acid gas picked up | lbmol a day |
| loadings and swing | mol acid gas per mol amine |
| amine strength | weight percent |
| circulation | gpm |
| regenerator duty | MMBtu an hour |
| removal and fraction | a fraction between zero and one |
| stages | theoretical stages, and fractional is correct |
| diameter, velocity, density | ft, ft per s, lb per ft3 |

A removal is a fraction and a mole percent is a percentage, and the two never substitute. A lbmol a day is not a gpm until the swing, the molecular weight, the strength and the density have all been applied. Quote a figure at the precision the engine prints it rather than rounding on the way to the answer, because a rounded restatement of an engine answer is indistinguishable from a number somebody invented.

Stage counts deserve their own line here. They come back fractional and a fractional stage count is the correct answer. Rounding one up to a whole number quietly turns a theoretical stage into a tray, which is a conversion this module does not carry.

## Never supply a number the engine declined to give

When the engine declines, the decline is the answer. A spec above the inlet is refused by name. A lean loading at or above the rich is refused by name. A removal a starved absorber cannot reach is refused with the ceiling and the remedy attached. Writing a plausible figure into any of those gaps turns a correct reading into a wrong answer, and the gap was the point of the question.

## Exercise

Take any figure you are about to write and name the reading it belongs to, then the chosen inputs behind it. Check its unit and its precision against the table above, and confirm it is a number the engine returned rather than one you supplied.
