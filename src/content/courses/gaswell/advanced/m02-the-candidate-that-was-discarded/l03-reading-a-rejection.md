# Reading a rejection

A rejected row is not a verdict of no. It is a ratio, and the distance from one is the whole content of it.

{{panel:pd-remedy-explorer}}

## Four rejections, four different statements

Under Coleman at the controlling station of the teaching well EBOCHA-5, which is not a published case, the rejected candidates are 3.958 in at 0.7729815504, 3.826 in at 0.8272385726, 3.740 in at 0.8657201053 and 3.548 in at 0.9619521855. Those are not four instances of the same answer. The first is a string that cannot carry this rate under any reading, and the last is a string that misses by less than a step in the shelf of sizes.

The same four under Turner read 0.6441512920, 0.6893654772, 0.7214334210 and 0.8016268212. Every one is further from the threshold, and the ordering does not change, because the correlation divides all of them by the same factor.

## The row that is already the well

3.548 in is the current string. Its rejection at 0.9619521855 is not a hypothetical: it is the well's own loading ratio at the controlling station. The profile computes it as the shoe station of the traverse and the sizing computes it as the fourth candidate on the list, and they are the same number because they are the same calculation. A sizing at the controlling station therefore contains the loading verdict, and a rejection that matches the current string is a statement that the well is loading now.

## What the row lets you say

| Field | 3.548 in under Coleman | 3.476 in under Coleman |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical velocity, ft/s | 6.1224977520 | 6.1224977520 |
| Actual velocity, ft/s | 5.8895500931 | 6.1360629550 |
| Critical rate, Mscf/d | 3222.613396799 | 3093.146724566 |
| Ratio | 0.9619521855 | 1.0022156322 |

Only two fields moved, and both moved because the area moved. The critical velocity is common to the whole list, so any comparison between candidates is a comparison of areas dressed up as a comparison of ratios.

## The mistake

Treating the rejections as a ranking of tubing quality. They rank by area, so the ordering is fixed before any of the physics runs and carries no information about the well. What carries information is where the threshold falls inside that fixed ordering, which is why the pick and the rejection immediately above it are the only two rows worth arguing about.

## What it refuses

No row says why it was rejected, beyond the ratio. There is no field naming the binding constraint, no field for the rate that would have made the candidate pass, and no depth on any row. A rejection also says nothing about erosional velocity or pressure drop, so a candidate that unloads is not thereby a candidate you should run.
