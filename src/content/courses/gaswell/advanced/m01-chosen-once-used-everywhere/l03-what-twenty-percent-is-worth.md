# What twenty percent is worth

Turner is Coleman multiplied by 1.200000. Not approximately, and not only in velocity, so the correlation name is worth twenty percent of every critical rate a study computes.

{{panel:pd-remedy-explorer}}

## The same physics, one factor apart

The terminal droplet velocity is identical under both names. At 1200.0 psia, 600.0 degR and z 0.9 the Turner terminal velocity is 6.3307682001 ft/s and the Coleman terminal velocity is 6.3307682001 ft/s, a difference of 0.0000e+0 ft/s, both built on the same droplet constant of 1.5935346111. The critical velocities there are 7.5969218402 ft/s under Turner and 6.3307682001 ft/s under Coleman. `LOADING_ADJUSTMENT` holds turner at 1.200000 and coleman at 1.000000, and that is the whole difference.

A lesson that says the two correlations use different physics is wrong. Turner et al. found their field data sat about twenty percent above the theoretical velocity and applied the adjustment; Coleman et al., working low-pressure wells, found the unadjusted equation fitted better.

## It survives the conversion to rate

Rate is linear in velocity at a fixed station and area, so the factor carries. Across all twelve published velocity rows, Turner over Coleman is 1.200000000000 in velocity and the Turner rate exceeds the Coleman rate by 20.00000000 percent. Published row 5, at 2500.0 psia and 540.0 degR, carries a Coleman rate of 2080.128829231 Mscf/d against a Turner rate of 2496.154595078 Mscf/d, a difference of 416.025765846 Mscf/d. A well making exactly the Coleman rate at that station is loaded under Turner, at a ratio of 0.8333333333.

## The mistake

Treating the twenty percent as a tolerance. A tolerance is a band you carry through a study and quote at the end. This is a factor applied before anything else happens, and it moves the verdict rather than blurring it: the same well reports a margin of -3.80478145 percent under Coleman and -19.83731788 percent under Turner.

## What it refuses

Neither name is calibrated by anything the module can see. Interfacial tension and liquid density are inputs, not correlations, and the Turner fluid properties are offered as labelled starting points. Nothing chooses between the two names on evidence from this well, and no returned field records which name produced a rate.
