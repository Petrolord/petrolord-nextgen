# Why a gas well loads up

Every gas well makes liquid, and the gas carries it only while the gas is moving fast enough. Below that speed the liquid falls back down the tubing.

## Weight against drag

Water and condensate ride up the string as droplets suspended in the gas. Each droplet has a weight pulling it down, reduced by the buoyancy of the gas around it, and a drag force pushing it up that grows with the gas velocity. One velocity balances the two exactly. Above it the droplet reaches surface. Below it the droplet falls, and the liquid that falls collects in the bottom of the string as a column the reservoir now has to push against. The added back pressure lowers the rate, the lower rate lowers the velocity, and the arithmetic runs again the next day from a worse number. That loop is why a loading well does not settle at a slightly lower rate. It keeps going.

## The velocity a station demands

Published water at 60.0 dyne/cm and 67.0 lbm/ft3, gas gravity 0.65, z 0.90 and 540.0 degR, through 2.441 in tubing.

| Pressure, psia | Gas density, lbm/ft3 | Terminal velocity, ft/s | Turner critical rate, Mscf/d |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 300.0 | 1.0829385833 | 12.1435923634 | 892.895047041 |
| 1000.0 | 3.6097952775 | 6.5866393859 | 1614.343188395 |
| 2500.0 | 9.0244881938 | 4.0737980096 | 2496.154595078 |

Denser gas both pushes harder and weighs the droplet less, so as density rises from 1.0829385833 to 9.0244881938 lbm/ft3 the velocity a droplet needs falls from 12.1435923634 ft/s to 4.0737980096 ft/s. The rate delivering that velocity moves the other way, from 892.895047041 to 2496.154595078 Mscf/d, because a cubic foot of high pressure tubing holds far more standard gas.

## The mistake

Reading the rate column as a statement about a well. Those three numbers are one fluid in one string at three pressures, and nothing about the well changed between them. A rate carries a verdict only once the pressure, the temperature, the compressibility and the diameter behind it are fixed.

## What it refuses

There is no inflow performance anywhere in these modules. The gas rate is an input, so a loading verdict is a verdict at a rate somebody supplied and not a prediction of what the well will do next. The physics is one droplet at its terminal velocity, not a population, not coalescence or break-up in transit, and not liquid flowing as a film on the wall, which is the other way a gas well carries liquid.
