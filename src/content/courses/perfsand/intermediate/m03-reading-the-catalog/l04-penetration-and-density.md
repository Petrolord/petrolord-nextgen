# Penetration and density

The two inputs a designer can buy, and what each one actually returns.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## Penetration

Doubling the tunnel length on the published high-shot-density gun improves the total. Halving it makes it worse by more than the doubling helped.

That asymmetry is the logarithm. The plane-flow term is a log of a ratio in which the tunnel length appears additively, so equal multiplicative steps do not give equal skin steps: they give diminishing returns going up and accelerating penalties going down.

## Where penetration also helps

In the converging term, through the dimensionless spacing, which is the spacing over the tunnel length. A longer tunnel lowers it.

And in the crushed-zone term, through the same ratio. A longer tunnel spreads the fixed shell resistance over more reach.

So penetration appears in three of the four components and helps in all three. It is the input with the broadest effect.

## Density

Doubling the shot density lowers the converging term and lowers the crushed-zone term. It does nothing at all to the plane-flow term or the blockage term, because neither contains the spacing.

So density is a narrower lever than penetration, acting on two components instead of three.

## Which to buy

It depends on which component is large, which is why computing all four separately is worth doing.

On a gun whose converging and crushed terms dominate, which means short tunnels and low density, more shots help most. On a gun whose plane-flow term dominates, which means long tunnels already, more penetration helps most.

The catalog gives an easy heuristic: if the total is positive, you are in the first regime and everything helps. If it is already well negative, further improvement is expensive.

## The cost side, which this course does not model

More shots means more gun, more explosive, more debris and more shock on the casing. More penetration means a bigger charge and often a bigger carrier, which may not fit.

Neither of those is in the engine, and both decide real jobs. The engine prices the benefit and the reader prices the cost.
