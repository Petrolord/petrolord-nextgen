# Onward

Where the Expert tier goes, and what it needs from elsewhere.

## The Expert tier

It leaves the flow problem and takes up the mechanical one.

First the completion type, from the ladder the Associate tier's two numbers feed. Then the hardware: Saucier sizes a gravel from the median, a screen gauge follows from the gravel, and a standalone slot window follows from the coarse decile. Each of those is short arithmetic with something surprising in it, and the surprise in the gravel step is that a sand can have no commercial match at all.

Then sanding onset. A Kirsch hoop stress at the cavity wall against the unconfined strength, giving a critical flowing pressure and therefore a maximum drawdown, swept along the perforated interval against a stress and strength profile.

And a module on a real defect this course found in that sweep, which is worth reading whatever you think of sand control.

## What it needs from elsewhere

A stress and strength profile: overburden, both horizontal stresses, pore pressure and unconfined compressive strength, on a depth grid. That comes from the Geomechanics course, and this course consumes it as data rather than deriving any of it.

A survey, to convert measured depth to true vertical depth along the interval. That comes from Well Design and Surveys.

## The join back to this tier

The perforation is the cavity. Everything the Professional tier computed about how well fluid enters that cavity assumes the cavity is still there, and the Expert tier asks whether it is.

A completion with an excellent perforation skin and a critical drawdown below the drawdown it needs is not a good completion. The two halves have to be read together, which is why they are one app.

## Where to go after this course

Stimulation Design, which is the other way of improving the connection. Completion Design, for what sits above the packer. Geomechanics, if you have not taken it, because the Expert tier consumes its output. And Well Integrity, for what all of this looks like after twenty years.
