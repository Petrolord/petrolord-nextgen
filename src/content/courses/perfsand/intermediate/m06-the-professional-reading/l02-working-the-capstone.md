# Working the capstone

The six graded values in this tier, and the traps in each.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## The conditions

The same capstone as the Associate tier: a larger hole, a charge that is not in the catalog, a phasing neither published gun uses, and an anisotropy that is not the lessons'.

Re-read the conditions. Three of the inputs differ from the published cases in ways that change more than one component each.

## The six values

Three skin components, the total, the productivity ratio, and one dimensionless group.

The plane-flow term needs the effective wellbore radius first. The phasing here is a real angle, so it is the tabulated constant times the SUM of the wellbore radius and the tunnel length. Get the constant for the right angle.

The converging-flow term needs both dimensionless groups. The dimensionless spacing carries the square root of the anisotropy; the dimensionless radius carries one plus the square root of its reciprocal. Getting those two square roots the wrong way round is the single most common error here.

The crushed-zone term needs the spacing over the tunnel length, the log of the radius ratio, and the contrast less one. The crushed radius here is larger than the published cases and the contrast is smaller.

The total is the sum of all four, including the blockage term, which is not separately graded but is not zero.

The ratio needs the drainage radius, which is not three hundred metres here.

The dimensionless perforation radius is graded on its own because it is what decides whether this case is inside the development range.

## Where the marks get lost

Using the entrance hole as the tunnel radius instead of half of it.

Using the shot density in shots per foot where shots per metre was wanted, or forgetting to invert it to a spacing.

Applying the zero-phasing formula for the effective wellbore radius at a real angle.

And forgetting the blockage term in the total. It is small at this phasing and it is not zero.

## Checking yourself

Three checks. The four components must sum to the total. A negative total must give a ratio above one. And the dimensionless perforation radius must be compared against the stated range, because whether this case is inside it is part of the answer you should know even though only the value is graded.

## Exercise
