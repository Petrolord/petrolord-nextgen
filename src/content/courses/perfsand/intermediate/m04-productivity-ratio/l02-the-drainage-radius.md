# The drainage radius

The one input to the ratio that nobody knows, and how much it matters.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## What it is

The radius at which the pressure is the reservoir pressure, in the idealised steady radial picture. Beyond it, nothing flows to this well.

In a bounded reservoir with several wells it is roughly half the well spacing. In an unbounded one it is a function of time and does not settle. In a real field it is a number somebody chose.

## How well it is known

Badly. A factor of two either way is normal, and in an appraisal well with no offsets it can be a factor of five.

That would be alarming if the ratio depended on it strongly. It does not.

## Why it barely matters

Because it enters through a logarithm, and only in comparison with the wellbore radius.

Sweeping the drainage radius over a factor of forty, from fifty metres to two thousand, moves the productivity ratio on the published high-shot-density gun by under a fifth. A factor of two, which is the realistic uncertainty, moves it by a few percent.

So the number nobody knows is the number the answer is least sensitive to, which is a comfortable arrangement and not a common one.

## The direction

A larger drainage radius gives a larger logarithm, which makes the skin a smaller fraction of the total, which pushes the ratio TOWARDS one from whichever side it is on.

So a large drainage area dilutes both good and bad perforating. A well draining two kilometres cares less about its perforation skin than a well draining fifty metres, because the reservoir resistance dominates.

## What that means for design

Perforation quality matters most in small drainage areas: infill wells, tight spacing, and short-radius horizontal completions. It matters least in a single well draining a large area.

That is a genuinely useful conclusion and it falls straight out of the shape of the expression.
