# What the ratio hides

Four things a single productivity number does not carry, and when each one matters.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## It hides the split

Two completions with the same total skin have the same ratio, and they can have got there completely differently: one with a good plane-flow term and a bad crushed zone, the other with the reverse.

Those two wells respond differently to treatment. The first will improve with an acid wash and the second will not. The ratio cannot tell them apart and the four components can.

## It hides the interval

The skin here is per unit length of a uniformly perforated interval. A well with half its interval perforated well and half not perforated at all has an average that no single geometry describes.

Partial penetration has its own skin term and it is not in this model.

## It hides the rate

Non-Darcy pressure drop in the tunnels rises with the square of the velocity, and it appears as a skin that grows with rate. A gas well with a computed skin of minus one can test at plus two at high rate, entirely from turbulence in the perforations.

The ratio computed here is the low-rate limit.

## It hides time

Perforations plug. Fines migrate into the crushed zone, scale precipitates, and asphaltenes deposit. A completion whose ratio was one point three at start-up can be at zero point eight in two years without anything in the geometry changing.

Nothing in this course is a function of time.

## What to report alongside it

The four components, so the split is visible. The interval fraction perforated, so partial penetration is on the record. A note that the number is the Darcy limit. And the date.

Those four lines cost nothing and they stop the ratio being read as more than it is.
