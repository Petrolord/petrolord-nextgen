# A ratio, not a rate

What the number is, what it is routinely mistaken for, and the difference.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## What it is not

Not a rate. Not barrels a day, not cubic metres a second, not anything with a dimension.

Not a comparison against the well as it is. A ratio of one point three does not mean the well will produce thirty percent more than it does today; it means it produces thirty percent more than an imaginary reference.

Not a prediction of a well test. A well test measures the total skin, which includes drilling damage, partial penetration, deviation and turbulence, none of which is in this number.

## What it is

A dimensionless comparison of one flow geometry against another, at the same everything else.

## The mistake that gets made

Somebody computes a ratio of one point two seven for the chosen gun and reports that the completion will deliver twenty seven percent more than expected.

More than what was expected by whom? If the expectation already assumed a good completion, the twenty seven percent is being counted twice. If it assumed an open hole, the comparison is right. Usually nobody has said.

The fix is to state the reference every time the number is quoted. It costs six words and it prevents the whole class of error.

## Where the ratio IS the right tool

Comparing two candidate completions in the same well. Both ratios share the same imaginary reference, so the reference cancels and the comparison is clean.

That is what the catalog sweep does, and it is the main use of the number in practice.

## The other honest use

As a sanity bound. A completion whose computed ratio is below one is worse than an open hole of the same radius, which is a statement worth acting on regardless of what the absolute rate turns out to be.
