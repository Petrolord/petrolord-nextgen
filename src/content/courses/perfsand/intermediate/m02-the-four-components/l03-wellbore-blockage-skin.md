# Wellbore blockage skin

The smallest of the four, why it exists, and the one case where it is not small.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## What it is

The wellbore itself gets in the way. Flow converging towards a perforation from the far side of the hole has to go around the wellbore, which is an obstruction of finite size sitting in the middle of the flow field.

The term is always positive and it is usually tiny.

## The form

An exponential in a single dimensionless group: the wellbore radius divided by the sum of the tunnel length and the wellbore radius. Call that the blockage ratio.

The ratio runs from nearly zero, for a tunnel much longer than the wellbore radius, to nearly one, for a tunnel much shorter than it. The two fitted constants per phasing turn that ratio into a skin, and both of them vary enormously with the angle.

## Why the constants vary so much

Because the geometry of "going around the wellbore" depends entirely on where the shots are.

At forty five degrees the shots are spread all around, so very little flow has to travel far around the obstruction, and the constants are minuscule: the blockage term on the published high-shot-density gun is about a ten thousandth of a skin unit.

At zero phasing every shot is on one side, so flow from the far side has to travel around a full half of the wellbore. The constants are three orders of magnitude larger, and the blockage term on the published through-tubing gun is nearly three tenths of a skin unit.

## The case where it is not small

A short tunnel in a big hole, shot in line. That maximises the blockage ratio and picks the worst constants at the same time.

That is exactly the through-tubing case, and it is one of the reasons through-tubing guns show positive skins. The blockage term alone contributes about a seventh of that gun's total.

## Why it is worth computing at all

Because it costs nothing and because it is the component that most clearly rewards phasing. Someone comparing two guns on penetration alone will miss that the phased one has essentially eliminated a term the in-line one is paying in full.
