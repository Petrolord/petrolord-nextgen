# Plane-flow skin

The first and largest component, and the only one that can be negative.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## What it is

The logarithm of the wellbore radius divided by the effective wellbore radius.

That single expression carries the whole idea of the perforations reaching out into the rock. If the effective radius is bigger than the actual one, the logarithm is negative and the term is a stimulation.

## Where the effective radius comes from

Two formulas, one for zero phasing and one for everything else, as the Associate tier established. For a phased gun it is a tabulated constant times the sum of the wellbore radius and the tunnel length.

Notice the SUM. The effective radius grows with the tunnel length essentially one for one, times the constant. A gun with a longer tunnel has a proportionally larger effective radius and a more negative plane-flow skin.

## Why it is the largest component

Because it is the only one with a logarithm of a ratio that can be far from one. The other three are all small positive corrections in a well-designed completion.

Across the nine gun catalog at one rock, the plane-flow term swings by nearly two and a half skin units while the converging term swings by about half a unit and the blockage term by about a third.

## The two things that move it

Phasing, through the constant, and the tunnel length, through the sum. Nothing else appears in the expression.

Not the shot density. Not the entrance hole. Not the anisotropy. Not the crushed zone. The plane-flow term is a function of exactly three numbers: the wellbore radius, the tunnel length and the phasing.

## Which of the two matters more

Phasing, and by a lot, because of the zero case. Moving from in-line shots to forty five degrees changes the FORMULA, and on the published high-shot-density gun that step is worth over one and four tenths of skin, of which more than ninety percent is this term.

Doubling the tunnel length on the same gun is worth much less, because it enters inside a logarithm.
