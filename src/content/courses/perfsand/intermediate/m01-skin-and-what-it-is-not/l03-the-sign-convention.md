# The sign convention

Positive, negative and zero, and what each one is comparing against.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## The reference

Skin is measured against an open hole of the SAME RADIUS with no near-wellbore effect at all. Not against another perforated completion, not against a different gun, and not against the well before it was cased.

That reference is imaginary. A well with casing and cement and no perforations does not flow, and a well with no casing at all is a different well. The zero-skin open hole is a calculating device.

## Positive

The perforated completion flows worse than that open hole. Fluid has to converge into a set of small tunnels rather than into the whole cylinder, and the extra convergence costs pressure.

Every through-tubing gun in the catalog gives a positive total at the published rock, because their tunnels are short, their shot densities are low and their shots are in line.

## Negative

The perforated completion flows BETTER than the open hole of the same radius, which sounds impossible until you notice what the tunnels are.

A perforation reaches out beyond the wellbore wall. A well with tunnels a third of a metre long has fingers of high conductivity reaching into rock that an open hole of the same radius never touches. The effective wellbore radius is genuinely larger than the drilled one.

So a negative perforation skin is not an accounting artefact. It is a real stimulation, and it is why perforating a well can be better than not casing it.

## Zero

The two effects exactly cancel. There is nothing special about the case; it is a crossing point, and in this course's catalog it falls between the two smallest guns and the rest.

## What the sign does not tell you

Whether the well is damaged. A perforation skin of minus two on a well whose drilling damage is plus six gives a total of plus four, and the well tests as damaged despite excellent perforations.

The perforation contribution and the total are different quantities and only one of them is computed here.
