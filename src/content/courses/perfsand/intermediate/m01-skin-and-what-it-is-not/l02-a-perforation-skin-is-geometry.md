# A perforation skin is geometry

What goes into the correlation, and the striking thing about what does not.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## What goes in

Four lengths, a phasing angle, and two permeability RATIOS.

## What does not

The absolute permeability. Anywhere. The rock could be one millidarcy or one darcy and every one of the four skin components would be identical.

That is worth sitting with, because it is counter-intuitive and it is correct. Skin is a dimensionless comparison between the actual flow geometry and an ideal radial one, and both geometries scale with permeability in exactly the same way. The permeability cancels.

## What the ratios are doing

The horizontal to vertical permeability ratio is not an absolute permeability; it is an anisotropy. It changes the SHAPE of the flow field, stretching the vertical convergence path relative to the horizontal one, and shape is what a dimensionless skin can see.

The undamaged to crushed permeability ratio is the same kind of thing: a contrast, not a level. A crushed zone that is five times worse than the rock costs the same skin whether the rock is one or a hundred millidarcies.

## What follows for design

A gun that is good in one rock is good in another rock of the same anisotropy. The perforation design does not need the permeability.

What DOES need the permeability is everything the perforation skin is then used for: the rate, the underbalance selection, whether the well is worth completing at all. Those are outside the skin calculation.

## The one place permeability appears in this course

The underbalance guideline bands are indexed by permeability class. That is not part of the skin; it is a separate piece of field guidance about how much drawdown is needed to clean a tunnel, and cleaning is a transient flushing process rather than a steady-state geometry.

So the whole course has exactly one input in millidarcies, and it feeds one output.
