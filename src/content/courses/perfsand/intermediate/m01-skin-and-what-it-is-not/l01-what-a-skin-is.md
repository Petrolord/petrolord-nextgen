# What a skin is

The definition, in the only form this course needs it.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## The definition

In steady radial flow, the pressure drop from the drainage radius to the wellbore is proportional to the logarithm of the radius ratio. A real well has an extra pressure drop, or occasionally less than that, from whatever is happening near the wellbore.

Skin is that extra pressure drop expressed as a dimensionless number added to the logarithm. A positive skin is extra resistance; a negative skin is less than the ideal.

## Why it is expressed that way

Because it makes the near-wellbore effect additive and separable. The reservoir contributes its logarithm, the near-wellbore contributes its skin, and the total is their sum.

That separation is what lets a perforation skin be computed from geometry alone, with no reservoir model at all. Whatever the reservoir does, it does through the logarithm, and the perforation adds its own term on top.

## What total skin contains in a real well

Perforation geometry, which is this course. Drilling and completion damage, which is a separate term. Partial penetration, if only part of the interval is open. Deviation, which reduces skin. Turbulence, which is rate dependent. Scale, fines and other things that accumulate.

A well test measures the SUM of all of those and cannot separate them. This course computes one term of that sum from first principles, which is a different thing from measuring it.

## Why that distinction matters

Because a measured total skin of plus three does not mean the perforations are bad. It means the sum of every near-wellbore effect is plus three, and the perforations could be contributing minus two while damage contributes plus five.

Conversely, computing a perforation skin of minus one point seven does not predict a well test result. It says what the perforation geometry contributes, all else equal, and all else is never equal.

## What the course can therefore claim

That it computes the perforation contribution to skin, from geometry, exactly as the published correlation defines it. Not that it predicts a well test, and not that it explains one.
