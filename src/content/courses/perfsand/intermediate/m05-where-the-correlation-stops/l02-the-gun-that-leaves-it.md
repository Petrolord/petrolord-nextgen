# The gun that leaves it

One row in nine, and it is the one that looks best.

{{panel:ps-skin-explorer}}

## The result

Run the whole catalog and exactly one row raises a warning: the seven inch big-hole casing gun. Its dimensionless perforation radius is about zero point two eight, against a stated upper limit of zero point one eight.

No row exceeds the spacing limit. One row out of nine, on one of the two groups.

## Why that gun

Because a big-hole charge is designed to make a wide entrance hole, and the dimensionless radius is the tunnel radius divided by twice the spacing. A seven tenths of an inch entrance hole at twelve shots per foot gives a large ratio.

It is not an error and it is not an unusual gun. Big-hole guns exist precisely because gravel packing needs wide tunnels, and gravel packing is a large part of what this course is about.

## The awkward part

That same row is the best row in the table. It has the lowest total skin and the highest productivity ratio of the nine.

So the most attractive number on the sheet is the least trustworthy one, and a reader scanning for the best gun will land on the flagged row without noticing.

## What to do about it

Three things, in order.

Notice. The warning is in the return value and the panel colours the value; neither helps if nobody looks.

Keep the ranking and distrust the magnitude. The physical argument that a wide long tunnel drains better than a narrow short one does not depend on the correlation, so the gun really is the best of the nine. What is uncertain is by how much.

And if the magnitude matters, get a number from somewhere else: a vendor's own modelling, a finite element solution, or a well test on an offset with the same gun.

## The general lesson

Warnings correlate with attractive answers, because both come from being at the edge of the input space. The rows in the middle of the range are the ordinary ones, and the ordinary ones are neither the most exciting nor the most suspect.

That is worth remembering in any model with a validity range.
