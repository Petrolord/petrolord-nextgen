# The effective wellbore radius

The idea that turns a set of tunnels into a single equivalent well.

{{panel:ps-shot-explorer}}

## The idea

Far from the wellbore, the flow into a perforated completion looks exactly like radial flow into a plain cylindrical well. It is only close in that the individual tunnels are visible.

So the plane-flow part of the perforation skin can be expressed as a comparison: the perforated well behaves like an unperforated well of some other radius. Call that the effective wellbore radius, and the skin is the logarithm of the actual radius divided by it.

If the effective radius is LARGER than the actual one, the logarithm is negative and the perforations have made the well behave bigger than it is. That is the normal case for a well phased gun with a decent tunnel.

## The two formulas

For a phased gun, the effective radius is a constant times the sum of the wellbore radius and the tunnel length. The constant depends on the phasing angle and comes from the published table: it is largest at forty five degrees and falls as the angle grows.

For an in-line gun at zero phasing, the effective radius is a quarter of the tunnel length, and the wellbore radius does not appear at all.

## Why the zero case is so different

Because in-line shots do not surround the wellbore. The flow cannot converge symmetrically; it has to reach around to one plane. The paper handles that with a separate expression rather than by giving zero phasing a small constant, and the result is a much smaller effective radius.

On the published gun in this course, moving from in-line shots to forty five degrees takes over one and a four tenths off the total skin, of which over ninety percent is the plane-flow term alone.

## What the constant does with angle

Above zero it falls monotonically: forty five degrees has the largest constant and a hundred and eighty degrees the smallest. So forty five degrees gives the largest effective radius and the most negative plane-flow skin.

That is not intuitive. More angles between shots sounds better distributed, and it is not: forty five degrees is the best of the six and a hundred and eighty is the worst of the phased ones.
