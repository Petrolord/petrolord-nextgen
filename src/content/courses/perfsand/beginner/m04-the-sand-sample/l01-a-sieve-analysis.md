# A sieve analysis

The measurement, how it is done, and what comes out of it.

{{panel:ps-shot-explorer}}

## The measurement

A sample of formation sand is dried, weighed and passed through a stack of sieves of decreasing mesh, largest at the top. Each sieve retains the grains too big to pass it. Weigh what is on each sieve and you have the mass distribution of grain sizes.

The sample comes from core, from sidewall cores, or from produced sand caught at surface. Each source has its own bias, and the third is the worst: what arrives at surface is what the formation was willing to give up, which is not the formation.

## What comes out

A table: for each sieve opening, the cumulative percentage of the sample retained at or above that size.

That table is the entire input to everything in the sand half of this course. Nine points is a normal number. Four is the minimum the engine will accept, and four is not many.

## What the numbers are

Percentages by WEIGHT, not by grain count. A sample dominated by weight by a few coarse grains can be dominated by count by many fine ones, and the sieve reports the first.

That matters for fines, which are light. A sample with two percent fines by weight has an enormous number of fine grains, and it is the count that plugs a screen.

## Why sizes are on a log scale

Because sieve series are geometric, not arithmetic. Standard sieve openings step by a roughly constant RATIO rather than a constant difference, so a plot against size on a linear axis crowds everything into the left-hand end.

And because natural grain size distributions are approximately log-normal, which makes a log axis the one on which they look like a distribution rather than a hook.

Every interpolation in this course is done on the log of the size for that reason.
