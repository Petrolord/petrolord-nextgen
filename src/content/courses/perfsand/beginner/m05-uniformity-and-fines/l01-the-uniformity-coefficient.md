# The uniformity coefficient

One number for how spread out a sand is, and what it is used to decide.

{{panel:ps-shot-explorer}}

## The definition

The uniformity coefficient is D40 divided by D90.

Both are on the retained convention, so D40 is coarser than D90 and the ratio is greater than one. A ratio of one would be a sand in which every grain is the same size.

## What it measures

The width of the middle of the distribution. Not the full range, which the sorting coefficient covers, but the span between the coarse-ish and the fine-ish parts of the sample.

A uniformity near one is a well sorted sand: a beach or a dune, where the transport process graded the grains before they were deposited. A uniformity above five is a poorly sorted one: a debris flow or a rapidly deposited turbidite, where everything arrived at once.

## Why it decides anything

Because a screen filters by bridging. Grains too large to pass an opening arrest at it and form a bridge, and the bridge then holds back grains much finer than the opening.

That works when the sand is uniform, because there are plenty of grains near the coarse end to build the bridge and not much material fine enough to pass through the bridge afterwards.

It works badly when the sand is poorly sorted, because the fine tail can pass through the bridge and keep going, and the well produces fines forever through a screen that is technically doing its job.

## The thresholds

The ladder in this course uses three below three, and three to five. Below three is uniform enough for a wire-wrap screen if the fines are also low. Below five is uniform enough for a premium screen. Above five, gravel.

Those are screening thresholds from the published selection literature, and they are round numbers because they are judgement calls that somebody wrote down.

## The published sand

Its uniformity is a little over three, which puts it just above the wire-wrap threshold and comfortably below the gravel one.

That is a common place for a real sand to land, and it is the place where the second number takes over.
