# The forty four micron cutoff

One size, one number, and the largest single influence on a completion type.

{{panel:ps-shot-explorer}}

## The number

Fines are defined here as the percentage of the sample finer than forty four microns, which is the opening of a three hundred and twenty five mesh US standard sieve.

It is a convention rather than a physical boundary. Nothing changes about a grain at forty three microns that does not change at forty five. The cutoff exists because a number is needed and because that sieve is in every standard stack.

## Why the fine end matters so much

Because fine material is what plugs.

A screen or a gravel pack works by arresting grains at its openings and letting fluid through. Material fine enough to enter the pack does not stop at the surface; it travels into the pack and lodges in the pore throats between the gravel grains, and the pack loses permeability from the inside.

A pack that has plugged is worse than no pack, because the drawdown needed to produce through it rises, and a higher drawdown is exactly what the sanding calculation says to avoid.

## Why weight percent understates it

The sieve reports mass. Fine grains are light. A sample with five percent fines by weight has, at a tenth of the diameter, roughly a thousand times as many grains per unit mass as the coarse fraction, so five percent by mass is an overwhelming majority by count.

It is the count that finds the pore throats. So a fines percentage that sounds small is a large number of particles.

## The thresholds

Two percent, five percent and ten percent, on the ladder in this course. Below two, a wire-wrap screen is viable if the sand is also uniform. Below five, a premium screen. Below ten, a gravel pack. Above ten, gravel with fines management or a frac pack.

The published sand sits between five and ten, which puts it on the gravel pack rung.

## What the number does not tell you

What the fines ARE. Forty four microns of quartz is inert. Forty four microns of kaolinite migrates, and forty four microns of smectite swells when the salinity changes.

A fines percentage is a size statement. The mineralogy is a different measurement and it changes the answer.
