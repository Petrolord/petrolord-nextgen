# Onward

Where the next two tiers go, and what else connects to this course.

## The Professional tier

It takes the four lengths and turns them into a number that says whether the well flows better or worse for having been perforated.

Four skin components, each with its own mechanism: the plane-flow term through the effective wellbore radius, the converging-flow term from the vertical path between shots, the blockage term from the wellbore itself, and the crushed zone.

Then the whole gun catalog at one rock, ranked, which crosses from damaging to stimulating in a place worth seeing. Then the productivity ratio, and the drainage radius it depends on. And then the module that matters most for judgement: where the correlation was developed, which catalog gun leaves that range, and why the most attractive row on the sheet is the least trustworthy one.

## The Expert tier

It takes the sieve statistics and turns them into hardware, then asks whether the rock will fail at all.

Saucier sizes the gravel from the median. A screen gauge follows from the gravel. A standalone slot window follows from the coarse decile. Each of those is a short calculation with a surprise in it: the Saucier band is narrower than the commercial sands are wide, so a sand can have no match at all.

Then sanding onset: a Kirsch hoop stress at the cavity wall against the rock strength, swept along the interval. And a module on a real defect this course found in that sweep.

## Elsewhere in the academy

Geomechanics supplies the stress and strength profile the sanding calculation runs on, and this course consumes it rather than computing it.

Completion Design decides what sits above the packer; this course decides what happens below it. Stimulation Design is the alternative to a perforation skin: where this course improves the connection geometrically, that one improves it chemically or by fracturing.

Well Integrity asks what the perforations and the screen look like after twenty years.

## What to do next

Pass the Associate capstone, then take the Professional tier before the Expert one. The Expert tier's sanding module is easier once you have seen the perforation as a cavity rather than as a flow path.
