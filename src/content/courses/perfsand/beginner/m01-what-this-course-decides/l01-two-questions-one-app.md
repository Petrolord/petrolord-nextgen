# Two questions, one app

Why perforating and sand control are taught together, and what each of them decides.

## The first question

A cased and cemented well is sealed off from the reservoir by a steel pipe and a sheath of cement. Perforating is how you open it: a gun is run to depth and shaped charges fire holes through the casing, the cement and some distance into the rock.

The question this course asks about that operation is a narrow one. Given a gun, a charge and a rock, how much better or worse does the well flow than it would with no casing at all.

## The second question

Some reservoirs are consolidated and produce clean. Some are not, and the rock arrives at surface with the oil. Sand production erodes chokes and valves, fills the wellbore, wears out separators and eventually shuts the well in.

The question this course asks about that is two questions in one. What size and shape of sand is this, and therefore what completion type does it need. And will this rock make sand at all at the drawdown the well needs.

## Why they are one app

Because they meet at the perforation. The tunnel a charge makes is the cavity whose wall either holds or fails, so the sanding calculation is about the same hole the skin calculation is about. And a gravel-packed completion is perforated with a different gun from a natural completion, because the pack has to be placed through the holes.

The design decisions interleave. A big-hole charge is chosen for gravel packing precisely because a wide tunnel takes a pack better, and that choice has consequences for the skin that the first half of the course computes.

## What the app produces

Five things. A perforation skin with its four components. A productivity ratio against an ideal open hole. A guideline underbalance band. A sieve analysis with the statistics a completion type is chosen from. And a critical drawdown profile along the perforated interval.

The Associate tier is about reading the two inputs those five things are computed from: what a charge leaves behind, and what a sand sample says.
