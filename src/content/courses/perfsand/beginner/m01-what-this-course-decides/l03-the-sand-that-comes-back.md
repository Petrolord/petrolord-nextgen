# The sand that comes back

Why a reservoir produces its own rock, and what a completion does about it.

## Where the sand comes from

Two mechanisms, and they are different problems.

Loose grains that were never cemented into the rock in the first place migrate with the flow and arrive at surface. That is fines migration and it happens in rocks that are otherwise perfectly competent.

Or the rock at the cavity wall fails mechanically. Drawing the well down lowers the pressure inside the perforation while the far-field stress stays where it is, and the difference is carried by the rock around the cavity. Past a point the rock cannot carry it, the wall spalls, and the failed material is carried away by the flow.

The second is what the Expert tier computes. The first is what the fines percentage in a sieve analysis warns about.

## What it costs

An eroded choke, which is a workover. A filled rathole, which is lost production and a cleanout. A cut-out safety valve, which is a well integrity failure. A separator full of sand, which is a facilities problem. And in the worst case a collapsed casing, because rock that has left the formation has left a void behind it.

None of those is recoverable by turning the well down after the fact. The grains are already out.

## The three answers

Do nothing, and manage the sand at surface. That is a real choice for a well producing a few grams a cubic metre.

Screen it. Put a filter in the wellbore that the formation sand cannot pass. Either the screen alone, sized against the sand, or a screen with gravel packed around it so that the gravel does the filtering and the screen only holds the gravel.

Or avoid the failure. Keep the drawdown below the point at which the cavity wall fails, which costs rate.

## What decides which

Two numbers from a sieve analysis, and one calculation from a stress profile. The sieve says whether a screen can be sized at all and whether gravel is needed. The stress calculation says whether the rock will fail at the drawdown the well needs.

Those are the Associate and the Expert halves of the sand side of this course.
