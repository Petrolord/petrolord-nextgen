# A shaped charge in one paragraph

Enough of the physics to read a charge data sheet, and no more.

{{panel:ps-shot-explorer}}

## The mechanism

A conical metal liner sits in front of an explosive pellet. Detonation collapses the cone from the apex forward, and the collapsing metal forms a jet at the axis, with the slower tail material forming a slug behind it.

The jet is not a projectile in the ordinary sense. Its tip travels at something like seven kilometres a second, and at that speed the strength of the target is negligible compared with the stagnation pressure at the tip. Penetration is set by the density and length of the jet against the density of the target.

## What follows from that

Penetration depends on the target. The same charge in steel, in cement, in concrete and in sandstone gives four different depths, because the densities differ. So a penetration figure is meaningless without saying what it was measured in.

Penetration and hole size trade against each other. A deep-penetrating charge concentrates its jet, which makes a narrower entrance hole. A big-hole charge spreads it, which makes a wider hole and a shallower tunnel. One charge cannot maximise both, and the catalog shows the trade in every row.

The tunnel is damaged. The same energy that removes the rock also compacts and fractures what remains, leaving a crushed shell around the tunnel.

## What the course takes from this

Three numbers per charge: an entrance hole, a penetration, and the target they were measured in. Everything else is the vendor's problem.

The course also takes one caution. A perforation is a violently made hole, and the model that follows treats it as a clean cylinder in undamaged rock with a stated damage ring. That is a model of a perforation, not a perforation.
