# Saucier

The gravel sizing rule, in one line, and the experiment behind it.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The rule

Size the gravel so that its median grain is five to six times the formation median.

That is the whole rule. One input, the formation D50, and one output, a band.

## Where it comes from

Saucier's 1974 work, which flowed formation sands through packs of different gravel sizes and measured what came through and what the pack permeability did.

Too fine a gravel and the pack itself impairs the flow: the gravel pore throats are small and the pack is a resistance. Too coarse and the formation sand enters the pack, migrates through it and either produces or plugs it from within.

The five to six band is where the pack retains the formation and keeps most of its own permeability.

## Why a ratio and not a size

Because bridging is scale-free. A grain bridges across a pore throat when it is a large enough FRACTION of the throat, and the throat size scales with the gravel size. So the criterion is a ratio, and it applies to a fine sand and a coarse one alike.

## Why the median and not the coarse end

Because the pack has to retain the BULK of the formation, not just its coarsest grains. Sizing on D10 would give a much coarser gravel that the median of the formation would walk straight through.

That is the opposite of the standalone screen rule, which sizes on D10 precisely because there the coarse grains are the ones doing the bridging across a single opening. Two different mechanisms, two different percentiles, and mixing them up is a real error.

## The band is narrow

Five to six is a ratio of one point two between the two ends. That is a tight specification, and the next lesson is about what happens when it meets a commercial catalog whose rows are much wider than that.
