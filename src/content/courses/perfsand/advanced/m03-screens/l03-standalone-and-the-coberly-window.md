# Standalone and the Coberly window

Sizing a screen against the formation directly, and why it uses a different percentile.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The window

For a standalone screen, the slot should fall between the formation D10 and twice the formation D10.

Two ends, and the engine returns both rather than a single value.

## Why D10 and not D50

Because in a standalone completion there is no gravel, so the formation itself has to bridge across the slot.

Bridging works when there are enough grains LARGER than the opening to arrest at it and build an arch. Those are the coarse grains, and D10 is the coarse decile. Sizing the slot at the formation median would mean half the sample passes straight through and there is nothing left to bridge with.

## Why a window rather than a value

Because both ends fail differently.

A slot much smaller than D10 will certainly bridge, and it will also plug, because it is now small enough for the middle of the distribution to reach it too.

A slot much larger than twice D10 has too few grains coarse enough to arrest at it, and the bridge never forms.

## Why twice

Because bridging across an opening works up to about twice the grain size: an arch of grains can span a gap two grain diameters wide and not much more.

That is the same arch mechanics that lets a gravel pack work, and it is why both rules are ratios rather than absolute sizes.

## What has to be true for any of it

The formation has to be uniform enough that the coarse decile is representative of a substantial fraction of the sample, and clean enough that whatever passes the bridge does not plug it.

Which is exactly what the ladder checked before recommending a standalone screen in the first place. The window is only ever applied to sands the ladder has already cleared, and applying it to a poorly sorted sand would give a perfectly computable and completely wrong answer.
