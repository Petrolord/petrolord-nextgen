# The gravel-pack rule

One sentence, and the reason it is that sentence.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The rule

The screen opening must be smaller than the SMALLEST gravel grain.

Not smaller than the gravel median, and not some fraction of it. Smaller than the smallest grain in the pack.

## Why the smallest

Because the screen's only job in a gravel pack is to hold the gravel. The gravel does the formation filtering; the screen is a retainer.

If the screen opening exceeds the smallest gravel grain, the fines of the pack pass through it. The pack loses its finest fraction, which is exactly the fraction that was doing the tightest filtering, and the pack coarsens in place.

## Where the smallest grain comes from

The lower sieve of the dual designation. A twenty forty gravel was cut between a twenty mesh sieve and a forty mesh sieve, so nothing in it is finer than the forty mesh opening.

That is a nominal statement about the cut and a real vendor certificate will show a tail below it, which is one more reason the catalog rows are flagged approximate.

## Why the rule is so much easier than the standalone one

Because a commercial gravel is far more uniform than a formation sand. A twenty forty cut spans a factor of two; a formation sand spans a factor of ten or more.

Sizing a filter against a narrow distribution is easy, and that easiness is precisely what the gravel bought. The hard filtering problem was moved from the screen to the gravel, and the gravel was chosen by a rule with one input.

## What the rule does not consider

Erosion. A screen with fluid at high velocity through a small open area erodes, and the open area depends on the gauge chosen. A tighter gauge is safer against gravel loss and worse against erosion.

Nothing here computes that trade.

## Exercise

State the gravel-pack screen rule and say why it is the smallest grain rather than the median.

Then say why this sizing problem is easier than the standalone one, and name what the rule ignores.
