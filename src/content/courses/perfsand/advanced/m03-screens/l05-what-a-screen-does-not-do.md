# What a screen does not do

Five things a correctly sized screen will not fix.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## It does not stop fines

A screen sized to retain the formation retains the coarse and middle fractions. Material fine enough to pass the bridge passes it, and that is by design: a screen that stopped everything would plug immediately.

So a well with a screen still produces fines, and the fines still erode surface equipment. The screen protects the wellbore, not the facility.

## It does not stop the formation moving

Grains that arrive at the screen and bridge have already left the rock. The void they came from is still there.

Over time that void grows, and in a weak formation the near-wellbore rock can collapse onto the screen. A screen full of collapsed formation is a screen with no annulus and a large pressure drop.

## It does not survive erosion indefinitely

A screen with a high velocity of sand-laden fluid through its slots erodes, and the erosion is concentrated wherever the flow is concentrated. A hot spot in one part of the interval erodes there first and then the hole grows.

Screen failures are more often erosion than sizing.

## It does not fix a bad drawdown

If the rock will fail at the drawdown the well needs, a screen catches the failed material rather than preventing the failure. That is a valid strategy and it is a different one from keeping the rock intact.

The Expert tier's sanding calculation is about the second. A screen is the fallback for when the first is not possible.

## It does not survive a chemistry it was not designed for

Acid, scale inhibitor squeezes, and changes in produced water salinity all reach the screen. A woven mesh in an incompatible fluid can fail in ways that have nothing to do with grain size.

## What that means for the course

Everything computed in this module is a SIZE. Size is necessary and it is not sufficient, and most screen failures in the field are not size failures.
