# The question the sieve answers

What a completion type decision actually is, and why two numbers can start it.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The five options

Natural completion, with no sand control at all, managing whatever arrives at surface.

A standalone wire-wrap screen, which is a slotted or wire-wrapped tube sized against the formation sand.

A standalone premium screen, which is a multi-layer woven mesh with a much tighter and more uniform pore structure.

A gravel pack, in which sized gravel is placed between the screen and the formation so that the gravel filters and the screen only holds the gravel.

And a frac pack or gravel pack with fines management, for sands that would plug anything simpler.

## What decides between them

Whether a filter can be sized at all, and whether it will stay open.

A filter can be sized when the sand is uniform enough that grains near the coarse end bridge across the openings and hold everything finer behind them. It stays open when there is not enough fine material to travel through the bridge and plug the pores behind it.

Those are the two questions, and they map exactly onto the uniformity and the fines.

## Why gravel changes the problem

A gravel pack does not filter with the screen. It filters with the gravel, and the screen only has to stop the gravel.

That decouples the two size problems. The gravel is sized against the formation, which is a size question with a published answer. The screen is sized against the gravel, which is a much easier question because a commercial gravel is far more uniform than a formation sand.

So gravel is what you buy when the formation is too badly sorted for a screen to be sized against it directly.

## What the ladder is

An ordered set of four rules on the two numbers. The first rule that holds is the indication. The last rule catches everything the first three do not, so there is always an answer.
