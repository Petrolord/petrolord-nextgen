# The ladder

Four rules, in order, and what each one is protecting against.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The rules

Uniformity below three AND fines below two percent gives a standalone wire-wrap screen.

Uniformity below five AND fines below five percent gives a standalone premium screen.

Uniformity below five AND fines below ten percent gives a gravel pack.

Uniformity at or above five OR fines at or above ten percent gives a gravel pack with fines management, or a frac pack evaluation.

## Why it is ordered

Because the rules overlap. A sand with a uniformity of two and fines of one percent satisfies the first three, and the ladder returns the first, which is the least intervention that works.

Evaluating them in order and taking the first match is what makes the ladder a recommendation rather than a list.

## Why the fourth rule is the complement

Look at the third rule and the fourth. The third is uniformity below five AND fines below ten; the fourth is uniformity at or above five OR fines at or above ten. Those are exact logical complements.

So the ladder is exhaustive by construction: any pair of numbers that fails the third rule satisfies the fourth. There is no sand for which the ladder has nothing to say, which is a property worth having in a rule that gets applied to whatever the lab sends back.

## What separates the first two rules

A wire-wrap screen is a single layer of wire wound on a perforated base pipe, with a slot of one width. A premium screen is a woven multi-layer mesh with a distribution of pore sizes and a much larger open area.

The premium screen tolerates more variability, because a woven mesh has many paths and a bridge that fails at one pore is caught at the next. So the uniformity threshold relaxes from three to five and the fines threshold from two to five.

## What separates the third from the fourth

Whether the fines can be lived with at all. Below ten percent, a conventional gravel pack with careful fluid selection generally holds. Above ten, the pack itself will plug, and the answer is either a fines-tolerant design or a fracture that bypasses the near-wellbore region entirely.
