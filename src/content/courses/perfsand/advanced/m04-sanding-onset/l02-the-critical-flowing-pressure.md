# The critical flowing pressure

Rearranging the criterion into the number a production engineer can use.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The rearrangement

Set the effective hoop stress equal to the strength and solve for the flowing pressure.

The critical flowing pressure is three times the larger far-field stress, minus the smaller, minus the strength, all divided by two.

## What it means

The lowest flowing pressure the cavity wall can survive. Draw the well down below it and the wall fails.

Because pressure and drawdown move opposite ways, a LOWER critical flowing pressure is better: it means more drawdown is available.

## The margin

The engine reports the critical flowing pressure and also the difference between the reservoir pressure and it, which is the drawdown available before onset.

A positive margin means there is drawdown to be had. A negative one means the cavity is already unstable at zero drawdown, which is a rock that will produce sand as soon as it flows at all.

## Why report the margin rather than the pressure

Because the pressure alone is not readable without knowing the reservoir pressure at the same depth, and the two vary together along an interval. The margin is the quantity that is directly comparable between depths and between wells.

## The three inputs

The two far-field stresses in the plane normal to the cavity, and the strength. Nothing else.

No Poisson ratio, no Biot coefficient, no friction angle, no cohesion, no intermediate principal stress. Three numbers in, one out.

## What the simplicity buys and costs

It buys transparency: a reader can check the arithmetic by hand and can see immediately how sensitive the answer is to each input. It costs realism: the criterion has no confining pressure dependence, so it treats deep strong rock and shallow weak rock with the same law.

The boost factor is the acknowledgement of that, and it is the subject of a later lesson in this module.
