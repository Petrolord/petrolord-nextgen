# A margin, not a sand rate

What the output is, and the four questions it does not answer.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## What it is

An ONSET criterion. It says whether the cavity wall reaches its strength at a given flowing pressure, and therefore what the maximum drawdown is before that happens.

## What it is not

It is not a sand rate. It says nothing about how many grams per cubic metre a failed cavity produces.

It is not a time. A wall at its failure stress does not fail instantly and completely; it degrades, and the timescale depends on the rock and the flow.

It is not a volume. The amount of material that can be produced from a failing cavity before the geometry stabilises is a different calculation.

And it is not a verdict on the well. Plenty of wells produce economically with sand, managed with a screen, with surface equipment, or by accepting periodic cleanouts.

## Why the onset is still the right thing to compute

Because it is the one question that has a defensible answer from the available data. Sand RATE prediction needs a failure mechanics model, an erosion model and a transport model, and all three are empirical and poorly constrained.

Onset needs three stresses and a strength, and it is a closed form. That is a much better place to draw a line than halfway into a model nobody can calibrate.

## What the margin is for

Three uses. Setting a maximum drawdown for a well that will be produced without sand control. Deciding whether sand control is needed at all. And comparing depths along an interval, to find where the problem is.

The third is the one the sweep in the next module supports, and it is the most useful of the three, because a completion decision usually turns on whether the weak zone is a metre thick or fifty.

## The label the engine attaches

Every result carries a screening grade flag. That is not modesty; it is a statement that the criterion has one strength parameter and no confining pressure dependence, and that a design decision needs core testing.
