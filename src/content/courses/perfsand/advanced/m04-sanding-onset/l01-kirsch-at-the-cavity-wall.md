# Kirsch at the cavity wall

The stress concentration behind the whole sanding calculation.

{{panel:ps-sand-explorer}}

## The classical result

A circular hole in an elastic plate under far-field stress concentrates the stress around its edge. The hoop stress at the wall, tangential to the hole, is three times the larger far-field stress minus the smaller one, at the point on the wall where it is largest.

That is the Kirsch solution, and it is over a century old.

## What it is being applied to here

The perforation tunnel, treated as a cylindrical cavity in the rock with the two far-field stresses acting in the plane normal to its axis.

Not the wellbore. The wellbore has its own Kirsch problem and it is the one the Geomechanics course solves for wellbore stability. This is the same mathematics applied to a much smaller hole with a different orientation.

## The effective stress

Rock strength responds to effective stress, which is the total stress less the pore pressure. At the cavity wall the near-wall pore pressure is the FLOWING pressure, because that is what the cavity is at.

So the effective hoop stress at the wall is three times the larger far-field stress, minus the smaller, minus twice the flowing pressure.

Twice, because the pore pressure subtracts once from the total hoop stress and once more through the radial equilibrium at a wall that is at the flowing pressure. That factor of two is what makes the drawdown so effective at causing failure.

## The failure criterion

Failure when the effective hoop stress reaches the rock's unconfined compressive strength.

That is the simplest possible criterion: no confining pressure dependence, no friction angle, no intermediate stress effect. It is chosen deliberately, and the next lessons say what the simplification costs and how the boost factor compensates.

## Why the factor of two matters

Because it means every unit of drawdown costs two units of effective hoop stress. Sanding is far more sensitive to drawdown than to anything else in the problem, which is why the answer is expressed as a critical drawdown.
