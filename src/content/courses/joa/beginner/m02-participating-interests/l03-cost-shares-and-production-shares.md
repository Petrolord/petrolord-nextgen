# Cost shares and production shares

{{panel:joa-account-calculator}}

A joint venture moves two things between its parties: the cost of the work and the production the work yields. The agreement gives each party a share of each. Most of the time the two shares are the same number, the participating interest, and it is easy to forget that they are two shares. This lesson separates them, because the rest of the course depends on the separation.

## Two names for two shares

The engine names both shares in every result:

* the **paying interest**, a party's share of cost: what it contributes to each cash call and each joint account bill;
* the **beneficial interest**, a party's share of production: what it lifts, sells or is credited with.

The engine's basis states where each comes from:

> beneficial interest = participating interest (the share of production); paying interest = participating interest - the carried part of a carried party's share + the carriers' shares of every carry

So the beneficial interest never moves away from the participating interest in this engine. The paying interest moves when a carry shifts one party's cost to others.

## When the two shares part

On the Ekene joint venture as the fixture states it, NOC is carried for 100 percent of its cost share, pro rata by the other three. The engine returns:

| party | participating interest | beneficial interest | paying interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EKO | 40.000000 | 40.000000 | 50.000000 |
| PA | 25.000000 | 25.000000 | 31.250000 |
| PB | 15.000000 | 15.000000 | 18.750000 |
| NOC | 20.000000 | 20.000000 | 0.000000 |

NOC keeps a beneficial interest of 20.000000 and pays 0.000000. EKO pays 50.000000 on a beneficial 40.000000. The course's short form of this is: a carry moves cost and never moves production.

## Which share a figure uses

Every figure in this course is split on one of the two shares, and you should know which before you trust it.

* A **cost** figure (a cash call, a party's share of the month's actual spending) is split on the paying interests.
* A **production** figure (a party's barrels, its revenue) is split on the beneficial interests.

This tier works only cost figures, so every split you compute in the account calculator uses the paying interests. The Professional tier uses the beneficial interests when it recovers a carry from the carried party's share of production.

## A common slip

A partner report that prints a single share column is ambiguous under a carry. If a table labels NOC at 20 percent and asks for its cash call, the answer on the Ekene terms is zero, because its paying interest is 0.000000. If a table labels EKO at 40 percent and splits a cash call on it, EKO is called for 40 percent of the cost when its paying interest is 50.000000. Always read which of the two shares a column holds.
