# Onward to Expert

This tier turned a fluid and a frame into a rock. The Expert tier turns that rock into a seismic response.

## What this tier established

You can take a logged point, recover the frame underneath it, and predict what the same rock would read with any fluid in its pores. That is the central skill of quantitative interpretation and everything above it depends on it.

The findings worth carrying are these.

The shear modulus is fluid blind, so a gas substitution raises the shear velocity while lowering the compressional one. Two velocities moving in opposite directions is the signature that separates a fluid effect from a porosity or lithology effect.

The dry frame is not the mineral frame. At Ekene they are 7.35 and 30.88 GPa, and the Biot coefficient of 0.80 is what says the rock is fluid sensitive at all.

The porosity is worth five times the mineral modulus, and both are assumptions rather than measurements.

And the gas effect saturates immediately. Five percent of gas delivers 77 percent of the velocity response, the velocity turns back up before pure gas, and the consequence is that a seismic amplitude cannot count gas.

## What the Expert tier does

It puts the Ekene shale over the sand and asks what a seismic survey would record from the interface.

At normal incidence that is a contrast in impedance, which this tier already computed: 7.200e6 for brine and 5.924e6 for gas.

Away from normal incidence it becomes amplitude versus offset, and the response depends on the shear velocity as well, which is where the fluid blindness of the shear modulus finally pays off. The brine case is class I and the gas case class III, so the fluid substitution flips the AVO class.

The tier also takes up resolution, because an anomaly has a thickness as well as an amplitude, and below the tuning thickness of 16 ms at 25 Hz the two stop being separable.

## Two things to expect

The first is that the class flip is less clean than the phrase suggests. The brine intercept sits only 1.7 times above the threshold that separates class I from class II, and that threshold is a convention somebody chose rather than a property of rock. The Expert tier measures how far the label is from changing.

The second is that the gas sand is still faster than the shale. The intercept goes negative on the density contrast, not the velocity contrast, which is a direct consequence of the density bookkeeping from module three of this tier. The easy half of the substitution turns out to drive the headline result of the whole ladder.

## What the Expert tier inherits

Everything. An AVO model is a substitution with a shale on top of it, so every assumption made here is load bearing there.

A wrong porosity moves the predicted gas velocity by 300 m/s, which moves both the intercept and the gradient. An estimated shear velocity that was not calibrated moves the gradient most of all, since the gradient is dominated by the shear contrast. And a substitution run on the wrong fluid state produces an AVO class prediction that is confidently wrong.

That is the general shape of this ladder: each tier is a thin layer of new arithmetic on top of a thick stack of inherited assumptions, and the assumptions are where the errors live.
