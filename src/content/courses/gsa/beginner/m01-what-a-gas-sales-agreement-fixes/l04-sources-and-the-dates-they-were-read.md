# Sources and the dates they were read

{{panel:gsa-quantity-calculator}}

Every rule this course teaches comes from a text, and every text has an edition. A contract clause read from one edition of a model agreement may read differently in the next, and a statutory rate may be adjusted by regulation. So the course names each text with its edition or gazette date and the date it was read, and every text below was read on 2026-09-26.

## What may be quoted

Only public texts are quoted, always with a citation. The Commonwealth model agreement says in its own front matter that it may be reused:

> "This work is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International." (Commonwealth model GSA (2025), front matter)

Model contracts sold under licence are another matter. The AIPN model gas sales agreement is a licensed text, so this course teaches its ideas as concepts and never quotes it. Every quoted clause you meet comes from the Commonwealth model agreement or from a public statute, manual or report.

## Figures the course will not invent

No legal rate, floor or adder in this course is typed from memory. Each is read from its cited section, and a figure that could not be read from a public text is a required input with no default. The clearest case is the domestic base price. The Authority determines it each year (PIA s.167(1)), and the regulator's own circular could not be retrieved. The engine's basis reports the figures in this form:

> a required input with no default: the Authority determines it each year under the Third Schedule (s.167(1)). US$2.18 per MMBtu (power) and US$2.68 (commercial), effective 1 April 2026, are reported by BusinessDay (31 March 2026) and by Advocaat Law Practice through Legal 500 (7 April 2026); the regulator's circular was not read

The course quotes the reported figures only in that form and grades none of them.

Flaring is taught as a concept only. The Act leaves the flare fine to regulations, and the regulations copy read is unnumbered and undated, so no rate is printed anywhere in this course.

## Where the engine cites its texts

Each engine result carries a basis block naming the rule it applied and where the rule comes from. The quantity calculator prints the rule lines under each result, so the working is always visible.

## Exercise

Open the quantity calculator, the course's own calculator panel. In "Volume to energy", run the starting case and read the three lines the engine prints under the tiles: the rule, the heating value wording and the units. Find the line that names NIST Special Publication 811 and its edition. Then switch to "Contract quantities and swing", run the starting case and read the day count line and the rule line. Write down each quantity the rule line defines, and beside it the text that defines it: the Commonwealth model agreement defines the ACQ and MaxDCQ, and HMRC's OT05402 defines effective swing.
