# Writing the contract report

{{panel:gsa-contract-calculator}}

A figure from a gas sales agreement means something only with the terms it rests on. The contract report writes those terms beside the figures, so a reader who did not run the engine can see what was computed, from what, and on which choices.

## What the report names

1. **The agreement.** Its parties (synthetic in this course), the delivery point and the term.
2. **The sources.** Every text applied, with its edition and the date it was read: in this course, 2026-09-26 for all of them.
3. **The quantities.** The DCQ, the heating value basis and reference conditions, the day count, MaxDCQ and the take-or-pay percentage.
4. **The make-up and carry-forward terms.** The recovery order, the make-up period, the end-of-term rule and any carry-forward period, base and cap.
5. **The price.** The formula with its index, averaging, lag, reset, floor, ceiling, S-curve and rounding, and the basis of the take-or-pay price: annual average or last month.
6. **The ledger.** Each year's Adjusted ACQ, take-or-pay quantity, deficiency and payment, make-up taken and expired, credit applied and damages, with the engine's reasons quoted whole.
7. **The money.** The royalty terrain and share, and the discount rate and base year beside every NPV.
8. **The readings and conventions.** Each stated reading the figures rest on, and each convention that moves them.
9. **Reported figures.** Any figure quoted only as reported, with its attribution.

## A figure with its terms

The rule behind the list is one sentence: a figure that depends on a term is quoted with it. A deficiency payment is quoted with its take-or-pay percentage and price. A make-up quantity is quoted with its order and period. A price is quoted with its formula, averaging, lag and reset. An NPV is quoted with its rate and base year. On the Ekene export feed (synthetic) that reads: the NPV of the seller revenue is 1,157,367,784.116539 at 0.1 to 2026, year-end flows, through the canonical npv, on the stated formula over a synthetic oil index.

## What a computed figure does not say

The report is honest about the limits of its figures. A deficiency payment is what the stated clauses produce on stated takes; it is no forecast of what a buyer will pay. An oil-indexed price follows the stated formula on the stated index series, which in this course is synthetic. An NPV depends on the discount rate and base year stated. A reopener month is reported and its outcome is not modelled. Saying so is what makes the figures usable.

## Where the figures come from

Every figure in the report is a return value the calculator printed at six decimals, and every reason is the engine's own, quoted whole. A figure a source prints to fewer decimals is quoted as the source's.
