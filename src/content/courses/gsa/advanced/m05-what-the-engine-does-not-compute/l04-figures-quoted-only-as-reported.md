Some figures cannot be read from the text that sets them. The domestic base price is the clearest case in this course, and its handling is a model for any figure that reaches a report second hand.

## Who sets the domestic base price

The Petroleum Industry Act 2021 gives the Authority the task, each year, under its Third Schedule:

> "determine the domestic base price under the Third Schedule to this" (PIA s.167(1))

The Third Schedule's first principle is that the price must bring gas to the domestic market voluntarily:

> "(a) the price must be of a level to bring forward sufficient natural gas supplies for the domestic market on a voluntary basis by the upstream petroleum industry ;" (PIA Third Schedule para 1(a))

So the law says who sets the price and on what principles, and prints no figure.

## How the course treats a reported figure

It is a stated input. The engine holds no default and refuses a domestic price call without one:

> domesticBasePrice must be stated in US$ per MMBtu: the Authority determines it each year (PIA s.167(1)) and the engine holds no default; got nothing

The course quotes each figure with its reporters and grades none of them. No capstone field uses the domestic base price.

## Figures that rest on it

The Ekene power plant (synthetic) prices its gas at the reported 2026 figure, held flat in every year of its term as a stated planning assumption. Its fixture note says so, verbatim:

> reported 2026 domestic base price (effective 1 April 2026) held flat as a planning assumption; the Authority re-determines it each year (PIA s.167(1))

Every money figure on that agreement rests on the assumption: the 2028 deficiency payment of 1501584.000000 and the NPV of the seller revenue of 81993119.091889 among them. A report that quotes either says, in the same sentence, that the price is a planning assumption resting on a reported figure.
