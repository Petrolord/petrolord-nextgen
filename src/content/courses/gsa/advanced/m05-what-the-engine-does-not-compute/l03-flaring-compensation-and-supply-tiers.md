# Flaring, compensation and the supply tiers

{{panel:gsa-contract-calculator}}

The Professional tier computed the Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation and its penalty. Around it the Petroleum Industry Act 2021 (Act No. 6; Official Gazette No. 142, Vol. 108, 27 August 2021) and the Domestic Gas Delivery Obligation Regulations 2022 (S.I. No. 74 of 2022; Official Gazette No. 206, Vol. 109, 23 November 2022; made and commenced 18 November 2022) carry rules the engine does not compute. Both were read on 2026-09-26.

## Flaring

The Act makes flaring or venting outside its listed exceptions an offence, and leaves the fine to regulations:

> "commits an offence under this Act and is liable to a fine as prescribed by the Commission in regulations under this Act." (PIA s.104(1))

It also lets the Commission take flare gas:

> "(2) The Commission shall have the right to take free of charge natural gas that is destined for flaring at the flare stack." (PIA s.105(2))

The Act prints no rate. The copy of the Gas Flaring, Venting and Methane Emissions (Prevention of Waste and Pollution) Regulations 2023 on the regulator's gazetted page is unnumbered and undated, so the course does not read a figure from it. The engine computes no flare penalty and this course prints no flare rate. Turning flare gas into value, and LPG and CNG, belong to the gas value course.

## Compensation to customers

Where a producer defaults on supply through the domestic gas aggregator, the Act requires compensation:

> "A producer-customer of the domestic gas aggregator shall pay compensation to customer-client for any loss suffered as a result of default to supply marketable natural gas" (PIA s.110(13))

The engine does not compute it.

## The 90-day investigation

The Regulations give the Commission a deadline to test a claimed excuse:

> "(b) Commission fails to investigate within 90 days, the lessee shall be deemed to have met the domestic gas delivery obligation" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.6(3)(b))

The engine applies the excuses as the caller states them and follows no timetable. Its basis says so, verbatim:

> the 90-day investigation rule of r.6(3) and the compensation to customer-clients of s.110(13) are not computed.

## The supply tiers and the supply curve

Before 1 March each year the Commission prescribes and allocates the obligation among lessees (s.110(1)(a)). The Third Schedule para 2 and regulations r.4 and r.5 set the tiers and a national supply curve.

> "The Commission shall identify on the supply curve the lowest level for the domestic gas supply price" (DGDO Regulations 2022 r.5(2))

The engine takes each lessee's obligation as a stated input and computes neither the allocation nor the curve.
