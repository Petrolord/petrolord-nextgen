# The single route quantities

{{panel:cq-fire}}

{{panel:cq-release}}

An engine and its oracle can agree perfectly and both be wrong. If the same mistake is typed into both, say a coefficient misread from the same page, the oracle confirms the engine and nobody notices. This lesson reads the engine's answer to that problem, which quantities it leaves exposed, and why this course grades only the ones something independent stands behind.

## The planted mistake

The engine's validation record plants the same mistake in both the engine and its oracle, then asks whether anything independent still catches it. Two kinds of evidence can. A second route derives the quantity another way, such as a numerical integral in place of a closed form. A published worked number checks the arithmetic against a figure someone else computed. Where either exists the planted mistake is caught. Where neither exists, it is not.

## What single route means

A single route quantity has only one path from its source to the number: the transcription from the book. The engine's formula for it may well be right. What is missing is any way to show it is right that does not pass through the same reading of the same page. A single route quantity is taught in this course, because an analyst needs it, and it never carries a graded answer.

Thomas in still air shows how narrow the line can be. The engine imports it from the facilities engine and restates nothing, and it sits beside Thomas with wind, which is graded. The difference is the evidence: the Yellow Book pool fire works its example with the wind form, so the wind form has a published worked number behind it and the still air form has none here. Two correlations from the same author land on opposite sides of the list.

## How the capstones stay on the graded side

Two quantities in that list sit in the middle of chains that the capstones do grade: the Bagster transmissivity inside a heat flux, and the TNT equivalence inside a blast. The course handles both the same way. Every capstone that needs a transmissivity STATES it, and every capstone that needs a TNT mass STATES it. The TNT equivalence is single route, so every graded blast states its TNT mass and grades what follows from it, which Kinney and Graham's published column does stand behind.

The toxic probits follow the same rule. The Purple Book coefficients have one worked case behind them, for carbon monoxide, so every graded toxic probit uses a Lees preset.

## Reading the list as a user

The list is also a guide to where your own work needs care. A figure resting on a single route quantity is exactly as good as the transcription behind it. Quote its source beside it, and where the choice matters, show it beside a figure from another method.
