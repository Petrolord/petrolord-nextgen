# What belongs to other courses

A consequence model sits in the middle of a larger body of safety engineering. Some neighbouring questions look so close to this course's that a learner could easily assume they are answered here. This lesson draws the three seams with the rest of the academy, so that a result from this engine is never mistaken for one of theirs.

## Point source heat radiation and setbacks

The facilities engines carry a point source model: the flame treated as a point that radiates a fraction of its heat release equally in every direction. From it come the flare and pool fire setbacks. The Facilities courses on relief and flare systems and on layout teach and grade them.

This engine does not re-expose that model, and its validation record carries a test that asserts it exports none of it. What this course adds is the solid flame model, which the facilities pool fire setback states it does not provide, and it grades only the solid flame. A setback distance and the heat radiation levels that define one belong to those courses, and none of their figures appears here.

## Frequencies and risk

A consequence model answers what happens if the release occurs. How often it occurs, and the risk a person or a population carries as a result, is the later quantitative risk course. Individual risk, the potential loss of life (PLL), the F-N curve for societal risk and the ALARP judgement that weighs them all belong there. This course never computes or grades any of them.

The academy's risk matrices belong to the risk and change course. A probability from a probit here is conditional on an exposure, and it becomes part of a risk figure only when the quantitative risk course combines it with frequencies and with the people present.

## Emissions and produced water

Routine emissions and their accounting, produced water treatment and tank vapour losses are other courses' subjects. This course models an accidental release and nothing routine.

## Why the seams are drawn so firmly

Each seam protects a graded answer. If a point source heat flux crept into a consequence note, a reader could confuse it with the solid flame figure this course grades. If a fatality probability were quoted as a risk, the frequency that turns one into the other would be missing without anyone noticing. Naming the owner of each question keeps each result in its own course, where its evidence lives.

## Exercise

Take a consequence note that ends with the sentence: "the fire gives an acceptable risk at the control room, which lies outside the flare setback." Rewrite that sentence so that it states only what this course computes, a solid flame heat flux and a thermal probit at the control room, and then add one sentence naming the course that owns each of the two claims you removed.
