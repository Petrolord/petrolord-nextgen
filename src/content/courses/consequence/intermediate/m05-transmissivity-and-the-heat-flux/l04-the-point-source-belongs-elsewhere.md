# The point source belongs to another course

A heat flux in W/m2 invites follow on questions. How far back should the next tank sit. How often will this fire happen. The academy answers each in a course built for it. This lesson draws the lines, so that a learner knows what this course grades and where the neighbouring questions are taught.

## The point source model lives in the Facilities courses

The facilities engines carry a point source model of heat radiation. It treats the flame as a single point that radiates a fraction of its heat release equally in every direction, and it underlies the flare and pool fire setback distances those engines compute. The Facilities courses on relief and flare systems, and on layout, teach and grade them, including the customary heat radiation levels a layout reads. This engine re-exposes none of it. Its validation record carries a test that asserts it exports no point source model and no setback distance.

## What this course adds

The facilities pool fire setback states that it does not provide the solid flame model. This course supplies exactly that: the cylinder of flame, its surface emissive power, its view factor and the heat flux they make. It grades only the solid flame. A capstone in this tier never asks for a point source heat flux or a setback, and no figure from the Facilities courses appears in its lessons.

## Frequencies, risk and matrices belong elsewhere

A consequence model answers what happens if the release occurs. How often it occurs, and the risk a person or a population carries as a result, belong to the later quantitative risk course. Individual risk, the potential loss of life (PLL), the F-N curve and the ALARP argument all belong there. This engine computes no frequency of any kind, and this course never computes or grades a risk figure. The academy's risk matrices, with their categories and scores, belong to the risk and change course. This course reports physical effects in physical units and places nothing on a risk matrix.

## Emissions and routine releases belong elsewhere too

Routine emissions and their accounting, produced water treatment and tank vapour losses are other courses' subjects.

## Why the lines matter

Each neighbouring course grades its own quantity with its own evidence behind it. A note that borrowed a point source figure would mix two bases in one number. Clean lines let each result carry its own basis block.

## Exercise

A manager reads ERHA's heat flux of 10412.965226 W/m2 at 40 m and asks four questions: what the heat flux would be at 60 m; how far back a new tank should sit by the Facilities layout rules; how often such a fire might occur; and whether the result belongs on the site's risk matrix. For each question, name the course that answers it and write one sentence saying why.
