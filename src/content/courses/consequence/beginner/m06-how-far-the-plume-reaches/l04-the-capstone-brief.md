# The capstone brief

{{panel:cq-release}}

The Associate capstone asks the tier's question on a facility of its own: how much gets out, and where does it go. It runs a release from a hole to a receptor downwind, one engine call at a time, and grades six fields. Every field is a quantity that a published worked number or a second route stands behind. The facility, its inputs and its answers appear only in the capstone.

## What the capstone asks

In general terms, the capstone gives you:

- **A liquid release** through a hole, with a stated coefficient, diameter, density, head and pressure above the liquid. You compute its mass rate, as in module two.
- **A gas release through one hole at two pressures**, one choked and one subsonic. You decide the regime from the pressure ratio and the heat capacity ratio, and compute each mass rate, as module three did.
- **A pool of stated thickness.** You compute its equivalent diameter from the volume and the thickness, as module four did.
- **A plume read at a receptor off the centreline and above the ground.** You compute the concentration with the release height, the receptor height and the crosswind distance all in play, as modules five and six did.
- **A distance to a concentration from an elevated release.** You find the root the brief asks for and read its state, as this module did.

## How to work it on the panel

Every quantity the capstone grades can be computed on the release explorer, with your own inputs typed in.

1. On the outflow view, type the liquid line's inputs and read the mass rate. Type every pressure as absolute.
2. On the same view, type the gas line at each pressure, and read the regime, the ratio, psi and the mass rate. Check the molar mass is in kg/mol.
3. On the pool view, type the volume and the stated thickness. A thickness replaces the bund.
4. On the plume view, type the release rate, the wind, the class, and the receptor position in all three directions, and read the concentration in mg/m3.
5. On the same view, type the target and read the state and the root the brief asks for.

## What is graded, and at what precision

Six fields are graded, each at the six decimals this course prints. Type your answers at that precision, and read the regime and the state beside each number. If the engine refuses an input, reread the brief: the capstone never asks for a refused input.

The single route quantities are left out. Mackay and Matsugu evaporation is taught in this tier and never graded, because nothing independent stands behind it.

## Exercise

Before you open the capstone, rehearse its steps on the teaching streams. Run the AMENAM liquid and gas lines, the 30 m3 spill at a stated thickness of 0.02 m, UBIT at 500 m with a crosswind offset of 20 m, and the stack's distance to 100 mg/m3. For each step, write down the panel view, the inputs you typed and the output field you read.
