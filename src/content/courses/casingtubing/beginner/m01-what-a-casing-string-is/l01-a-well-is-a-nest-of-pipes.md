# A well is a nest of pipes

What the string is for, and what has to be true of every joint in it.

{{panel:ct-rating-explorer}}

## The idea

A well is drilled in stages. Each stage is drilled to a depth, then lined with steel and cemented, and the next stage is drilled through the inside of that steel.

So a finished well is a nest: a wide pipe near surface, a narrower one inside it going deeper, a narrower one still, and finally the tubing the fluid actually flows through.

## What each string is there for

**Conductor and surface casing.** Hold the top of the hole up, carry the wellhead, and give the blowout preventer something to be bolted to.

**Intermediate casing.** Isolate a problem: a weak zone that would break down, a high pressure zone that would flow, a salt that would creep.

**Production casing.** Isolate the reservoir from everything above it for the life of the well.

**Tubing.** Carry the produced or injected fluid, inside the production casing and usually sealed to it by a packer.

## The four sizes this course uses

| outside diameter | typical role |
|---|---|
| 20 inch | surface |
| 13-3/8 inch | intermediate |
| 9-5/8 inch | production casing |
| 7 inch | liner or slim production casing |
| 3-1/2 inch | tubing |

The catalog in this course carries 22 casing rows and 6 tubing rows, 28 in total, and every one of them can be ordered in any of 10 steel grades.

## What has to be true of every joint

Four things, and each of them has its own formula.

1. It must not **burst** when the pressure inside exceeds the pressure outside.
2. It must not **collapse** when the pressure outside exceeds the pressure inside.
3. It must not **part in tension** under the weight of everything hanging below it.
4. It must not **yield** under all three of those acting together.

That is the whole of casing design. Everything else in this tier is how each of those four numbers is computed, and everything in the next tier is what to compare them against.

## What the string is NOT

It is not a pressure vessel with one rating. The same joint has a burst rating and a collapse rating that differ by a factor of two, and which of them matters depends entirely on what the well is doing at the time.
