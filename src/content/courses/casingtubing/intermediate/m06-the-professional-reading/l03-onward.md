# Onward

Where the string check stops and the tubing starts.

## What this tier gave you

A verdict per section per case, and the ability to say which check produced it and at what depth.

## What it did not give you

Anything about the string after the well is completed.

Every case in this tier is a drilling case or a test case. The gas kick, the pressure test, the evacuation, the cement job, the running of the string: all of them happen before the well makes anything.

## The Expert tier

The tubing, and the thing that makes it a different problem: it is not cemented.

A cemented casing cannot move. A tubing string can, and it does, every time the well is started up or shut in or stimulated. It gets longer when it warms and shorter when it cools, and at the bottom there is a packer that either lets it move or does not.

Three forces, a length change, a stroke limit and a buckling limit. And a result worth waiting for: the three published operating cases each hit a DIFFERENT one of those limits, so there is no single design case at all.

## Where else this goes

**Cementing** computes the real external column for the cementing case, with placement and friction rather than a static slurry density.

**Torque, Drag and Casing Wear** computes the wall this string will actually have when the next section is drilled through it, and every rating in this tier should be recomputed on it.

**Well Design and Surveys** provides the trajectory that sets the true vertical depths and the dogleg the triaxial term uses.

**Well Control** sets the shoe pressure the gas kick case is built on.

## One thing to keep

Compute the gap. Any time a report tells you that one check is the binding one, work out by how much, and perturb the input that would change the answer.

The finding in module 5 was true. It was also two thousandths deep, and knowing both of those things is the whole skill.
