# The regime along the string

The collapse regime is reported per section, and here it is the same one everywhere.

{{panel:ct-loadcase-explorer}}

## What is reported

Every section that has a positive collapse differential somewhere reports a collapseRegime along with its safety factor: the regime AT THE GOVERNING DEPTH, not an average and not a list.

## What the published string reports

Plastic. On both sections, on all four collapse-loaded cases, every time.

| case | section 1 | section 2 |
|---|---|---|
| full evacuation | plastic | plastic |
| partial evacuation | plastic | plastic |
| cementing | plastic | plastic |
| custom gradient | plastic | plastic |

## Why it is so uniform

Because both sections are 9-5/8 inch casing at 20.391949152542374 and 17.660550458715594, and the plastic regime at P-110 runs from 12.440631229717088 to 20.406388232261058 while at L-80 it runs from 13.38484007633007 to 22.471346692258702.

Both pipes sit inside their own plastic band, and the axial derating at these tensions is not enough to move either of them out.

## Why it is worth reporting anyway

Because the moment it is not uniform, you need to know.

A three-section string with a 13-3/8 inch tieback on top would report elastic there and plastic below, and the advice for the two sections would be different: thicken the top, upgrade the bottom.

## And because the regime can move under load

The collapse check is run at each depth with the axial stress AT THAT DEPTH, so the effective yield differs down the section and in principle the regime can differ too. What is reported is the regime at the worst point, which is where the advice is needed.

## The one regime you should be alarmed by

yield-exhausted. It means the axial stress at that depth reached the yield strength, so the collapse rating there is zero and the safety factor is zero with it.

If you ever see it, the string has an axial problem and the collapse number is a symptom rather than the disease.
