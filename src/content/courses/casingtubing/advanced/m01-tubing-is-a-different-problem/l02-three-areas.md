# Three areas

Ai, Ao and Ap, and the differences between them are what the pressures push on.

{{panel:ct-tubing-explorer}}

## The three

    Ai = pi/4 x (tubing inside diameter) squared
    Ao = pi/4 x (tubing outside diameter) squared
    Ap = pi/4 x (packer seal bore) squared

For this string: Ai is 0.0045360777821594625, Ao is 0.006207166618944346 and Ap is 0.008107319665559963, all in square metres.

## And a fourth

    A = Ao - Ai = 0.0016710888367848843

The steel cross-section. The pressures never act on it, and it is the only one of the four the thermal force uses.

## Why the seal bore is the biggest

Because the packer bore is larger than the tubing it seals into. The seals sit on a mandrel that passes through a bore of 4 inches while the tubing outside diameter is 3.5 inches.

That ordering, Ap greater than Ao greater than Ai, is the normal one and it decides the SIGN of the piston force. Reverse it, with a seal bore smaller than the tubing outside diameter, and the piston force changes direction.

## What each difference means physically

**Ap minus Ai.** The area over which the bore pressure acts upward on the packer assembly. Bore pressure pushing on that area tries to push the tubing UP out of the packer.

**Ap minus Ao.** The area over which the annulus pressure acts downward. Annulus pressure pushing on that area tries to push the tubing DOWN into the packer.

Those two areas are 0.0035712418834005005 and 0.0019001530466156167 square metres respectively, so the bore has nearly twice the leverage the annulus does on this geometry.

## The one to keep in your head

The seal bore is a PACKER property, not a tubing property. Change the packer for one with a 3.5 inch seal bore, leaving the tubing alone, and every piston force in this tier changes.

That is a real design lever and it is one of the few in a completion that is genuinely free: a smaller seal bore reduces the piston force at no cost in tubing.
