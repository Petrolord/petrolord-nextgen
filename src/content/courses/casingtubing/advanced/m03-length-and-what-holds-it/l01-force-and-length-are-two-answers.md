# Force and length are two answers

The same event, reported twice, and neither is derivable from the other.

{{panel:ct-tubing-explorer}}

## The two questions

**How hard does the string pull on the packer?** A force, in newtons, compared against the packer rating.

**How far does the string try to move?** A length, in metres, compared against the stroke the seal assembly allows.

They are different questions with different answers and different limits.

## Why not just one

Because a packer is one of two kinds of thing.

An ANCHORED packer holds the tubing and does not let it move. All of the length change is prevented, and the whole of it appears as force.

A packer with a SEAL ASSEMBLY lets the tubing move up and down inside a polished bore. The length change happens, no force is generated by it, and the only limit is that the seals must stay inside the bore.

Most real completions are somewhere in between, and this engine reports both numbers so that either reading is available.

## What the engine actually computes

Both, independently, from the same three mechanisms.

    forces:        pistonN, ballooningN, thermalN, totalN
    lengthChanges: pistonM, ballooningM, thermalM, totalM

The force set assumes complete restraint. The length set assumes complete freedom.

Neither is the answer for a real limited-motion packer, and the engine says so: the length changes are described in the code as planning forms, reported and not fed back.

## The signs are opposite

Production heating: total force minus 123684.94705447978 N, total length plus 0.8947604591459051 m.

Compression and elongation. Same event. The string wanted to get longer by 0.89 m and, if it had been fully anchored, would have pushed with 123.7 kN instead.

## What you cannot do

You cannot take the reported force, divide by stiffness, and get the reported length. They are not two views of one restraint condition, they are two different restraint conditions.

    force set:  what happens if NOTHING moves
    length set: what happens if EVERYTHING moves

A real answer lies between them and requires knowing the stiffness of the packer and the friction in the seals, neither of which this model carries.
