# Onward

Where this course ends and the well begins.

## What the three tiers gave you

A rating, for any joint in the catalog at any grade under any axial load.

A load case, seven of them, and a verdict per section at every depth.

A tubing force set, a length change, a stroke verdict and a buckling state.

## What they did not give you

A well.

Every number in this course is about steel. Nothing here decided where the casing shoe should go, what the mud weight should be, whether the cement will reach surface, or what the well will produce.

Casing design is a downstream activity. It takes a casing scheme and a set of pressures from elsewhere and converts them into a purchase order.

## Where the inputs come from

**Well Design and Surveys** gives the trajectory, and with it every true vertical depth and every dogleg in this course.

**Geomechanics and Wellbore Stability** gives the pore pressure and fracture gradient that set the casing seat depths and the gas kick shoe pressure.

**Well Control** gives the kick tolerance the gas kick case is a consequence of.

**Drilling Fluids and Hydraulics** gives the mud weights and the equivalent circulating densities that the load cases use.

**Torque, Drag and Casing Wear** gives the wear, which is the single biggest reason a real rating differs from a published one.

## Where the outputs go

**Cementing** takes the string and the annular geometry.

**Completion Design** takes the production casing and the tubing and builds the rest of the completion around them.

**Well Integrity and P and A** takes all of it and asks, twenty years later, whether the barriers are still there.

**Well Cost and Time** takes the purchase order.

## Three habits to keep

**Ask which regime.** Before saying anything about how to make a collapse rating bigger.

**Scan the profile.** Before quoting a safety factor for a section.

**Compute the gap.** Before reporting that one thing is the binding limit and another is not.

All three of those are the same habit in different clothes: the answer is only as strong as the thing that would have to change to make it different.
