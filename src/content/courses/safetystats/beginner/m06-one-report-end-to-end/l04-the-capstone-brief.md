# The capstone brief

{{panel:ss-rates-explorer}}

The Associate capstone hands you a workplace and asks for six numbers. Every one of them is a rate: a count times a named base over exposure hours. None of them needs an interval, a test or a chart. The capstone asks exactly this tier's question, what is the rate, and grades it with numbers the engine returns.

| a recordable rate for a workforce in parts | 200000 | sum then divide | KWALE pooled, 0.968312 |
| a lost time rate | 1000000 | the named base | UGHELLI lost time, 0.862575 |

| a severity rate | 200000 | days over hours, no time charges | UGHELLI, 8.280716 |

| a rolling twelve month recordable rate | 200000 | one sum then divide per window | AKASO window 1, 1.208038 |

## What each field asks of you

The recordable rate for a workforce in parts asks you to pool: add every part's recordables, add every part's hours, divide once. The mean of the parts' rates is a different number and is not what is asked for.

The lost time rate asks for the IOGP base. Read the base in the brief before you compute, because the same count on the OSHA base is five times smaller.

The observed FAR is fatalities over hours on 100,000,000, with no base to choose. The severity rate is days lost over hours on the base the brief names, with no time charges added. The Tier 1 PSE rate takes the Tier 1 count the brief states; you are not asked to classify anything, because the engine never does.

The rolling rate asks for one window, ending at a month the brief names. Add that window's counts and hours, and divide once. A month with no hours in the window adds nothing to either sum, and a short month counts for its own hours.

## How to work it

Each field has a worked twin in the table, taken from the teaching streams of this tier. Before you open the capstone, reproduce every twin by hand and then in the rates explorer, and check your answer against the figure in the table to the last printed digit. The arithmetic you use on the twins is the arithmetic the capstone grades.

When you work the capstone itself, write down for each field the count, the hours and the base before you compute anything. Most wrong answers at this tier come from the wrong base or from pooling the wrong way, and both are visible on paper before any division happens.

## Two things the capstone will not ask

It will not ask how sure you are of any rate. That is the Professional tier's question. It will not ask whether anything changed over time. That is the Expert tier's. A capstone answer that brings an interval or a limit into a rate at this tier has answered a question nobody asked.

## Exercise

Reproduce all six worked twins from the table without reading the answers first: KWALE's pooled rate from its three sites, UGHELLI's lost time rate on the IOGP base, the IOGP 2024 FAR, UGHELLI's severity rate, its Tier 1 PSE rate, and AKASO's first rolling window. Check each against the table, and for each one write down the base you used and why.
