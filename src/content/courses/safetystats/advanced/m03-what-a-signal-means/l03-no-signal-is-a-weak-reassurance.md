## Quiet is a statement about the evidence

A point inside its limits means the chart cannot distinguish that month from the ordinary process at that exposure. It does not mean the month was ordinary. Month 3 had 4 events on 0.486150 units, and on that little exposure the chart as drawn set its upper limit at 10.211920, far above the reading. A real rise in the rate, in a month that thin, could easily stay quiet.

A quiet chart can be a chart with too little exposure per point to see anything.

## The same lesson from the zero count

The Professional tier taught this for a single rate. The ABO crew worked 41300 hours with no recordables. The observed rate is 0, and yet at 95 percent the crew cannot rule out a true rate as high as 17.863823 per 200,000 hours. Zero is still a measurement: it bounds the rate from above, and on few hours the bound is high.

The Professional tier also printed how much exposure it takes for zero events to bring the 95 percent upper limit down to a target.

| target per 200,000 | hours needed, derived |
| --- | --- |
| 2.000000 | 368887.945411 |
| 1.000000 | 737775.890823 |
| 0.500000 | 1475551.781646 |

Month 3's 97230 hours sit far below even the first row. A month that thin cannot demonstrate much of anything, in either direction.

## What the note should say

A monitoring note that says "no signals this year" invites the reader to conclude the process was stable. The honest note says what the chart could have seen. Month 3 is the case to name. Its reading was the highest of the year, it did not signal, and the reason is its exposure. That sentence stops a reader treating the quiet as a clean result.

Where many points are thin, the remedy is a coarser grain. Charting by quarter pools the hours so each point carries more exposure, and the limits tighten. The cost is that a change shows up only once a quarter has closed. That trade is the analyst's to make and to state.

## Quiet is also good news, within limits

None of this makes a quiet chart worthless. Eleven of EGBEMA's months sit inside limits drawn around a pooled centre, and that is consistent with a stable process at the exposure recorded. The claim is only as strong as the exposure underneath it, and the note should put the two side by side.
