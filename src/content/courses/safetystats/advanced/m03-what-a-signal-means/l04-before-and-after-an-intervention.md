# Before and after an intervention

On EGBEMA an intervention went in at the start of month 7. With every month in, the after period recorded 35 events in 2389010 hours against 28 in 1965920 before, a rate ratio of 1.028627 with a 95 percent interval of 0.607952 to 1.755328 and a central p-value of 1.000000. Set month 8 aside and the after period holds 19 events in 1967920 hours, the ratio is 0.677882, the interval 0.357764 to 1.257771 and the p-value 0.241552.

| comparison | rate ratio, after over before | lower 95 | upper 95 | central p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| all months | 1.028627 | 0.607952 | 1.755328 | 1.000000 |
| month 8 set aside, valid only with a found cause | 0.677882 | 0.357764 | 1.257771 | 0.241552 |

## One month turns the ratio over

With month 8 in, the after period reads slightly worse than the before. With it out, the after period reads clearly better. One month turns the ratio from above 1 to below it.

Neither comparison is significant at 0.05. Both intervals include 1, and the data cannot say the intervention changed the rate in either direction. This is the Professional tier's comparison of two rates, the conditional exact test, used here on two stretches of one chart.

The p-value of 1.000000 is the engine's cap. The central p-value is twice the smaller tail, capped at 1, and here twice the smaller tail is a derived 1.015454.

## The month set aside falls after the intervention

Month 8 is the month the chart flags, and it falls after the intervention. That makes the second row a flattering comparison for the programme: removing the worst month from the after period can only improve the after rate. The rule from the revision lesson applies with extra force here. Month 8 may be set aside only if an investigation found an assignable cause for it, and that cause must be one the intervention did not produce. If month 8's excess came from the very work the intervention changed, it belongs in the after period.

Without a found cause the first row is the comparison, and it shows no change.

## What a before-and-after claim has to state

A before-and-after claim has to say which months it used and why. At minimum the note states the month the intervention started, the months in each period, any month set aside with the cause that justified it, the count and hours on each side, the rate ratio, its interval and its central p-value.

A reader given only "the after period's ratio is 0.677882" has been handed the favourable half of the table. A reader given both rows, with the reason month 8 was or was not set aside, can judge the claim.

## Why no difference found is a weak claim

The all-months interval runs from 0.607952 to 1.755328. The data are consistent with the intervention cutting the rate by a large margin and with it raising the rate by a large margin. A p-value above 0.05 says the data cannot tell, and it does not say the rates were equal. Six months a side may be too little exposure to see a modest effect.
