## The rule for setting a month aside

A month is set aside when an investigation has found an assignable cause for it: something specific that made that month different, identified and recorded. The chart is then redrawn on the rest, so the centre and the limits describe the process as it runs without that cause.

Setting a month aside needs a found cause. Setting it aside because it is high is removing the data that disagree.

The difference between the two is invisible on the redrawn chart. Both give the same centre of 2.389523. What separates them is the note beside the chart, which says what the cause was and how it was confirmed. A revised chart with no stated cause should be read as the drawn one with a month deleted.

## What moves when the month goes

The centre is sum then divide, so removing month 8 removes its 16 events and its 2.105450 units from the totals. The count loses a far larger share of its total than the units do, and the centre drops from 2.893273 to 2.389523. Every limit on the chart is measured from the centre, so every limit comes down with it.

That is why the redrawn chart has to be read again from the start. Tighter limits could put a month that was quiet on the drawn chart outside its revised limits, and the first chart's clean bill of health does not carry over. On EGBEMA the revised chart flags nothing, and that is a result the engine returned, which the note should quote. The panel's second view redraws the chart with any months you choose set aside, so that check costs a click.

## What revision is for

The revised centre is the better description of the ordinary process, if the cause was real. It is the line future months should be judged against, because it no longer carries a one-off that has been explained. A chart that keeps a known one-off in its centre has limits that are wider than the process deserves, and a later real change has more room to hide.

Revision is also a claim, and it has to be tracked as one. If the cause found for month 8 recurs, it was not a one-off, and the month belongs back in the centre.

## One cause, one month

Set aside only the months the cause explains. If the investigation finds that month 8's excess came from a single event with several injured workers, that is one month set aside. It is no licence to set aside other months that look high while the file is open.
