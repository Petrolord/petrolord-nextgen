## What the signal says

A signal says that something in that month may differ from the rest. It says that the month's reading is further from the centre than the chart expects from ordinary variation at that exposure. That is a statement about the numbers, and it is where the engine stops. There is no cause field in the result. The engine does not know what happened on site in month 8, and nothing in its output claims to.

A signal is a reason to go and look, and it carries no finding about a cause.

## The question it asks

The question is always the same: what was different about month 8? The answers that matter fall into a few families, and an investigation checks each one.

The work may have changed. A new scope, a turnaround, a campaign of heavy lifts or a new contractor on site all change the hazards.

The workforce may have changed. New starters, a crew change or a supervisor on leave all change how the same work is done.

The counting may have changed. A reclassification drive, a new register, a backlog of earlier cases entered in that month, or a single incident with several injured people all move the count without the usual link to the month's work.

The hours may be wrong. If month 8's 421090 hours are short, the rate is too high, and the chart has flagged a data error.

## What the investigation produces

The investigation ends with one of two outcomes. Either an assignable cause is found, a specific thing that was different about month 8 and explains the excess, or no cause is found and the month stays on the chart as part of the process. Both outcomes go in the monitoring note. The next lesson covers what happens to the chart when a cause is found. What never happens is that the month is removed because it was high.

Nor does a signal say whether an intervention worked. A high month after an intervention does not by itself show the intervention failed, and this module's fourth lesson tests exactly that.
