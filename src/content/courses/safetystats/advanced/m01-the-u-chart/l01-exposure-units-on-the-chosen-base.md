## What a unit is

The u-chart is the Expert tier's instrument, and it starts from the same arithmetic the Associate tier taught. A month's exposure units are its hours divided by the base, so n equals hours over 200,000. A unit is one base's worth of exposure. The month's u is its count divided by its units, and that is exactly the month's rate on the chosen base: count times base over hours, rearranged.

So u carries no new meaning. It is the rate you already know, written in the form the chart needs. What the chart adds is the pair of limits around each u, and those limits are built from n. That is why the chart keeps the units column visible. A month with 2.011750 units and a month with 0.486150 units are different amounts of evidence, and the chart has to know which is which.

## The base is still chosen

The u-chart takes a named base for the same reason every rate the engine returns names one. UGHELLI's 9 recordables read 0.776317 per 200,000 hours and 3.881586 per 1,000,000 hours, and the count and hours did not change. Only the base moved. Chart EGBEMA on the million hour base and every n shrinks and every u grows by the same factor, and the chart's shape and its signals do not change at all. The printed numbers do change, so a chart without its base named is a chart nobody can read against another.

EGBEMA is drawn on the 200,000 hour base throughout this module. Every u, every limit and the centre line are per 200,000 hours, and the note that reports them has to say so.

## Why the chart works in units

A monthly chart is tempting to read as twelve equal points. They are unequal. Month 3 had under a quarter of month 1's hours, so its u of 8.227913 rests on a thin slice of exposure. Converting hours into units puts that fact into the one column the limits are computed from, and the next three lessons follow it through the centre line and the limits.
