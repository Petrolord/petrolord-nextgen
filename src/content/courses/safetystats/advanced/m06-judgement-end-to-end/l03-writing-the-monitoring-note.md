## The shape the Expert tier grades

The note has five parts, in this order: the chart and its centre, the months that signal and what was found, whether the limits were revised and on which months, any before-and-after claim with the months it used, and every rate with its base and its interval.

The order matters. A reader who meets the before-and-after ratio first has no way to know whether a month was set aside to produce it. A reader who meets the chart first knows which months were unusual before any claim is made about them.

## What each part must say

The chart part names the base, the grain and the centre, and says that every lower limit was floored, so the chart could only find high months.

The signal part names each flagged month in the reader's numbering, with its u and its limit, and states what the investigation found. Where nothing was found, it says so, and the month stays.

The revision part says whether the chart was redrawn, which months were set aside and the cause for each. On EGBEMA the revised centre is 2.389523 and the revised chart flags nothing, and both belong in the note only if month 8's cause was found.

The before-and-after part gives the intervention month, the months on each side, any month set aside, the ratio, its interval and its central p-value. For EGBEMA with every month in, that is 1.028627, 0.607952 to 1.755328, and 1.000000.

## Every rate with its interval

A rate in a note carries its count, its hours, its base and its exact interval with the confidence stated. No P label is used for a confidence interval anywhere in this course. The interval is what tells a reader how much the rate can be leaned on.

Where a reader might meet a predicted FAR in the same document, write "observed FAR" for the one the note computed.
