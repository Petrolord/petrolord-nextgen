## Reading it in order

A year on a chart is read in a fixed order, so that nothing gets skipped because something else looked more interesting.

First the base and the grain. EGBEMA is drawn on 200,000 hours, month by month. Months range from 97230 hours in month 3 to 421090 in month 8.

Then the centre. The centre line of 2.893273 is pooled, sum then divide, and it sits below the mean of the monthly u of 3.202958 because the thin month 3 carries little weight in the pooled figure.

Then the limits. They move with each month's exposure, and every lower limit is floored at zero and flagged. So this chart could only ever find high months, and the note says that.

Then the signals. Month 8 is strictly above its limit and is the one signal. The engine lists it as index [7]. Month 3 is the highest reading and does not signal, because its exposure is thin.

## Then what was done about it

A signal asks a question, and the next part of the reading is the answer. Was a cause found for month 8? If one was found and recorded, the chart is redrawn without month 8 and the centre falls to 2.389523, with no month flagged on the revised chart. If none was found, month 8 stays in, and the drawn chart with its 2.893273 centre is the one that stands.

Then any intervention. The programme started in month 7. With every month in, the after period reads a rate ratio of 1.028627 over the before, with a 95 percent interval of 0.607952 to 1.755328. With month 8 set aside it reads 0.677882, and that row is only admissible if the cause for month 8 was found. Neither row is significant at 0.05.

## What the year says

Put in one sentence, EGBEMA's year says: one month signalled high, the rest are consistent with a stable process at the exposure recorded, the chart could not have detected a fall, and the intervention cannot be shown to have changed the rate. Each clause rests on a figure the chart printed. None of them rests on the month that looked worst to the eye.
