# The five year view

IOGP's own five-year observed FAR for 2020 to 2024 is 0.826095 by sum then divide. The mean of the five yearly rates is 0.833228. The yearly rates themselves run from 0.550271 in 2020 to 1.279566 in 2022.

| year | fatalities | hours | observed FAR that year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2020 | 14 | 2544201000 | 0.550271 |
| 2021 | 20 | 2679026000 | 0.746540 |
| 2022 | 33 | 2579000000 | 1.279566 |
| 2023 | 27 | 3291382000 | 0.820324 |
| 2024 | 32 | 4158877000 | 0.769438 |

## Quote the pooled figure

IOGP computes its five-year rolling average as the sum of fatalities over the sum of hours. The engine reproduces that exactly: run as a rolling window of 5 years over the five years, it returns 1 window with a rate of 0.826095, the same figure as the pooled rate, with a relative difference against the golden of 0.

So the figure to quote is 0.826095, IOGP's own method. The mean of the yearly rates, 0.833228, weights 2022's 2579000000 hours the same as 2024's 4158877000, and the Associate tier's rule applies: the pooled rate is the rate of the whole workforce, and the mean of rates is a different number.

## Why five years

A single year's observed FAR rests on a few dozen fatalities spread across billions of hours, and it moves. From 2022 to 2023 it fell from 1.279566 to 0.820324, and from 2023 to 2024 it fell again to 0.769438. The Professional tier printed the 2024 FAR with its interval: 0.769438, with a 95 percent interval of 0.526295 to 1.086218, on 32 fatalities. The 2023 figure of 0.820324 sits inside that interval, and so does 2021's 0.746540. The fall from 2023 to 2024 is smaller than 32 fatalities can resolve, and a headline that calls it an improvement is reading the count's own scatter.

Pooling five years puts more fatalities under one figure and steadies it. That is the purpose of the rolling rate the Associate tier taught, at industry scale.

## What the five year view hides

A five-year figure is steady because it averages. The price is that a real change shows up slowly, diluted by the years before it. The 2022 peak is visible in the yearly column and invisible in 0.826095. A company that sets its own performance against the five-year figure should read the yearly column too, and should know that the most recent year carries the largest share of the hours.

The five-year view also inherits everything the previous lesson said about a population figure. It is many companies pooled over many years, and the membership and its hours change from year to year: 2544201000 hours in 2020 and 4158877000 in 2024.
