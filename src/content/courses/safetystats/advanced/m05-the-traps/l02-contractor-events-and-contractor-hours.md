# Contractor events and contractor hours

{{panel:ss-rates-explorer}}

AMUKPE's company rate is 0.945403 per 200,000 hours, on 4 recordables in 846200 hours. The whole site, company and contractors together, reads 1.161259 on 13 recordables in 2238950 hours. The contractors worked 1392750 of those hours and recorded 9 of those events.

| workforce | recordables | hours | rate per 200,000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| company | 4 | 846200 | 0.945403 |
| company and contractors | 13 | 2238950 | 1.161259 |

## Which rate answers which question

Both rows are honest. The company rate answers how the company's own employees fared. The site rate answers how everyone exposed to the site's hazards fared. They are different questions, and a report has to say which one it is answering.

On AMUKPE the answers differ in a way that matters. The contractors worked more hours than the company did and recorded more than twice as many events. The site rate is higher than the company rate, which tells you the contractor workforce's rate is higher still. A report that quotes only the company figure has left out most of the site's exposure and most of its harm.

## The site rate is sum then divide

The combined figure is the Associate tier's pooled rate: the sum of the counts times the base over the sum of the hours. It is the rate of the whole workforce, and each group carries its share of the hours. Contractors worked most of AMUKPE's hours, so they carry most of the weight. The mean of the two groups' rates would weight the two groups equally whatever their hours, and it would be a different number again.

Pooling is also why the combined rate lies between the two groups' own rates. A pooled rate is a weighted average of the group rates, with hours as the weights, so it can never fall outside them. When a combined figure lands outside the range of its parts, something in the counting has gone wrong, and the previous lesson's mismatched row is exactly such a case.

## What a site report states

A site report states which workforce each rate covers, in words beside the number. "Recordable rate 1.161259 per 200,000 hours, company and contractor, 13 cases in 2238950 hours" is a complete statement. "TRIR 0.945403" is not, because a reader cannot tell whether contractors were left out of both halves, or one.

Many operators report both figures, and that is a good habit. The company figure shows how the operator's own people fared. The site figure shows the exposure the operator controls, and on a site where contractors do most of the work it is the figure that says the most about the site.

When the engine is given the site figure, it has no field that records whose workforce is in it. That is the caller's to record, in the note, every time.
