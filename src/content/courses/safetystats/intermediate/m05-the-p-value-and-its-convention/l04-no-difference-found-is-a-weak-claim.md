# No difference found is a weak claim

{{panel:ss-intervals-explorer}}

Two comparisons in which the engine finds no difference at 95 percent:

| comparison | rate ratio | 95 percent interval | central p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ERHA, east over west | 2.047326 | 0.621694 to 6.039396 | 0.256209 |
| UTOROGU, north over south | 3.445148 | 0.991404 to 12.408545 | 0.051759 |

Both p-values are above 0.05 and both intervals include 1. Both intervals also reach far above it.

## What the result says

On UTOROGU the rate-ratio interval runs from 0.991404 to 12.408545. It includes 1, so the data do not rule out equal rates. It also includes a north rate 12.408545 times the south one. Both statements are true of the same data, and a report that gives only the first has told half the story.

A p-value above 0.05 says the data cannot tell. It does not say the rates are equal. The difference between those two sentences is the difference between an investigation closed and an investigation that never had enough evidence to begin.

## Why it happens

A comparison finds nothing when the counts are too small to separate the two rates. UTOROGU has 13 events in all, ERHA has 17. The IMO ladder showed how wide an interval on a single count of that size is, and a ratio of two counts inherits both widths. With counts like these, even a large true difference will often go undetected.

So a finding of no difference usually says more about the counts than about the rates. Read the interval to see which: an interval from just below 1 to just above it is a strong statement of similarity, and an interval from below 1 to 12.408545 is a statement that the data are thin.

## The ERHA case in plain words

East's rate is 2.047326 times west's on the counts. The interval allows anything from 0.621694 to 6.039396. A manager who reads the p-value of 0.256209 as permission to treat the two sides alike has ignored the upper end of that interval, which says east could be six times worse. The honest summary is that east looks about twice as bad, and that the data are too few to confirm it or rule it out.

## How to write it

Write the comparison with its interval and say what the interval allows at each end. For UTOROGU: north's rate is 3.445148 times south's, with a 95 percent interval of 0.991404 to 12.408545, so the data cannot rule out equal rates and cannot rule out a north rate more than twelve times the south one. That sentence is longer than a p-value. It is also a form that no reader can mistake for a clean bill of health.

## What would settle it

More exposure. Both sides keep working and recording, and the counts grow. The interval narrows as they do, just as the ladder's did. A comparison that finds nothing today is a reason to keep counting and to compare again once the counts have grown.
