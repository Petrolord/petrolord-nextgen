# The economic limit

Every EUR you will ever book has a second number hiding inside it, and this lesson is about that number. The estimated ultimate recovery of a well is not the oil in the ground and it is not the area under the decline curve out to infinity. It is the volume produced from the start of the decline down to a chosen abandonment rate, and that rate is called the economic limit.

## What the limit represents

A well costs money to keep on production. Somebody pays for power, chemicals, water handling, maintenance, insurance and staff whether the well makes 100 barrels a day or 12. Revenue, on the other hand, scales with rate. As the decline carries the rate down, there comes a day when the revenue from the oil no longer covers the cost of producing it, and past that day every barrel loses money.

The economic limit is the rate at which revenue and operating cost break even. In its simplest form,

$$q_{limit} = \frac{\text{operating cost per day}}{\text{price per barrel} \times \text{net revenue fraction}}$$

Suppose a well costs 400 dollars a day to operate, oil sells for 50 dollars a barrel, and after royalty and taxes the operator keeps 80 percent of that. The limit is 400 divided by 40, which is 10 stb/d. Below 10 barrels a day this well pays to exist.

This course uses 10 stb/d as the working limit for every Ekene producer. Hold on to where it came from: not from the reservoir, not from the decline curve, but from a price, a cost and a fiscal split.

## Why EUR needs it

You might hope to sidestep the whole question by integrating the decline curve to infinity. For an exponential decline that integral exists, but for the harmonic model it diverges: the area under q = qi / (1 + Di t) grows without bound as t grows. A harmonic well produces an infinite volume on paper. On paper only, because long before infinity the rate has dropped below what anyone will pay to produce.

So the limit is not an accounting nicety bolted onto the physics. It is the thing that makes EUR a finite, meaningful number at all for fat-tailed declines, and it is part of the definition for every model. Module 5 writes every EUR as EUR at a stated limit, and the capstone grades EUR at the 10 stb/d limit, with the limit named in the field label.

## The limit moves

Because the limit is built from a price and a cost, it is not constant over the life of a field. Oil at 80 dollars instead of 50 drops the limit of the example well from 10 stb/d to 6.25 stb/d, and the well produces longer and books more. A jump in water handling cost pushes the limit up and cuts the booking. Two companies with different cost structures can hold the same well and book different EURs from the same decline curve, and both are right.

That is the sense in which EUR is a booking rather than a property of the reservoir. The reservoir holds what it holds. The decline model describes how the rate falls. The limit decides where you stop counting, and it belongs to the business, not to the rock. When module 5 shows you the same well booked at limits of 5, 10 and 20 stb/d, the differences you will see are entirely real and entirely commercial.

## One misconception to bury now

The commonest beginner error is to read EUR as the technically recoverable volume, or worse, as the oil in place. Ekene's volumetric oil in place is 12.14 million stock tank barrels, from the ReservoirCalc course. The four producers' EURs at the 10 stb/d limit sum to a few hundred thousand barrels for the primary declines. Those numbers are three orders of magnitude apart and both are correct, because they answer different questions. Oil in place asks what is there. EUR asks what this well, on this decline, produced and will produce down to this limit.

## Exercise

A well produces into a facility that costs press 300 dollars a day to run, allocated to this well. Oil nets 60 dollars a barrel after royalty. Compute the economic limit. Then recompute it for a year in which the oil price halves. State, in one sentence each, what happens to the well's EUR in the second year and whether the reservoir changed. You should get 5 stb/d, then 10 stb/d, and the reservoir did not change at all.
