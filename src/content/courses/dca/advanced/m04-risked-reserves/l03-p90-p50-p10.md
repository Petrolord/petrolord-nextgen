# P90, P50, P10

A distribution becomes a deliverable when you can read three numbers off it. This lesson inverts the cumulative distribution of the previous lesson, works the three standard quantiles on the Ekene field triangle by hand, and settles which convention the course uses everywhere.

## The convention, stated so it cannot drift

Petroleum reserves labels count **probability of exceedance**, not probability of being below.

- **P90** is the low case: a 90 percent chance of recovering at least this much. It is the 10th percentile of the distribution, so you find it by solving $F(x) = 0.10$.
- **P50** is the median: $F(x) = 0.50$.
- **P10** is the high case: a 10 percent chance of recovering at least this much. It is the 90th percentile, so you solve $F(x) = 0.90$.

The mnemonic that survives pressure is that the P number is the confidence, so a bigger P is a smaller volume. P90 is the number you are most confident about and least happy with.

## Inverting the CDF

Both branches of $F$ are quadratic in $x$, so both invert in one line each. Solve $F(x) = p$ on the lower branch:

$$\frac{(x-a)^2}{(b-a)(m-a)} = p \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad x = a + \sqrt{p\,(b-a)(m-a)}$$

and on the upper branch:

$$1 - \frac{(b-x)^2}{(b-a)(b-m)} = p \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad x = b - \sqrt{(1-p)(b-a)(b-m)}$$

**The branch rule.** Use the lower form when $p \le F(m) = (m-a)/(b-a)$, and the upper form otherwise. Getting this wrong is the most common arithmetic error in the whole topic, and it is silent: the wrong branch still returns a plausible-looking number inside the range. Compute $F(m)$ first, every time, before you compute a single quantile.

## Worked example: the Ekene field triangle

The inputs are $a = 380000$ stb, $m = 461709.132532792$ stb, $b = 580000$ stb, giving $b - a = 200000$, $m - a = 81709.1325327920$ and $b - m = 118290.867467208$. The threshold is $F(m) = 0.408545662663958$.

Apply the branch rule to the three targets. $p = 0.10$ is below 0.408545662663958, so P90 uses the **lower** branch. $p = 0.50$ and $p = 0.90$ are both above it, so P50 and P10 use the **upper** branch. Note the asymmetry that produces: two of your three headline numbers are governed by the maximum, not by the mode.

**P90**, at $p = 0.10$:

$$0.10 \times 200000 \times 81709.1325327920 = 1634182650.65584$$
$$\sqrt{1634182650.65584} = 40425.0250544862$$
$$\text{P90} = 380000 + 40425.0250544862 = 420425.025054486 \text{ stb}$$

**P50**, at $p = 0.50$ on the upper branch, where $1 - p = 0.50$:

$$0.50 \times 200000 \times 118290.867467208 = 11829086746.7208$$
$$\sqrt{11829086746.7208} = 108761.605112837$$
$$\text{P50} = 580000 - 108761.605112837 = 471238.394887162 \text{ stb}$$

**P10**, at $p = 0.90$ on the upper branch, where $1 - p = 0.10$:

$$0.10 \times 200000 \times 118290.867467208 = 2365817349.34416$$
$$\sqrt{2365817349.34416} = 48639.6684748587$$
$$\text{P10} = 580000 - 48639.6684748587 = 531360.331525141 \text{ stb}$$

A note on the last digit, in the spirit of the float honesty this course insists on. If you subtract the rounded square root 108761.605112837 from 580000 on a calculator you get 471238.394887163, one unit in the fifteenth figure above the value the full-precision path returns. The engine and the panel both carry the unrounded square root through, so the graded value is 471238.394887162. Round your reported number to the barrel and the question disappears, but know why the digit moved.

## Reading the result

Set the four summary numbers beside each other.

| Quantity | Value (stb) |
|---|---|
| P90 (low) | 420425.025054486 |
| Mode | 461709.132532792 |
| P50 (median) | 471238.394887162 |
| Mean | 473903.044177597 |
| P10 (high) | 531360.331525141 |

They are strictly ordered mode, then median, then mean, which is the signature of a right-skewed distribution. The median exceeds the mode by 9529.26235437050 stb and the mean exceeds the median by a further 2664.64929043484 stb. The P10 to P90 spread is 110935.306470655 stb, and the ratio P10 divided by P90 is 1.26386466042614, which is a tight range by reserves standards and reflects a deliberately restrained bracket rather than a well-constrained field.

## Stop and check it yourself

In the panel's triangular block, confirm the P90, P50 and P10 tiles against the three hand calculations above. Then run the inverse check in the other direction: the definition says $F(\text{P90}) = 0.10$ exactly, so put 420425.025054486 into the lower branch of $F$, using $(x - a)^2$ over 16341826506.5584, and confirm you get 0.100000000000000. A quantile that does not round-trip through its own CDF is a branch-rule error, and this is the fastest way to catch one.

## The misconceptions to retire

**"P50 is the most likely outcome."** The most likely outcome is the mode, 461709.132532792 stb. The P50 is 471238.394887162 stb. They differ by 9529.26235437050 stb here, and on a more skewed triangle they differ by much more. The median is the value with equal probability either side, which is a different idea from the value the density peaks at.

**"P50 is the average, so use it in economics."** The mean is 473903.044177597 stb, above the P50. An expected-value calculation wants the mean. A reserves booking wants the P50. Using one where the other belongs is a real and recurring error, and on this triangle it moves your answer by 2664.64929043484 stb.

**"P10 minus P90 is a two-sigma range."** The standard deviation of this triangle is 41051.8343295015 stb, so the P10 to P90 spread of 110935.306470655 stb is 2.70232276541495 standard deviations, where the same span on a normal distribution would be 2.56310313108924 standard deviations. Bounded distributions do not obey normal rules of thumb, and the triangular is as bounded as they come.
