# What ED is not

This module has produced impressive looking numbers. Half the oil displaced by a third of a pore volume. A ceiling of 0.6153846153846154. It is time to say, precisely and before the capstone, what these numbers are not. Displacement efficiency is one factor in field recovery, and treating it as the whole answer is the most expensive misreading this course can prevent.

## Three efficiencies, one recovery factor

The oil a waterflood actually recovers from a field is governed by a product of efficiencies:

$$RF = E_D \times E_A \times E_V$$

$E_D$ is displacement efficiency, everything this course has computed: within the rock the water actually sweeps, what fraction of the oil is pushed out. $E_A$ is areal sweep efficiency: seen from above, what fraction of the pattern area the injected water visits at all. $E_V$ is vertical sweep efficiency: of the layered section, what fraction of the thickness takes water. The last two live in map view and cross section, driven by well placement, pattern geometry, heterogeneity, and the mobility ratio's tendency to let water finger past oil.

Multiply honestly and the arithmetic is sobering. If the areal and vertical sweeps were each 0.7, plausible mid life numbers for a pattern flood, the field recovery factor built on Ekene's displacement ceiling would be 0.6153846153846154 times 0.49, roughly 0.30 of the oil in place. The one dimensional theory is the best behaved factor of the three, which is exactly why it is taught first and why its numbers must not be quoted alone.

## Where each factor is taught

The division of labour across the academy is deliberate. This course owns the one dimensional displacement: relative permeability, fractional flow, the Welge construction, and $E_D$. The Waterflood Management course owns the flood as a system: patterns, voidage balance, surveillance, and the sweep efficiencies. When that course computes what a pattern recovers, it imports this course's displacement mathematics and multiplies it by the geometry it owns. Nothing here is discarded; it is composed.

The same boundary explains a scoping choice you may have noticed: this course never once asked where the injector and producer are. In one dimension there is nowhere for them to be except the two ends. The moment well positions matter, you have left $E_D$ and entered $E_A$.

## Reading the model's own honesty

The engine behind this course states its assumptions plainly, and they are worth restating as limits on the numbers. The displacement is one dimensional and immiscible, capillary pressure is neglected in the flow equation, and the system is a single homogeneous rock with one relative permeability set. Under those assumptions the front is a clean shock, breakthrough is a single date, and the water fraction jumps from zero to 0.8682763300877854 in a day.

A real well does none of this so crisply. Layers with different permeability break through at different times, smearing the jump into a climb over months. The unswept fraction of the pattern keeps producing clean oil after first water. Capillary forces hold water back in tight streaks and imbibe it into others. None of that makes the shock model wrong; it makes it a component. The clean jump is what one layer, one dimension, one rock does, and the field's smeared response is a sum of such components weighted by the geometry the sweep efficiencies describe.

Even the breakthrough volume deserves its honest reading. The 0.33077027444818546 pore volumes is the injection needed for the front to cross the system once. In a field, pore volume means the pattern's swept pore volume, not the tank total, and the water that goes into unswept rock or out of pattern does not advance this clock. The number is a floor on real breakthrough, not a forecast of it.

## The misconception to avoid

Never present 0.6153846153846154, or any $E_D$ figure, as the expected recovery factor of the Ekene flood. It is the ceiling of one factor of three, valid inside swept rock only, after unbounded injection. Recovery factors for waterfloods live where the product of the three efficiencies puts them, and the product is always below its smallest factor. When a reserves reviewer sees a displacement efficiency quoted as a recovery factor, the whole submission loses credibility, because the error is never subtle: it typically doubles the answer.

## Worked example

An interview style question, worked in full. A screening study quotes Ekene at a 30 percent recovery factor and a colleague objects that your own course materials show 50.9 percent displaced at breakthrough already. Reconcile the two. The 0.5088773453049006 is $E_D$ at breakthrough inside swept rock. Suppose the pattern study carries areal sweep 0.75 and vertical sweep 0.78 at that stage of the flood. The field level recovery at breakthrough is then 0.5088773453049006 times 0.75 times 0.78, which is 0.2977, and the 30 percent quote is seen to be the composed number, not a contradiction. The two figures answer different questions, and both are needed.
