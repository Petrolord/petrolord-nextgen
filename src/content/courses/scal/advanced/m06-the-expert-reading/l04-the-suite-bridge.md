# The Suite bridge

You can now take a rock from the core lab to a design decision. Fit the exponents a lab table hides and say how certain you are. Carry a curve shape between rocks without smuggling the old endpoints along. Average a family of J curves and quote the result with its bias named. Price a dip, a rate change, and a polymer slug in efficiency at breakthrough, each on its stated case. And integrate a transition zone honestly enough to know what a flat booking left out.

That is the full working range of special core analysis as this platform practices it, and it is the second pillar of the reservoir path: material balance told you how much oil the tank holds, and this course told you what fraction of it the rock will let you move.

## The certificate and the bridge

Passing the Expert capstone issues the Expert certificate in SCAL and Displacement, verifiable by anyone through the public verification page. Because this course is mapped to the Reservoir module, the certificate also opens the Suite bridge: a 50 percent discount on the Petrolord Suite's Reservoir module, where SCAL Studio, the Fractional Flow Calculator, and the rest of the professional toolset run the same central engines you have been learning against.

That last clause is the point of the whole design. The panels in these lessons were not simulations of the product; they called the identical validated code the Suite ships. The numbers you learned to derive, and the provenance discipline you learned to attach to them, transfer to the professional tools without translation.

## Where the path goes next

The natural next course is Waterflood Management. It picks up displacement exactly where this course leaves it, and it does so structurally: the waterflood engine imports this course's displacement analysis as its one sanctioned cross-domain import. There is one Buckley-Leverett construction on the platform, you now own it, and the waterflood course builds the field-scale machinery around it: voidage replacement, pattern surveillance, Hall plots, injection response, and the difference between displacement efficiency, which you finished here, and sweep, which decides what the pattern actually recovers.

Beyond it sits simulation, where saturation functions stop being curves you study and become deck sections you QC. The kr table disciplines from this course, endpoints, monotonicity, the branch question, reappear there as the difference between a deck that runs and a deck that lies.

## Leaving

Work the capstone with the case discipline from lesson two, and keep the habit that this tier kept insisting on long after the arithmetic was easy: a number wearing its provenance is an answer, and a bare number is a liability, no matter how many digits it carries. That habit, more than any curve or constant, is what the certificate certifies.
