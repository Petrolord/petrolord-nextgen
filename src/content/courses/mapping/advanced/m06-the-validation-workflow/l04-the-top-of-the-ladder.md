# The top of the ladder

This is the last lesson of the Mapping ladder. There is no tier above the Expert tier, so it closes the app rather than pointing at the next rung.

## What three tiers have given you

**From the Associate tier, you can build a surface and read it honestly.** You can turn tops into control points, choose a frame and a cell size, grid with a thin-plate spline, mask at an extrapolation limit and know that the blanks are ignorance rather than absence. You know that the crest of that map is a spline overshoot 1.28 m past the shallowest pick, that live node counts are a setting rather than a fact, and that a coarser grid can carry a finer contour interval.

**From the Professional tier, you can combine surfaces.** You can derive one frame and reuse it, grid a second horizon on it, subtract them into an isochore, and know that the live set of a combination is the intersection of its inputs. You know that gridding the differences and differencing the grids are the same calculation because the spline is linear in its control values, that the map mean and the well mean answer different questions and differ by 1.09 m on this field, and that only the first belongs in a volume.

**From this tier, you can say what the map is worth.** On Ekene that means one cross-validatable well of six, a leave-one-out residual of $+9.84$ m at Ekene-6, a six-well prediction of 1543.33 m at Ekene-7 against a pick of 1549 m for a blind residual of $-5.67$ m, a crest that moves to 1540.71 m when the new well is added while the live count stays at 201, and a jackknife spread of 7.77 m at the prospect. You know why five of six wells cannot be cross validated, why two residuals of opposite sign rule out a correction, and why a mean over two residuals is the most misleading summary available.

That is a complete skill set for making and defending a map. There is no fourth move.

## What mapping can guarantee

It can guarantee that a surface honours every control point exactly and that every blank is a place the control does not reach.

It can guarantee reproducibility: a frame, a method, a cell size and a mask setting are enough for anyone to rebuild the same map from the same picks.

It can guarantee that combinations are node for node consistent, and that a derived map like an isochore covers only the intersection of what its inputs cover.

And, uniquely to this tier, it can guarantee a **measured** statement about predictive skill wherever the control geometry allows one, together with an honest count of how many such statements are available.

## What it cannot guarantee

It cannot make six wells resolve structure at a shorter wavelength than their spacing. Both residuals in this tier are that limitation measured twice, and no interpolator removes it.

It cannot tell you whether the picks are right. A map through six wrong picks is exactly as smooth, as well honoured and as convincing as a map through six right ones. That check belongs to well correlation.

It cannot make a validation available where the geometry forbids it. On a linear or perimeter well pattern the cross-validatable count is zero and no technique changes that.

It cannot turn a small sample into a statistic. Two residuals are two residuals however they are summarised.

And it cannot decide anything. It supplies a number and a range; the decision belongs to whoever owns the risk.

## Where these skills feed

**Into volumetrics**, which consumes the top surface, the isochore and the mask without rechecking them, and which will treat a mapped depth as an exact input unless you attach a range to it. The jackknife is the thing to attach.

**Into well planning**, where the choice between an interior and an exterior appraisal well is exactly the trade this tier quantified: interior buys validatability and local accuracy, exterior buys coverage.

**Into seismic interpretation**, where depth-converted picks become control points like any other, and where the cross-validatable count stops being one and starts being large enough for the statistics this tier had to decline.

**Back into well correlation**, because the residuals measured here are the strongest available argument for re-examining picks in the parts of a field where the map keeps being wrong.

## The three habits worth keeping

**Withhold something.** A map validated against its own control has not been validated. The whole tier follows from that one idea.

**Count what you can honestly test, and report the count.** One of six is a finding. Six of six on a six-well field is a mistake.

**Attach a range to every number that leaves your desk.** The jackknife costs six regridding runs and it is the difference between a depth somebody trusts too much and a depth somebody can reason with.

## Exercise

State what each of the three tiers can do that the tier below cannot, then give the single sentence you would use to justify a jackknife range to a colleague who thinks it is extra work.

As a self-check: the Associate tier builds and reads a single masked, contoured surface; the Professional tier grids a second surface on the same frame and combines them, and distinguishes an area-weighted mean from a well-weighted one; the Expert tier measures predictive skill by withholding control, and produces a per-location range for a depth that would otherwise be quoted as exact. The justification is that six regridding runs turn a number a reader will over-trust into a number they can reason with, and on this field that range was 7.77 m at the prospect and correctly contained the 4.49 m the answer actually moved when the next well was drilled.
