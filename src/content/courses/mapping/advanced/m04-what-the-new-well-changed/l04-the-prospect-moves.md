# The prospect moves

The crest moved by a metre. The prospect moved by four and a half. This lesson reads that change and works out what it costs.

## The two readings

| Control set | Depth at P-1 (1600, 1600) |
| --- | --- |
| Six wells | 1542.619873046875 m |
| Seven wells | 1547.105224609375 m |

The prospect moved **4.4854 m deeper** on the arrival of one well.

## Why it moved so much

P-1 is 141 m from Ekene-7. That is closer to the new control point than anything else on the map except the immediate neighbourhood of the well itself.

The change at Ekene-7's own node is 5.6729 m, the blind residual. P-1, 141 m away, took 4.4854 m of that, which is 79 percent. The influence of a new control point is strongest at its own location and falls off with distance, and 141 m on a field with 700 m well spacing is very close indeed.

So the prospect was sitting in the part of the map that the new well had the most authority over, and it inherited most of the correction.

## What it costs

Three consequences, in increasing order of seriousness.

**The prospect is deeper than mapped.** By 4.49 m, on a map whose entire structural relief is 49 m.

**Its position relative to the crest has changed completely.** On the six-well map P-1 was 2.90 m below the crest. On the seven-well map it is $1547.1052 - 1540.7056 = 6.40$ m below the crest. The prospect has gone from near-crestal to distinctly flank, and the mapped closure available above it has shrunk accordingly.

**Any contact-dependent volume changes.** At a 1560 m oil-water contact, the column at P-1 was $1560 - 1542.62 = 17.38$ m on the six-well map and is $1560 - 1547.11 = 12.89$ m on the seven-well map. That is a 26 percent reduction in column at the prospect location from one well.

## What did not change

The reservoir. No new picks were made at any existing well, no correlation changed, and nothing about the rock at P-1 is different from what it was.

What changed is the map's belief about it, and by an amount that would materially alter a ranking. That is the point of the module: the number that moved is a property of the interpolation, and the interpolation was never a measurement.

## The uncomfortable question

If one well moved P-1 by 4.49 m, how much would the next one move it?

Nothing in the six-well map answered that. Nothing in the seven-well map answers it either, on its own. It is the question module 5 exists for, and the answer turns out to be available from data already in hand.

## The comparison worth making

Put the three interior numbers this tier has produced side by side.

| Measurement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Leave-one-out residual at Ekene-6 | 9.84 m |
| Blind residual at Ekene-7 | 5.67 m |
| Movement of P-1 on adding one well | 4.49 m |

All three are between four and ten metres, and all three describe the same underlying fact: an interior location on this map is uncertain at the scale of several metres, whether you measure it by withholding a well, by drilling a new one, or by watching the map move when data arrives.

Three independent routes to the same order of magnitude is a much stronger statement than any one of them alone.

## Worked example

A second appraisal well is proposed at (1700, 1700), 141 m from P-1 on the other side. Before it is drilled, what can be said about how much it would move the prospect?

By analogy with Ekene-7, a well that close would take most of its own correction into P-1, so the movement would be roughly the size of that well's blind residual. The residuals measured so far are 5.67 m and 9.84 m, so a movement of several metres should be expected unless the new well confirms the map.

The useful part of that statement is its converse. If the map is right at (1700, 1700), P-1 barely moves and the drilling confirms it; if the map is wrong there by the amounts already demonstrated, P-1 moves by metres. Either outcome is informative, which is what makes an interior appraisal well a good use of money on a map this thinly controlled.
