# Reading structure

You now know what a contour is and how the interval was chosen. This lesson is the act itself: standing in front of the Ekene map and saying out loud what the structure does.

Read it in a fixed order. Range first, then where the shallow ground is, then which way it deepens and how fast, then what the map refuses to tell you. Doing it in that order stops you jumping straight to the prospect, which is the mistake that produces confident nonsense.

## The range

The mapped surface runs from a crest of 1539.72 m to a deepest mapped value of 1590 m. That is about 50 m of relief across the whole map, and the mean of the live nodes is 1550.27 m, which sits well up towards the shallow end of the range.

Note where the two extremes come from. The deepest value, 1590 m, is not an interpolated guess at all. It is Ekene-4's own pick, sitting at (2600, 2500) in the far northeast corner of the control. The map deepens to that number because a well measured it there. The crest of 1539.72 m has no such backing, and the next section of this course returns to that point.

## Where the shallow ground is

The shallow part of the surface sits in the middle and north of the mapped area. Two wells provide that control. Ekene-3 at (1400, 2300) picks 1541 m, and Ekene-6 at (1900, 1800) picks 1546 m. Between and around them the grid stays in the 1540s, so this is where the shallowest contour value in the fixture, 1540 m, has any chance of appearing at all. On a depth map the smallest values are the shallowest rock, so the middle and north of the Ekene map is the structural high of the field.

Ekene-1 at (1000, 1000) picking 1548 m and Ekene-5 at (600, 1900) picking 1552 m frame the western side. Neither is dramatic. They sit 7 to 11 m below Ekene-3 and hold the western flank down gently, so the map falls away slowly in that direction rather than plunging.

## Which way it deepens, and how fast

From that shallow core the surface deepens in every direction, but not evenly, and the asymmetry is the most useful thing on the map.

The strongest deepening is towards the northeast. Ekene-6 at 1546 m and Ekene-4 at 1590 m are 700 m apart in x and 700 m in y, so about 990 m apart on the ground, with 44 m of depth between them. That is roughly 44 m per kilometre, and it is the steepest gradient the control supports anywhere on the map. On the contoured display it shows as the closest spacing on the map, with the deep lines at 1570, 1580 and 1590 m crowded into the northeast, since Ekene-4 is the only well in the fixture deeper than 1565 m.

The deepening towards the south is much gentler. Ekene-6 to Ekene-2 at (2200, 1150) picking 1565 m is about 716 m on the ground for 19 m of depth, roughly 27 m per kilometre, a little over half the northeast gradient. The map falls away to the south as a broad, moderately dipping flank with widely spaced contours, not a sharp edge.

Put in a sentence: Ekene is a shallow area in the centre and north, held by Ekene-3 and Ekene-6, that falls gently west past Ekene-1 and Ekene-5, moderately south towards Ekene-2, and steeply northeast towards Ekene-4. That sentence is a complete and defensible reading of the structure, and you could say it in a meeting.

## What the map does not tell you

Now the discipline. The obvious next sentence, that Ekene has a closed structural high in the centre, is not one the map supports, and the reason is the mask.

The gridded surface is masked at 800 m from control. Nodes further than that from every well are dead and are not drawn, which is why only 201 of the 500 nodes in the frame carry a value. The mapped area is a blob around the six wells, not the full rectangle from 400 to 2800 in x and 800 to 2700 in y.

A closure is a statement that the surface turns over and comes back up on every side. To demonstrate one you need contours that close all the way round, and to have contours that close all the way round you need the surface to be mapped all the way round. On Ekene the 1540 m contour is drawn only where there are live nodes. Beyond 800 m from the nearest well the surface is not shallower, and it is not deeper. It is unknown, and the map correctly refuses to draw it.

So the honest reading is that the control is consistent with a high in the centre and north, that the high is open in the directions where the mask cuts the map off, and that whether it closes is a question the six wells cannot answer. Someone will want you to say the word closure anyway. The answer is that closing the structure needs either more wells or seismic coverage across the flanks, and until then the shape outside the mapped area is an assumption, not a result.

## Exercise

Without looking back at the numbers, write three sentences describing the Ekene structure: one on the range, one on where the shallow area is and which way it deepens, one on what the map cannot support. As a self-check, your answer should contain roughly this: the surface spans 1539.72 m to 1590 m, about 50 m of relief; the shallow area is central and northern around Ekene-3 at 1541 m and Ekene-6 at 1546 m, deepening steeply northeast to Ekene-4 at 1590 m and gently south to Ekene-2 at 1565 m; and the high cannot be called a closure because the map is masked at 800 m from control and the structure beyond that is unmapped.
