# Ahmed Example 10-10

Four lessons have built the pieces. This one runs the whole case end to end, against a published worked example with a printed answer at every stage, and leaves you with the four values the Professional capstone asks for.

The example is from Ahmed's Reservoir Engineering Handbook, fourth edition, chapter 10 on water influx, Example 10-10 at pages 726 to 729. Its footnote credits the data to Dake, so it is the same wedge reservoir and aquifer that appears elsewhere in the literature, here worked by Fetkovich's method with a step by step influx table printed in full.

## What you are given

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| aquifer thickness $h$ | 100 ft |
| porosity $\phi$ | 0.25 |
| total compressibility $c_t$ | 0.000007 per psi |
| initial pressure $p_i$ | 2740 psia |
| permeability $k$ | 200 md |
| encroachment angle $\theta$ | 140 degrees |
| water viscosity $\mu_w$ | 0.55 cp |
| reservoir radius $r_e$ | 9200 ft |
| aquifer radius $r_a$ | 46000 ft |
| dimensionless radius $r_{eD}$ | 5 |
| timestep $\Delta t$ | 365 days |

and a reservoir pressure history of 2740, 2500, 2290, 2109 and 1949 psia at one year intervals.

Everything else is computed. Note that no oil properties appear anywhere. This is a pure aquifer calculation: given a pressure history at the contact, how much water crossed it? What the reservoir does with that water is a separate question, and the material balance that consumes $W_e$ is a separate calculation.

## The pipeline

Six steps, in strict order, because each consumes the one before.

**1. Full circle pore volume.**

$$W_i = \frac{\pi \left( r_a^2 - r_e^2 \right) h \phi}{5.615} = 28413649389.1192 \ \text{bbl}$$

**2. Wedge share.** Multiply by $f = 140/360 = 0.388888888888889$:

$$W_{i,\text{wedge}} = 11049752540.2130 \ \text{bbl}$$

**3. Maximum expansible water.**

$$W_{ei} = c_t \, W_{i,\text{wedge}} \, p_i = 211934253.721285 \ \text{bbl}$$

Printed as 211.9 MMbbl. **Capstone value one.**

**4. Productivity index**, with the pseudo-steady-state denominator $\ln(5) - 0.75 = 0.859437912434100$:

$$J = \frac{0.00708 \times 200 \times 100 \times 0.388888888888889}{0.55 \times 0.859437912434100} = 116.496154838747 \ \text{bbl/d/psi}$$

Printed as 116.5. **Capstone value two.**

**5. Decay term.** With $J p_i / W_{ei} = 0.00150612493569796$ per day and $\Delta t = 365$ days:

$$1 - e^{-0.549735601529755} = 0.422897624804177$$

Printed as 0.4229. **Capstone value three.**

**6. March.** With the reduced constant $(W_{ei}/p_i) \times 0.422897624804177 = 32710.3987275100$ bbl per psi, step through the history using the midpoint reservoir pressures and updating the aquifer pressure after each step.

|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 3925247.84730120 | 3.92524784730120 |
| 2 | 13550357.4169008 | 13.5503574169008 |
| 3 | 25499913.9622649 | 25.4999139622649 |
| 4 | 37973154.4101719 | 37.9731544101719 |

with per-step increments of $3925247.84730120$, $9625109.56959957$, $11949556.5453642$ and $12473240.4479069$ bbl.

The final cumulative influx is $37.9731544101719$ MMbbl against the printed $37.971$, a relative difference of $0.00567383048077399$ percent. **Capstone value four.**

## How to read that agreement honestly

Five parts in a hundred thousand is a good match, but the number itself is not the point. The point is what the residual is made of.

The book rounds. It prints its aquifer pressures to whole psi, 2689 and 2565 and 2409, and then uses those rounded values to form the next drawdown, so its rounding compounds through the march. It prints $\Delta W_e$ to three or four significant figures. It prints $W_{ei}$ as 211.9 MMbbl and the decay term as 0.4229. Every one of those is a place where the printed chain and the engine's chain part company in the fourth figure and then walk on together.

That leads to a rule for comparing against any published table when you cannot see the author's intermediates: expect agreement to within about one unit in the last printed place, and do not assert anything tighter. A tight relative tolerance against a printed number is a tolerance on the author's rounding, not on the physics. Here every entry passes that test, including step 3, where the engine's $\Delta W_e$ of $11.9495565453642$ MMbbl sits against a printed 11.97 because the book entered that step from a rounded 2565 psia rather than from $2564.81369259386$.

## Where the aquifer ends up

After four years the aquifer has delivered $37973154.4101719$ bbl out of a maximum $211934253.721285$ bbl, which is $0.179174219095844$ of its capacity. Its average pressure has therefore fallen to

$$2740 \times (1 - 0.179174219095844) = 2249.06263967739 \ \text{psia}$$

a drop of $490.937360322614$ psi from its initial 2740. The reservoir over the same period fell from 2740 to 1949, a drop of 791 psi. The aquifer is following the reservoir down but lagging it, and the gap between the two is exactly the drawdown that keeps water moving. That lag is the whole physical content of the method.
