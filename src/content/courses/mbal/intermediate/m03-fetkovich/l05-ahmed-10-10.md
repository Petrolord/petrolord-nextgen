# Ahmed Example 10-10

Four lessons have built the pieces. This one lays out the published worked example the Professional capstone is set on, runs the whole pipeline end to end on the teaching aquifer, and leaves the published case for you to run.

The example is from Ahmed's Reservoir Engineering Handbook, fourth edition, chapter 10 on water influx, Example 10-10 at pages 726 to 729. Its footnote credits the data to Dake, so it is the same wedge reservoir and aquifer that appears elsewhere in the literature, here worked by Fetkovich's method with a step by step influx table printed in full.

## What you are given

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| aquifer thickness $h$ | 100 ft |
| porosity $\phi$ | 0.25 |
| total compressibility $c_t$ | 0.000007 per psi |
| initial pressure $p_i$ | 2740 psia |
| permeability $k$ | 200 md |
| encroachment angle $\theta$ | 140 degrees |
| water viscosity $\mu_w$ | 0.55 cp |
| reservoir radius $r_e$ | 9200 ft |
| aquifer radius $r_a$ | 46000 ft |
| dimensionless radius $r_{eD}$ | 5 |
| timestep $\Delta t$ | 365 days |

and a reservoir pressure history of 2740, 2500, 2290, 2109 and 1949 psia at one year intervals.

Everything else is computed. Note that no oil properties appear anywhere. This is a pure aquifer calculation: given a pressure history at the contact, how much water crossed it? What the reservoir does with that water is a separate question, and the material balance that consumes $W_e$ is a separate calculation.

## The pipeline, worked on the teaching aquifer

Six steps, in strict order, because each consumes the one before. The Professional capstone asks for four of these numbers on the published geometry above, so this lesson runs the pipeline end to end on the teaching aquifer of lessons 1 to 3 (thickness 80 ft, porosity 0.22, total compressibility 0.000006 per psi, initial pressure 2740 psia, permeability 150 md, encroachment angle 180 degrees, water viscosity 0.55 cp, reservoir radius 9200 ft, aquifer radius 36800 ft, $r_{eD}$ 4, timestep 365 days) over the same pressure history. Then you run it yourself on the book's inputs.

**1. Full circle pore volume.**

$$W_i = \frac{\pi \left( r_a^2 - r_e^2 \right) h \phi}{5.615} = 12502005731.212427 \ \text{bbl}$$

**2. Wedge share.** Multiply by $f = 180/360 = 0.5$:

$$W_{i,\text{wedge}} = 6251002865.606214 \ \text{bbl}$$

**3. Maximum expansible water.**

$$W_{ei} = c_t \, W_{i,\text{wedge}} \, p_i = 102766487.11056614 \ \text{bbl}$$

**4. Productivity index**, with the pseudo-steady-state denominator $\ln(4) - 0.75 = 0.6362943611198906$:

$$J = \frac{0.00708 \times 150 \times 80 \times 0.5}{0.55 \times 0.6362943611198906} = 121.38464263682319 \ \text{bbl/d/psi}$$

**5. Decay term.** With $J p_i / W_{ei} = 0.003236404495047678$ per day and $\Delta t = 365$ days:

$$1 - e^{-1.1812876406924024} = 0.6931166713789784$$

**6. March.** With the reduced constant $(W_{ei}/p_i) \times 0.6931166713789784 = 25996.045793936602$ bbl per psi, step through the history using the midpoint reservoir pressures and updating the aquifer pressure after each step.

## Reading a published table honestly

On the book's own geometry the engine reproduces Ahmed's printed influx column, and the residual is worth understanding before you trust it. The book rounds. It prints its aquifer pressures to whole psi and then uses those rounded values to form the next drawdown, so its rounding compounds through the march. It prints each $\Delta W_e$ to three or four significant figures, and it prints $W_{ei}$ and the decay term rounded too. Every one of those is a place where the printed chain and the engine's chain part company in the fourth figure and then walk on together.

That leads to a rule for comparing against any published table when you cannot see the author's intermediates: expect agreement to within about one unit in the last printed place, and do not assert anything tighter. A tight relative tolerance against a printed number is a tolerance on the author's rounding, not on the physics. Step 1 shows it cleanly: the book's first influx is the reduced constant times the 120 psi first drawdown, printed to four figures, and your full-precision product agrees with it to that fourth figure.

## Where the teaching aquifer ends up

After four years the teaching aquifer has delivered $23729965.552293256$ bbl out of a maximum $102766487.11056614$ bbl, which is $0.23091151813686364$ of its capacity. Its average pressure has therefore fallen to

$$2740 \times (1 - 0.23091151813686364) = 2107.3024403049935 \ \text{psia}$$

a drop of $632.6975596950065$ psi from its initial 2740. The reservoir over the same period fell from 2740 to 1949, a drop of 791 psi. The aquifer is following the reservoir down but lagging it, and the gap between the two is exactly the drawdown that keeps water moving. That lag is the whole physical content of the method.
