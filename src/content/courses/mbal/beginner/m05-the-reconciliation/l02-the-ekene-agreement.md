# The Ekene agreement

Here are the two numbers, side by side, at full precision.

| Route | Oil originally in place |
|---|---|
| Volumetric, from the map and the contact | 12139208.107496763 stb |
| Material balance, from the Havlena-Odeh slope | 12139208.1074968 stb |

The absolute gap is 7.45058059692383e-8 stb. The relative gap is 6.13761666407432e-15.

Take a moment with that. One stock tank barrel is 42 US gallons, which is 158.987294928 litres, so the disagreement between the two routes is 0.0118454765474796 millilitres. A quarter of a drop. Two methods that share no measurements, run over a reservoir of twelve million barrels, differ by less oil than clings to the inside of a pipette.

## Read it in the panel

Open the tank explorer and leave the aquifer selector on "None (the truth)". The other option belongs to the Professional tier and you have no use for it yet.

{{panel:mb-tank-explorer}}

Three things to look at, in order.

**The two tiles.** "OOIP from the slope" and "Geoscience volumetric STOIIP" sit next to each other deliberately. They are the two routes of lesson 1, and they read the same to every digit the tile shows. Nothing in the panel forces that. The volumetric tile comes from the field fixture, and the slope tile comes from running the survey history through the material balance engine.

**The F/Et column.** Scroll the survey table to the last column. It reads 12139208.1074970 at the first survey and 12139208.1074968 at the sixth, and every value in between sits in that same range. Across all six surveys the largest and smallest differ by 3.48314642906189e-7 stb, a relative spread of 2.86933579045473e-14. That constancy is the whole Havlena-Odeh idea made visible: if $F = N E_t$ with $N$ a constant, then $F/E_t$ is that constant at every survey, on every day, at every pressure.

**The plot.** Six points, a straight line, no curvature, an intercept of -6.11180439591408e-10 rb which is zero for any practical purpose, and an R-squared of 1.00000000000000. The line goes through the origin because at the first survey nothing had been produced and nothing had expanded, and a tank that has done nothing balances trivially.

## Why the agreement is this exact, honestly

You should be suspicious of a relative gap of 6.13761666407432e-15. Nothing in field engineering agrees to fifteen digits. So here is exactly what you are looking at.

The Ekene dynamic fixture is engine-consistent by construction. Its pressure history was not measured on a real field and it was not invented freehand. It was generated by taking the geoscience STOIIP of 12139208.107496763 stb, choosing a production schedule, and inverting the material balance equation to find the pressure at each date that makes the tank balance. The pressures are a consequence of the booking.

That means this reconciliation is a closed loop. Material balance recovers the number the pressures were built from, so what you are watching is a verification of the method and of the implementation, not a result from the field.

Do not let that word "verification" sound like a demotion. It is a strong statement, and here is what it actually certifies:

- The units are consistent from end to end. Reservoir barrels stay reservoir barrels, stock tank barrels stay stock tank barrels, and the two never get added together anywhere in the chain.
- The rock and connate water term is grouped correctly. Get the grouping wrong and the slope moves by tens of percent, as module 6 will show you.
- The regression recovers the physical constant it should, and not something close to it.
- Your own hand arithmetic can be checked against a known truth. When you work a survey by hand and land on 12139208.1074967, you know your method is right, because the target is exact.

And here is what it certifies about nothing at all: whether a real map is right, whether a real gauge was calibrated, whether the tank is a tank. The fixture cannot tell you those things because it was never asked to.

## What a real field looks like instead

On a producing field, two independent routes landing within 10 to 20 percent of one another is a good day. That is not sloppiness. Both routes are inferences built on measurements with real uncertainty, and a 15 percent spread between an inference from geometry and an inference from thermodynamics is a normal, healthy, publishable result.

The interesting work on a real field is not eliminating the gap. It is explaining it. A 15 percent gap with a physical story behind it is worth more to a reserves committee than a 2 percent gap nobody can account for, because the story tells them which number to use and what would change it. Lesson 3 is that story.

So carry two habits out of this lesson. On teaching data, expect exactness and use it to prove your method. On field data, expect a gap and spend your time on its direction and its cause.

## Worked example: one survey, by hand

You do not need the regression to get the answer here. Because $F/E_t$ is constant, any single survey gives you the oil in place. Take the third survey, on 2021-07-01, and read two numbers from the panel table: $F$ is 183689.695545334 rb and $E_t$ is 0.0151319339712031 rb/stb. Then

$$N = \frac{F}{E_t} = \frac{183689.695545334}{0.0151319339712031} = 12139208.1074967 \text{ stb}$$

Against the volumetric booking of 12139208.107496763 stb, that single survey has already reproduced the booking to eleven significant figures. Now do the same with the fifth survey, $F$ of 280421.174118849 rb and $E_t$ of 0.0231004503453291 rb/stb, and you get 12139208.1074969 stb. The last digit wobbles at the level of floating point arithmetic and nothing else does.

The reason to work it this way once is to see that the straight line is not doing anything mysterious. Regression is how you average six imperfect measurements when they scatter. When they do not scatter, one point is the answer and the other five are confirmation.
