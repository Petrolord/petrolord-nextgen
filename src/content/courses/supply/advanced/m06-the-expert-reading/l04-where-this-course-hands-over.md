# Where this course hands over

Terminals, Depots & Fuel Supply is one of three Commercial & Trading courses in the Midstream & Downstream module. The other two are `crude`, Crude Assay & Blending, and `refinery`, Refinery Feasibility & Planning. Each owns a stretch of the chain, and a learner should know where this course's stretch ends and what to take to the next one.

## What this course owns

This course owns measurement and logistics. Across its three tiers that means strapping, the volume correction form, free water, stock reconciliation, the Erlang C queue at a rack and at a forecourt, the tank farm, the lane, the fleet, landed cost, the pump price and the exchange rate at which a cap breaks. Every figure in it comes from two engine modules, `terminalDepot` and `fuelPricing`, and every rate in it is invented for this course.

## Where a cargo's quality comes from

This tier took the BADAGRY cargo's density, 742.8 kg/m3, as an input read off a certificate. It never asked where that density comes from, how a crude's assay predicts its products, or how a blend is made to meet a specification. Those questions belong to `crude`, which owns linear programming, assays and blending. A learner who needs to know why a cargo has the quality it has should go there. This course picks the cargo up once its quality is stated.

## Where a refinery's plan belongs

A refinery's plan, its schedule, the variance of its actuals against both, and the screen that tests whether a modular refinery is feasible belong to `refinery`. The line is drawn at the gate. A refinery's product leaving by truck is this course's lane: the cycle, the trips, the fleet and the cost of a litre delivered. The refinery's margin on that product is `refinery`'s.

## What no course here computes

None of the three Commercial & Trading courses teaches or computes a net present value, an internal rate of return, a Monte Carlo simulation or a decision tree. Those belong to the Economics courses. The one money figure in this course that could be mistaken for a valuation is the throughput margin at the IBAFO depot, 4988.00 USD for the period, on a fee of 7.80 USD a cubic metre that is invented like every rate here. It is a period's margin, the revenue less the variable and fixed costs of that period, and it is never a valuation. Nothing in this course discounts it or adds it up over years.

## What to carry forward

Three habits carry to any course that follows. Name the unit of every figure, because m3 at standard and gross observed m3 are different quantities, and dollars and naira are different currencies. Name the base of every percentage, because the base is part of the answer. And treat a missing input as missing: a floor is labelled as a floor, a refusal is quoted in the engine's words, and a figure that cannot come out wrong proves nothing.

## Exercise

Record the BADAGRY density and the landed cost at the depot gate in naira a litre, and the IBAFO throughput margin in US dollars for the period. For each, name which course owns the question behind it: where the density comes from, how the landed cost is built, and whether the margin is a valuation. Say what the margin's unit, read against the landed cost's unit, shows about why the two figures cannot be added or compared.
