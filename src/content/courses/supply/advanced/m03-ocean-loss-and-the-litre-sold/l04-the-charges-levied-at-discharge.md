# The charges levied at discharge

Two lines in the BADAGRY walk are charged where the cargo comes ashore: the jetty throughput and discharge line and the storage and handling line. Both are per m3. This lesson asks which cubic metres they are per, finds that the engine has only one answer, and teaches that answer as a stated limit of the engine. There is no figure in this lesson to compute with.

## What the engine bills them on

The engine levies both lines on the bill-of-lading volume. With the course's invented jetty rate of 1.2 and storage rate of 2.65 a cubic metre, the lines print:

- Jetty throughput and discharge, 54927.30 USD, on 45772.752 m3;
- Storage and handling, 121297.79 USD, on 45772.752 m3.

The volume that actually crosses the jetty and enters the storage tanks is the outturn. At the invented ocean loss of 0.45 percent, the outturn is 45566.774 m3.

So both discharge lines are levied on 45772.752 m3, and the volume discharged is 45566.774 m3. Those are the two quantities. This lesson quotes both and computes no line on the second, because the engine does not.

## Why this is held

Whether a terminal bills its jetty and storage charges on the bill-of-lading quantity or on the outturn is a term of the contract between the importer and the terminal. A contract can state either. The engine does not know which contract a cargo is under.

The engines repository records this as a held finding, H1. The final module of this tier reads it beside the other held finding. A held finding is a limit the engines repository states. It is taught as a limit, and it is never graded.

## What the limit means for a reader

It means two things, both about reading.

First, a landed total from this engine assumes the bill-of-lading basis for its per-m3 discharge lines. If the contract in front of you bills on the outturn, those two lines in the engine's build-up are levied on a volume your terminal does not bill on, and you should say so wherever you quote the total.

Second, the limit reaches every line charged per quantity. The engine charges each of them on the bill-of-lading quantity, the per-litre regulatory line included: 109854.60 USD on 45772751.75 bill-of-lading litres, and the port line 103700.00 USD on 34000.0000 bill-of-lading tonnes. H1 names the discharge charges, and the same contract question applies to every line charged per quantity. The percentage lines are charged on values, and the rule of the previous two lessons stands whatever the basis: the landed total is divided by the outturn to give the cost of a litre sold.

The sentence the course uses for this is the engine repository's own: whether a terminal bills on the bill of lading or on the outturn is a contract term the engine does not know.

## Exercise

Record the invented jetty and storage rates, the two discharge lines in US dollars, the bill-of-lading volume they are levied on and the outturn volume, each in m3. Then record the regulatory and port lines with the bill-of-lading quantities they are charged on. Then say what the two volumes, read side by side, show about the question the engine cannot answer, and why this course teaches that question as a limit and never as a figure.
