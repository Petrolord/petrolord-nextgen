# The water cut through the same table

{{panel:supply-tank-explorer}}

## Two heights, one table

The water cut is a height, and a height means nothing in cubic metres until a strapping table reads it. dipToStandardVolume reads the water cut through the SAME table as the dip. The water lies at the bottom of the same tank, so the calibration that turns the product level into a volume is the one that turns the water level into a volume too.

The engine then takes the water volume off the volume at the dip:

gross observed volume = volume at the dip less volume at the water cut

## AK-02 with the water cut swept

Here is AK-02 dipped at 5406 mm, with only the water cut changing:

| water mm | the engine answers |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | water 0.000 m3, gross 1086.941 m3 |
| 95 | water 19.101 m3, gross 1067.840 m3 |
| 400 | water 80.425 m3, gross 1006.516 m3 |
| 5406 | water 1086.941 m3, gross 0.000 m3 |

With no water the gross is the volume at the dip, 1086.941 m3. At the morning's 95 mm the water reads 19.101 m3 and the gross 1067.840 m3. At 400 mm the water reads 80.425 m3 and the gross 1006.516 m3. With the water standing at the dip itself, 5406 mm, the whole volume at the dip is water, 1086.941 m3, and the gross is 0.000 m3: the tank holds water and no product.

## The shortcut that reads the wrong volume

There is a tempting shortcut. Take the water height off the dip height first, and read the table once at the difference. This lesson measures it on two tanks.

On the bullet AK-03, dipped at 1847 mm with 41 mm of water, subtracting heights first gives a single reading at 1806 mm. That reads 51.126 m3, against the gross 52.161 m3 the engine returns. The reason is the curve: the water fills the narrow bottom of a curved tank, and a height taken off the top of the product column is a different slice of that tank.

On the vertical AK-01, dipped at 9318 mm with 212 mm of water, the same shortcut reads the table at 9106 mm and gives 3461.488 m3, against the gross 3461.489 m3. A vertical cylinder holds the same volume in every millimetre of height, which is why the shape matters to the shortcut.

The engine never takes the shortcut. It reads each height through the table where it was measured, on every tank whatever its shape.

## Why this is one of the course's rules

A water cut is converted through the table or it is not converted at all. The next lesson reads the cases the engine refuses.

In the panel, open AK-03 and turn on the shortcut reading beside the engine's figure. It is labelled as the reading the engine does not use. Move the water cut and watch the two figures.

## Exercise

Read the AK-03 and AK-01 comparisons: 51.126 m3 against 52.161 m3 on the bullet, and 3461.488 m3 against 3461.489 m3 on the vertical tank. Say which figure in each pair is the engine's gross observed volume, and what the shape of each tank has to do with the pair.

Self check: 52.161 m3 and 3461.489 m3 are the engine's gross volumes, read through the table at each height. The shortcut gives 51.126 m3 on AK-03 because the water fills the narrow bottom of a curved tank. On the vertical AK-01 the two readings are 3461.488 m3 and 3461.489 m3.
