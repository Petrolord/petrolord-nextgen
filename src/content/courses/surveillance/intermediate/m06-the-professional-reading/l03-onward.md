You can put two numbers side by side and report the difference. The next tier asks which reading of the rows produced each.

## What the Expert tier adds

**One period, two ratios, two severities.** `computeKpis` forms a period watercut and gas-oil ratio volumetrically. `detectExceptions` forms the same two as the mean of the daily ratios. On the published seam well the gas-oil ratio rise reads 70.033482142857 per cent one way and 42.737789203085 per cent the other, an overstatement of 19.122961825433 per cent, high by the first reading and medium by the second. The golden publishes that disagreement as a measurement rather than resolving it.

**A column that is computed on every point and read by nobody.** `detectExceptions` sets its rate key to the calendar volume. On OGUTA, invented for this course and neither real nor published, OGUTA-6 holds a producing-day oil rate of 512.000000000000 stb/d across its recent window while its mean calendar oil falls from 502.666666666667 stb to 307.504761904762 stb. The engine reports a drop of 38.825312618416 per cent on a well whose producing-day rate rose by 1.856763925729 per cent, and the downtime exception that would have named the cause does not fire, because the mean recent hours are 14.414285714286 h against a threshold of 12 h.

**The same allocation with the refused tests put back.** On the same 21 teaching days, `includeInvalidTests` moves the theoretical oil from 54713.920833333 stb to 61028.920833333 stb and the last-day oil factor from 1.190463125091 to 1.001040970439, on an unchanged meter.

**A decline read off a fit.** `annualEffectiveDecline` treats an unusable hyperbolic exponent as falsy, so a fit with a NaN, a null or an undefined exponent returns 42.160601062199 per cent, which is the exponential answer at the same nominal decline of 0.0015 per day.

**One rate handed to two modules that disagree about its phase.** `liftScreening` documents its target rate as liquid and `liftAdvisor` uses the same input as oil. At 300.000000000 bbl/d of oil and a water cut of 70.000000 per cent, the liquid rate is 1000.000000000000 bbl/d, and the rod pump scores 80 read one way and 40 read the other.

## Before you go

**Carry the reading with every ratio.** A watercut, a gas-oil ratio and a rate all have two defensible readings on one set of rows, and no returned object says which it took.

**Everything from this tier stays in force.** Windows still anchor on the data, a factor is still an output, and closure still proves only that one factor multiplied every share.
