# The Minister and the Commission

{{panel:farmout-earning-calculator}}

Two authorities give consent to an assignment, and which one depends on the licence. For a petroleum prospecting licence (PPL) or a petroleum mining lease (PML), the Minister consents on the Commission's recommendation. For a petroleum exploration licence (PEL), the Commission consents. This lesson reads each rule and the time limits the texts attach to them.

## A PPL or a PML: the Minister, on the Commission's recommendation

The Act gives the decision to the Minister and the recommendation to the Commission:

> "(2) The consent of the Minister under subsection (1) shall be granted upon the recommendation of the Commission." (PIA s.95(2))

The 2024 Regulations send the application through the Commission:

> "(2) Application for consent of the Minister under the Act and these Regulations shall be made to the Commission in the manner prescribed in these Regulations," (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 4(2))

The Act also sets a clock on the Minister. If no response arrives within 60 working days of the Commission's recommendation, consent is deemed granted:

> "(b) no response on the application has been received within 60 working days from the receipt of the recommendation of the Commission, the consent of the Minister under subsection (1) shall be deemed to have been granted." (PIA s.95(7)(b))

The Act lets the Minister set conditions too; one it names is that the transferee is a company incorporated in Nigeria (PIA s.95(11)(a)).

## A PEL: the Commission

An exploration licence is assigned with the Commission's own prior written consent:

> "(15) A holder of a petroleum exploration licence shall not assign, novate or transfer his licence or any right, power or interest without prior written consent of the Commission." (PIA s.95(15))

The Regulations give the Commission 60 working days to answer a PEL application in writing:

> "the Commission shall communicate the reason for the refusal or approval of an assignment of an interest in a PEL in writing to the applicant within 60 working days of the receipt of the application." (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 18(3))

## Why the difference reaches the engine

The gazetted fee of reg. 19(2) is the fee for the Minister's consent. A PEL's consent is the Commission's, so the engine will not apply the gazetted fee to a PEL. The Professional tier's fee view refuses it in these words:

> licence must be "PPL" or "PML" under basis "nuprc-2024-r19": reg. 19(2) sets the fee for the consent of the Minister, and a PEL assignment needs the consent of the Commission (reg. 16); state its rates under basis "stated"; got "PEL"
