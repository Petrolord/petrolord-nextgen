# A checklist for reading a farm-out

{{panel:farmout-earning-calculator}}

This tier has taken a farm-out apart one term at a time. A real reading puts the pieces back together, in an order that catches a misread term before it reaches a figure. The course's checklist below is that order. Each line names what to find, and every line is either a stated term of the deal or a figure the engine returns on those terms.

## The checklist

1. **The licence and its parties**, with each party's participating interest before the deal. Check that they sum to 100.
2. **The farmor and the farminee.** The farmor is a licence party; the farminee is a newcomer.
3. **Each event's terms**: the gross cost, the share paid, the participating interest earned and the cap.
4. **The vesting rule and the events completed.**
5. **The cash**: the cash bonus, and the past costs with the share reimbursed. A deal with none states each as 0.
6. **The consideration and the equivalent working interest**: what the farmor receives, and the heads-up share the farminee's money would buy.
7. **The participating interests after the deal**, which still sum to 100.
8. **The consent needed and the fee basis**: who consents for this licence type, and which basis the fee is computed on.
9. **Every source applied**, with its edition and the date it was read.

## Using the checklist

Lines 1 to 5 are inputs. Read them off the deal before you look at a single result, and check each against the box: a mistyped share or a missing term will either be refused by name or give you a different deal from the one on paper. Lines 6 and 7 are results. Read them from the tiles and the table, at six decimals, and check them against each other: the consideration is the carry, the bonus and the reimbursement; the participating interests after move only between the farmor and the farminee. Line 8 is read from the texts, since the earning calculator computes no consent; the Professional tier computes the fee. Line 9 is read from the Source block.

## What the checklist leaves to later tiers

A full reading of a deal adds its value to each side, the break-even promote, the effect of a cap and the timing of the fee. Those need the chance of success and the well costs, and the Professional tier adds them to this same list.
