# Sources and the dates they were read

{{panel:farmout-earning-calculator}}

A farm-out lives inside law and practice, and both change. This course therefore names every Act, regulation and guide it uses with its edition or gazette date and the date it was read. Every text below was read on 2026-09-27. A later amendment would need a later reading, and the date tells you which version the lessons describe.

From the Act the course reads the farm-out of s.94, the assignments and consent of s.95, the decommissioning plan of s.233(10), and the two tax sections that make assignment fees non-deductible. From the 2024 Regulations it reads the consent and the application, the rules for a petroleum exploration licence, the fee and the definitions. From HMRC's manual it reads what a farm in, an earn-in, a reimbursement and a development carry are. Each HMRC page carries its own date: OT30020 was updated 8 August 2019 and OT30021 on 2 February 2021, for example. The manual is United Kingdom tax guidance, and the course takes its descriptions of the trade and applies none of its tax rules.

## Three rules about quoting

**Only public texts are quoted, with their citation.** Every quotation in this course is checked against the text it came from.

**Licensed texts are taught by concept.** Model farm-out and operating agreements sold under licence carry ideas this course needs, such as a promote or a cap on a carry. The course teaches each idea from the public texts and the engine's stated arithmetic, and quotes no licensed form.

**The Penn State page is used for its figures alone.** Its licence is non-commercial and this course is sold, so no sentence of it appears here. Its printed payoffs and EMVs are cited as figures at a later tier, where the engine reproduces them.

## Where no text prints a schedule

No public text works a farm-in through to a table of payments. The engine's validation record says so in its own words:

> No public text prints a farm-in schedule or a break-even promote: those goldens come from the stated deal arithmetic in the oracle.

The payments, promotes and considerations in this tier are therefore the stated deal arithmetic, run by the engine on stated terms and checked by an independent oracle. They show what a set of terms produces. They are no survey of what deals in the market look like.

## The engine carries its citations

Every result the engine returns carries a `basis` block with its source, so the working can be printed with the text behind it. For an earning obligation the source line names HMRC's page OT30021 and the Act's s.94(8)(b) and s.95.
