A gazetted text is quoted as printed, including where it is untidy. The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum (Assignment of Interests) Regulations, 2024 (S.I. No. 67 of 2024, Official Gazette No. 61, Vol. 111, 9 April 2024, made 13 March 2024) and the texts around them were read on 2026-09-27. Three features of them reward a careful reader.

## A regulation numbered twice

The arrangement at the front of the Regulations lists the last four provisions like this:

> "22. General provisions 23. Revocation 24. Interpretation 25. Citation" (AOI Regulations 2024, the arrangement of regulations)

The body prints them differently. Its regulation 23 is on guidelines, and it numbers the citation 26:

> "23. The Commission may from time to time, make and publish guidelines" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 23)

> "26. These Regulations may be cited as the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum" (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 26 (the citation, as the body numbers it))

A citation of "reg. 25" follows the arrangement; the body prints no regulation of that number.

The gazette cover lists the instrument under a shorter title:

> "67 Nigeria Upstream Petroleum (Assignment of Interest) Regulations, 2024" (AOI Regulations 2024, the gazette cover (S.I. No. 67))

The course uses the name the citation gives.

## One value defined twice

Reg. 19(3) makes the value of the transaction either the amount payable to the assignor stated in the application or contract, or an amount the Commission prescribes from its metrics. Reg. 24 defines it again:

> "“Value of the Transaction” means the amount determined by the Commission to be the value receivable by the Assignor for an Assignment," (AOI Regulations 2024 reg. 24, "Value of the Transaction")

The first names two sources for the amount; the second names the Commission alone. The engine does not choose between them. It takes the amount and its source, "contract-amount" or "commission-determined", as stated inputs, and charges the gazetted rates on the amount stated.

## One party, five names

The engine says farmor and farminee. The Petroleum Industry Act 2021 (Official Gazette No. 142, Vol. 108, 27 August 2021) says "farmee" (PIA s.94(5)). HMRC's manual says Farmer Out and Farmer In (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT30020) and, on another page, calls the incoming party the increasing interest party:

> "The increasing interest party (farmer-in or farmee) undertaking a work programme at his own expense," (HMRC Oil Taxation Manual OT18320)

Each text's own word is quoted as it prints it. Outside a quotation the course uses the engine's names. The Act's own definition of a farm-out also sits in its marginal field section, s.94(8); the engine's arithmetic applies to any farm-out a caller states.
