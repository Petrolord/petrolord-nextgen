# What the engine leaves out

{{panel:farmout-valuation-calculator}}

An engine that is honest about its scope says what it does not compute and what it does in its place. This lesson lists what the farm-out engine leaves out, where each item would come from, and what stands in for it in a call. None of it is graded, and each is taught as a concept only.

## The list

| not computed | where it comes from | what the engine does in its place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| which consideration of a farm-out counts in the value of the transaction | AOI 2024 reg. 19(3) and reg. 24 | takes the amount and its source as stated inputs |
| the Commission's metrics for good and valuable consideration | AOI 2024 reg. 19(3)(b) | takes a Commission-determined amount as a stated input |
| the application fee | AOI 2024 reg. 19(1), set by other regulations of the Commission | nothing |
| the fee for a PEL assignment | the Commission's regulations (reg. 16 gives the consent to the Commission) | refuses a PEL under the gazetted basis; charges stated rates under basis "stated" |
| the administrative fines for an assignment without consent or a late beneficial ownership notice | AOI 2024 reg. 21 | nothing |
| a decommissioning and abandonment plan funded by the incoming parties | PIA s.233(10) | nothing; concept only |
| the tax on the deal (capital gains, hydrocarbon tax, companies income tax) | HMRC Oil Taxation Manual (UK); PIA s.264(f) and s.302(12)(c) for the fee | reports the fee as not tax deductible |
| royalty, net profit and other subordinated interests granted as consideration | HMRC OT18320 and OT30131 | nothing; a subordinated interest is valued outside |
| well costs discounted to their own dates | the valuation timing | costs at the valuation date |
| a market value for an interest | a transaction market | value per percent of stated figures and ratios of stated prices |
| when a back-in is triggered | the contract | the caller reports the event and states the target |

## In the engine's own words

Two of these the engine states in its bases, verbatim:

> the value of the transaction is a stated input: the sum payable to the Assignor stated in the application or contract, or an amount the Commission determines (reg. 19(3)); the engine does not decide which consideration of a farm-out counts

> transaction metrics are ratios of stated inputs (price, volumes, rates), reported only: no market value is asserted

## Decommissioning, as the Act states it

The Petroleum Industry Act 2021 requires a farm-out agreement to provide for decommissioning:

> "(10) Where the licensee or lessee is a party to a farm out agreement with one or more third parties, a decommissioning and abandonment plan funded in whole or in part by the applicable third parties shall be provided for in the applicable farm out agreement." (PIA s.233(10))

The engine computes no decommissioning share. A farminee's share of a future abandonment cost is a real liability, and a deal team values it outside the call and states it beside the engine's figures.

## Tax

The engine reports only that the consent fee is not tax deductible. The tax on the deal itself, for either side, depends on the regime; the Nigerian fiscal system belongs to the Petroleum Industry Act course. HMRC's manual is United Kingdom tax guidance, and the course quotes it for what a farm in, an earn-in, a reimbursement and a development carry are, applying none of its tax rules.

## Why the list matters

A figure from the engine is complete on its own terms and incomplete as a deal value. A farmor's EMV after the farm-out leaves out tax, decommissioning and the application fee. A report that quotes it says so, and names where each missing item would come from, so a reader knows which figures to add before comparing the deal with another.
