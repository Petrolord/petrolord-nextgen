# Who does not get a curve

Ekene has four producers. The Chan diagnostic returns two. This lesson is about the two it does not return, because an absent result is a result and reading it correctly is a skill.

## The two absences

**Ekene-5** never produces water at all. Zero wet months in the entire 36 month record. There is no water oil ratio to plot, so there is no series, so there is no classification.

**Ekene-1** produces water in 6 of the 36 months, starting in June 2025. The engine's minimum is ten points with a positive water oil ratio, so at six it is excluded.

Neither well appears in the results. Neither is marked "indeterminate" in the producers list; they simply are not in it.

## Reading Ekene-5's absence

Ekene-5 sits at the far west of the field, 1767.0597047072292 m from Ekene-2 and 2088.06130178211 m from Ekene-4. It is the furthest producer from both injectors. Under the allocation matrix it receives 8999.01708204866 barrels over the whole record, against Ekene-6's 92239.92509099872. About one tenth.

So the absence of water is exactly what the rest of the analysis predicts. It is not a puzzle; it is a confirmation. A well that is barely being flooded should not be seeing flood water, and the fact that it does not is one of the few places where the allocation matrix makes a falsifiable prediction that the data agrees with.

That is worth noting explicitly, because it is thin evidence for the matrix and it is the only evidence there is. If Ekene-5 HAD been making water on schedule, the matrix would have been in trouble.

## Reading Ekene-1's absence

Ekene-1 is a different case. It has water, it just started recently: six months at the end of the record.

Six months of water is genuinely not enough to classify. A log-log slope on the last forty percent of six points is a slope on two or three points, which is not a measurement. Excluding it is correct.

But the exclusion should not be read as "no information". The information is that Ekene-1's water arrival is RECENT, and that is a dated fact worth acting on. A well that has just started making water is the well whose next six months of data will be most informative, and it is worth putting a monitoring flag on it rather than waiting for it to cross a threshold and appear in a table.

## The difference between the two absences

This is the point of the lesson. Both wells return nothing, and they mean opposite things.

Ekene-5's absence means the flood has not reached it. The right response is to ask whether it ever will, and the pattern analysis says probably not on the current allocation.

Ekene-1's absence means the flood has reached it and there is not yet enough history. The right response is to wait and watch.

A results table that shows two wells and omits two others gives you neither of those readings. You have to go and look at why each is missing.

## The general habit

Whenever a diagnostic returns fewer rows than there are wells, enumerate the missing ones and classify each absence into one of three buckets:

**No data at all.** The measurement was never taken, or the phenomenon has not occurred. Ekene-5.

**Not enough data yet.** The phenomenon has started and the record is short. Ekene-1.

**Data excluded by a quality rule.** Present but rejected: bad dates, negative rates, a gauge failure.

The three call for different responses: change the surveillance, wait, or fix the data. Collapsing them into "not available" loses all of that.

## Why the engine does not fabricate

The alternative would be to classify Ekene-1 on six points and return a label. That label would print in the same table, in the same font, with the same wording as Ekene-6's twelve-times-better-supported one, and nothing in the output would distinguish them.

This is the same design rule the allocation lesson met: when the data cannot support a result, return the absence rather than a plausible substitute. Here the absence is expressed by the row simply not existing, which is the weakest form of it. A stronger design would return the well with a reason attached, and if you were extending this engine that would be a worthwhile change.

## The misconception to avoid

"Ekene-5 has no water problem." Ekene-5 has no water, which is a different statement. Its problem, if it has one, is that it is not being flooded at all: it receives a tenth of the injection that Ekene-6 does, from an injector two kilometres away, and its production is declining on primary depletion while the rest of the field is on flood support. Absence of a water problem is not evidence of health.
