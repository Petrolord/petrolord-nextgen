# When injectivity improves

A slope ratio at or below 0.8 raises the other alert:

> Hall slope down, improving injectivity (possible fracturing or thief-zone channeling).

Note the parenthesis. The engine's message for a well that has become easier to inject into names two causes and neither of them is good news. This lesson is about why.

## The three ways injectivity improves

**The well cleaned up.** Completion fluid displaced, near-wellbore damage removed by the first months of injection, or a deliberate stimulation. Benign, expected in the early months, and usually visible as curvature in the first third rather than a step.

**The formation parted.** Injection pressure exceeded the fracture pressure and the rock opened. The well now takes water into a fracture rather than through the matrix, so the resistance drops sharply. This is a step, not a trend, and it usually coincides with a rate increase or a pressure excursion in the operating record.

**A thief zone opened.** Water found a path into a high permeability streak or out of zone, through a channel behind casing, a fault, or a permeable interval the completion was meant to exclude. Resistance drops because the water is going somewhere easy.

## Why two of the three are bad

Because injectivity is not the objective. The objective is displacing oil, and both fracturing and thief-zone channelling make the well better at taking water and worse at doing anything useful with it.

A fracture that grows out of zone puts water into rock you are not producing. A fracture that grows toward a producer creates a direct path, and the water arrives having swept almost nothing. A thief zone does the same thing without needing the pressure.

So an improving-injectivity alert on a mature injector is often more serious than a declining one. Declining injectivity costs you rate, which is measurable and usually fixable. Improving injectivity may be costing you sweep, which is neither.

## The signature to look for

Three checks that separate the benign case from the other two.

**Timing.** Clean-up happens in the first months. A step improvement after two years of stable behaviour is not clean-up.

**Shape.** Clean-up is gradual and curved. Parting and channelling are steps.

**Correlation with pressure.** If the improvement coincides with the injection pressure reaching a maximum and then flattening while the rate rises, that is the classic parting signature: the well has found a pressure ceiling and is taking more water at it.

And one confirmation that is worth its cost: a water arrival response at a producer that is too fast for matrix displacement. ## Ekene has none of this

Both Ekene injectors run at a constant injectivity index of 0.5 until Ekene-4 degrades. Neither ever improves, and the above-reference run reports Ekene-2's ratio as exactly 1.

That absence is worth stating, because it means this lesson has no worked example from the fixture and you should treat the material as pattern recognition rather than as something you have seen demonstrated. The fixture teaches the declining case exactly; the improving case is here because you will meet it and the reflex it needs is counter-intuitive.

## What to do with the alert

**Do not increase the rate.** The natural response to a well that takes water easily is to give it more. If the cause is a fracture or a thief zone, that accelerates the damage.

**Find the date.** Cross-reference the operations log for a workover, a pressure excursion, a water source change, or a rate step.

**Check the producers.** A thief zone or a fracture toward a producer shows up as a water arrival, and the arrival's timing and its water oil ratio shape are the next module's subject.

**Consider a pressure test.** A step rate test can identify the parting pressure directly, and if the well is being operated above it the fix is operational rather than diagnostic.

## The misconception to avoid

"Improving injectivity means the well is performing better." It means the well is taking water more easily. Whether that is performance depends entirely on where the water goes, and the Hall plot cannot see where the water goes. A well can improve its injectivity index by a factor of three by fracturing into a thief zone, and every barrel it takes after that is wasted.
