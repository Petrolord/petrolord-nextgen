# Onward to Expert

Two tiers have measured this flood. Neither has asked the question that decides whether it was worth doing: how much of the reservoir is the water actually contacting.

## The gap

Everything so far is a volume balance or a diagnostic on a rate history. The Associate tier counts barrels in and out. This tier splits that count by geometry and reads two pressure diagnostics.

None of it contains a model of the displacement. Nothing so far can tell you what fraction of the rock the water has swept, what fraction of the oil in that rock it has displaced, or how much oil the flood will ultimately recover. Those questions need a model, and the Expert tier builds one.

## What it builds

**Vertical sweep.** A reservoir is not one permeability. Split the Ekene sand into five layers and the fast ones flood out long before the slow ones, so the fraction of the column that has been swept when water first breaks through is well below one. The Dykstra-Parsons and Stiles methods give that fraction, and the permeability variation that drives it.

**Areal sweep.** Even within a swept layer, water does not reach the whole pattern before it reaches the producer. Published five-spot correlations give the swept area at breakthrough as a function of mobility ratio, and its growth afterwards.

**Displacement efficiency.** How much of the oil is removed from the rock the water does pass through. This is the SCAL course's answer and the Expert tier imports it rather than re-deriving it.

The three multiply. A recovery factor is the product of a displacement efficiency, an areal sweep and a vertical sweep, and one of the tier's lessons is about which volume a recovery factor is "of", because changing that changes the headline without changing any arithmetic.

**A rate-time forecast.** Combining all of it into oil rate, water rate, water oil ratio and cumulative production against time, stopping at an economic water oil ratio limit.

## And then the part that matters

The forecast is a screening tool of the Craig monograph lineage, and screening tools are only worth anything if you check them.

The Expert tier checks. It runs the forecast on the Ekene pattern element at the injection rate the field actually used, and the answer is that the element never breaks through in a thirty year horizon. Not late: never. Every barrel injected is still displacing oil at the end of the run.

And yet Ekene-6 saw water 425 days after the flood started.

Reconciling those two facts is the strongest result in the course. Working backwards from the injection actually allocated to Ekene-6 by its breakthrough date, the pore volume that water could have contacted is 1.4697005138728763 percent of the pattern element. The water did not sweep a pattern. It found a path through one fiftieth of it.

That is a volumetric argument. It uses no curve shapes, no classifications, and no thresholds, and it is entirely independent of the Chan diagnostic that reached the same conclusion by a completely different route. Two independent methods agreeing is worth more than either of them alone, and it is the closest this course comes to proving something.

## What changes about the work again

The Associate tier had objective numbers. This tier had judgement in the inputs. The Expert tier has judgement in the MODEL: piston displacement, no crossflow between layers, equal porosity per layer, piston-like areal growth, constant injectivity, no interference between patterns.

The engines surface those as warnings rather than hiding them. When the forecast prints "vertical sweep applied as a constant multiplier on the flooded volume (screening simplification)", that is the tool telling you the edge of its own competence, and reading those warnings is part of the tier.

## Before you go on

Two things from this tier that the Expert tier assumes.

**Allocation is a hypothesis and it propagates.** The Expert tier's channelling result uses the allocation to decide how much water reached Ekene-6. A different matrix gives a different swept fraction. The conclusion is robust because the fraction would have to be wrong by a factor of twenty to change the story, but the dependence is real and should be stated.

**Two independent methods beat one good method.** Chan said channelling from the shape of a curve. The volumetric back-out says channelling from a volume. Neither would be conclusive alone. Look for that structure in your own work.
