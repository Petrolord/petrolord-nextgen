# Onward

Three tiers of waterflood management, from a monthly voidage ledger to a sweep forecast checked against the field. This last lesson is about what to carry forward.

## The three things this course actually taught

**Which question a number answers.** Cumulative VRR, rolling VRR and instantaneous VRR are three numbers. Field VRR and pattern VRR are two more. Recovery factor of the flooded volume and of the element differ by a factor of two. Reservoir fractional flow and surface water cut differ by the formation volume factors. In every case both numbers are correct and only one answers the question in front of you, and every serious error in this course came from that confusion rather than from arithmetic.

**Which conventions a number carries.** The pressure a formation volume factor was evaluated at. Whether the solution gas subtraction fired. Whether a Hall plot integrated absolute pressure or pressure above a reference. Which plotting position a Dykstra-Parsons fit used. Whether a rate series was summed as volumes or averaged as rates. None of these is discoverable from the answer, and the difference between a reproducible result and an anecdote is whether they travel with it.

**Where a tool stops.** The withheld pattern advice with a reason instead of an assumed split. The producers excluded from the Chan table for insufficient data. The warnings the forecast prints about piston layers and constant multipliers. A tool that says what it does not know is more useful than one that always returns a number, and the habit to copy is to make your own work behave the same way.

## The three findings on Ekene

The flood replaced its voidage, at a cumulative 1.034899536109, and the pressure recovered 34.4931292839633 psi from a trough that a six-monthly survey cadence could not see.

The field average hides two elements at 1.2024353717815623 and 0.6097477559533482, needing opposite corrections, and an injector that lost thirty percent of its injectivity without the volume data showing anything.

And the water reaching the nearest producer contacted at most 1.4697005138728763 percent of the pattern element on its way, which two independent methods agree on and neither of them proves.

## What to do next on a real field

The order that this course implies:

**Get the ledger right first.** Rows in, rows out, wells by name, conventions stated. Everything above it inherits its errors.

**Check the proxy against the measurement.** Voidage against pressure, whenever a survey arrives. A structural disagreement there is worth more than any refinement of the ledger.

**Split by geometry before drawing conclusions about wells.** And carry the allocation with every conclusion that depends on it.

**Look at pressure as well as volume.** The Hall analysis found a problem the entire volume record was blind to.

**Build a forecast in order to be surprised by it.** A screening forecast that agrees with the field tells you nothing. One that disagrees structurally tells you which assumption is violated, provided the assumptions were stated.

**Seek a second, independent method before acting.** And then, if both agree and the decision is expensive, seek a direct measurement.

## Where the rest of the subject lives

**Displacement physics** in SCAL and Displacement, which this course imports throughout.

**Volumetrics** in ReservoirCalc, which this course uses and never recomputes.

**Material balance** in Material Balance, which supplied the pressure inversion module 4 extended.

**Decline analysis** in Decline Curve Analysis, which is how the producers in this record were characterised before the flood arrived.

**Valuation** in Petroleum Economics. Nothing in this course is a discounted cash flow, and the rate-buys-time-not-oil result exists precisely to hand that decision across intact.

**And simulation**, for everything these analytical methods deliberately exclude: crossflow between layers, pattern interference, real three-dimensional geometry, and compositional effects. The analytical toolkit is not a poor substitute for a simulator; it is the thing you use to know what to ask the simulator, and to check its answer when it arrives.

## The last habit

Every number in this course was derived by running an engine on a committed dataset, and every number in these lessons could be reproduced by a reader with the same fixture. That is not a teaching convention; it is what makes a technical result a result.

When you produce a number, be able to say what produced it, what it is a fraction of, what convention it carries, and what would falsify it. If you cannot answer all four, the number is not finished.
