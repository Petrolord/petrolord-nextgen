# Onward to Professional

You can now read a waterflood's voidage ledger and defend every number in it. This lesson is a short map of what the next tier does with that, and why it is a different kind of work.

## The one fact that motivates everything next

Ekene's field cumulative VRR is 1.034899536109. Split that same record by which producers each injector actually supports, and it becomes two elements: one at 1.2024353717815623 and one at 0.6097477559533482.

Neither of those is visible from anything in this tier. The field number is not wrong, and it is not an approximation to either element. It is an average over a geometry that the ledger deliberately does not model, and averaging a flood over geometry is exactly the operation that hides a flood's problems.

## What the Professional tier builds

**Allocation.** A matrix saying what fraction of each injector's water supports each producer. It is judgement, informed by distance, streamline models, or capacity resistance modelling, and the engine never invents it: a pattern with no allocation gets no analysis, with a reason, rather than an even split silently assumed.

**Pattern ledgers.** The same voidage equation from this tier, applied to allocated subsets. Everything you learned transfers directly; only the inputs change.

**A conservation audit.** Every injected barrel is either allocated to a producer or booked as out-of-zone. Ekene's matrix leaves twelve percent out of zone, and that twelve percent is a reportable quantity, not an error.

**Injectivity.** Hall plot analysis on injection pressure. Ekene-4 loses thirty percent of its injectivity partway through the record and the volume data shows nothing. The pressure data shows it clearly, and only under one of two defensible pressure conventions, which is the lesson.

**Water arrival mechanism.** Chan diagnostics read the shape of the water oil ratio over time to distinguish orderly displacement from channelling.

**The limits of correlation.** Cross-correlating injector rates against producer rates looks like it should reveal which injector supports which producer. On Ekene it cannot, for a reason that is structural rather than statistical, and understanding why is worth more than the technique.

## What changes about the work

This tier had one honest answer to every question. The next tier has judgement in it. An allocation matrix is a hypothesis; a Chan classification is indicative; a lag table has known failure modes. The engines are built to say so, returning withheld results with reasons rather than plausible numbers, and printing the computed slope next to every classification label.

That change is not a loss of rigour. It is where reservoir engineering actually lives. The discipline is to keep the objective foundation, this tier, separate from the judgement built on it, so that when someone disagrees with your conclusion you can say precisely which layer they are disagreeing with.

## And then the Expert tier

Briefly, so you know where it goes. Sweep: how much of the rock the water actually contacts, down a layered column and across a pattern, and a rate-time forecast built from published correlations. Then the part that makes it worth doing, which is checking that forecast against what Ekene actually did, and finding that they disagree by a factor of about seventy.

## Before you go on

Two habits from this tier that the later ones assume.

**State your conventions with your number.** Which pressure the factors came from, whether gas voidage is counted, whether the solution gas subtraction fired. Three clauses, every time.

**Check the counts before the values.** Rows in, rows out, wells by name against the field's well list. A silently incomplete input produces a perfectly self-consistent wrong answer at every level above this one.
