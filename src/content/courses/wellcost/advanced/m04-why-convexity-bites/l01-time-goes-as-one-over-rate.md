# Time goes as one over rate

An estimate is written in rates, but a schedule is written in hours, and the map between the two bends.

{{panel:wc-risk-explorer}}

## The shape every rate driven activity has

Drilling is the interval divided by the rate of penetration. A round trip is twice the depth divided by the trip speed. Running casing is the depth divided by the run speed, plus a flat allowance.

In every one of those the rate sits in the denominator, so time is proportional to one over rate. That is not a modelling preference, it is what a rate means, and it is why hours plotted against rate of penetration give a hyperbola rather than a line.

Rate multiplied by time is the interval. On the 1,500 m intermediate section that product is 1,500 at every rate in the sweep, from 5 m/hr to 40 m/hr. The interval is fixed, so the two factors trade against each other, and they trade unevenly.

## Equal steps in rate are not equal steps in time

| Rate of penetration (m/hr) | Hours on 1,500 m | Hours relative to 15 m/hr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 300 | 3 |
| 10 | 150 | 1.5 |
| 15 | 100 | 1 |
| 20 | 75 | 0.75 |
| 25 | 60 | 0.6 |
| 30 | 50 | 0.5 |
| 40 | 37.5 | 0.375 |

Walk upward from the planned 15 m/hr in equal steps of 5 m/hr. The first step buys you 25 hours, the second buys 15, the third buys 10. Each identical improvement in rate is worth less than the one before it.

Now walk downward in the same steps. The first step from 15 to 10 costs 50 hours. The next, from 10 to 5, costs 150. Each identical disappointment in rate hurts more than the one before it.

Same size of step, same units, very different consequence. That asymmetry is the subject of this module.

## The part of an activity that does not respond

A casing activity is a running term plus a flat term, and only the running term sees the speed. At 2,000 m with 19 hours of flat time, raising the run speed from 100 m/hr to 1,000 m/hr takes the activity from 39 hours to 21 hours. Raising it to 5,000 m/hr only reaches 19.4 hours.

The flat 19 hours is a floor no rate improvement can touch, which is worth knowing before anybody promises time savings from a faster crew.
