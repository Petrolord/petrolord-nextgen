# Onward

This is the last lesson of the last course in the Drilling and Completions module.

## Where the estimate goes next

An estimate is not finished when it is approved. It is finished when it has been compared with what happened.

The first thing that follows is actual against estimate. The same activity structure that produced the plan can be filled with what the rig actually did, so a variance has an owner rather than a total. A well that came in over is not informative. A well whose intermediate section took far longer than planned while every cost line billed as expected is very informative.

The second is the offset well database. One well tells you almost nothing about a range. Ten wells in the same field, with their rates of penetration and their non-productive time honestly recorded, turn the ranges in this course from opinions into measurements.

The third is the learning curve. Across a campaign, days per well usually fall in a shape that can be fitted and forecast. The first well of a programme should not be costed like the sixth.

## The limit worth remembering

All of this improves the number. None of it improves the decision.

The best cost estimate in the world does not survive a decision to drill the wrong well. A perfectly built, correctly risked, honestly labelled estimate for a well in the wrong place is a precise account of money that should not have been spent.

Cost engineering earns its place by making the trade visible, not by making the figure smaller.
