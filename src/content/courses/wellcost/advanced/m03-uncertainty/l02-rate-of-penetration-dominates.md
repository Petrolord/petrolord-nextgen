# Rate of penetration dominates

Of everything you could put a distribution on, rate of penetration moves the answer most, for two reasons that compound each other.

{{panel:wc-risk-explorer}}

## It has the widest honest range

Compare the four uncertainties in the published risk case on the ground each one covers. The rig day rate runs from 85,000 to 130,000 USD around a mode of 100,000, and it is a contracted number. The completion services lump runs from 350,000 to 800,000 around 500,000.

Now the rates. The intermediate hole runs from 10 to 22 m per hour around a mode of 15. The production hole runs from 6 to 14 around a mode of 10, so the top of its range is more than twice the bottom.

Those are not pessimistic ranges. They are what offset data looks like when you plot achieved rates by section.

## It enters every drilling activity

The golden programme has three drill activities and they hold 220 of the 384 productive hours. Every one of those hours came from an interval divided by a rate.

Rates do not stop there. The two round trips and the three casing runs are metres divided by a speed, so five more of the ten activities are rate driven. Only the rig move and the completion carry an hours figure that no rate produced.

## The shape of what it does

Hold an interval at 1,500 m and vary the rate. That is the a4 interval exactly, from 500 m to 2,000 m, and at its mode of 15 m per hour it is the 100 productive hours the programme carries.

| ROP m/hr | Hours | Hours relative to 15 m/hr |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 300 | 3 |
| 10 | 150 | 1.5 |
| 15 | 100 | 1 |
| 20 | 75 | 0.75 |
| 25 | 60 | 0.6 |
| 30 | 50 | 0.5 |
| 40 | 37.5 | 0.375 |

Look at the product of rate and hours in every row: 1,500 in each, because the hours are the interval divided by the rate and nothing else.

Now read the two directions away from 15 m per hour. Dropping to 5 triples the hours to 300. Going the other way, 30 m per hour halves them to 50, and even 40 m per hour, well outside anything the a4 range allows, only reaches 37.5. The downside has no floor and the upside runs into one.

That asymmetry is why a symmetric range on a rate does not produce a symmetric range on cost. A later module takes that apart properly.

## Exercise

Using the table, state how many hours the 1,500 m interval takes at 10 and at 20 m per hour, both of which sit inside the a4 range, and how far each is from the 100 hours at the mode.

Then convert each of those two differences into money at 160,000 USD per elapsed day, and say which of the two is larger.
