# Why that is the right choice

Excluding the provision from the curve is a decision somebody made, and it is worth being able to defend it rather than merely report it.

{{panel:wc-risk-explorer}}

## What a curve is for

A cost time curve is a statement of what you expect to spend as the well is drilled. Every point on it is a claim that by this hour, this much will have been committed for work actually done.

A provision for events that have not happened does not satisfy that claim. Putting the 538,000 USD onto the curve would assert that some part of it becomes owed at some hour, and no such hour exists.

## Every accrual rule you could pick is wrong

Spread the contingency evenly through the 432 hours and you assert that trouble arrives at a constant rate. It does not. Trouble is lumpy, and a smooth provision line is a picture of a well nobody has ever drilled.

Put it all at spud and you assert the money is already gone, which makes the plan line unreachable from hour zero.

Put it all at the end and you assert that trouble arrives at handover, which is the one place it usually does not.

There is no defensible answer because the question is malformed. The provision is not a function of elapsed time.

## What it would cost you in the field

The practical damage is worse than the philosophical damage. If contingency accrued, a well running exactly to programme would sit below the plan line at every hour of its life, by a growing amount, and finish 538,000 USD under.

A crew that sees the line beaten every single day stops reading the line. You would have built a chart that is wrong when the well is right, which is the fastest way to make an operations chart ignored.

## The convention it keeps faith with

The AFE convention holds contingency as its own line, visible and separate. On the golden case it is 538,000 USD, and it ranks fourth among the nine lines, behind the rig dayrate at 1,800,000, the services spread at 1,080,000 and casing and accessories at 800,000.

A line that large deserves to be looked at directly. Smearing it into an accrual hides it in a slope.

And nothing is lost. Because the identity is exact, the accruing view is one subtraction away for anyone who wants it. What the design refuses is to make that view the default.
