# What a contingency percentage buys

A contingency fraction is an input. The confidence it delivers is an output, and nobody can quote the second by looking at the first.

## The arithmetic you already know

Contingency in this engine is a fraction of the base subtotal, carried as its own line. At the golden's fraction of 0.1 on a base of 5,380,000 USD it is 538,000 USD, and the total for approval is 5,918,000.

The Professional tier established the two traps in that arithmetic, and they are worth restating because they set up the point of this lesson.

A fraction of the base is not a share of the total. A fraction f is f over one plus f of the total, so 0.1 of the base is 0.09090909090909091 of the total, not a tenth of it.

And contingency is a real line that competes with contracted lines. At the golden fraction it ranks fourth among the nine lines, at 0.29888888888888887 of the largest. It passes the largest real line, the rig dayrate at 1,800,000 USD, at a fraction of 0.3345724907063197, and it passes the second largest at 0.20074349442379183.

## What the table cannot tell you

Read down that table and you know exactly what each fraction costs. You know nothing whatever about what any of them buys.

Ten per cent is not a confidence level. It is not a probability. It is a number somebody chose, usually because the last well used it, and it carries no information about whether the well in front of you is more or less uncertain than that one.

Two wells with the same base and the same ten per cent can occupy completely different percentiles, because the percentile depends on the spread of the distribution and the fraction knows nothing about the spread.

## The only meaningful statement

A contingency percentage becomes meaningful the moment you convert it into the percentile it reaches.

Take the total at your chosen fraction, locate it in the sampled distribution, and say what fraction of realisations it covers, in a stated convention. That sentence is defensible. The bare percentage is not.

It also works in reverse, and that is the better habit. Decide the confidence the business wants to fund, read the cost at that percentile, and derive the fraction from it. Then the number has a reason rather than a precedent.
