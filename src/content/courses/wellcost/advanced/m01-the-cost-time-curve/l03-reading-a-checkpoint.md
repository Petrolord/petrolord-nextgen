# Reading a checkpoint

A single point on the curve is a three way statement about the well, and knowing which three things it fixes is what makes it useful at three in the morning.

{{panel:wc-risk-explorer}}

## The published checkpoint

The golden case publishes one checkpoint: elapsed hour 211.5, cumulative 2,260,000 USD. It sits immediately after a6, the run and cement of the 13-3/8in casing. Reproducing it from the programme and the cost lines returns the same figure, absolute error 0 USD.

## What it fixes

It fixes depth. At that boundary the hole has been drilled to 2,000 m of the 3,000 m total depth, and the per metre line has billed on that and nothing more.

It fixes time. 211.5 hours of the 432 scheduled hours have gone, non-productive allowance included, and the per day lines have billed on all of them.

It fixes money committed against the base. 2,260,000 USD of the 5,380,000 USD base. Not against the total. The 538,000 USD contingency is untouched at this checkpoint and at every other one, because it never appears on the curve at all.

## What is behind you and what is ahead

Two lumps have landed by hour 211.5: the 250,000 USD wellhead at the end of a3 and the 300,000 USD cementing services at the end of a6. Three are still ahead: 200,000 USD of wireline logging, 800,000 USD of casing and accessories, and 500,000 USD of completion services.

That matters for how you read the position. A curve that looks comfortable at a checkpoint can still have most of its tangible spend in front of it.

Look at the a6 step itself. It adds 480,000 USD over 27 hours. Of that, 300,000 is the cementing lump arriving whole, and 180,000 is the per day accrual over the 27 hours. Two different things happened in one boundary, and only one of them is affected by how long the job took.

## A checkpoint is a plan value

Nothing on this curve is an actual. It is what the programme says should have been committed by that hour if the well ran as costed.

Its value is as a reference. Read against a real cost report it tells you the size and the sign of the difference. Read on its own it tells you only what you planned.

## Exercise

Read the checkpoint off the panel and write down all three of its statements: metres drilled, hours elapsed, USD committed.

Then split the a6 step into its lump part and its accrual part, and say which of the two would change if the casing job overran by six hours.
