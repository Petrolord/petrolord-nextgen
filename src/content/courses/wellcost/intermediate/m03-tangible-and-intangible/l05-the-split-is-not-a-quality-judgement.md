# The split is not a quality judgement

Intangible is not a synonym for waste, and 80.48 percent of the golden well is intangible by construction.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## The misreading

The word invites it. Intangible sounds like something you cannot point at, and that sounds like something you should have spent less on.

So people see 4,330,000 USD of a 5,380,000 USD base in the intangible column and read it as evidence that the well is inefficient. It is evidence of nothing of the kind.

## What a well actually is

A well is a hole. The dominant cost of a hole is the act of making it, and that act leaves nothing behind that anyone could sell.

The rig hours are gone. The mud is spent. The cementing job happened once. The logging run has been made. On the golden those consumed activities are most of the sheet, and there is no version of this well where they are not.

The tangible column is the short list of things that stay: 800,000 USD of casing and a 250,000 USD wellhead, 1,050,000 USD in total. That is what a completed 3,000 m well leaves in the ground.

## The perverse alternative

Suppose you set out to raise the tangible fraction. The only way to do it is to buy more steel or buy fewer days.

Buying more steel than the well needs is a worse well at a higher cost with a better looking ratio. Buying fewer days is a real improvement, but you would pursue it for the 160,000 USD per day it saves rather than for what it does to the ratio.

Optimise the ratio directly and you will make bad decisions. The ratio is an output.

## What a low tangible fraction does tell you

It tells you the well is mostly a services and time purchase, which the basis analysis already told you from another direction. That is a description of what a drilling operation is, not a verdict on how well it was run.

## The one honest comparison

The split is worth comparing between similar wells drilled by the same organisation on similar programmes, because a real difference then points at a real difference in scope. It is not worth comparing across well types, regions or contract structures, where the categories are answering different questions entirely.
