# Per metre

The basis that buys hole rather than time, and is completely deaf to the calendar.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## The arithmetic

A per-metre line carries a rate and is multiplied by drilled metres. The engine spells the basis `per-meter`; the quantity it multiplies is the length actually cut, not the depth reached.

The golden has one such line. Mud and consumables at 150 USD per metre over 3,000 m gives 450,000 USD, which is 8.36 percent of the base. It is the smallest of the three bases on this well by a wide margin.

## It cannot hear a delay

Hold the hole at 3,000 m and stretch the schedule as far as you like. The per-metre line stays at 450,000 USD. Double the non-productive allowance, add a week of waiting on weather, lose a bit in the hole and fish for it: none of that consumes another metre, so none of it moves the line.

This is the property that makes the basis worth choosing deliberately rather than by habit. It is not that per-metre lines are cheap. It is that they are inert with respect to time.

## How it accrues

On the cost-time curve a per-metre line advances only while a drill activity is running. During trips, casing runs and flat time it contributes a horizontal segment, because drilled length is not advancing.

So the per-metre contribution to the curve has the same shape as the depth curve itself, sloped while making hole and flat otherwise, while the per-day contribution slopes constantly. Reading the two together tells you which part of the cost is buying progress.

## What belongs here

Consumables whose usage tracks the hole. Drilling fluid and its chemicals, cuttings handling, and bits and downhole tools priced against footage.

The test mirrors the per-day one. If the quantity you consume is set by how much rock you cut rather than by how long you are on location, the line is per-metre.

## The honest limit

Real mud consumption is not purely footage driven. A well that circulates for three days without drilling still burns chemicals, and a per-metre line will not see it.

That is a known approximation rather than a defect, and it is the right one at AFE stage. The alternative is splitting the vendor across two bases, which buys a little accuracy and costs a lot of clarity on a sheet whose purpose is to be read and signed.
