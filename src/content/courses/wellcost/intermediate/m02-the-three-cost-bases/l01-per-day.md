# Per day

The basis that hires a clock, and the only one of the three that hears a schedule slip.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## The arithmetic

A per-day line carries a rate and nothing else. Its amount is that rate multiplied by the total day count handed to the cost run.

The golden's rig dayrate is 100,000 USD per day, and over 18 days that is 1,800,000 USD. The integrated services spread is 60,000 USD per day and comes to 1,080,000 USD. Together the two per-day lines bill 2,880,000 USD, which is 53.53 percent of the 5,380,000 USD base.

Add the two rates and the well is renting 160,000 USD of clock every day it is out there.

## The clock is elapsed time

The day count is elapsed, not productive. The rig is on hire while it waits on weather, while it fishes and while it sits at surface, and the per-day lines bill straight through all of it.

That is not a modelling convenience. It is the actual commercial arrangement, and it is why the non-productive allowance in the time model is a cost input rather than a scheduling curiosity.

## How it accrues

On the cost-time curve, per-day lines rise as a straight slope in time. They never step and they never pause. Whatever the hole is doing, whether it is being drilled, tripped, cased or nothing at all, the per-day slope is the same 160,000 USD per day of combined rate.

Compare that with the depth curve, which is sloped while drilling and vertical while flat. The cost curve has no flat segments from the per-day lines at all, which is exactly why a well that stops making hole keeps making cost.

## What belongs here

Anything you are paying for the availability of, rather than for a quantity delivered. Rig hire, the integrated services spread, standby boats, rented equipment on a daily hire, personnel on a day rate.

The test is simple. If the invoice arrives whether or not the well made hole that day, the line is per-day.

## What does not

A one-off job that happens to take three days is not a per-day line. You are buying the job, not the days, and if it overruns the vendor absorbs it rather than you. That is a lump line with a duration attached to it somewhere else.
