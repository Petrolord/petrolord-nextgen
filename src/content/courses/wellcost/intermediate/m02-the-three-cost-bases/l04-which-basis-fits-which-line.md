# Which basis fits which line

Four vendors, four different answers, and one question that settles all of them.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## The question

For every line, ask what the vendor is actually selling you. Availability, quantity, or a finished job. Those three map onto the three bases exactly, and nothing else about the vendor matters.

## A rig

A rig contractor sells availability. The rig is yours from acceptance to release and the invoice does not ask what you did with it.

So the rig is `per-day`, at 100,000 USD per day on the golden. The integrated services spread sits alongside it on the same basis at 60,000 USD per day, because a spread is a collection of people and kit held on location for the duration.

## A mud company

A mud company mostly sells quantity. Barite, polymer, base oil and the engineering to keep the system in specification all scale with the hole you cut.

So mud and consumables is `per-meter`, at 150 USD per metre on the golden, coming to 450,000 USD. Some contracts pay the mud engineer on a day rate and the products on volume, which would be two lines on two bases. The golden takes the simpler route and puts the whole vendor on footage.

## A bit supplier

A bit supplier can be either, and the contract decides. A footage contract sells you metres and is `per-meter`. A bit sold outright is a purchase and is `lump`.

The standalone cost-per-metre worked example in this course prices a single bit at 50,000 USD as a fixed purchase. That example is not a section of the golden programme, so do not carry its numbers across; carry the reasoning. The bit is a thing you bought, and things you bought are lump.

## A mobilisation

A mobilisation sells a finished job. Getting the rig to location has an agreed price, and if the tow takes an extra two days that is the contractor's exposure, not yours.

So mobilisation is `lump`, and it will usually carry no `atActivityId`, which means the engine steps it onto the cost curve at spud.

## The trap

Do not choose a basis by which one gives the answer you want on the base case. All three can be calibrated to produce the same figure today. What separates them is what happens tomorrow, which is the next lesson.

## Exercise

Write down the four vendors above and assign each a basis without looking back, then check yourself against the panel.

Then take one vendor you have actually worked with and say which basis their contract really implies.
