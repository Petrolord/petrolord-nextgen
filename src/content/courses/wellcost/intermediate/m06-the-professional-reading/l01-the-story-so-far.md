# The story so far

Five modules, and one idea underneath all of them.

## The claim

Every number on an AFE is a rate multiplied by an exposure. The rate is quoted by somebody. The exposure comes from the schedule, or from the hole, or from nowhere at all. The whole skill of this tier is knowing which exposure a line is attached to, because that is what decides whether the line moves when the well does.

There are only three answers. A day. A metre. Nothing.

## What each module established

**Module 1.** An AFE is a request for permission with a number attached, and the number is a structure rather than a total. Every line carries a BASIS and a CATEGORY, and those are independent questions. The basis says what the line is multiplied by. The category says how it is treated afterwards. Eight priced lines and two labels each is the whole form.

**Module 2.** Three bases, three exposures. Per day is exposed to elapsed days, per metre to metres drilled, lump to nothing. The same 1,800,000 USD calibrated all three ways bills identically on the base case and behaves completely differently afterwards: elasticity to elapsed days is 1.0, 0.0 and 0.0. On the golden estimate 2,880,000 USD of the 5,380,000 USD base is per day, which is 0.5353159851301115 of it, so one extra day costs 160,000 USD that nobody can negotiate after spud.

**Module 3.** Tangible against intangible is decided by one question: does the item stay in the ground with salvage value. Casing and the wellhead do, at 1,050,000 USD. Everything else is intangible, at 4,330,000 USD, which is 0.8048327137546468 of the base. The split changes nothing about the well and everything about the tax treatment, and that is why it is on the form.

**Module 4.** Contingency is a rate too, with the base itself as its exposure. It is not an item, it has no basis and no category, and it is applied last. At 0.1 it is 538,000 USD and the fourth largest number on the estimate, above the completion services contract. It passes the largest contracted line at 0.3345724907063197, which is exactly that line's share of the base. And a fraction on the base is f/(1+f) of the total, so the golden's 0.1 is 0.09090909090909091 of what gets approved.

**Module 5.** Cost per metre is a rate multiplied by hours, over an interval. It answers bit questions and section questions and nothing larger: the three golden sections come to 0.31115241635687735 of the AFE base. Its ranking can invert the total ranking, between 9.027081243731192 and 14.423076923076923 m/hr on the intermediate section. And the published 770 USD/m fixture is a worked example rather than a section of this well.

## The numbers to carry

- Per day share of the golden base: 0.5353159851301115, and 160,000 USD per extra day.
- Tangible 1,050,000 USD, intangible 4,330,000 USD, base 5,380,000 USD, total 5,918,000 USD.
- The fraction that lets a provision outrank the rig: 0.3345724907063197.
- Whole well, base over metres drilled: 1793.3333333333333 USD/m.

## The one sentence

Every line is a rate times an exposure, and reading an AFE is knowing which of the three exposures each line is attached to before you argue about the rate.
