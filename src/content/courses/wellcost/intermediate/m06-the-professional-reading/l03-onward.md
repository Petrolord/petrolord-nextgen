# Onward

From one number to a curve, and from single inputs to ranges.

## What the Expert tier adds

This tier produced one estimate from one schedule. The Expert tier asks two harder questions about it.

**When the money is spent, not just how much.** The same programme that gave you 18 days and 3,000 m also gives a time and a depth at every activity boundary, and the per day, per metre and lump lines can be accrued along it. That is the cost time curve: money against time, built from the schedule you already have.

It carries one exact property worth knowing in advance. The curve ends at the AFE base cost, with an absolute error of zero across every combination tested, because contingency was never attached to an activity and so never accrues. The gap between the end of the curve and the approved total is always exactly the contingency.

**What happens when the inputs stop being single numbers.** Every rate in this tier was a point value. Replace them with ranges and the estimate becomes a distribution, and the question a contingency percentage could not answer becomes answerable: which percentile of the outcome does the approved total reach.

That is where the shape of the model matters. A rig dayrate enters the cost linearly, so its average passes straight through. A rate of penetration does not, because time goes as one over it, so the average time is not the time at the average rate. The cost at the most likely inputs is therefore not the most likely cost, and the gap runs one way rather than either way.

## Before you go

Two habits from this tier.

**Name the exposure before you argue the rate.** Most arguments in an AFE review are about a rate when the disagreement is really about the days or the metres it is multiplied by.

**Trace every rate to the section it came from.** A cost per metre with no interval and no hour terms behind it is a number nobody can check, and the published worked example in this course is the standing reminder of why.

## The one sentence

You can now build an estimate, defend where each number came from and say what its provision does and does not cover. The next tier is when that money is spent and how wrong it might be.
