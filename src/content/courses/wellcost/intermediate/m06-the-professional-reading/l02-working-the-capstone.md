# Working the capstone

The order you do it in, and the four checks that catch the mistakes people actually make.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## The method

The capstone runs on its own conditions, not on the golden well. What carries across is the order of work, and the order is not negotiable because each step needs the one before it.

**One. Get the schedule first.** Days and metres are outputs of the tier below, not assumptions. Build the activity programme, apply the non-productive allowance as a stretch on productive time, and take two numbers out: elapsed days and metres drilled. Nothing on the AFE can be priced until you have them.

**Two. Price each line on its own basis.** Per day multiplies its rate by elapsed days. Per metre multiplies its rate by metres drilled. Lump is its own value and is multiplied by nothing. Do not average, do not convert a line to a different basis to make it tidier, and do not price a per day line on productive days.

**Three. Split by category.** Every line is tangible or intangible, and the sum of the two is the base.

**Four. Apply the contingency to the base.** Not to the total, not to a subtotal, not to the intangibles only. One fraction, one multiplication, at the end.

**Five. Compute cost per metre section by section.** Each section uses the hours of that section, taken from the same schedule the days came from, and divides by the interval that section drilled. Never by the section total depth.

## The checks

Run all four before you report anything.

**Tangible plus intangible equals the base.** If it does not, a line has a category the engine does not recognise, or a line has been counted twice. On the golden, 1,050,000 plus 4,330,000 is 5,380,000 USD.

**Total less base equals the contingency exactly.** Not approximately. The difference should be the fraction times the base and nothing else. On the golden, 5,918,000 less 5,380,000 is 538,000 USD, which is 0.1 of 5,380,000.

**The contingency share of the total is f/(1+f), not f.** If your reported percentage of the total equals the fraction you typed, you have applied it to the wrong denominator.

**Every cost per metre comes from its own section's hours.** Not from a fixture, not from another well, not from a number somebody remembered. The published 770 USD/m example has a 16 hour trip in it and no trip in the golden schedule is 16 hours, so it belongs to no section of this well. A rate you cannot trace to an interval and three hour terms is a rate you cannot defend.

## The two failures to expect

Pricing a per day line on productive days rather than elapsed days, which understates every rig and spread line at once.

Dividing a section cost by the section total depth rather than its interval, which flatters the deepest section most.

## Exercise

Work the golden well end to end in the order above and reproduce 5,380,000 USD and 5,918,000 USD, then run all four checks on your own arithmetic.

Then deliberately price the rig dayrate on productive days instead of elapsed days, and say whether any of the four checks catches it. Then say what would have.
