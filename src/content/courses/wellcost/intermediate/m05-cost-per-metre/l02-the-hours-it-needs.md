# The hours it needs

Three hour terms, three different origins, and one of them is often invented.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## Drilling hours

The interval divided by the rate of penetration. This is the only term that shrinks when the bit improves, and it is the largest of the three on every deep section.

In the golden programme the three drilled sections give 20, 100 and 100 productive hours, from 500 m at 25 m/hr, 1,500 m at 15 m/hr and 1,000 m at 10 m/hr. The rotating total for the well is 220 hours out of 432 elapsed, which is 0.5729166666666666 of the schedule.

## Connection hours

Time on the section that is not rotating and not tripping. Making connections, surveying, circulating bottoms up, reaming a tight spot.

The section pricing in this course allows 4 hours per section, flat. That is a modelling choice, not a measurement, and it is the term people are most casual about. On a 20 hour surface section, 4 hours is a fifth of the drilling time. On a 100 hour section it is a twenty fifth. The same flat allowance means something quite different in the two places.

## Trip hours

The round trip at the section's total depth. In the golden programme trips are priced from a speed, and the round trip factor is 2, so a trip at depth D at 500 m/hr takes two D over 500 hours.

That gives 8 hours at 2,000 m and 12 hours at 3,000 m, and those are the numbers the section costs use.

## The one that is invented

The surface section has no trip in the golden programme. It drills to 500 m and runs 20 inch casing directly.

The section pricing gives it 2 hours anyway, and the digest marks that hour as synthetic. It is there so the three sections can be compared on the same form, and it is not in the schedule. Two hours at 6,000 USD/hr is 12,000 USD on a 206,000 USD section, so it does not change any conclusion, and you should still know it is there.

## Productive hours, not elapsed

Every hour in the form is productive. None of them carries the non-productive time allowance, which on the golden well stretches productive time by 12.5 percent and is 0.1111111111111111 of elapsed time.

So a cost per metre built this way is the rate of a section that goes to plan. The rig is paid for elapsed hours, not productive ones. Compare a cost per metre against an AFE line and the two are counting different clocks.
