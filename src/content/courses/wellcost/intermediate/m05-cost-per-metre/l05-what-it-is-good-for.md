# What it is good for

A good instrument for one comparison and a poor instrument for every other.

{{panel:wc-afe-explorer}}

## What it is for

Comparing bits in the same hole, and comparing sections priced the same way.

Both of those hold the pricing fixed and vary the hours. That is the comparison the formula was built for, and on it the formula is decisive, because the rig hours are where almost all the money is. On the published fixture the rig is 0.935064935064935 of the rate and the bit is 0.06493506493506493. A bit that costs more and saves hours wins easily, and the form shows exactly by how much.

## What it is not for

Comparing wells.

The three golden sections total 206,000, 722,000 and 746,000 USD. Together that is 1,674,000 USD against an AFE base of 5,380,000 USD, or 0.31115241635687735 of it.

The other two thirds are outside the form entirely. There is no casing in it, no cement, no wellhead, no logging, no completion, no mud, no rig move, no non-productive time. Two wells with identical cost per metre on every section can differ by millions on the parts the form never touched, and the well with the better rate can be the more expensive well.

## What it hides

**Which hours.** Drilling, connection and trip collapse into one bracket. A section that is slow on bottom and a section that trips badly look the same.

**How much of it is the bit.** The bit share is 0.24271844660194175 on the surface section and 0.06702412868632708 on the production section. On a fast shallow section the bit is a quarter of the number, on a deep one about a fifteenth, so the same bit price is a different argument in the two places.

**The ranking.** Between 9.027081243731192 and 14.423076923076923 m/hr on the intermediate section, the rate ranking and the total ranking disagree. A rate on its own cannot tell you it is in that band.

**Its own provenance.** The 770 USD/m published fixture is a worked example with a 16 hour trip that matches no trip in the golden schedule, and the golden's deepest trip is 12 productive hours. A number quoted with no section attached is a number nobody can check.

## How to use it honestly

Quote the interval, the three hour terms and the pricing alongside the rate. Compare only against another rate built the same way. Never let it stand in for a total.

## Exercise

Then write down the four things you would have to publish next to a cost per metre for a reader to be able to reproduce it, and check the published fixture against your list.
