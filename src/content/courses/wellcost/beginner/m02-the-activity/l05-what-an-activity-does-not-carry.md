# What an activity does not carry

An activity has a kind, a label, some geometry and a rate. It has no money on it at all.

## The separation

Look at any activity in the golden programme and you will find a depth, a rate, a duration and a name. You will not find a dayrate, a unit price or a lump sum. Cost lives in a completely separate list, and the two lists are joined only where they have to be.

That separation is not an oversight. It is the reason the model works.

## How cost finds the schedule

The cost list on the golden case has eight items. Each one names a basis that says which schedule number it reads.

Some items read total days. Some read drilled metres. Some read neither and are simply a value, and five of the eight are lump items of that kind, each naming the identifier of the activity it belongs to so the cost lands at the right moment in the schedule.

So the link runs one way. A cost item points at an activity. An activity never points at a cost.

## Why one way is the right way

Because the same programme has to be priced more than once.

The same 18 day schedule gets priced against a tender rate, against a signed contract, against a partner's own rate sheet and again after the well is over. If the money were welded onto the activity you would have to rebuild the programme every time. Keeping the schedule clean means one programme can be repriced without a single duration changing.

It also runs the other way. Change the rate of penetration in the production hole and you have changed the days, and every cost line that reads days moves with it, without you touching a single price.

## What this means for you now

At this tier your job is the left hand side. Get the activity list right: the kinds, the depths, the rates, the flat hours and the order. That list is the entire input to everything that follows.

Cost arrives in the next tier, where the eight items, the three bases, the two categories and the contingency are the subject. None of it will change a single hour of the schedule you build here.

## Exercise

Take the golden programme's ten activities and write down, for each one, whether you would expect its cost to be driven by days, by metres, or by a fixed price.

Then say which of the ten you would attach a lump sum to, and name the moment in the schedule at which that money should land.
