# The flat activity

Time that no rate and no depth can explain, and the reason a programme needs somewhere to put it.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## The kind with no formula

Three of the four activity kinds compute a duration from geometry and a rate. The fourth does not. A flat activity has one field, a duration in hours, and its duration is that number.

That looks like a cheat until you try to write a real programme without it.

## What actually goes in there

Rigging up and rigging down. Spudding. Nippling up and testing the blowout preventer. Waiting on cement. Waiting on weather. Logging runs. Displacing to completion fluid. Running the completion. Handing the well over.

None of those is a function of depth or of a rate. A cement job does not take twice as long at 3,000 m as at 1,500 m. It takes as long as it takes, and pretending otherwise by inventing a fictional rate would be worse modelling, not better.

## How much of a well it is

The golden programme has two flat activities: a rig move and spud of 24 hours and a completion and handover of 60 hours. Together they carry 84 of the 384 productive hours.

Compare that against the 20 productive hours of tripping in the same programme. Flat time is more than four times the tripping time, and it is the second largest block after rotating hours.

## Flat time also hides inside casing

There is a second home for flat time. A casing activity carries its own flat term alongside its running term, and on the golden programme those terms are 11 hours on the 20in job, 19 hours on the 13-3/8in job and 14 hours on the 9-5/8in job.

That flat term is why a casing activity is not just a slow trip. Most of a casing job is not running pipe.

## Why the kind earns its place

A programme that can only express rate driven work will always be optimistic, because every hour it cannot describe simply disappears. The flat activity is the honest place to put known, planned, rate independent time before any allowance for trouble is applied on top.

If you find yourself inflating a rate of penetration to make a schedule come out right, what you actually needed was a flat activity.
