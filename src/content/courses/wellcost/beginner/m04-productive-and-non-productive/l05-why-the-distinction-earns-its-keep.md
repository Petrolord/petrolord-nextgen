# Why the distinction earns its keep

The two buckets are kept apart because they are bought back in completely different ways.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## Different levers

Productive time is bought back with rate and with planning. A better bit, a faster running speed, a flat activity you can shorten or delete, a section drilled in one run instead of two. Every one of those changes an input to a closed form, and the effect is arithmetic and immediate.

Non-productive time is bought back with risk management. Hole cleaning practice, mud design, casing points set above the trouble zone, spares on the rig, a weather window chosen properly. None of that appears in a closed form at all, and none of it changes a single productive hour.

If you cannot say which bucket an hour came from, you cannot say which of these two programmes of work would have saved it.

## What the model lets you test

Change an ROP and only the drill rows move. Change the allowance and every row moves in proportion while the productive total stays at 384, because the stretch never touches the closed forms.

That separation is the point. It means a schedule saving can be attributed. On the golden case 384 of the 432 hours answer to rate and planning, and 48 answer to trouble.

## Where the argument usually goes wrong

Two failure modes are common, and both collapse the split.

The first is padding the ROP. Carry your trouble expectation by quoting a rate lower than the bit will really achieve and the schedule may come out right, but the estimate now says the formation is hard when it means the hole is unstable. Nobody downstream can tell the difference.

The second is hiding rate problems in the allowance. A section that consistently drills slowly is a productive time problem with a technical answer. Carried as trouble time it looks like bad luck, and bad luck does not get a review of the bit programme.

## The habit worth forming

Quote both numbers, always. On the golden programme that is 384 productive hours and 48 non-productive hours, not simply 18 days.

The 18 days is what you are billed for. The 384 and the 48 are what you can do something about, and they point at different people.
