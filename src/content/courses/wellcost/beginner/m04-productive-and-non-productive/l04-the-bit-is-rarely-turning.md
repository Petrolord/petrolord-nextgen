# The bit is rarely turning

Most of the time you pay for on a well, nobody is making hole, and the published case shows how little of the clock the bit actually owns.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## The golden case by kind

The programme has ten activities. Group their productive hours by kind and the picture is immediate.

| Kind | Productive hours |
| --- | --- |
| Drill | 220 |
| Flat | 84 |
| Casing | 60 |
| Trip | 20 |
| Total | 384 |

Only the drill row makes hole. Everything else in that table, 164 hours of it, is work that has to happen and advances the well by not one metre.

Because the allowance stretches every activity by the same factor, those proportions carry straight through to elapsed time. Drilling occupies 57.29 percent of the 432 elapsed hours, and it is the same 57.29 percent at any allowance you choose.

## Why the number is lower than it looks

Even 57.29 percent flatters the bit. The 220 productive rotating hours sit inside 432 elapsed hours, which is barely more than half, and part of the drilling share is the allowance rather than rotation.

The rates in the programme are 25, 15 and 10 metres per hour for the three hole sections. Averaged over the drilling activities alone the well makes 13.636363636363637 metres per hour.

Averaged over the elapsed clock it makes 6.944444444444445 metres per hour, which is 166.66666666666666 metres per day.

That second figure is the honest one for planning, and it is roughly half the first. A well quoted at 15 metres per hour does not reach 3,000 m in 200 hours.

## Why this surprises people

The instinct is to argue about ROP, because ROP is the number that gets reported daily and the one a bit supplier will discuss. It is also the only one of these numbers with a supplier attached to it.

But the 164 hours of trips, casing and flat work are untouched by any bit. Rig move, wellhead, cementing waiting time and completion sit in flat activities. Module 3 already showed that a casing run has a flat term no running speed can reduce.

Halving every rotating hour in this programme would not halve the well. It would take 110 hours out of 384 productive hours, and the other 164 would still be there.
