# What counts as NPT

The engine gives non-productive time exactly one number, so all of the judgement sits in what you decide to put inside it.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## The standard list

Non-productive time is time on the well that buys no progress. The usual entries are familiar from any daily drilling report.

**Waiting on weather.** The rig is on hire, the crew is on shift, and nothing can be run. Common offshore, and it is not a failure of anyone's planning.

**Waiting on parts, materials or a boat.** A logistics gap rather than a downhole one.

**Fishing.** Something is in the hole that should not be, and recovering it takes hours or days that advance the well by nothing.

**Stuck pipe.** Working, jarring and freeing the string. If it cannot be freed, the pipe is cut and left.

**Sidetracks.** A new hole around a stuck fish or a failed section. Metres are drilled, but they are metres you have already paid for once.

## Where the boundary is a judgement

A wiper trip that was in the programme is productive time: it is a `trip` activity with a speed, and Module 3 prices it. The same trip run because the hole was packing off is non-productive.

Drilling slower than planned is not non-productive time at all. It is productive time at a lower rate, and the right place to record it is the ROP on the drill activity, where Module 3 showed a third of the rate triples the hours. Recording it as trouble time hides the cause.

A sidetrack is the hardest case. The engine refuses a drill activity that does not start where the hole currently is, so a redrilled interval cannot be entered as drilling at all. It belongs either inside the allowance or as an explicit flat activity with a stated duration.

## Consistency matters more than the list

An allowance is only meaningful against the offset wells it came from. If those wells counted rig repair as non-productive and you do not, your allowance is too low before you have chosen a number. Write the boundary down with the estimate. On the golden case the allowance of 0.125 contributes 48 hours, and 48 hours is worth arguing about.
