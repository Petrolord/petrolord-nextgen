# Running casing is both

One leg of pipe plus a flat term, and no running speed on earth can touch the flat term.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## The form

Casing hours are the depth divided by the run speed, plus a flat term in hours. The first part scales with depth and speed exactly like a trip, except there is no factor of two, because casing goes in and stays there. The second part is a constant.

So a casing activity is the only kind that mixes both behaviours in one line.

## The flat term is a floor

Hold the depth at 2,000 m and the flat term at 19 hours, then sweep the run speed:

| Run speed, m per hour | Hours | Above the floor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 39 | 20 |
| 200 | 29 | 10 |
| 300 | 25.666666666666668 | 6.666666666666668 |
| 400 | 24 | 5 |
| 600 | 22.333333333333332 | 3.333333333333332 |
| 1000 | 21 | 2 |
| 5000 | 19.4 | 0.3999999999999986 |

The last row is the point of the lesson. A run speed of 5,000 m per hour is absurd, faster than any casing crew has ever run pipe, and the job still takes 19.4 hours. The running leg has collapsed to 0.3999999999999986 hours and the 19 hour floor has not moved at all.

Speed buys you the part above the floor and nothing else.

## What the flat term is made of

Now hold the speed at 400 m per hour and sweep the flat term instead:

| Flat hours | Total hours | Flat share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 5 | 0 |
| 5 | 10 | 0.5 |
| 11 | 16 | 0.6875 |
| 19 | 24 | |
| 30 | 35 | |
| 48 | 53 | |

The running leg stays at 5 hours in every row. By the time the flat term is 11 hours it is already 0.6875 of the activity, and the job is mostly not about running pipe.

That is right, because the flat term is the cement job, the circulation, the landing, the waiting on cement and the pressure test.
