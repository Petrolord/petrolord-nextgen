# Tripping is twice the depth

The factor of two is in the formula, not in your judgement, and it never varies.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## The form

Trip hours are twice the measured depth divided by the trip speed. The two is there because a trip in this engine is always a round trip: out of the hole and back in.

There is no half trip kind. If you need one leg only, halve the depth or use a flat activity. You do not get to change the two.

## The factor is exact at every depth

Sweep the depth at a trip speed of 500 m per hour:

| Depth, m | One way, hours | Round trip, hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500 | 1 | 2 |
| 1000 | 2 | 4 |
| 2000 | 4 | 8 |
| 3000 | 6 | 12 |
| 4000 | 8 | 16 |
| 5000 | 10 | 20 |

The round trip is exactly twice the one way in every row, and the ratio does not drift with depth. Notice the other invariant: at 500 m per hour a trip costs 4 hours per thousand metres, at every depth.

## Straight in depth, hyperbolic in speed

Trip time behaves in speed exactly as drilling time behaves in rate. Hold the depth at 2,000 m:

| Trip speed, m per hour | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| 250 | 16 |
| 400 | 10 |
| 500 | 8 |
| 750 | 5.333333333333333 |
| 1000 | 4 |

Halving the speed from 500 to 250 adds 8 hours. Doubling it from 500 to 1000 saves only 4.

## The comparison that catches people

A trip and the running leg of a casing job, at the same depth and the same speed, are not the same duration. At 400 m per hour, a trip at 500 m takes 2.5 hours against 1.25 hours of running, at 2,000 m it is 10 hours against 5, and at 3,000 m it is 15 hours against 7.5.

The ratio is 2 in every case, for the single reason that a trip goes both ways and casing goes down once. The casing job then adds a flat term on top, which is the next lesson.
