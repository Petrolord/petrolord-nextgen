# Onward

Two quantities leave this tier, and the Professional tier is about what happens when they meet a rate sheet.

## From days and metres to money

The next tier gives every cost item a basis. A per-day item multiplies elapsed days, a per-metre item multiplies drilled metres, and a lump item multiplies nothing. Items also carry a category, tangible or intangible, which is how an AFE separates steel you keep from services you consume.

That is the whole mechanism, and its consequences are sharper than it looks.

## What you will find there

**The basis decides who carries a schedule slip.** The same amount of money, calibrated three ways, bills identically on the base case. Let the schedule run long and the per-day line grows while the per-metre and lump lines do not move at all. Their sensitivity to elapsed days is 1.0, 0.0 and 0.0.

That turns the day count of this tier into an exposure. On the golden estimate, 53.53 percent of the base cost is per-day exposed, so one extra day is 160,000 dollars, and it is not negotiable once the rig is on the well.

**Contingency is a provision that can outrank a contracted line.** It sits as its own line rather than inside the estimate, and on the golden case it passes the largest real cost line at a fraction of 0.3346, and the second largest at 0.2007. Those are not extreme numbers in a frontier well.

## And after that

The Expert tier follows the money along the clock. It builds the cumulative cost-time curve, establishes what the curve's endpoint is and is not, and then stops treating the estimate as a single answer at all, putting distributions on the inputs and reading the result as percentiles.

The question changes at that point, from what the well will cost to how a well that is running behind gets recognised early enough to matter.
