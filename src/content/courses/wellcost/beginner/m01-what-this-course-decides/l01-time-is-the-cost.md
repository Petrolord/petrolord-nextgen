# Time is the cost

A drilling rig is rented by the day, so a schedule is a cost estimate wearing different units.

## The rental logic

Almost nothing about a well is bought outright. The rig, the crews, the mud engineers, the directional service, the boats and the helicopter are all hired, and they are hired by the day. The moment the well takes an extra day, every one of those lines takes an extra day too.

That is why a drilling engineer who is asked "what will this well cost" starts by answering a different question: how long will it take. Get the days right and the money mostly follows. Get the days wrong and no amount of care over unit rates will rescue the estimate.

## What the golden well shows

The worked programme this course uses has ten activities and reaches a total depth of 3,000 m. The engine schedules it at 432 hours, which is 18 days at 24 hours per day.

Two of its cost lines are charged per day: a rig dayrate of 100,000 dollars and an integrated services spread of 60,000 dollars, so the well burns 160,000 dollars a day before anything else is bought. Over 18 days that is 2,880,000 dollars.

The whole base estimate for the well is 5,380,000 dollars. So 53.53 percent of the base is exposed directly to the number of days, and that exposure is the largest single block in the estimate.

## The number that should frighten you

One extra day on this well costs 160,000 dollars.

Not "about" 160,000, and not negotiable. Once the well is spudded the dayrate is contracted, the spread is mobilised, and the only variable left is how many times the sun comes up before the rig releases. A single stuck pipe event that takes a day has already spent more than the wellhead.

## Why the schedule is the estimate

You can argue about a unit rate in a tender. You cannot argue with the calendar. So the discipline this course teaches is to build the schedule honestly first, activity by activity, and only then to hang money on it.

Everything in the Associate tier is about where the days go.

## Exercise

Write down the three activities in your own experience that most often overrun, and for each one estimate how many hours the overrun usually costs.

Multiply your total by 160,000 dollars per day and compare it against the 5,380,000 dollar base above. Decide whether that share would change how you plan.
