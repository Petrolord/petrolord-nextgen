# Two currencies

A well is measured in hours and in metres, and confusing the two is how estimates go wrong.

{{panel:wc-time-explorer}}

## Hours accumulate always, metres do not

Every activity in a programme consumes hours. Only one kind of activity produces metres.

On the golden programme the hole reaches 500 m at hour 49.5, sits at 500 m until hour 63 while casing is run, then advances again. Between hour 324 and hour 432 the hole does not deepen at all: that is a trip, a casing run and the completion, 108 elapsed hours in which the bit never turns.

This is why the classic drilling curve is a staircase. Sloped while drilling, flat while everything else happens.

## The rotating share

Of the 384 productive hours in the golden programme, 220 are rotating hours in the three drill activities. The rest is 84 hours of flat activity, 60 hours of casing and 20 hours of tripping.

So even before any allowance for trouble, the bit is turning for well under two thirds of the productive time, and for less than that of the 432 elapsed hours.

## The same well, three rates

| Reading | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Metres per rotating hour | 13.636363636363637 |
| Metres per elapsed hour | 6.944444444444445 |
| Metres per elapsed day | 166.66666666666666 |

All three describe the same 3,000 m well. The first is a drilling performance number and belongs in a bit or bottomhole assembly review. The second and third are schedule numbers and belong in a cost estimate.

Quote the first where the second is wanted and you will understate the well by roughly half.

## Which question each currency answers

Hours answer: when does the rig release, how many days do I pay for, when does the completion crew need to be on location.

Metres answer: how much casing, how much mud, how many bits, how much of anything that is consumed by depth rather than by time.

A cost line is attached to one currency or the other, and that choice decides whether a schedule slip touches it. That is the subject of the next tier.
