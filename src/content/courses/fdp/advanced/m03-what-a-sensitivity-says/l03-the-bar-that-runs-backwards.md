# The bar that runs backwards

Three of the four drivers raise the value when they rise. Capex does not. Its minus 30 percent end is the high one at 2658.9995 and its plus 30 percent end is the low one at 1371.8250.

## Reading the direction off the row

| driver | minus 30 percent | plus 30 percent | swing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oil Price | 466.5563 | 3564.2683 | 3097.7119 |
| Production | 592.9935 | 3437.8310 | 2844.8375 |
| CAPEX | 2658.9995 | 1371.8250 | 1287.1745 |
| OPEX | 2178.5655 | 1852.2591 | 326.3064 |

Oil Price and Production run low to high. CAPEX and OPEX run high to low. Both cost drivers move the value against themselves, because more money spent is less money kept, and the sweep prints the ends in a fixed order of minus then plus rather than in order of size.

## Capex hits once and hard

The base capex is 2250.0000 million USD and it lands in year 0, before a barrel is produced, so a 30 percent move changes the deepest cash position of -2250.0000 directly, in the year discounting touches least. The swing is 1287.1745 million USD, a share of base of 0.638666.

Operating cost behaves differently. It is spread across the producing years, so it is discounted year by year and the later portions count for less. Its swing is 326.3064, a share of base of 0.161906, on the same 30 percent move.

## The sign of the move is the reader's problem

Nothing in the numbers is wrong. The ends are 2658.9995 and 1371.8250 and both are correct runs of the full case. The difficulty is that a chart drawn from a table where two rows run one way and two run the other places the minus 30 percent end of the capex row on the same side as the plus 30 percent end of the price row, and a reader skimming the ends reads a capex saving as a capex overrun.

The habit that avoids it is to read the label before the value: 2658.9995 is what the case is worth if capex comes in 30 percent under, and 1371.8250 if it comes in 30 percent over.

## On a marginal case the direction decides

The published case with 900 capex at 45 has a base of 212.7034 and capex ends of 470.1383 and -44.7315. An overrun of 30 percent takes that case below zero. Reading the row backwards there turns a case that fails on a cost overrun into a case that thrives on one.

## The mistake

The mistake is ranking the bars by their right hand end rather than by their swing, which sorts the cost drivers as though their overruns were upsides. The other mistake is assuming the capex bar is symmetric about the base of 2015.4123: it is not, and 2658.9995 and 1371.8250 are not the same distance from it.
