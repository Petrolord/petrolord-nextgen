# What a tornado is not

The sweep moves one driver by 30 percent either way and re-runs the case. Nothing in it says how likely a 30 percent move is, so nothing in it is a probability.

## The chart says what it was asked

| driver | minus 30 percent | plus 30 percent | swing | swing as a share of base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oil Price | 466.5563 | 3564.2683 | 3097.7119 | 1.537012 |
| Production | 592.9935 | 3437.8310 | 2844.8375 | 1.411541 |
| CAPEX | 2658.9995 | 1371.8250 | 1287.1745 | 0.638666 |
| OPEX | 2178.5655 | 1852.2591 | 326.3064 | 0.161906 |

Every one of those eight ends answers a conditional question: what would this case be worth if that one input were 30 percent different and nothing else changed. The answer is exact. It is also conditional on an assumption the analyst supplied, and the 30 came from the sweep's own convention rather than from any study of how these inputs actually move.

## Not a distribution

The ends are not percentiles and they never take a P-label. A P90 and a P10 belong to a reserves distribution, one fluid at a time: EGINA's oil P50 is 130.0000 MMbbl and its gas P50 is 70.0000 Bcf, and those labels came from a distribution somebody built. The value 466.5563 is not a low case in that sense. It is one run. Calling it a P90 NPV asserts that a 30 percent price fall has a particular chance of being exceeded, and the sweep contains no such claim.

## Not a range of outcomes

The widest end and the narrowest end do not bracket the project. The sweep never moves two drivers at once, so a price fall arriving with a capex overrun sits outside the table entirely. On the published case with 900 capex at 45, base 212.7034, the price downside is -225.0477 and the capex overrun end is -44.7315. Both are in the table. The case where both happen is not, and it is worse than either.

## Not a statement about the base

The base of 2015.4123 million USD is a screening result on the EGINA plan's own capex of 2250.0000, at 70.0000 USD a barrel, post royalty at 12.5000 percent and tax at 30.0000 percent, discounted at 10.0000 percent. A wide sweep around a wrong base is a wide sweep around a wrong base. The chart is silent on whether the inputs were right, and it draws with equal confidence on a case the engine would otherwise refuse to price.

## What it is genuinely good for

It ranks the drivers of one case by arithmetic leverage, and that ranking is a work list: Oil Price, Production, CAPEX, OPEX. It says where to spend effort tightening an estimate. A driver with a swing of 326.3064 does not repay much study on this case, and one with a swing of 3097.7119 does.

## The mistake

The mistake is presenting the chart as a risk picture, with the ends read as a likely range and the widest bar read as the biggest risk. The biggest risk is the driver most likely to move by an amount that hurts, which requires a view on likelihood that lives nowhere in these eight numbers. Label the chart with its assumption, 30 percent each way, one driver at a time, and the confusion does not start.
