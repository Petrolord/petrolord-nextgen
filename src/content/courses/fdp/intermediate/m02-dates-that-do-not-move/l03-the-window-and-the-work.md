# The window and the work

EGINA's network says the work must take 870 days. The dates typed on the same eight activities span 933 days. Both figures are right, and they answer different questions.

{{panel:ec-schedule-explorer}}

## Two numbers, two questions

| figure | days | where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| network duration | 870 | durations and the finish to start links |
| calendar span | 933 | the earliest typed date to the latest |

870 days is the logic: the longest chain through the durations somebody entered and the dependencies somebody typed, with no date taking part in producing it. 933 days is the calendar: a count of whole days between two entries in a date column, with no duration taking part. One says how long the work must take and the other how wide the window is.

## 63 days already spent

933 less 870 is 63. Those 63 days are float, sitting in the calendar rather than in the logic.

They are not contingency somebody set aside. They are contingency somebody has already used, by typing dates that leave the work 63 days of room it did not ask for. That room can be spent without any activity slipping and without a warning appearing, because neither number is measured against the other.

## Float in the logic, and float in the calendar

The network's own float belongs to named activities: detailed engineering 180 days, topsides fabrication 180 and subsea installation 480. Each is an amount by which one activity can move without moving first oil.

The 63 days are different in kind and belong to no activity. They are the difference between two answers about the same eight rows, and what they tell you is that the dates and the logic were not derived from each other.

## A window is not a promise

Nothing reconciles the two numbers automatically. A window wider than the network holds slack; a window narrower than it would be a plan asking for work in days it does not have. The engine reports both figures without comment either way, because each is a correct answer to its own question.

## Which one a question is asking for

A partner asking how long the project takes wants 870 days. A logistics lead asking when a vessel is needed wants dates, and the window those dates sit in is 933 days wide. A reviewer asking whether the schedule is achievable wants both, because the answer lives in the 63 days between them.

The discipline is the same in every reconciliation: hold both answers, print both, and treat the gap as something to explain rather than average away.

## The mistake

Printing one of them under the other's name. 933 under the word duration claims the project holds 933 days of work, and it does not. 870 offered as the gap between sanction and first oil claims the window is 870 days wide, and it is not. The discipline is to say which number was asked for, compute the other as well, and treat the difference as a question rather than a rounding.
