# A risk nobody scored

One row in the register carries an impact but no probability, so no score can be formed for it and its band reads Unscored. It neither helps the register's numbers nor hurts them, and that is a deliberate design rather than an oversight.

## The row in full

| field | value |
| --- | --- |
| risk | Host government approval |
| source | Regulatory |
| probability | none |
| impact | 5 |
| score | none |
| band | Unscored |
| cost impact | 400.0000 |
| mitigation | Early engagement |

The impact is a 5, the top of the scale. The cost impact of 400.0000 million USD is the largest in the register, larger than the 180.0000 on the Critical row and the 300.0000 on the Medium row. Somebody has clearly thought about this risk. What nobody did was put a probability on it.

## What it does to the totals

The register's consolidated score is 44, which is 20 plus 12 plus 8 plus 4, the four scored rows and nothing else. Unscored risks read 1. Portfolio health reads 58. Take the unscored row out of the register altogether and health still reads 58. The count by band still shows Critical 1, High 1, Medium 1, Low 1, and now Unscored 0.

## What it does to the exposure

Risk exposure is 269.0000 million USD, and the unscored row contributes 0.0000 of it. Its probability is missing, so there is no factor to apply, so its 400.0000 of cost impact never enters the expected value. The largest money in the register sits outside the number that is supposed to summarise the money.

## The behaviour in a published case

A published register with one Medium risk and one risk whose probability is missing returns a consolidated score of 9, an exposure of 0.0000 and a portfolio health of 80, with the bands reading Medium 1 and Unscored 1. A register holding one Medium risk on its own returns a consolidated 6 and the same health of 80. The unscored row moved neither number. It is reported, and it is excluded from every statistic.

## What it used to do

Before the repairs made ahead of this course, a risk with no probability was treated as Low. It went into the band counts as a Low, it went into health as a Low, and because health is an average across the scored risks, adding an unscored row raised it. A register could be improved by writing down a risk and leaving the probability blank. Treat that as history.

## The mistake

The mistake is reading Unscored as a verdict. It is not a low risk and it is not a small one. It is a row waiting for a number, and the honest reading of Unscored 1 beside a cost impact of 400.0000 is that the register's headline figures are incomplete by exactly one row. The second mistake is deleting the row to make the register tidy, which changes no total and loses the only record that the question was ever asked.
