# What this studio does not do

The studio holds a development plan together, totals it, dates it and prices it. It does not re-derive the subsurface, the well designs or the facility sizing, so a figure typed into it is only as good as the work behind it.

## Held, not derived

Nothing in the plan recomputes the study that produced it. A reserves row of 95.0000 MMbbl at P50 for Egina Main is a number a subsurface team handed over. A well at 14200 ft measured depth is a trajectory somebody designed. The studio adds the oil rows to 130.0000 MMbbl, prices the concept, lays the activities out on a network and reports what it finds. It never checks the geology.

## Three estimates of one cost

The studio shows three different figures for what this development costs, and they answer three different questions.

| figure | value | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| concept capex | 2250.0000 | what the development is estimated to cost |
| cost items CAPEX | 2250.0000 | what the plan has budgeted line by line |
| facility screening estimate | 1363.3524 | a class 5 figure from type and nameplate, FPSO only |

The first two agree at 2250.0000 million USD because this plan was costed against the concept it is running. The third, 1363.3524 million USD, covers the Egina FPSO alone and is derived from the facility type and its 60000 bopd nameplate. A plan where these agree has been reconciled. A plan where they differ has a question in it, and the studio will not answer that question for you.

## A blank field is not an unknown

Flow assurance on the Deep tie-back scores 3, level Medium, with hazards Hydrates High and Wax Medium. Put 12 ppm of H2S on the same fluid and the hazards become Hydrates High, Wax Medium and Corrosion High. Without an H2S figure the corrosion screen cannot fire at all, so the blank field reads as no corrosion risk rather than as a question nobody has asked. That is a property of the screen as published, and it is a reason to check which fields the fluid card is missing before trusting a Low.

## What it does say when it cannot read

Where the studio genuinely cannot read something it says so. A plan whose reserves table cannot be read reports P50 0.0000 and completeness 89 percent rather than a plausible figure. An unreadable reserves table scores 22 percent with the error "Reserves (P50) not estimated."

## The mistake

The mistake is to treat a screening estimate as a costed number. The 1363.3524 million USD facility figure comes from a type and a nameplate, and the 2250.0000 million USD of cost items comes from seven lines somebody wrote. Both print to four decimals and they are not the same class of number. Precision on the screen says nothing about the work behind it.
