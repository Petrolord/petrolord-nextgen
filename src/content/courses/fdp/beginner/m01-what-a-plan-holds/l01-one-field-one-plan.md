# One field, one plan

A field development plan covers one field and everything that field needs to become a producing asset: its reserves, one or more development concepts, the wells and facilities a concept implies, a schedule, a cost breakdown, a risk register, and the economics all of that implies.

## Nine sections, one field

The document the studio generates checks nine sections by name, and it reports what is there and what is missing rather than filling a gap. On the EGINA plan every one of them carries something, which reads as completeness 100 percent, 9 of 9 sections.

A plan with no economics written scores 89 percent and carries the error "Total CAPEX is zero or missing." The count is nine sections, not nine chapters of prose, and a section counts when it holds a figure somebody entered.

## The headline the plan reports

Six figures come off the EGINA plan as its headline: P50 oil 130.0000 MMbbl, P50 gas 70.0000 Bcf, total CAPEX 2250.0000 million USD, NPV 2015.4123 million USD, IRR none, and 4 wells. The rate of return reads none because the plan pays 260.0000 million USD to abandon the field in production year 20, so its cash flow changes sign twice and no single rate is reportable.

## Every figure has a home

None of those six is free standing. The 130.0000 MMbbl is the arithmetic sum of two oil reservoir rows at P50, 95.0000 for Egina Main and 35.0000 for Egina Deep. The 70.0000 Bcf is a single gas row and stays in its own unit. The 2250.0000 million USD is the FPSO concept's three capex fields added up, 520.0000 for drilling, 1350.0000 for facilities and 380.0000 for subsea. The NPV of 2015.4123 million USD is what that concept is worth at 70.0000 USD a barrel on the studio's default screening terms.

## The parts have to agree

A plan is a set of numbers that must agree with each other, so the interesting question is never what a figure is but where it came from and what it was measured against. The concept's capex of 2250.0000 million USD and the cost items' CAPEX total of 2250.0000 million USD are two different numbers that happen to match here, because this plan was costed against the concept it is running. The schedule carries the same discipline: the network says the work must take 870 days, the dates typed on the activities span 933 days, and the 63 day difference is float somebody has already spent in the calendar.

## The mistake

The mistake is to read the plan as one document with one answer in it. The plan holds several answers to overlapping questions, and a reader who quotes NPV 2015.4123 million USD without knowing which capex it ran on, which price deck it priced, and which production shape it burned has quoted a number and understood none of it.
