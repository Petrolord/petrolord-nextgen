# Two concepts, one price

Read at the same 70.0000 USD a barrel, the FPSO concept is worth 2015.4123 million USD and the subsea tie-back 1013.7182, and the tie-back is the better of the two per million USD of capex it asks for.

## The two concepts side by side

| concept | drilling | facilities | subsea | total capex | annual opex | life | peak kbpd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FPSO development | 520.0000 | 1350.0000 | 380.0000 | 2250.0000 | 95.0000 | 20.0000 | 60.0000 |
| Subsea tie-back | 240.0000 | 180.0000 | 310.0000 | 730.0000 | 40.0000 | 15.0000 | 25.0000 |

These are two ways of developing one field, and almost nothing about them matches. The FPSO asks for 2250.0000 million USD and produces at a peak of 60.0000 kbpd for 20.0000 years. The tie-back asks for 730.0000 and produces at a peak of 25.0000 kbpd for 15.0000 years.

## What each earns at one price

| scenario | concept | capex | NPV | IRR | payback years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | FPSO development | 2250.0000 | 2015.4123 | none | 3.8273 |
| Tie-back base | Subsea tie-back | 730.0000 | 1013.7182 | none | 3.2035 |

The FPSO earns nearly twice the value. The tie-back earns its money faster, paying back in 3.2035 years against 3.8273, and neither row carries a rate of return at all: both concepts pay to abandon the field at the end of their lives, so both report the status multiple-roots. Both runs use the same price, the same royalty of 12.5000 percent, the same tax of 30.0000 percent and the same discount rate of 10.0000 percent, so the only things that differ between the two rows are the capex, the operating cost, the peak rate and the life that each concept carries on its own record.

## Value per million of capex

Divide each value by the capex that bought it and the ranking turns over. The tie-back returns 1.388655 per million USD of capex and the FPSO returns 0.895739. On a balance sheet with 730.0000 to spend and no more, the tie-back is the only one of the two that can be built at all. On a balance sheet that can carry 2250.0000, the FPSO leaves 2015.4123 where the tie-back would have left 1013.7182. The two measures answer different questions: one asks what the field is worth developed this way, the other asks what each million USD of the budget bought.

## The mistake

Picking the higher ratio and calling it the better development. A ratio has no size in it. The lifecycle figures say the same thing from the other side: the FPSO costs 4150.0000 over its life, made of 2250.0000 of capex and 1900.0000 of operating cost across 20.0000 years, and the tie-back costs 1330.0000, made of 730.0000 and 600.0000 across 15.0000 years. The cheaper plan is cheaper because it is smaller, and it leaves barrels behind that the larger plan produces.

## What it refuses

Neither row is a recommendation. The engine will not choose a concept, will not combine the two into a phased development, and will not tell you whether the tie-back's shorter life reflects the reservoir or the vessel it hangs off. Both figures are screening values at one price on one shape, and both change with the price at their head. The engine also reads each concept's capex from the three fields that concept carries, so a tie-back whose subsea line of 310.0000 was never typed would be screened on a smaller number than it costs.
