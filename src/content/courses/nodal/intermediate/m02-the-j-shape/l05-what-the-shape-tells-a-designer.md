# What the shape tells a designer

Four readings you can take off a tubing curve before anyone computes an operating point, and one thing the shape is never allowed to say.

{{panel:pd-vlp-explorer}}

## One: the dead column against the reservoir pressure

BONNY-7's dead column of 2570 psia stands 170 psi below its 2740 psia reservoir pressure. FORCADOS-3's 4310 psia stands 590 psi above its 3720 psia. One subtraction, no curve and no sample count. BONNY-7 lifts its own standing column and restarts itself. FORCADOS-3 does not, and every later question about that well is asked against that fact.

## Two: how deep the J is

| Well | Loaded end, psia | Minimum, psia | Friction end, psia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BONNY-7 | 2545.501142 | 1476.243252 | 12560.087474 |
| FORCADOS-3 | 4293.189726 | 2348.191408 | 3310.421637 |

Depth is the size of the restoring force, not a cosmetic property. BONNY-7 climbs to five figures, so a disturbance in rate is pushed back hard and the well is quiet. FORCADOS-3 ends BELOW where it started, so it barely pushes and it wanders.

## Three: where the bottom sits on the axis

BONNY-7's bottom is at 627.069742 stb/d on an axis running to an absolute open flow of 4324.444444 stb/d. FORCADOS-3's is at 1843.619418 stb/d on an axis of almost the same length, 4135.949669 stb/d. Most of BONNY-7's range is on the rising limb, much more of FORCADOS-3's on the falling one.

That axis came from the inflow, so two tubing curves compare only if their bounds and sample counts match. Here they do not, 37 points against 65, so the smoother picture is a fact about the plot.

## Four: the erosional ceiling, which the J knows nothing about

BONNY-7's screen, 52 lb/ft3 in a 2.875 in bore, gives erosional velocities of 13.867505, 17.334381 and 24.268134 ft/s at C 100, C 125 and C 175, for rate limits of 9620.5002, 12025.6252 and 16835.8753 bbl/d in situ. FORCADOS-3's, 41 lb/ft3 in 3.548 in, gives 15.617376 ft/s and 16500.5690 bbl/d in situ at C 100. A quoted erosional limit without its constant is not usable.

## What the shape refuses to say

Nothing on the curve is a rate the well makes, and nothing on it is a stability verdict. Which side of the bottom a well sits on takes the reservoir's curve too.

## The mistake

Quoting the far right. BONNY-7 demands 12560.087474 psia at 4324.44 stb/d against a reservoir holding 2740 psia. That is a correct evaluation of an unreachable state. It belongs in the picture to show the curve keeps climbing, and not in a report as a pressure.
