# Tubing size

Diameter is worth five powers in the friction group. That is why it is the strongest lever a designer has, and why bigger is not simply better.

{{panel:pd-vlp-explorer}}

## The fifth power

In the published dry gas construction the friction group is a Moody factor times rate squared over the FIFTH power of the inside diameter.

A 2.992 in string at 6 MMscf/d carries a group of 0.00142657, less than a 2.441 in string at 4 MMscf/d on 0.00182455. Rate up by half, friction down, because half an inch of bore is worth more than that. Hold the bore instead, 4 to 9 MMscf/d, and the group runs 0.00182455 to 0.00907182 while the Moody factor only moves from 0.01481660 to 0.01455199.

Flow area says it in velocities: 0.03249847 ft2 at 2.441 in carries 17.996304 ft/s at 9000 bbl/d, while 0.08544349 ft2 at 3.958 in carries 15.210896 ft/s at 20000 bbl/d.

## The half nobody costs in

| BONNY-7 friction constant | Minimum rate, stb/d | Minimum bhp, psia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00032 | 845.667814 | 1309.348403 |
| 0.00064 | 627.069742 | 1476.243252 |
| 0.00128 | 455.992990 | 1656.374688 |

Halving the constant, which is what a bigger string does, drops the minimum demand from 1476.243252 psia to 1309.348403 psia. That is the half everyone quotes. It also slid the bottom from 627.069742 stb/d out to 845.667814 stb/d, and the bottom is the loading threshold. A rate that sat on the rising limb of the tighter string can sit on the FALLING limb of the roomier one.

FORCADOS-3 is worse in absolute terms: friction constants of 0.0000525, 0.000105 and 0.00021 put its bottom at 2448.723537, 1843.619418 and 1367.221296 stb/d. A wide new band of falling limb, bought with a change made to reduce pressure. The field rule that oversized tubing loads up is that sentence.

## What the instrument refuses to model

Its friction constant is a pure friction lever: change it and the gravity term is untouched at every rate. A real string is not like that. Changing the bore changes velocity, which changes slip, which changes holdup, which changes the WEIGHT of the column. A larger string usually carries a heavier column at a given rate, which is a second and independent reason oversized tubing loads up. The instrument gets the direction right by half the mechanism.

It carries no velocity ceiling either, and that ceiling tightens as the bore does. In a 2.441 in bore at C 100 the erosional limit is 44.721360 ft/s at a mixture density of 5 lb/ft3 and 12.659242 ft/s at 62.4 lb/ft3, for 22365.2719 and 6330.9209 bbl/d in situ.

## The mistake

Reporting only the pressure column. The change that took 1476.243252 psia to 1309.348403 psia took the threshold from 627.069742 stb/d to 845.667814 stb/d, and a well that will decline through those rates has been handed a worse problem than the one it solved.
