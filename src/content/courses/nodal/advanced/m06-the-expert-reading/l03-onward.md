# Onward

A node solution is a small object with a long reach. Be precise about what it earns and where it hands over.

## What it earns

It turns two independent models into one testable prediction. The inflow was fitted to a production test and the outflow built from a tubing description; neither alone says what the well does. Together they say a rate and a pressure, a claim a meter can refute.

It also prices physical changes in the currency of rate: BONNY-7 gives 1424.991968 stb/d at a wellhead of 280 psia and 1319.581001 stb/d at 490 psia. And it reports a margin, 585.899719 psi of clearance above the tubing minimum on BONNY-7 against 18.717814 psi on FORCADOS-3.

## Where it hands over

Sooner or later the residual is positive everywhere and there is no crossing. The published dead case never falls below 988.172727 psi. That verdict is a change of question, not the end of the analysis, and it has two answers: lighten the column, or add energy to it.

Gas lift is the first. It moves the outflow curve down, and the node picture is unchanged: two curves, a residual, crossings, a window, a stability question. What is new is that injection depth and rate are choices.

A pump is the second. It keeps the inflow relation untouched, because what the reservoir gives at a sandface pressure does not care what stands above it, and replaces the outflow curve with a pump curve.

## The limit worth remembering

None of that improves the decision. A perfectly solved node on a well that should not have been drilled is a precise account of rate nobody wanted.

## What you have finished

How many crossings there are, which one holds, whether the solver can see them, and how far the answer moves. Read the residual rather than the verdict, and report a margin beside every point.
