# Onward

The Pore Pressure ladder is complete. Three tiers, one well, and a chain that now runs unbroken from a density log to a defensible mud program: a frame built by hand, a prognosis that recovers its well's encoded truth exactly, and a window in the driller's units, calibrated, priced and cross-checked by a second method.

## What you can now do

Stated as capabilities, because that is what a certification means. You can build and audit a pressure frame from first principles, with the unit and gravity discipline that survives a 0.01 MPa tolerance. You can run an Eaton prognosis and, more importantly, defend one: trend, exponent, screening, onset rule, each named and priced. You can convert a pressure model into floor, ceiling and window, on a stated datum, and spend a window against margins and uncertainties without double-counting. You can run Bowers in both directions, choose between loading and unloading on evidence, and read a cross-check's residual back to its source. And you can write the four-clause sentence a well plan carries, with every clause auditable.

That skill set has a name on a rig: the pressure half of well planning. The other half, casing design, kick tolerance, wellbore stability, belongs to drilling engineering proper, and you now speak its input language fluently.

## Where the ladder connects

Sideways, first. The Well Data course made the logs this course consumed trustworthy; Petrophysics decided which points were shale; this course turned them into pressure. The Rock Physics ladder runs the other side of the same street: where this course read velocity as EFFECTIVE STRESS, that one reads it as fluid and frame moduli, and the two readings meet in every velocity anomaly you will ever interpret, a gas effect and an overpressure effect can look alike in the sonic, and telling them apart uses both courses at once. The Basin course closes the loop from the far end: its decompaction arithmetic is where an uplifted well's sigma_max comes from, and its heat-flow column is why source rocks generate the late fluids that cause the unloading this course taught you to detect.

Upward, the geoscience ladder itself: ten applications, each with this same three-tier structure, each ending in a capstone graded against engine truth. If you have climbed this one, you know the shape of all of them: the frame tier owns the arithmetic, the middle tier owns the method, the top tier owns the decision, and every number is derived or it does not ship.

## The habits that transfer

Beyond the machinery, the course has been installing habits, and they are the part that transfers to problems no course covers. Derive every number; a drafted value is a hypothesis until an engine or a hand chain confirms it. Close whatever loop the data affords, and state its strength honestly. Price every choice in the units of the decision it feeds, megapascals for geoscientists, kg/m3 for the rig. Publish the band, not the compromise line, and name in advance what evidence would collapse it. And when two independent routes agree, trace the residual anyway, because knowing WHY you are right is the only version of right that survives the next well.

## The capstone, and after

The final exam gates the capstone; the capstone grades six numbers you have now computed, checked and re-derived from three directions each. Sit it when the six routes feel like recall rather than reconstruction.

After it: the certification closes the Pore Pressure ladder, and the natural next climbs are the Rock Physics ladder, for the other reading of velocity, or the Basin and Charge ladder, for the burial histories this course kept borrowing. Wherever next, the standard travels with you: every number derived, every choice defended, every uncertainty priced.
