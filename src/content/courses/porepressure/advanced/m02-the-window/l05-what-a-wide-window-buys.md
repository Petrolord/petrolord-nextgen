# What a wide window buys

This well's window is wide, 724.82 kg/m3 at TD, 601 kg/m3 operationally from the 2500 m shoe. This lesson spends that width deliberately, itemising what a real mud program buys with window room, so the number stops being abstract. The purpose is calibration of judgement: you should finish knowing what 600 buys, what 100 buys, and what 50 forces.

## Purchase one: margins

The first spending is safety. A kick margin, mud carried above the floor so that a swab or a small prognosis error does not put the well underbalanced: 30 to 60 kg/m3 is common practice. A trip margin for the same reason on pipe movement. Together, call it 60 to 100 kg/m3 off the bottom of the window.

On the ceiling side, dynamic effects spend from the top. Circulating adds friction: the equivalent circulating density downhole exceeds the static mud weight by tens of kg/m3 in normal geometry, more in slim hole. Surge while running casing spends similarly. Call it 50 to 100 off the top.

A 600 window funds all of it with 400-plus to spare. A 150 window funds it barely, with nothing left for error. A 100 window cannot fund both sides, and something gives: slower pipe speeds, managed-pressure equipment, or another string of casing.

## Purchase two: uncertainty

Second spending: the walls themselves are estimates. This course has priced this well's own wall uncertainties precisely. Exponent: the floor moves 87.22 kg/m3 between n 3 and n 1.2 at TD. K: about 54 kg/m3 per five points at TD. Trend: the Professional tier's fitted-trend error converts to $6429177.480013587 / 40207.265 = 159.9$ kg/m3 of floor error at TD. A defended prognosis carries the spread; the window must accommodate the spread's width, not the central line.

Here, all three spreads stack inside the room with ease. The discipline this buys on hard wells: when the stacked uncertainty exceeds the window, the honest statement is not a thinner central line, it is that the well as designed cannot be certified drillable, and the response is measurement, a pressure point, a leak-off test, a recalibration, to shrink the spread until it fits.

## Purchase three: simplicity

Third spending, the quiet one: a wide window buys a simple well. One mud system from shoe to TD, no interim weight-up, no managed pressure drilling, fewer casing strings, wider bit and completion choices. Each of those is cost and failure-rate removed. Much of the value of a good pressure prognosis is realised here, in the wells that turn out NOT to need heroics, which is invisible in any incident count precisely because the incidents do not happen.

The converse also deserves a sentence: a window can be too comfortable to respect. Crews on benign wells drift toward the floor to maximise rate of penetration, and the margin quietly becomes the plan. The floor of this well still marks a real formation pressure; 724 kg/m3 of ceiling room does not change what happens if the mud slips under 1179 at TD.

## What none of this changes

The window is a static hydraulic statement. It says nothing about wellbore stability, hole cleaning, differential sticking against permeable beds at high overbalance, or the chemistry between mud and shale. A mud can sit beautifully inside the pressure window and fail the well by other physics; carrying maximum overbalance because the ceiling allows it is how differential sticking happens, the failure the Associate tier's opening lesson filed under too much mud weight. The window is necessary, never sufficient, and the mud program is where all the constraints meet.

## Worked example

Build the mud program ledger for the 2500 m to TD section, spending the window explicitly. Static requirement: floor at TD, 1179.10 kg/m3. Add kick and trip margin, 50: minimum practical mud 1229.1. Ceiling at shoe: 1780.09. Subtract ECD allowance, 60, and surge allowance, 40: maximum practical static mud 1680.1. Practical interval: 1229.1 to 1680.1, still 451 kg/m3 wide. Choose, say, 1250 kg/m3: 71 above the floor at TD, 530 under the shoe ceiling static, 430 under it circulating. Every number in the ledger traces to a curve this course computed or a margin this lesson named, which is what a defensible mud recommendation looks like at this level of idealisation.

## Exercise

Rerun the ledger under the n 1.2 calibration, floor 1091.88 at TD, and answer: does the exponent choice change the RECOMMENDED mud, and what does that imply about arguing over calibration on wide-window wells?

Self check: minimum practical mud becomes $1091.88 + 50 = 1141.9$; the ceiling side is unchanged at 1680.1; the interval is 1141.9 to 1680.1. A 1250 kg/m3 mud sits comfortably inside BOTH calibrations' practical intervals, so on this well the recommendation need not change at all. That is the honest resolution of the exponent argument on a wide window: the calibrations disagree about the floor by 87 kg/m3, and the window is wide enough that the disagreement does not bind. Module 3 sharpens exactly this: when the spread fits, document both and drill; when it does not, the argument must be settled by data, not preference.
