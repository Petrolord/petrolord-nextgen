# Defending a trend

On this well the right trend is knowable, which made the last two lessons possible. On a real well no answer key exists, so the question changes from which trend is right to which trend can be defended. This lesson is the checklist, each item anchored to something this course has already computed.

## Check one: the shallow section must stay quiet

Run the prognosis and look FIRST at the section everyone agrees is normally pressured, usually the shallow hole that drilled easily with light mud. A defensible trend returns approximately hydrostatic pressure there. The fitted trend failed this check spectacularly, reporting an onset at 120 m and 1.6 MPa of overpressure by 1000 m in section with no mechanism.

This check is powerful because it uses drilling history as data: hole already drilled is a pressure measurement of a rough kind. Overpressure reported where a previous section drilled fine with seawater-weight mud is not a discovery, it is a trend artefact, and the burden of proof sits entirely on the trend.

## Check two: the parameters must be physical

A trend has three numbers, and each has a physical meaning that bounds it. The mudline value is the transit time of uncompacted mud, and 650 or 656 us/m both sit in the plausible range; a fit returning 400 there is describing something other than surface mud. The matrix value is the fastest the fully compacted rock can be, bounded by mineralogy near 180 to 220 us/m for shale. The decay constant sets how fast burial stiffens the rock; values far outside a basin's usual range mean the fit chased structure that is not compaction.

Note what this check would NOT have caught here: 650 and 0.7 are individually as physical as 656 and 0.6. Parameter plausibility screens gross errors only. The dangerous errors are plausible ones, which is why the checklist has five items rather than one.

## Check three: the picks must be interrogated, not just fitted

The fit was perfect; the picks were the problem. Ask of any pick set: are they all in confirmed shale, in good hole, away from organic-rich intervals? Do they span the depth range the trend will be used over, or is the deep half extrapolated from shallow control? Do picks from a neighbouring well share this well's burial history, or has erosion or uplift shifted its trend? One biased subpopulation of picks bends the whole fit, and the fit's own statistics will not complain, as the zero residuals here proved.

The Associate tier's rule, twelve picks that you can defend beat forty that you cannot, is the same rule seen from the fitting side.

## Check four: perturb the trend and report the spread

Since the trend cannot be certified, an honest prognosis quantifies its leverage: rerun with the trend's plausible alternatives and report the pressure spread, exactly as this module ran both of this well's trends. If the shallow-pick trend and the header trend bracket 47.4 to 53.8 MPa at TD, that spread IS the trend uncertainty, and stating it is more professional than silently picking one and being precise about it.

The three-stage estimate from last lesson makes this cheap: $n\,\epsilon\,(S - P_h)$ per candidate, no reruns needed for screening.

## Check five: calibrate the moment calibration exists

The first measured pressure, kick, or connection-gas trend in the new well outranks every prior argument. A defensible prognosis states, in advance, what measurement would change which choice: if the RFT at 3100 m reads near 33.3 MPa the header trend stands; near 39.6, the fitted one was right after all. Committing to the test before the data arrives is what separates calibration from curve-fitting after the fact.

On this synthetic well the ramp played the role of the measurement, and the header trend passed. That is the loop of module 3 restated as fieldwork.

## The checklist is the deliverable

A prognosis defended this way ships as: the chosen trend with its parameters and pick basis; the shallow-section quietness demonstration; the spread under alternative trends; and the stated calibration triggers. Four items, none expensive, and a reviewer can now disagree with reasons rather than with taste. The capstone does not grade the checklist; every well after the capstone does.

## Worked example

Apply check one numerically, as a reviewer. A prognosis on this well, submitted with the fitted trend, quotes pressures only below 2500 m, where its numbers are wrong but not absurd, 53.8 against 47.4 at TD. Nothing shown looks alarming. The reviewer asks for the one number the report omitted: pressure at 1000 m. It reads hydrostatic plus 1.5885506433234309 MPa, in section that drilled with 1030 kg/m3 mud and no losses or gains, which is impossible: that mud could not have balanced 1.6 MPa of overpressure at that depth. The trend is dead in one question. Reports that only show the interesting section are hiding the section that would convict them.
