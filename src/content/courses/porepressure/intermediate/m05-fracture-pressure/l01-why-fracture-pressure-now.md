# Why fracture pressure now

The Associate tier's map of the three pressures ended with a promise: fracture pressure exists, it is not computable from the frame alone, and the Professional tier adds it once a pore pressure curve exists for it to stand on. The prognosis now exists. This module pays the debt.

## What fracture pressure is

Fracture pressure is the wellbore pressure at which the formation fails in tension and takes fluid from the well. Push mud pressure past it and the rock parts, mud disappears into the crack, the annulus level drops, and the well can go from losses to a kick in the same hour, since losing mud lowers the pressure the well is exerting everywhere else.

It is the upper wall of everything a driller does. The pore pressure says how heavy the mud must be; the fracture pressure says how heavy it may be. The Associate tier could bound the ceiling only by the overburden, an absolute physical limit far above the practical one. The fracture pressure is the practical one.

## Why it needs the pore pressure

The reason the Associate tier could not compute it: rock fails when the stress pushing outward exceeds the confinement holding it shut plus what strength the rock adds. The confinement that matters for a vertical well is the smallest horizontal stress, and horizontal stress is not an independent quantity; it is generated by the rock pushing sideways as the vertical load squeezes it, and the vertical load the GRAINS feel is the effective stress, overburden minus pore pressure.

So the chain is: pore pressure sets effective vertical stress, effective vertical stress sets effective horizontal stress through the rock's elastic response, and effective horizontal stress plus the pore pressure back again sets the pressure the wellbore must exceed to split the rock. Pore pressure enters twice, and without it the chain has no first link. That is the dependency, and it is why this module sits after the prognosis rather than after the overburden.

The chain also predicts something that surprises people, and the next lesson derives it exactly: raising the pore pressure RAISES the fracture pressure. An overpressured zone is easier to kick and also, partially, easier to protect, because the same overpressure that endangers the well from below props up its ceiling.

## What form this course uses

The engine computes the coefficient form:

$$FP = K\,(S - PP) + PP$$

with $K$ a stress-ratio coefficient. It is the workhorse form of fracture gradient practice, common to Eaton's fracture method and to Matthews and Kelly, who differ only in where $K$ comes from. The capstone specifies $K$ from Poisson's ratio at $\nu = 0.4$, giving $K = \nu/(1-\nu) = 2/3$, and grades the TD value of 76.55157117548856 MPa.

Read the form once, mechanically: take the effective stress $S - PP$, keep the fraction $K$ of it as horizontal confinement, add back the pore pressure. Everything the module does unpacks those three motions.

## What this module will not claim

Honesty about scope, since fracture pressure is a field with real depth beyond this tier. The coefficient form predicts fracture INITIATION in a vertical well from vertical stress alone. It knows nothing of tectonic stress, of anisotropy, of wellbore inclination, of the difference between initiating a fracture and propagating one, and its $K$ is a calibration parameter dressed as an elastic constant, a costume the last lesson takes off. Leak-off tests, the field's actual measurements, calibrate all of that into $K$ in practice.

The module teaches the form because it is what the engine and most quick-look software run, because its structure, floor and ceiling coupled through effective stress, is the true structure, and because knowing exactly what it omits is the difference between using it and believing it.

## Worked example

Verify the promised surprise with the numbers already in hand, before the formula lesson. This well at TD, true trend: $FP = \tfrac{2}{3}(91.12306695073282 - 47.408579625) + 47.408579625 = 76.55157117548856$ MPa. Fitted trend, where pore pressure is 6.43 MPa higher: $FP = \tfrac{2}{3}(91.12306695073282 - 53.83775710501359) + 53.83775710501359 = 78.69463033549308$ MPa. The pore pressure rose 6.429177480013587 MPa and the fracture pressure rose 2.143059160004525, exactly one third of it. Higher floor, higher ceiling, by a fixed fraction.
