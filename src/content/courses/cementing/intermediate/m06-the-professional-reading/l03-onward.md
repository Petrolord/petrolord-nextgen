# Onward

Where the pressure calculation stops and the mud starts.

## What this tier gave you

Where every fluid is at every moment of the job, what the pumps see, what the weakest formation sees, and whether there is a rate that satisfies both.

## What it did not give you

Any statement about whether the cement isolated anything.

Every number in this tier would be identical for a casing perfectly centred in the hole and for one lying flat against the low side. The engine has no term for where the casing is in the hole, and the placement simulation is a one-dimensional column calculation from end to end.

That is the single biggest thing it cannot see, and it is the next tier.

## The Expert tier

Standoff. How far off centre the casing is, expressed as a fraction of the clearance, and what decides it.

A bow spring deflects under the lateral load and the load is the buoyed weight times the spacing times the sine of the inclination. Between two of them the pipe sags, and the sag goes as the FOURTH power of the span.

Which means the thing that binds is almost never the centralizer. On both of this course's wells the minimum standoff comes from the mid-span sag and not from the deflection at the centralizer, and halving the spacing helps far more than doubling the spring.

Then the spacing that would just reach the API 67 percent target, bisected. On the slant well it is 13.05523892558449 m and on the horizontal one 11.187558579905271, and the job runs 12 on both. So the same centralizer programme passes on one well and fails on the other.

And then a checklist of five items with no overall percentage on it, and a lesson about why there is no percentage.

## Where else this goes

**Drilling Fluids and Hydraulics** owns the loss kernel and the rheology fits this tier imports.

**Geomechanics** owns the fracture gradient that field 5 of the capstone is compared against.

**Well Integrity and P and A** asks whether the sheath is still a barrier decades later, which is where a mud channel finally shows up.

## One thing to keep

Bisect the edge and quote the number. An answer of the form "somewhere between 0.02 and 0.03" is not a result anybody can check, and the bisection costs eighty runs of a calculation that takes a millisecond.
