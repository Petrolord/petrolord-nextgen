# Choosing a programme

Four questions, in order, and the rate is the last of them.

{{panel:cm-placement-explorer}}

## One: where does the cement have to reach

A regulatory requirement, a lap over the previous shoe, or an isolation requirement across a specific zone. This sets the top of cement and therefore the volume.

It is the least negotiable of the four.

## Two: what density profile gets it there

Neat, or lead and tail, or more slurries than that. This is where the free fall and the circulating density are actually decided, and it is decided before anybody thinks about a rate.

The comparison in this module is the whole argument: the two programmes place the same volume to the same top and differ on every pressure in the job.

## Three: is there a rate window

Compute both edges. If the window is open, note its width and put the design rate near the middle of it. If it is closed, go back to question two.

A window that is open but narrow is a warning in itself. The slant well's neat programme has a window 0.0001944956449708296 cubic metres a second wide, which is a fifth of a percent of the rate. No pump can be held that accurately and no ECD estimate is that good.

## Four: what rate

Inside the window, and then adjusted for the things this engine does not model: the mixing rate at surface, the annular velocity needed for mud removal, and the thickening time.

## The mud removal pull, which pulls the other way again

The Expert tier adds a third constraint on the same knob: annular velocity has to be high enough to displace the mud, and the checklist wants at least 0.3 m/s.

So the rate is being pulled by three things, two of which want it high and one of which wants it low. On this job the velocity constraint is not binding, because 0.02 cubic metres a second gives 1.4973771889585683 m/s in the cased annulus and 1.476222714457212 in the open hole, both five times the target.

## What a programme document should carry

The top of cement and its justification. The fluid list with densities, volumes and rheologies. The excess assumed. The design rate AND the window it sits in. The peak circulating density and the leak-off value it is compared against. And whether the job free falls.

Six of those seven come out of this engine. The seventh, the leak-off value, comes from the Geomechanics course and from the actual test at the shoe.
