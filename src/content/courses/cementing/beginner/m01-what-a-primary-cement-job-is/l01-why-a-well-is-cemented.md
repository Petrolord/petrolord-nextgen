# Why a well is cemented

The steel is only half of a barrier. The other half is what is behind it.

## The idea

A casing string is a pipe with a hole around it. Run it and cement it, and the annulus between the steel and the rock becomes a solid ring. Run it and do not cement it, and that annulus is an open channel from the reservoir to surface.

## What the cement is for

**Zonal isolation.** Keeping the fluid in one formation out of every other formation, and out of the annulus. This is the reason that matters.

**Support.** Holding the casing in the hole and carrying part of its weight, so the wellhead does not carry all of it.

**Protection.** Keeping corrosive formation water off the outside of the steel for the life of the well.

**A foundation.** Giving the next hole section something competent to be drilled out of, and giving the shoe track something to hold pressure against during the leak-off test.

## What a failure looks like

Not usually a dramatic one. A cement job that leaves a channel of mud on one side of the pipe does not announce itself. It shows up years later as sustained casing pressure at the wellhead, or as water in a well that should be dry, or as a regulator refusing to sign off an abandonment.

That delay is the whole difficulty of the subject. The job is done in a few hours and judged decades later.

## What this course computes

Three things.

**Volumes**, in the Associate tier: how much of each fluid to order and pump.

**Placement**, in the Professional tier: where each fluid is at every moment of the job, what pressure the pumps see, and what the equivalent circulating density does at the weakest point in the hole.

**Centralization and quality**, in the Expert tier: whether the casing is centred well enough for the cement to displace the mud all the way round it.

## What it does not compute

Cement chemistry. Not one line of this engine knows what is in the slurry. Thickening time, compressive strength development, free water, fluid loss, gas migration after placement: all real, all decisive, none of them here.

This engine places a fluid of a stated density and rheology in a stated geometry. What that fluid becomes afterwards is a laboratory question.
