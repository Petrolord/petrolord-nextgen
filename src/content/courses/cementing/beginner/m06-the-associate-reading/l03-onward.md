# Onward

Where the volume sheet stops.

## What this tier gave you

A purchase order and a pumping schedule for any full casing string in any hole geometry: how much of each fluid, how many sacks, and how long it takes.

## What it did not give you

Any statement at all about whether the job will work.

The volume sheet cannot tell a slant well from a horizontal one. It gave both of this course's wells the same slurry volume, and one of them has a shoe 1300 m shallower in true vertical depth than the other.

## The Professional tier

Placement. Where every fluid is at every moment, on a flow path parametrised by pumped volume rather than by depth.

Then the U-tube: annulus head plus friction less inside head, which is what the pumps actually see. When that goes negative the column falls under its own weight, the surface pressure reads zero, and the rate stops being yours to choose.

Then the circulating density at the weakest exposed formation, which rises through the job and peaks near the end.

And then the result the tier is built on: the pump rate is a straight trade between those two. Faster cures the free fall and raises the circulating density. On one of this course's wells with one of its two programmes, the two constraints cross and there is no rate that satisfies both.

## The Expert tier

Whether the mud got out of the way.

Standoff, the API 67 percent, the bow spring and the rigid blade, and the sag of the pipe between two centralizers, which turns out to be what actually binds. The spacing that would just reach the target, bisected. And a five-item checklist with no overall percentage on it, because a cementing model that returns an efficiency of 87 percent is claiming something no model of this kind can know.

## Where else this goes

**Casing and Tubing Design** sized the string this job places cement around, and its cementing collapse case is exactly the load this course's cement column applies.

**Drilling Fluids and Hydraulics** supplies the loss kernel the friction term uses and the rheology fits that feed it.

**Geomechanics** supplies the fracture gradient the circulating density is compared against.

**Well Integrity and P and A** asks, twenty years later, whether the sheath this job placed is still a barrier.

## One thing to keep

Write the excess next to the volume. A slurry volume with no excess figure attached is not a result, and the answer is linear in it.
