# The story so far

Five modules, two terms, and the one that binds is the absence between the centralizers.

## The claim

Standoff decides whether the mud leaves, and standoff is decided by the span rather than by the device.

## What each module established

**Module 1.** Cement takes the wide side of an eccentric annulus, because resistance in a slot goes as the inverse cube of the gap, and it leaves the narrow side as a mud channel that shows up decades later. Standoff is the narrow gap over the concentric clearance, running from zero at the wall to one at centre, and it is one less the eccentricity. The clearances here are 0.021348699999999998 m cased and 0.019049999999999997 m in the open hole. The API 67 percent is a convention, and the same 0.67 appears again as the standoff at which a bow spring's restoring force is quoted, which is a different use of the same number.

**Module 2.** A bow spring is a spring and a rigid centralizer is a spacer. The lateral load is the buoyed weight times the spacing times the sine of the inclination, which is 345.6133299031847 N per metre times the span at 1440 kg/m3. The spring rate is the restoring force over the deflection at which it was quoted, which puts the CLEARANCE inside the spring rate, so one device has different rates in different hole sizes. And a rigid blade's standoff is a pure ratio of diameters that it can never exceed.

**Module 3.** The mid-span sag is the fixed-end beam deflection, quartic in the spacing against the centralizer's linear. At 6 m the sag is a fifth of the centralizer deflection and at 12 m it is 1.6 times it. On BOTH wells the reported minimum is the mid-span value, and on the horizontal well the centralizer value passes the target while the reported one fails it. The sag reaches the whole clearance at 17.021127200569346 m at 90 degrees and 19.009532790549464 at 40, which is where the standoff hits exactly zero. And heavier mud IMPROVES standoff, because buoyancy reduces the lateral load.

**Module 4.** The required spacing is bisected, with a guard that returns NULL rather than the tightest spacing when the target cannot be reached. The two wells need 13.05523892558449 m and 11.187558579905271, and the job runs 12 on both, so the same programme passes one and fails the other. The standoff calculation runs on the NOMINAL bore while the volume calculation runs on the washed-out one, which is a decision the engine makes silently. And tripling the restoring force buys less than dropping the spacing from 12 m to 10.

**Module 5.** Five items, counted, with each one carrying the number that decided it, and no weighted score, because the weights would be invented and a percentage would hide which item failed. Annular velocity is the third pull on the pump rate and on this job it is slack. And nine things are taught here and certified nowhere.

## The numbers to carry

- Standoff is the narrow gap over the concentric clearance, and it is one less the eccentricity.
- The lateral load and the sag both carry the sine of the inclination, so a vertical well has no standoff problem.
- The deflection is linear in the spacing and the sag is quartic.
- The mid-span value always binds.
- The spring rate has the clearance inside it.

## The one sentence

The centralizer is not what binds; the span between two of them is, and it binds as a fourth power.
