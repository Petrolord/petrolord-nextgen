# Onward

Where the cement job ends and the well's life begins.

## What the three tiers gave you

A purchase order and a pumping schedule. A placement simulation, a pressure history, a circulating density at the weakest formation and a rate window. And a standoff profile, a required spacing and a five-item checklist.

## What they did not give you

Whether the cement set, whether it bonded, whether the mud left, and whether the well is isolated.

Those four decide whether the job worked, and this course computes none of them. It says so in its own last module rather than in a footnote, and its go-live migration refuses to grade any of them.

## What this course is for

Making the decisions that CAN be made before the job, correctly, on stated assumptions.

How much to order, and with what excess. Which fluid programme. What rate, and whether one exists. How many centralizers, and where.

Get those four right and the job might still fail. Get any of them wrong and it certainly will.

## Where else this goes

**Casing and Tubing Design** sized the string, and its cementing collapse case is exactly the column this course places. The two courses meet at the same annulus from opposite sides.

**Drilling Fluids and Hydraulics** owns the loss kernel this course imports and the rheology fits that feed it, and its hole cleaning module computes the cuttings bed the standoff calculation ignores.

**Geomechanics** owns the fracture gradient the circulating density is compared against, and the breakout that made the hole bigger than the bit.

**Torque, Drag and Casing Wear** computes whether a string with that many centralizers on it will reach bottom at all.

**Completion Design** takes the cemented production casing and builds on it.

**Well Integrity and P and A** asks, decades later, whether the sheath is still a barrier, and it is where a mud channel from this job finally becomes visible.

## Three habits to keep

**Write the excess next to the volume.** The answer is linear in a number nobody measured, and a slurry volume with no excess attached is not a result.

**Bisect the edge.** An answer of the form "somewhere between 0.02 and 0.03" cannot be checked by anybody else, and the bisection costs eighty runs of a millisecond calculation.

**Compute the sag before choosing the centralizer.** The device is what you buy and the span is what binds, and the arithmetic that tells them apart takes two minutes.

All three are the same habit in different clothes: find out which number the answer actually depends on, before spending any effort on the others.
