# Cement follows the wide side

An off-centre pipe makes two annuli, and only one of them gets cemented.

{{panel:cm-standoff-explorer}}

## The picture

A casing string lying against one side of the hole. On the wide side there is a large crescent of annulus; on the narrow side, a thin gap.

Pump cement up that annulus and it goes where the resistance is least. Resistance in a slot goes roughly as the inverse cube of the gap, so a narrow side half as wide as the wide side offers eight times the resistance per unit length.

The cement takes the wide side. The mud on the narrow side stays where it is.

## What that leaves

A channel of mud running the length of the interval, on one side of the pipe, from bottom to top.

It is continuous, it is connected to whatever is below it, and it will eventually be connected to whatever is above it. It is a flow path around the barrier, and the barrier looks fine on every other measure.

## Why it does not show up

The volume balance is unaffected: the same cement went into the same annulus and the top of cement is where it should be.

The pressure record is unaffected: the friction of an eccentric annulus differs from a concentric one, but not by enough to see on a chart.

A bond log sees it, if one is run and if the channel is on the side the tool looks at.

The well sees it years later.

## The three things that fix it

**Centralization.** Reduce the eccentricity so the two sides are more nearly equal. That is what this tier computes.

**Casing movement.** Rotate or reciprocate the string during the job, so no side of the pipe is permanently the narrow side. More effective than centralization on many wells, and not modelled here.

**Fluid design.** A spacer with the right rheology and a turbulent or effective-laminar displacement regime scours better than a plug of the same density. Not modelled here either.

## Which means what this tier gives you

One of the three, quantified precisely, and the honest statement that it is one of three.

A well-centralized job with the casing sitting still and a poorly designed spacer can still channel. A poorly centralized job with rotation and a good spacer often does not.

## The one that always applies

Gravity. On a deviated well the casing lies on the low side unless something holds it up, and the narrow side is at the bottom of the hole where the cuttings bed also is.

That is why every number in this tier contains the sine of the inclination, and why a vertical well has no standoff problem at all.

## Exercise

A pipe is centred so that the narrow gap is half the wide gap.

Using the inverse-cube rule, estimate the ratio of the flow rates the two sides would carry under the same pressure gradient, and say what fraction of the total goes down the narrow side.
