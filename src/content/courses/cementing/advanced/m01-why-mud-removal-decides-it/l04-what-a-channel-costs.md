# What a channel costs

The failure mode, its timescale, and why it is the expensive one.

## What a channel is

A continuous path of un-displaced mud through the cement sheath, usually on the low side of a deviated hole, running the length of a poorly centralized interval.

Mud in a channel does not set. It dehydrates over months into a permeable, weak filter cake, and the channel becomes a flow path.

## What flows through it

**Formation water into a hydrocarbon zone**, or the reverse, defeating the isolation the job existed to provide.

**Gas up the annulus**, which appears at surface as sustained casing pressure: the annulus is bled down and it comes back.

**Injected fluid out of zone**, on an injector, which is a containment problem and a regulatory one.

## The timescale

Months to decades. That is what makes it the expensive failure.

Nothing on the job says it happened. The volumes balanced, the plug bumped, the pressure test held. The next section was drilled and the well was completed and produced for years.

By the time it appears, the well is producing, the casing above is worn, and the remedy is a remedial squeeze through perforations, which is expensive, unreliable and sometimes impossible.

## What the pressure test does not catch

A pressure test loads the casing and the shoe. It does not load the sheath along its length, and a channel that runs from 1400 m to 2800 m is not tested by anything a rig does after the cement sets.

## What a bond log catches

A cement bond log measures the acoustic coupling between the casing and what is behind it, and a variable-density or segmented tool can see azimuthal variation, which is exactly what a channel is.

It is the only routine measurement that sees the failure, it is not always run, and it is run over a limited interval when it is.

## Which is why this tier exists

Because the alternative to computing the standoff before the job is finding out about it afterwards, and afterwards is the wrong time by a factor of a thousand in cost.

Twenty minutes of centralizer arithmetic against a remedial squeeze on a producing well is not a close comparison, and it is one of the few places in drilling engineering where the analysis is unambiguously cheaper than the consequence.

## The honest limit of the argument

Standoff is one of three levers and the model has only that one. A job that computes 80 percent standoff and is pumped with the casing stationary behind a poorly designed spacer can still channel.

So the arithmetic is necessary and it is not sufficient, and this tier says so in its last module rather than in a footnote.
