# Naming the controlling resistance

The reason to assemble a coefficient out of five parts is to find out which part is in charge. That one word decides where money goes: a bigger fan, a cleaner service, a different tube metal, a shorter cleaning interval. So the engine names it, along with the runner up and the gap between them.

## The verdict on six cases

| case | controlling | runner up | margin, percent of the controlling term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the studio case | outsideFilm | insideFouling | 51.612903 |
| ORON | outsideFilm | insideFouling | 44.757033 |
| published case 1 | outsideFilm | insideFouling | 51.612903 |
| published case 2 | insideFilm | insideFouling | 70.000000 |
| published case 3 | outsideFilm | insideFilm | 71.970982 |
| published case 4 | outsideFilm | insideFilm | 68.047035 |

Every one of those six is reported as clear of the declared threshold. Read the middle two columns together: on four of the six the runner up is a fouling allowance, and on two it is the other film. Those are different situations under the same one word verdict.

## Why the runner up is on the answer at all

A verdict is a comparison, and a comparison without its second place is not reportable. If the controlling term leads by a wide margin, the word is worth acting on. If it leads by a sliver, the word is an accident of two numbers that were nearly equal, and acting on it spends money on the wrong side of the tube.

So the engine reports three things where a simpler tool would report one. Which term is largest, which is next, and the gap between them as a percentage of the largest. A reader who quotes only the first of those has thrown away the part that says whether to believe it.

There is a fourth thing on the answer, and it is the simplest. The engine states whether the margin is clear of the threshold this module declares, as a plain yes or no, so a reader need not remember the threshold. Where the answer is no, a note comes with it, and the next module is about that case.

## What the word is worth on the studio case

On the studio exchanger the controlling term is the outside film and the runner up is the inside fouling allowance, with a margin of 51.612903 percent. That is a comfortable lead, and the engineering reading is that the shell side is where this exchanger is limited. A better inside film would buy little here.

Notice what the verdict does not say. It does not say the outside film can be improved, or that improving it is cheap, or that the fouling allowance beside it was well chosen. It says which term is largest in this stack at these conditions. Everything else is a judgement a person makes.

It is also a verdict about one operating point. The stack was assembled at particular flows, fouling allowances and a tube count, and each of those is an input a reader can change. Move the tube count and the inside film moves with it, which can change the verdict. So the word belongs to the case rather than to the exchanger.
