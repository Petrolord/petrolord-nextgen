# Which area a coefficient is referred to

A tube has two surfaces and they have different sizes. So a coefficient built across that tube has to say which of the two it is written against, and one that does not say is not a number anybody can use. This engine says it on every answer, in words, on a key that is part of the return.

## The engine states it, every time

The coefficient this module computes is referred to the outside tube surface, and the answer carries that as text: outside tube surface (do). It is not a footnote in a manual and it is not a convention a reader has to remember. It arrives beside the number.

There is a concrete reason for that here. A second export in this engines package is also an overall coefficient, and that one is referred to a stated bore instead. The two are different quantities on the same tube, and each reports its own reference so a value already in hand can be identified.

## Why a reference area is not a detail

The duty through a wall is fixed by physics. What changes with the reference is how that duty is written down. A coefficient referred to the outside surface times the outside area gives the same UA as one referred to the bore times the bore area. Both statements are true and the two coefficients are different numbers.

So the failure mode is not an obviously wrong answer. It is an answer that looks entirely reasonable and is wrong by the ratio of two areas. On the studio tube that ratio is not small. A coefficient taken from one convention and used in the other carries that error straight into the surface, and the surface is what gets bought.

The quantity that escapes the problem is the product of the coefficient and the area. UA has no reference to state, because the area in it is the area the coefficient belongs to, and the two conventions give the same UA on the same exchanger. That is why ratings are often written in UA, and the Expert tier of this course rates a machine that way.

## The habit this builds

Never write down a U without writing its reference in the same breath. When a coefficient arrives from a vendor, a textbook table or another engine in this package, the first question is which area it belongs to, and the second is whether the area you are about to multiply it by is that one.

This module makes the first question easy by answering it unasked. Other sources will not, and a coefficient quoted with no reference is a number that has to be sent back rather than used.

There is one more place the habit pays. The two inside resistances in this stack are converted onto the outside surface before they are added, which is the next lesson. A stack that mixes surfaces adds numbers that are not comparable.
