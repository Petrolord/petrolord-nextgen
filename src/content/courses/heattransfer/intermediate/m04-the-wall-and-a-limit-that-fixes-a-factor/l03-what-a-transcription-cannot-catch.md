# What a transcription cannot catch

There is a class of error that a second file cannot find, however carefully the second file is written. The wall term is the clearest example of it in this module. The previous lesson showed the limit and this one is about why that limit outranks an agreement between two implementations.

## Two files can agree and both be wrong

Take the factor of two in the cylindrical wall expression and move it, the same way in the engine and in the oracle that checks the engine. The two files go on agreeing with each other perfectly, every published row still matches, and neither of them agrees with the flat plate.

That is the sentence to carry out of this tier. A check comparing two implementations of one expression can only find a difference between them. It cannot find a shared mistake, because a shared mistake produces no difference to find. A suite that goes green on a moved factor has told you it cannot see that factor.

## What an independent route buys

The oracle behind the published coefficient rows does not transcribe this engine. It builds the stack on each term's own area and refers the result to the outside only at the end, and it takes the wall term by quadrature rather than by a logarithm. Its agreement with the engine is worth something: two routes that could have differed did not.

But route independence has a limit of its own. Two routes can still share an assumption, and the factor of two in that expression is exactly the kind of thing two routes written by the same hand share. That is why the analytic limit sits above both of them.

The same argument applies to a pinned number. Several constants in this module are pinned by their literal value in the engine gate. Pinning a number does not make it right. It makes changing it a reviewed act instead of a silent one, which is worth having and is a different thing from evidence. Move a pinned constant in the engine and in the oracle together and every published case stays green, so on a shared constant every case green is the finding.

## An analytic limit outranks both

| what it is | what it can find |
| --- | --- |
| the same expression twice | a typing error between the two copies |
| a second route to the same answer | an error in either route that the other does not share |
| an analytic limit | an error both routes share, when the limit is violated |

This repository carries no published heat exchanger case for the wall term, so the third row is what stands in for one. The golden file for this module is declared synthetic for that reason, and it says so rather than dressing an invented row as a citation. What stands in are route independence, the constant pins and the analytic limits, and the limits are the strongest of the three.

There is one more habit in here. The way to find out whether a check can fail is to break the thing it checks, on purpose, and see whether the check notices. A gate restating the formula it is checking has checked nothing at all.
