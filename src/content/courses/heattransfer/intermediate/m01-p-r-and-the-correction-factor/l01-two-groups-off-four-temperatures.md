# Two groups off four temperatures

Four terminal temperatures go in and two dimensionless numbers come out. Every correction factor in this tier is written in those two and in nothing else. P is the cold rise over the span between the two inlets. R is the hot drop over the cold rise. The engine hands them back together, on two keys and no more, so a reader can see both groups before any correction is asked for.

## The letter P in this course

P here is the dimensionless temperature group and it is nothing else. It is not a percentile, and no percentile label belongs anywhere in this course. That has to be said at the first use, because the letter carries a different meaning elsewhere on this platform and a learner arriving from one of those places will read it wrongly for a whole module.

R is the second group and it is a ratio of two temperature changes. Both groups are pure numbers. Neither one carries a unit, and that is what makes a correction written in them usable on any exchanger at all.

## The two groups on the teaching cases

| case | P | R |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the studio case | 0.171875 | 2.909091 |
| ORON | 0.204107 | 2.416739 |

Those two exchangers have their groups out of a balance rather than typed. The studio case is the shipped default of the live app and ORON is a four-pass exchanger with its own loop, and they share nothing. A figure from one never belongs to the other.

## What the two groups are asked to do

The groups exist to answer one question: how far from counter-current flow does this arrangement sit. A shell and tube unit with one shell pass and two tube passes has some of its length in counter-current flow and some of it in parallel flow, so its true driving force sits below the counter-current log mean. P and R are what the size of that shortfall is written in, and the correction factor is the shortfall expressed as a multiplier.

The groups themselves are patient. They can be formed from any four terminal temperatures that make physical sense. What comes next is less patient, because the closed form for the correction is defined over part of the P and R plane and not over all of it.

## Read the pair before reading anything downstream

A P of 0.171875 sits low, and a low P is a small cold rise against a wide span between the two inlets. An R of 2.909091 says the hot stream falls through nearly three degrees for every degree the cold stream climbs. Those two sentences describe the exchanger, and everything the rest of this module does to it starts from them.

An exchanger whose groups you cannot state is an exchanger whose correction you cannot check.
