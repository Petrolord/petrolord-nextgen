# A surface out of three numbers

The surface an exchanger needs is the duty divided by the coefficient, the correction factor and the log mean driving force. Nothing else goes into it. There is no geometry in that division, no fluid, no arrangement beyond what the log mean and the correction factor already carry, and no allowance for anything.

## One division and three divisors

Three of the four numbers are quantities in their own right. The duty came out of the balance. The log mean came out of the driving force door. The coefficient is the one this tier takes as given, and it is assembled from named parts in the next tier of this course.

The fourth, the correction factor, is one on a straight counter-current unit and below one on a shell exchanger. Where it is one it drops out of the arithmetic entirely, which is why this course calls the surface a division by three numbers and then names the fourth.

Both coefficients in that table are referred to the outside tube surface, in Btu an hour per ft2 per degF, and both areas are therefore outside tube surfaces too. The two published rows carry a golden figure, which the oracle wrote by its own route, so those two rows are two methods agreeing rather than one method printed twice.

Published case 1 is the only row with a correction factor below one, at 0.900000, and it was given rather than computed there.

## Where an error goes

The division is the reason this lesson comes before the tubes. The area is proportional to the duty and inversely proportional to each of the other three, so an error in any one of them arrives in the area unchanged in size. There is no averaging, no damping and nothing else in the expression to absorb it.

That cuts both ways and the useful direction is diagnosis. If an area looks wrong, there are exactly four places to look, and three of them you can check independently by reopening the doors that produced them. It is a short list, and a short list is worth having when a surface comes out of a screen looking half of what you expected.

## What the door returns

The area door answers with an object carrying one key, which is the area in ft2. It refuses a non-positive coefficient and it refuses a correction factor outside its band, and both of those are the next lesson.

One key is the right width here. An area is a single number with no parts, unlike a coefficient or a tube count, so there is nothing to hand back beside it.
