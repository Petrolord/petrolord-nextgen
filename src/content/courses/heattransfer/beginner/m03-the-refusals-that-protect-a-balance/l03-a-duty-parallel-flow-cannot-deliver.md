# A duty parallel flow cannot deliver

Some duties are impossible for any exchanger. Others are impossible only for the arrangement you asked about. This lesson is one duty on the studio streams, put to the balance twice, and the two answers are a refusal and a number.

{{panel:fc-exchanger-explorer}}

## The refusal and its evidence

Ask for 5200000 Btu an hour in parallel flow and the balance refuses. The message says the two streams would leave at 110.91 F hot and 165.00 F cold, so they must have crossed inside, that parallel flow cannot take the hot outlet below the cold outlet at any area, and that a counter-current unit can. Beside it sit the two evidence keys again, holding 110.909091 and 165.000000 degF.

Read what has happened to the two temperatures. The hot stream leaves at 110.909091 degF and the cold stream leaves at 165.000000 degF, so the hot outlet is the colder of the two outlets. Both are comfortably inside the range between the two inlets, so nothing here crosses the inlets. What crosses is the pair of outlets.

## The same duty, answered

| arrangement | duty, Btu an hour | hot outlet, degF | cold outlet, degF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| parallel | refused | 110.909091 | 165.000000 |
| counter | 5200000.0000 | 110.909091 | 165.000000 |

Nothing in those two rows differs except the word in the first column and whether an answer came back. The evidence the parallel refusal handed over is, figure for figure, the answer a counter-current unit gives.

The balance had worked out both outlet temperatures before it decided to refuse, and it refused on what they turned out to be. The computation is the same computation. Only the test applied to its result changed.

## Why the two ends differ

In parallel flow both streams enter at the same end and leave at the same end. They travel alongside each other, so at every point the hot stream is hotter than the cold stream, right up to the outlet. At the far end the two are still in contact, so the hot one cannot have got below the cold one.

In counter-current flow they enter at opposite ends. The hot stream leaves where the cold stream enters, so the coldest thing the leaving hot stream ever meets is the entering cold stream at 100 F. It is free to go below the cold outlet, because it never meets the cold outlet. That is the whole of the difference, and it is why counter-current reaches duties parallel flow cannot.

## The arrangement is an input

The arrangement goes into the balance with the temperatures and the capacity rates, and the test the balance applies is the test for the arrangement it was given. There is no single feasibility check here that both arrangements share. Ask under parallel flow and the parallel test runs. Ask under counter-current and it does not.

So the arrangement is not a label on an answer. It is part of the question, and changing it changes which states are reachable. A study whose arrangement was set once and never looked at again can refuse duties the machine on the plot would deliver.

## Exercise

Write down the two outlet temperatures for this duty and mark which is the hot one. Then explain in your own words why that ordering is allowed in one arrangement and impossible in the other. Finish by naming the one input that differed between the refusal and the answer.
