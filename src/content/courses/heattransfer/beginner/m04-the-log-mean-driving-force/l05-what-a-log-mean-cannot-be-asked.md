# What a log mean cannot be asked

A log mean needs two positive end differences and nothing else will do. That one requirement produces every refusal this door gives, and each of them is worth recognising on sight because each of them means something different about the study that produced it.

{{panel:fc-exchanger-explorer}}

## Four terminals or nothing

Give it three of the four terminal temperatures and it refuses, naming the one that is missing: the log mean needs all four terminal temperatures, and tcOut was not given.

It names the missing terminal rather than saying that an input is missing, and that is the difference between a message you can act on and a message you have to investigate. A log mean is not something that can be worked out from a duty or a capacity rate. It is built from four temperatures by subtraction, so all four have to be there, and the door will not accept a partial state and fill in the rest.

## Two ends that are not positive

The other refusal is the one that matters physically. Ask for a counter-current log mean on a pair of streams whose ends have closed up and the answer is a temperature cross: in counter flow the two ends of this exchanger have driving forces of 0.00 and 0.00 F, and one of them is not positive.

An end difference of zero means the two streams have reached the same temperature at that end of the exchanger. A negative one means they have passed each other. Neither can be fed to a logarithm, because a ratio involving zero or a negative number has no logarithm to take. The requirement is arithmetic before it is physical, and the physics agrees with it: two streams at the same temperature exchange nothing, however much surface you give them.

Notice that the message names the arrangement it was working in. It has to, because the arrangement decided which two ends were formed, and a pair of terminals that crosses in one pairing need not cross in the other.

## Evidence on a refusal

This refusal carries evidence. Beside the message sit the two end differences it found, `dt1` and `dt2`, reading 0.000000 and 0.000000 degF, so three keys come back in all.

That is worth noting against the earlier count of how many doors hand back evidence. That count was measured on one set of probes, and this is a door whose refusal carries evidence on a probe those did not include. The message shows the two ends rounded to 0.00 F for reading. The keys carry them at full precision, and the keys are what you use.
