# The shell reading and its note

The third arrangement this module carries is called shell1, and it stands for a one-shell-pass, two-tube-pass exchanger. Ask it for a log mean and you get a number, and you get a sentence warning you what the number is. This lesson is about the sentence, because the sentence is the whole content of the lesson.

{{panel:fc-exchanger-explorer}}

## What comes back

On the studio terminals the shell reading returns a log mean of 130.064846 degF with its basis reported as `counter`. That figure is the counter-current log mean, to the last digit. Nothing about the shell arrangement changed the number.

Read the basis key first and it tells you as much. It says counter, because counter is the pairing that was used. It does not say shell1. The engine is reporting the method it ran rather than the arrangement you asked about, which are two different facts and it publishes the one that matters for checking.

## The engine's own wording

Beside the number sits a note, and it is the engine's own string rather than this course's prose. It opens:

`a 1-2 shell exchanger is rated on the counter-current log mean multiplied by F`

The engine's sentence then continues, in a contrastive form the owner's copy rule keeps off a learner's page, to say that the figure just returned is the log mean and is not yet the corrected driving force. The first half above is quoted exactly. The second half is put here in this course's words, and the wording you will see on a screen is the engine's.

## A sixth key

The counter and parallel readings hand back five keys: the log mean, the two end differences, the equal-ends flag and the basis. The shell reading hands back six of them, because the note rides along as a key of its own.

That is why this is a note rather than a refusal. A refusal would be wrong, because the counter-current log mean genuinely is the first half of what a shell exchanger is rated on. A silent answer would be worse, because the figure is one multiplication short of usable and it looks finished. A sixth key is the honest middle.

## What remains to be done to it

A 1-2 shell exchanger is rated on that log mean multiplied by a correction factor. The factor is computed rather than read off a chart in this module, it is always at or below one, and everything about it belongs to the next tier of this course.

What belongs here is the habit. If you take 130.064846 degF from the shell reading and put it straight into a surface, you have sized an exchanger on a driving force larger than the one it will actually run at, so the surface will be smaller than it should be. The number is right. The use of it is wrong, and the note is sitting there saying so.

## Exercise

Write down the six keys the shell reading returns and say which of them the counter reading does not carry. Then say what the basis key reads and why it reads that. Finish with one sentence on what would go wrong if a caller used the shell log mean directly as a driving force.
