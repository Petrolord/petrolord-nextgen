# The whole chain on one machine

One bay, read from its design sheet through its worst afternoon and then checked by a second method. Every figure below belongs to one machine, and each is either an input somebody chose or an answer the engine computed. Knowing which of the two you hold is the discipline of this course.

## Chosen

A duty of 20000000.0000 Btu an hour, a process stream entering at 250.000000 degF and leaving at 150.000000 degF, a design ambient of 95.000000 degF, an air rise of 30.000000 degF, a coefficient of 4.500000 on the bare surface, forced draft, and a barometric pressure of 14.700000 psia.

Not one of those is computed. The air rise in particular is a trade rather than a result: a larger rise needs less air and more bundle. The coefficient is given to this module rather than derived by it.

## Computed at the design point

Everything in this paragraph follows from the list above and from nothing else. The air leaves at 125.000000 degF, which is the ambient plus the rise. The log mean is 85.263896 degF. The bare surface is 52125.749338 ft2 and the air mass 2777777.7778 lb an hour. The fan draws at 95.000000 degF, where the air density is 0.071524401 lb per ft3, giving 647279.7513 actual ft3 a minute, 94.003933 brake horsepower and 102.178189 motor horsepower.

## Declined

The correction factor comes back empty. An air cooler is cross-flow and this module cannot source the factor, so the surface above is sized on the counter-current log mean and is a counter-current-basis surface. A real bay needs more metal, and how much more is not available here.

## Rated on a hot afternoon

Hold the surface and the air mass, because those are what the plant owns once the bay is built. UA is 234565.8720 Btu an hour per degF, the capacity ratio 0.300000, the NTU 1.172829 and the effectiveness 0.645161, and none of those four moves with the ambient. The effectiveness is taken from its definition at the design point, so this rating assumes no arrangement and applies no correction factor.

At 112.000000 degF the bay delivers a duty fraction of 0.890323, a duty of 17806451.6129 Btu an hour, a process outlet of 160.967742 degF and an air rise of 26.709677 degF, across a hot-day log mean of 75.912371 degF.

## Checked by the other method

UA times that hot-day log mean is 17806451.6129 Btu an hour, against a rated duty of 17806451.6129, a ratio of 1.000000 to the precision printed here. The effectiveness route never evaluated the surface equation and the surface route never evaluated the effectiveness relation, so the agreement is two methods meeting rather than one method restated.

## What to hand over

Give the four fixed quantities, the two that moved and the basis the rating states. Give the design surface with the word counter-current attached to it. A reader who receives the numbers without those two qualifications has been handed a sized bay rather than a rated one.

## Exercise

Sort every figure above into chosen, computed, declined and rated. Reproduce the second-method check at 112.000000 degF from the UA and the hot-day log mean. Then write the two sentences you would attach to the design surface and to the hot-day duty before sending either one on.
