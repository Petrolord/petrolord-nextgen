# The length the liquid needs

ABANA-2 holds a retention volume of 584.852431 ft3, and at a level of 0.500000 in an 8.000000 ft drum that volume lies in 25.132741 ft2 of liquid area, which asks for 23.270539 ft of length.

## A volume divided by an area

The liquid requirement is one division. The retention volume is the liquid rate held for the retention time, and the liquid area is the segment the level cuts out of the circle. The volume over the area is the length of drum needed to contain it.

Nothing in that division knows anything about the gas, the pressure or the mist extractor. It is a statement about holding a quantity of liquid for a stated time in a vessel of a stated bore at a stated level.

## The same duty in six drums

| diameter ft | liquid area ft2 | liquid length ft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.000000 | 9.817477 | 59.572579 |
| 6.000000 | 14.137167 | 41.369847 |
| 7.000000 | 19.242255 | 30.394173 |
| 8.000000 | 25.132741 | 23.270539 |
| 9.000000 | 31.808626 | 18.386599 |
| 10.000000 | 39.269908 | 14.893145 |

Every row holds the same 584.852431 ft3 at the same level. The area rises with the square of the diameter, so the length falls away quickly: going from 5.000000 ft to 10.000000 ft of bore takes the requirement from 59.572579 ft down to 14.893145 ft. Two feet of extra diameter on the 5.000000 ft drum is worth almost thirty feet of length.

## Where the volume comes from

The retention volume is a rate times a time, and both are inputs somebody typed. ABANA-1, the vertical test separator on the same field, holds 3000.000000 bpd for 3.000000 minutes, which is 35.091146 ft3. ABANA-2 carries far more liquid, 24000.000000 bpd of oil and 6000.000000 bpd of water, and its 584.852431 ft3 follows from that.

A retention time is a specification rather than a measurement. Doubling it doubles the volume and doubles every liquid length in the table, so the single most powerful number on the sheet is one that was chosen.

## Two published horizontal cases

A 6.000000 ft drum at a level of 0.500000, taking 25000.000000 bpd on 3.000000 minutes, needs 20.684923 ft for its liquid. An 8.000000 ft drum at a level of 0.400000, taking 30000.000000 bpd on 5.000000 minutes, needs 31.149488 ft. The second vessel is wider and still longer, because it is running at a lower level with a bigger duty and a longer hold.

## The mistake

The mistake is dividing by the wrong area. Using the total cross-section of 50.265482 ft2 in the place of the liquid area of 25.132741 ft2 halves the requirement on a drum at half full, and the error grows as the level falls. At a level of 0.300000, where the liquid holds 12.682775 ft2 of the same circle, the same slip is worth a factor of four.
