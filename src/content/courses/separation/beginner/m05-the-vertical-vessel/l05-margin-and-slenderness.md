# Margin, and slenderness

Two numbers judge a finished vertical vessel. The velocity margin says whether it can carry its gas, and the slenderness says whether its shape is one anybody builds.

{{panel:fc-separator-explorer}}

## The margin is a verdict

The margin is the settling velocity divided by the actual gas velocity. Above 1 the gas is slower than a drop falls and the vessel works. Below 1 it does not. At exactly 1.000000 the vessel is at the gas-required diameter, where a drop is in perfect balance.

| diameter ft | gas velocity ft/s | margin | L/D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.000000 | 1.536071 | 0.949450 | 8.584929 |
| 2.500000 | 0.983085 | 1.483516 | 5.259484 |
| 3.000000 | 0.682698 | 2.136263 | 3.654794 |
| 3.500000 | 0.501574 | 2.907691 | 2.756372 |
| 4.000000 | 0.384018 | 3.797800 | 2.198116 |

The margin is the number to quote when somebody asks how much room a design has. A vessel at 2.136263 can take more than twice its gas rate before it stops separating, and one at 1.483516 can take about half as much again.

## Slenderness is a shape

L/D is the height over the diameter and it carries no unit. It runs from 8.584929 at the narrowest vessel to 2.198116 at the widest, and it moves fast, because the height is falling while the diameter is rising.

Very slender vessels are awkward for reasons outside this calculation: wind and seismic loading, foundation and support cost, the platform space a tall column needs, and the practicalities of getting a mist extractor in and out. Very squat ones waste steel in heads and give the gas a large distance to cross. Neither limit is physics, and this method reports the ratio rather than judging it.

## The two answer different questions

The 2.000000 ft vessel has a margin of 0.949450 and a slenderness of 8.584929. It is unusable on the first count and awkward on the second, and only the first is disqualifying. The 4.000000 ft vessel is comfortable on both.

Between them the 3.000000 ft vessel, which the studio prefers on this stream, carries a margin of 2.136263 and a slenderness of 3.654794, and stands 10.964382 ft tall. That combination is what a reasonable answer looks like here: real margin against the gas, and a shape a shop will quote without comment.

## What neither number knows

The margin is built on a settling velocity whose K was read from a table and derated by a rule recorded as customary practice with an unchecked published form. So a margin of 2.136263 is a ratio against an allowable rather than against a measurement, and it inherits whatever the allowable is worth.

Slenderness knows even less. It is a ratio of two dimensions, and it has no opinion about wall thickness, pressure rating, weight or anything that would make one shape cost more than another.

## The mistake

Reading a high margin as a better design. Margin rises with diameter for free in this calculation and costs money in the yard. The question is how much margin the duty justifies, and a vessel at 3.797800 is carrying capacity nobody asked for.
