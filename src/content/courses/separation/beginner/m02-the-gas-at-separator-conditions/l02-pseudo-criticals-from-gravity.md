# Pseudo-criticals from gravity

A gas gravity of 0.680000 is one number standing in for a whole composition, and the Sutton correlations turn it into the pseudo-critical pair that every reduced property is measured against.

{{panel:fc-separator-explorer}}

## One number in, two out

| stream | gas gravity | Tpc degR | Ppc psia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABANA-1 | 0.680000 | 372.642400 | 666.055360 |
| ABANA-2 | 0.680000 | 372.642400 | 666.055360 |
| AGBAMI | 0.700000 | 377.590000 | 663.336000 |

A heavier gas has a higher pseudo-critical temperature and a lower pseudo-critical pressure. Moving the gravity from 0.680000 to 0.700000 raises Tpc from 372.642400 to 377.590000 degR and lowers Ppc from 666.055360 to 663.336000 psia. The two move in opposite directions, so their effects on the reduced pair do not cancel.

## What the pair is for

The correlations that follow do not work in psia and degF. They work in reduced coordinates, where a pressure is stated as a multiple of the pseudo-critical pressure and a temperature as a multiple of the pseudo-critical temperature. That is the whole point of the pair: it puts gases of different compositions onto one surface where a single fitted correlation can describe them all.

So the pseudo-criticals are a change of coordinates rather than a physical property of the gas. Nothing in the vessel is at 372.642400 degR, and nothing ever reaches 666.055360 psia.

## The reduced pair on the teaching streams

| stream | absolute psia | degF | Ppr | Tpr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABANA-1 | 614.700000 | 95.000000 | 0.922896 | 1.488478 |
| ABANA-2 | 614.700000 | 95.000000 | 0.922896 | 1.488478 |
| AGBAMI | 364.700000 | 110.000000 | 0.549797 | 1.508700 |

Ppr is the absolute pressure over Ppc and Tpr is the absolute temperature over Tpc, which is why the temperature has to reach the correlation in degrees Rankine: 95.000000 degF is 554.670000 degR, and it is that figure, over 372.642400, that gives Tpr 1.488478.

AGBAMI is at a lower pressure and a higher temperature than the ABANA streams, and both differences push the same way. Its Ppr of 0.549797 is well under the ABANA 0.922896 and its Tpr of 1.508700 is a little above 1.488478, which places it on an easier part of the surface.

## A gravity carries an assumption

One number cannot describe a gas. Two streams with the same gravity and different amounts of carbon dioxide, nitrogen or hydrogen sulphide have genuinely different pseudo-criticals, and this route through gravity gives them the same pair. Where a stream carries appreciable non-hydrocarbons, the gravity route is an approximation that a composition and a mixing rule would replace.

## The mistake

Reusing a pseudo-critical pair after the gravity has changed. ABANA-1 and ABANA-2 share 372.642400 degR and 666.055360 psia because they share a stream, and that sharing is a fact about the field rather than a convenience. AGBAMI has its own pair because it has its own gas, and borrowing the ABANA figures for it moves everything downstream.
