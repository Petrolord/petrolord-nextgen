# National reserves and the Commission

{{panel:prms-classification-calculator}}

Every project classification in a country feeds, in the end, into one national position. In Nigeria that position is the work of the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission. This lesson reads what the Act asks of the Commission, the national figures it published for 1 January 2026, and one thing the course looked for and did not find.

## The Commission's published gas figures

| figure, as at 1 January 2026 | trillion cubic feet |
| --- | --- |
| 2P associated gas | 100.21 |
| 2P non-associated gas | 114.98 |
| 2P gas in total | 215.19 |

These are reported reserves, published by the Commission in its media release of 1 April 2026 and read on 2026-09-27. The release's page reads all rights reserved, so the course cites its figures and its date and quotes none of its words. Each figure is a single published number, with no low or high beside it.

## What the Act asks of the Commission

Among the Commission's functions, the Act lists the evaluation of national reserves:

> "(i) undertake evaluation of national reserves and develop policies for prudent reservoir management practices ;" (PIA s.7(i))

The national figure is the Commission's evaluation. The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum (Commercial) Regulations, 2025 (S.I. No. 7 of 2025) ask a licensee for a status report that includes a statement of its reserves position (reg. 6). The course uses that regulation by concept only.

## What was not found

No gazetted NUPRC regulation or guideline on how reserves are booked or reported was found on the Commission's list of gazetted regulations, read on 2026-09-27. So this course teaches the PRMS classes and states no Nigerian booking rule, because none was read.

## From projects to a nation

A national 2P is a total of many project figures. Only quantities that are Reserves in the strict sense belong in a reserves total: a Contingent Resources project such as Ekene East gas, however large, stays out until it becomes commercial. That is why the class of each project matters to the national position. How project figures are added into a total, and what the arithmetic sum of low estimates does and does not mean, is taken up at the Expert tier.

## Reading a national figure with care

A reported reserves figure should always travel with four things: who reported it, the date it applies to, the category, and the unit. The Commission's total above is 2P, as at 1 January 2026, in trillion cubic feet. Quoting "Nigeria's gas reserves" without those four leaves a reader unable to compare it with anything.

## The field development plan in the same picture

A commercial discovery leads to a field development plan within two years (s.79(1)), and development plans are among the facts that let a project pass the commerciality test. The Act's steps and the PRMS classes run side by side: the Act says what the licensee must do and when, the classes say how mature each project is.
