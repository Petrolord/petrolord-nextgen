# Deterministic and probabilistic methods

{{panel:prms-classification-calculator}}

There are two broad ways to arrive at a low, a best and a high estimate. A deterministic method works out each one as its own scenario, with a single set of inputs chosen for that case. A probabilistic method builds a distribution of possible outcomes and reads the three figures off it. The engine takes the three estimates however they were made, and its wording shows where the probabilities apply (PRMS 2.2.1.2 and 2.2.1.4).

## The two methods side by side

| method | how the three estimates are made | what P90 means for the low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| deterministic | three scenarios, each worked out on its own | an outcome label for the low scenario |
| probabilistic | a distribution, read at three points | at least 90 percent probability of being met or exceeded |

## Deterministic

An engineer picks a set of inputs for the low case: a thinner net pay, a lower recovery factor, a shorter well life. The result is the low estimate. The same is done for a best and a high set. No distribution is drawn. The low scenario is chosen so that the estimator has a high degree of confidence the outcome will reach it, and the course teaches that as the deterministic reading of the low estimate. The label P90 is still attached, as the outcome label of the low case.

## Probabilistic

An estimator describes each uncertain input as a range, combines them into a distribution of the outcome, and reads the low, best and high off it. Here the probability wording is literal: the low is the figure met or exceeded with at least 90 percent probability. How such a distribution is built and sampled belongs to the uncertainty course; this course takes the three figures that come out.

## The engine's wording covers both

Every category line the engine prints ends its probability with the words "when probabilistic". The 1P line for the Ekene Main Reserves reads:

> 1P (low estimate, P90: at least 90% probability of being met or exceeded when probabilistic, PRMS 2.2.1.2): 8.89 MMbbl

That phrase is the engine being careful. It does not know which method produced your figures, so it states the probability only for the probabilistic case. For a deterministic set, P90 is an outcome label and nothing more: the engine does not claim that a stated scenario has a 90 percent chance.

## Where the figures come from

Deterministic and probabilistic estimates are both made outside this course. A volumetric estimate from maps and rock properties belongs to the reservoir volumetrics course; a forecast from production history to the decline curve analysis course. Either can be run as scenarios or as a distribution. This course starts once the three estimates exist.

## Say which method you used

Because the engine cannot tell the methods apart, a report must. Write the method beside every category table, so a reader knows whether P90 is a probability or a label.
