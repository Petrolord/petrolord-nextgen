# Sources and the dates they were read

{{panel:prms-classification-calculator}}

A reserves figure is only as good as the rules behind it, and rules change. This course therefore names every standard, law, regulation and release it uses with its edition or gazette date, its licence and the date it was read. Every text below was read on 2026-09-27, and the course cites each as it stood on that date. A licensed text is taught by concept with its section numbers; a public text is quoted word for word with its citation.

## Why the licence decides how a text is used

SPE-PRMS 2018 may be shared for non-commercial purposes and may not be adapted. A paid course is commercial, so the course writes its own explanations and points to the section each idea comes from. The FAQs and the 2011 Guidelines carry no open licence at all, so the course takes from them only the figures they print and the numbers of their answers and sections. The Act, the Nigerian regulations and the US federal rules are public texts, and the course quotes them exactly.

## Three things said plainly

**The English text of the standard** was read from the SPE-hosted English-Chinese edition, whose English is the 2018 text; the section numbers are the 2018 numbers.

**The 2022 edition of the Application Guidelines** is sold and was not read. When the course cites the Guidelines, it names the 2011 edition by its year.

**No Nigerian booking rule was found.** The Commission's list of gazetted regulations, read on 2026-09-27, carries no regulation or guideline on how reserves are booked or reported. The course teaches the PRMS classes and states no Nigerian booking rule, because none was read.

## The engine cites its sources too

Every result the calculator prints ends in a Source block, the engine's `basis`. For a classification it names the edition and licence of the standard, and for a project with a Nigerian declaration it adds the Act with its gazette. A figure you copy into a report should carry the same citation, so you can always say which edition of which text it rests on. The Source block is written to be copied: it already names the edition, the licence and the section of each text it applies.
