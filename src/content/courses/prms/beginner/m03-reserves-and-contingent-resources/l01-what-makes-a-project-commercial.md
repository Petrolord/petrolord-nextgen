# What makes a project commercial

{{panel:prms-classification-calculator}}

A discovered accumulation with a recovery project faces one more question before it can be called Reserves: is the project commercial? The framework answers with a checklist. PRMS 2.1.2.1 lists seven conditions and adds the commitment of the company to go ahead. The engine reads each as a stated fact and calls the project Reserves only when every one is met with established technology.

## The checklist

| criterion key | what it asks, in the course's words | section | EKN-1 (engine) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| developmentPlan | is there a mature, workable plan to develop it? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(1) | met |
| financialAppropriations | is the money committed, or very likely to be? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(2), 2.1.2.4 | met |
| timeFrame | will development start within a reasonable time? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(3), 2.1.2.3 | met |
| economicStatus | do the economics work (economic status "viable")? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(4), 2.1.3.7.1 | met |
| market | can the product reasonably be sold? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(5) | met |
| facilities | are the plant and pipelines there, or can they be? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(6) | met |
| approvals | are the legal, contractual, environmental and government approvals in hand or coming? | PRMS 2.1.2.1(7) | met |
| firmIntention | has the company firmly committed to develop? | PRMS 2.1.2.1, 2.1.2.3 | met |

Ekene Main waterflood, EKN-1, meets all eight, and the engine's class decision reads "Reserves: every commerciality criterion is met with established technology", citing PRMS 2.1.2.1 and Table 1.

## Stated facts, one by one

Six of the criteria are stated as true or false, each with its own control in the calculator. Two are read from other inputs. The time-frame is read from a stated number of years until development starts, with a stated flag for a longer time-frame that is justified. Positive economics is read from the stated economic status, which is "viable", "not-viable" or "undetermined". The engine works out none of this itself: it holds no forecast, price or cost at this stage. Whether the economics are viable is a judgement made elsewhere and stated here; the Professional tier shows how the engine tests economics on stated forecasts, and it also takes up the five-year benchmark behind the time-frame.

## All or nothing

The test is strict. One criterion not met is enough to keep a project out of Reserves, and the engine names every criterion that fails. The class is then Contingent Resources, which the next lesson opens. The engine prints the list after the words "held back by", so you can see at a glance what stands between the project and Reserves.

## Established technology

The last condition is about the recovery project itself. A project that relies on technology still under development cannot be Reserves, even when all eight criteria are met: the engine names "technology under development" as a blocker of its own. The course's small case "Technology under development alone" shows exactly that.
