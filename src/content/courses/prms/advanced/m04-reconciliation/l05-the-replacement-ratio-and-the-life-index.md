# The replacement ratio and the life index

{{panel:prms-aggregation-calculator}}

Two figures summarise a reconciliation that carries production. The replacement ratio compares what the period added with what it produced. The life index divides the closing estimate by the period's production rate. Both are simple arithmetic on the stated movements, and both rest on choices the engine states in its own words.

## The engine's line

On the Ekene field Reserves, verbatim:

> 2P replacement ratio: every movement other than production (additions, revisions and transfers) 5.7 over production 1.1 = 5.181818; 2P life index 23.272727 years at the period's production rate

| figure | value (engine) |
| --- | --- |
| replacement ratio | 5.181818 |
| life index, years | 23.272727 |
| production | 1.100000 |

The ratio says the period added a little over five times what it produced at the 2P. The life index says the 2P closing of 25.600000 would last a little over twenty-three years at this year's production of 1.100000 over 1 year.

## Two readings

The first reading: the replacement ratio counts every movement other than production, so revisions and transfers are in the numerator with the additions. The alternative the engine names counts the additions alone, leaving revisions and transfers out. On Ekene the transfer of the infill wells is most of the 2P movement, so the two readings would give very different ratios. A report that quotes a replacement ratio says which movements it counted.

The second reading: the life index is read at the best estimate, the 2P. The alternative reads it at the low estimate, the 1P. A life index at the 1P would be shorter.

Neither reading is the law. Each is the engine's stated choice where the texts leave room, and no graded figure in this course depends on either.

## When the ratio is negative

With divestments larger than the additions the ratio is negative. The golden case "rec-divest-acquire" returns -0.750000: leaving production aside, the period's movements took away more than they added.

## When there is no production

The ratio divides by production and the life index by the production rate. A reconciliation with no production prints neither: the golden case "rec-no-production" returns none for both, and the calculator's tiles read "none (no production)".

## A national index

Regulators publish life indices too. The NUPRC release of 1 April 2026 prints reserves life indices beside its national figures; the course cites that release for its figures and date only. A published index is reported reserves over a reported rate, and it carries the reporter's choices: which category, which rate, which date. The engine's index carries its own, stated in its line.
