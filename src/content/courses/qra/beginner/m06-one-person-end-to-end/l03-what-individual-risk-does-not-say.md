# What individual risk does not say

An LSIR and an IRPA are precise answers to precise questions. Each is a frequency per year with which one person, at one place or over one roster, is killed. That precision makes it easy to ask them questions that lie outside what they measure. This lesson lists what individual risk does not say, so you can read an Associate answer for exactly what it is and hand the other questions to the tier that owns them.

## What this tier's figures do say

| per year | what it answers |
| --- | --- |
| 0.000054000000 | how often EREMOR's release explodes |
| 0.000148150000 | the individual risk at the process deck, someone there all year, outdoors and unprotected |
| 0.000017541379 | the individual risk the EREMOR operator carries over the roster |

Each of those follows one outcome, one place or one person. None of them counts people.

## How many at once

An IRPA says nothing about how many people might die together. Two facilities can give every worker the same IRPA while one places a single person on the deck and the other places a whole crew there together. Each person's figure is about that person alone, so it cannot tell the two facilities apart. The question of how many die at once is societal risk, and it has its own measures: potential loss of life, the fatal accident rate and the F-N curve. The Professional tier teaches them, and this tier quotes none of their figures.

## Whether the figure is acceptable

An individual risk of 0.000017541379 per year is a number. Where it falls against published thresholds is a comparison made with thresholds the analyst supplies, and whether a further measure is reasonably practicable is a test that weighs the measure's cost. Both belong to the Expert tier. This tier stops at the number.

## Where the inputs came from

An individual risk is only as sound as its inputs, and this course takes the most physical of them as given. Every probability of death is a stated input from the consequence course. Every scenario frequency and branch probability is typed by the analyst. The engine invents none of them, does no grid or wind rose bookkeeping, and cannot tell whether a closing tree means the right thing. An individual risk carries all of those choices inside it without showing them. The contributions and the basis are where they become visible, which is why a reviewer reads them first.

So when a figure arrives, ask three questions before quoting it. Which frequencies and which stated probabilities of death went into it? Is it a place or a person? And is the question being asked of it one this tier answers at all?
