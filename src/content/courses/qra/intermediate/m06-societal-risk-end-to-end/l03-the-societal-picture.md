## The crew

The JISIKE crew faces four scenarios with stated frequencies and stated expected deaths. The engine returns a PLL of 0.002420000000 fatalities per year. Over 60 people each exposed 2000 hours a year, 120000 exposed hours, derived, the FAR is 2.016667 per 100,000,000 exposed hours. The fall from height carries the largest share of the PLL, and the spill with no one near is kept in the list with a contribution of zero.

## The population off site

The curve is the engine's own figure under the declared "N or more" reading, checked by self-consistency, with no published worked example behind it.

## Beside individual risk

The Associate tier followed one person at EREMOR, whose IRPA was 0.000017541379 per year. That figure and the JISIKE societal figures answer different questions about different facilities, and a full assessment of any one facility reports both: the individual risk of the most exposed person, and the societal picture if the population warrants it. The event tree panel beside this lesson builds the frequencies both sides start from. Whether the individual risk is tolerable, and whether a further measure is reasonably practicable, are Expert tier questions.

## What the picture says

The crew figures say how many deaths are expected among the workforce and how that compares with the industry per 100,000,000 exposed hours. The off-site curve says how often events of each size could happen, and the criteria say how that compares with published views of what society will accept. The two halves together tell a duty holder where the next measure should look: here, the toxic cloud decides the Dutch comparison, and the fall from height decides the crew PLL.
