# The published worked example

{{panel:qr-alarp}}

A cost benefit engine earns trust by reproducing a published case. The HSE CBA checklist, 2003 values, works one explosion measure end to end, and it is the published check on the gross disproportion arithmetic in this engine. Its measure runs over 25 years, undiscounted, with a deltaPLL of 2e-4, a VPF of 1336800 and three injury lines beside the fatalities; every input is golden, read from the source. This lesson sets the checklist's printed figures beside the engine's, line by line, and shows where they agree and where the print was rounded.

## Printed against engine

| figure | printed | engine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fatalities | 6684 | 6684.00 |
| permanent incapacity | 2072 | 2072.00 |
| serious injury | 512 | 512.50 |
| slight injury | 15 | 15.00 |
| total benefit | 9283 | 9283.50 |
| largest reasonable cost at DF 10 | 93000 | 92835.00 |

Three of the benefit lines match exactly. The serious injury line is 512.50 and the checklist prints 512, dropping the half. The total is 9283.50 and is printed 9283, carrying the same dropped half. Both differences are the SOURCE's rounding to whole units of currency. The engine's arithmetic on the stated inputs is exact, and it prints money to two decimals.

## The largest reasonable cost

The last row is the one a decision turns on. The largest reasonably practicable cost is the DF times the present value of the benefit. The checklist uses a DF of 10, and says in its own example that "a DF of more than 10 is unlikely". The engine gives 92835.00, which is DF 10 times 9283.50. The checklist prints 93000, a figure in the region of the computed one, and the gap is the source's rounding again. The next lesson shows what happens when a cost is weighed against that printed 93000.

## What the reproduction proves

The engine agrees with every printed figure, and each figure that differs does so by the source's rounding alone: a half dropped from the serious injury line and the total, and a limit rounded up to 93000. That makes the checklist example the published evidence behind the cost to benefit ratio and the largest reasonably practicable cost. It checks the benefit arithmetic line by line, the sum over a whole number of years, and the multiplication by the DF.

It is one of two published checks. The other is R2P2's margin: with a VPF of 1000000, deltaPLL 1e-5 for 1 year gives 10.00 through the engine, the "about 10" the source prints. The checklist example is undiscounted, so it tests no discount rate; the discounting conventions are the subject of the next module.

## Reading a published example well

Three habits come out of this reproduction. Compare line by line, so that any difference is located at once. Explain each difference before accepting it: here two are a half dropped in print, and the third is a limit rounded up. And carry the engine's figure forward into your own note, with the printed one beside it as a check, because a decision computed from a rounded figure can land on the wrong side of a limit.

## Exercise

Take the engine's total benefit of 9283.50 and the DF of 10. Multiply them and check that you reach the engine's largest reasonable cost of 92835.00. Then find the difference between the printed 93000 and that figure, and write one sentence saying which of the two an ALARP note should carry and why.
