# Standing up instead of lying down

{{panel:fc-fire-drum-explorer}}

Stand the same vessel on end and the wetted area is a different shape of answer. A vertical vessel wetted to a height presents a band of shell to the fire, and the area is the circumference times the wetted height. No segment, no arc angle, one product.

## The same vessel, read both ways

| the teaching vessel at a level of 4.200000 ft | value |
| --- | --- |
| wetted area lying down | 683.6960 ft2 |
| wetted area standing up | 158.3363 ft2 |
| ratio of the two | 4.317999814064 |

That ratio is printed by the course, so it may be quoted. The two readings are the same steel and the same level number, and they differ by that factor. Orientation is no detail.

## Why the two forms look so different

The shapes are worth holding in your head, because they explain every number below.

Lying down, the liquid touches the shell along two lines that run the whole length of the vessel, and the wetted surface is the strip of shell between them measured round the arc. A little liquid in a long vessel wets a long, broad strip.

Standing up, the liquid touches the shell round one circle, and the wetted surface is the band from the bottom tangent up to the level. A little liquid in a tall vessel wets a thin ring.

So the same level number describes two different amounts of steel. Neither reading approximates the other. They are two exact answers to two different questions, and the orientation field is where you say which question you asked.

The direction is the lesson: the gap between the two readings is widest at a low level, because a lying vessel wets a broad band of shell for very little liquid while a standing one wets only a thin ring.

Note what the table licenses: the direction, and the printed ratios on its own rows. A ratio assembled from two different rows is yours.

## Why the wrong orientation is expensive

A vertical reading carries straight through the fire chain. On the teaching vessel, read standing up at the same level, the chain gives a duty of 1336211.5771 Btu/hr, a relief load of 10439.1529 lb/hr, a required area of 0.475609 in2 and orifice G. Lying down the same vessel lands on orifice K. Three rungs apart, from one word in one field.

## How the published set keeps both branches honest

Three of the seven published wetted-area rows are vertical. That matters because the vertical branch has its own derived oracle route, so a defect in one orientation cannot hide behind a clean result in the other. A published set exercising only horizontal geometry would pass a module with the vertical branch entirely wrong, and would report a clean run while doing it.

The three rows are chosen rather than sampled. One tower is wetted above its own diameter, one to the height limit, and one is empty. Each is an edge some plausible implementation gets wrong.
