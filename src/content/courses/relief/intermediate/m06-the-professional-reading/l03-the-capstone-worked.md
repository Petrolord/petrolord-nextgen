# The capstone worked

{{panel:fc-fire-drum-explorer}}

The graded exercise is marked on the figures produced. Most lost marks come from answering a nearby question rather than the one asked, and four habits carry it.

## Which reading is wanted

Almost every quantity in this tier has a near neighbour that is also a correct number about the same equipment. A wetted area lying down and standing up. A length from a level fraction and from an area fraction. A duty with drainage and without. A load at two latent heats. A required area at two overpressure allowances.

In every pair both figures are correct answers to different questions. Before working anything, write down which question was asked. A figure produced from a different reading is a different vessel or a different drum.

## Conditions first

Every answer here is conditional on something stated. List, before you start: the orientation, diameter, length, level, drainage answer, environment factor, latent heat, set pressure, overpressure allowance and gas description for the fire half. Then the vapour rate with its unit and conditions, the settling velocity, the diameter and the level fraction for the drum half.

Pay particular attention to a value the question states that differs from a default. A stated settling velocity is the one to use even where a droplet size appears elsewhere in the question, and a stated environment factor is the one to use even where the vessel sounds bare.

## Check the unit and the precision

| quantity | how it is written |
| --- | --- |
| wetted area | ft2 |
| pool fire duty | Btu/hr |
| relief load | lb/hr |
| set pressure | psig, and relieving pressure psia |
| required and orifice area | in2 |
| segment and area fractions | a fraction between zero and one |
| vapour and settling velocity | ft/s |
| drum length and diameter | ft |
| L over D and margin | dimensionless |

Two of those rows do the most damage. A set pressure in psig carried into a place wanting psia is out by one atmosphere. A level fraction written as a percentage is out by a factor of a hundred, and the route refuses it only once it reaches one.

Quote every figure at the precision the engine returns it. A rounded restatement of an engine answer is indistinguishable from a number somebody invented, and rounding partway through a chain moves everything after it.

## The letter with its margin

An orifice letter alone is an incomplete answer. The selection returns the letter, the standard area behind it, the required area it selected for, and the margin between them. The margin says how firmly the case sits inside its letter.

Quote the letter with its area and the margin beside it, because several required areas map to one letter.

## Never fill a refusal

Where the engine refuses, the refusal is the answer. An orientation it does not recognise refuses by name. A level fraction at or above one refuses by name. A wetted area of zero gives no fire duty at all. Writing a plausible figure into any of those gaps turns a correct reading into a wrong answer, and the gap was the point of the question.

A warning differs from a refusal. A near-critical latent heat returns a load and attaches a warning, and the load is still the answer to quote. Report the warning beside it.

## Exercise

Take any figure you are about to write and name the reading it belongs to and the stated conditions behind it. Check its unit and precision against the table, confirm it is a number the engine returned, and say in one sentence how a refusal differs from a warning.
