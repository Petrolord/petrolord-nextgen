# The chain the capstone walks

{{panel:fc-fire-drum-explorer}}

The graded exercise for this tier is a flare system: a vessel in a pool fire and the knockout drum behind it. This lesson is the shape of the walk, with no numbers in it, because the numbers are the exercise.

## The fire chain as questions

Each step asks one question and each answer is the next step's input.

What orientation is the vessel and what is its diameter and length. What liquid level does it carry, and has that level been trimmed. That gives a wetted area.

Does the plot have adequate drainage and firefighting, and what environment factor does the insulation earn. That gives a pool fire duty.

What is the latent heat of the boiling liquid at the relieving pressure. That gives a relief load.

What is the set pressure and what overpressure is the fire case allowed. That gives a relieving pressure, which with the load and the gas description gives a required area, and that gives a letter and a margin.

## The drum chain as questions

What rate does the header carry, in what unit, at what conditions, against what standard base. That gives an actual volumetric rate.

What settling velocity applies. In this course that velocity is stated, for the reason the fifth module gave: the correlation that would compute one is held for literature.

What diameter are you testing and what level fraction does the drum hold. The level fraction gives a segment area fraction, then a vapour area, then a vapour velocity, and it also gives a fall distance. Those two give a required length, and the length against the diameter gives an L over D and possibly a note.

## The two places both chains can be read twice

Two inputs in this tier have a second reading that is also a valid number.

A level on a vessel reads lying down or standing up, and both orientations return an area. Only one is the vessel in front of you.

A fraction on a drum reads as a level fraction or as an area fraction, and both produce a length. Only the level reading is what this route takes. Before writing either answer, say which reading you used.

## What the walk excludes

Two things the tier has taught are worth naming as absences, because looking for them wastes time.

There is no route that picks the governing case. The exercise says which case to size, or gives you enough to size more than one, and the comparison is yours.

And no route converts a relieving load into a drum rate. The two halves are independent, so a figure from the fire chain never appears as a drum chain input.

## The order to work in

| order | what you write down |
| --- | --- |
| first | every stated condition, with its unit |
| second | which quantity each question is asking for |
| third | the engine call and its answer at full precision |
| fourth | the boundary the answer depends on |

The fourth row is the one people skip and the one this tier exists to teach. Every answer in the walk depends on something the engine was told rather than something it worked out, and naming that dependency is the difference between an answer and a defensible answer.

## Exercise

Write the fire chain and the drum chain as sequences of questions, with no numbers. Then name the two inputs that have a second valid reading, say which reading each route takes, and give the four-step order to work in.
