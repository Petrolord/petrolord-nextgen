# What was repaired, and what was not

Everything in this lesson is history. It describes what this engine did before it was repaired, and each item is a general lesson with an example here. Nothing here describes the engine you have been using, and no other lesson in this course is history.

## The frame, stated once

This module went through a repair before the course was written. Read every paragraph below as an account of a former state.


Three other places carry this history without a frame: the engine's source comments, the wave's recon record, and the repair's findings file. All three are provenance. A sentence lifted out of one arrives with no frame, and nobody can frame what they did not know was history.

## Item one, an output that read as reassurance

The general lesson first, because it outlives the example. The worst failure of a calculation is not a wrong number. It is a wrong number shaped like the answer somebody wanted. A depressuring study asks whether a vessel is down inside the customary time, so the dangerous failure answers yes.

Before the repair, when the first step of the march would have removed more mass than the vessel held, the loop broke with the clock still at zero. The time limit test was therefore false, and the function returned a time of zero seconds with the vessel at its start pressure and no error key. Every caller guard passed, and the screen printed a depressuring time of zero minutes under a hint saying that was inside the customary fifteen.

Read the shape rather than the arithmetic. A failure returning nothing gets investigated. A failure returning zero, where a small number is the good news, gets believed.

Today the same geometries march properly, because no step may remove more than a twentieth of the inventory. The three cases in the third module of this tier are those geometries, and each reaches its end pressure with a time above zero.

## Item two, an input that moved nothing

The general lesson: two factors that cancel algebraically leave a box a user will turn, watching nothing happen, and conclude the quantity does not matter. One sweep finds it.

Before the repair the drum used the same factor for the vapour cross-section and for the fall distance, and the two cancelled exactly. The holdup box moved the required length by less than a millionth of a millionth of a foot across its whole range, while the vapour velocity beside it moved by a factor of ten. The input was ambiguous too, used once as an area fraction and once as a depth fraction.

Today the input is the liquid level as a fraction of the diameter, which is what a level instrument reads. The vapour cross-section is the exact circular segment above it, and the engine returns the depth, the area fraction, the vapour area and the fall distance, so the convention is visible. The audit lesson on an input that changes nothing uses that holdup as its example of one that works.
