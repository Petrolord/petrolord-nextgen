# Onward

This lesson is about the engine as it ships and about where the rest of the subject lives. You have finished the Expert tier of a course whose whole argument was that the engine never chooses the case.

## What this tier added to that

The Associate tier could say, of any sizing route, which correction the engine computed and which one somebody typed. The Professional tier could say what a stated fraction is a fraction of. This tier asked the harder question: of every number an answer rests on, is there anything in this package that would notice if it moved.

For most of this engine there is, and the course names the route that would notice. For the viscosity fit and the sphere-drag correlation there is not, because both are shared with the checking side on purpose, and the honest form of that statement is the one you met: move either of them in both files and every published case still passes.

That is not a flaw to be embarrassed about. It is a limit that has been located, written down, and kept out of everything graded. An unlocated limit is the dangerous kind.

## Where the neighbouring subjects live

Five boundaries are worth carrying away, because each of them is a place this course deliberately stops.

The flare setback, the pool fire behind it and the customary allowable intensities belong to the Separation and Slug Catching course, which computes and grades a setback of its own.

Critical against subcritical flow through a port belongs to the Gas Lift course.

The droplet terminal velocity balance belongs twice over, to the Gas Well course and again to Separation, and the method you met here is the third answer this academy gives to one question.

Cooldown and no-touch time belong to the Flow Assurance course, which names no depressurisation as its own exclusion and so points straight back at this one.

What a knockout drum is for belongs to the Gas Processing course.

## The habits worth keeping

Four of them, and none is about relief valves.

Ask what a number rests on before you ask whether it looks right. The classes are computed, typed, and held with nothing behind it.

Measure a constant out of behaviour rather than assuming it. Ask the routine a question whose answer is that constant and nothing else.

Walk an input across its range and print the spread of the answer. It is one sweep and it tells you whether a box means anything.

When two things agree, ask how independent they were before they were compared. Exact agreement between an engine and a copy of itself is the weakest result on offer.
