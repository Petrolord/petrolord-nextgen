# Margin, and what it is worth

{{panel:fc-sizing-explorer}}

Every selection comes back with a margin beside the letter. The margin is the purchased orifice area divided by the required area, and the engine returns it so that nobody has to form that quotient by hand.

## The three streams of this tier, each to its own letter

| stream | fluid | required area in2 | orifice | orifice area in2 | margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORUBIRI | gas | 2.223779 | L | 2.853000 | 1.282951 |
| AKASO | liquid | 1.867758 | L | 2.853000 | 1.527500 |
| TEBIDABA | steam | 0.949984 | J | 1.287000 | 1.354759 |

Two of the three land on the same letter with quite different margins, and that is the clearest thing this table teaches. The gas case needed most of an L orifice. The liquid case needed rather less of one and got the same valve, because there is nothing between K and L to buy.

## What a margin is and what it is not

A margin is an artefact of the ladder. It is not a safety factor, it is not a design allowance, and it is not a measure of how conservative the sizing was. Two cases with identical engineering behind them can end up with very different margins purely because of where they fell between two rungs.

That is why nothing graded in this course is a margin or an orifice letter. Both are downstream of a published table this package cannot derive, so grading them would be grading a lookup.

Do not divide one margin by another, and do not read the spread of the margin column as telling you anything about the three cases. The digest prints the margin the engine returned on each row and prints no relation between the rows, and a quotient of two margins would be a number about the ladder wearing the clothes of a number about the plant.

## What it takes to move a letter

The obvious follow on question is how much the load would have to change before the letter changes. It is a good question and this tier does not answer it with a figure.

At its stated 68000.000000 lb/hr the ORUBIRI selection is L. The digest's attempt to find the load at which that selection changes did not return a figure, so no figure is quoted here. An invented one, or one interpolated off the ladder, would read on the page exactly like a measured one, and that is precisely the trade this course refuses to make.

What can be said without a figure is the shape of the answer. The load and the required area move together, so the letter changes when the required area crosses a rung, and the rungs are unevenly spaced. A case whose required area sits just below a rung takes that rung with a margin near 1.000000 and steps up on a very small increase in load. A case whose required area has just passed a rung has already stepped, so it carries a large margin and a great deal of room before it steps again.
