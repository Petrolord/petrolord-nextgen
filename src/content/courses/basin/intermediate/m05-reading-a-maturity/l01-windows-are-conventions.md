# Windows are conventions

The oil window is the most quoted idea in petroleum geochemistry and the least precisely defined. This lesson separates the physical content of maturity windows from their conventional edges, using the machinery you now own, so that you can use window language without being used by it.

## What is physical, what is chosen

The physical content: as the vitrinite ladder drains, source kerogens pass from unreacted, through a band of maximum oil generation, into conditions where earlier products crack toward gas. The read-out places those regimes on the reflectance scale, and the ordering, immature then oil then gas, is real, universal and kinetic.

The chosen part is the edges. This course quotes a common convention, oil window from Ro 0.55 to 1.3, but published schemes place the top anywhere from 0.5 to 0.7 and the base from 1.0 to 1.35, differing by author, region and kerogen type. Those differences are not carelessness. Generation is continuous, so any line drawn through it is a decision about how much conversion counts as "begun" and how much cracking counts as "over", and different operational purposes draw it differently.

The numbers you should refuse to treat as conventional are the kinetic ones underneath: at Ro 0.55 the ladder's F is 0.2709; at 1.3 it is 0.5033. Whatever edges a document picks, the inverse map tells you exactly what state it is committing to, and two documents' windows can be compared on the F scale even when their reflectance edges disagree.

## Windows do not carry temperatures

Module 3 established this and it bears repeating in window language: the same Ro 0.55 edge is crossed at 94 degC by a rock heating at 1 degC per Ma and at 107 by one at 10 degC per Ma. Ro 0.5 spans 86 to 99 across the same rates. A window quoted in degrees or in kilometres has silently fixed a heating rate; within one basin that may be harmless, across basins it is a 13 to 15 degree error waiting to happen.

## Windows do not carry yields

The subtler misuse: treating position-in-window as fraction-generated. The window is defined on the vitrinite clock; yield lives on the kerogen clock, and module 4 showed the two decouple by type. At mid-window maturity, a Type I source may be substantially converted while a Type III source has barely begun. "Mid oil window" is a statement about thermal history; how much oil it implies depends entirely on whose kerogen sat in that history.

For the fixture's Type II kerogen the coupling is well behaved, which is why the capstone can grade both clocks on shared histories, but the well-behavedness is a property of Type II, not of windows.

## Using windows well

Three habits make window language safe. Quote the reflectance edges you are using, since the reader cannot guess your convention. Convert edges to F when comparing documents, since F is the invariant state. And never let a window substitute for running the kerogen clock when yield is the question, since the window cannot see the spectrum.

Used this way, the window remains what it was always good for: a fast, portable summary of where a thermal history has taken a rock, calibrated by the one measurement, vitrinite reflectance, that outcrops of that history reliably offer.

## Worked example

Two reports describe the same source interval. Report A, using edges 0.6 to 1.2, calls it "upper third of the oil window". Report B, using edges 0.5 to 1.35, calls it "middle of the window". Reconcile them. Report A's description spans Ro 1.0 to 1.2, F 0.4324 to 0.4817. Report B's middle third spans Ro roughly 0.78 to 1.05, F 0.366 to 0.446. The overlap, F 0.43 to 0.45, is presumably where the rock sits: near Ro 1.0. The reports agree about the rock and differ about the ruler, which the F conversion makes visible in two lines.

## Exercise

Convert window edges 0.5, 0.7, 1.0 and 1.35 to F values. Then answer in one sentence each: what physical claim does any window convention encode correctly, and what two pieces of information does a window position never carry?

As a self check: the F values are $(\ln R_o + 1.6)/3.7$ = 0.2451, 0.3360, 0.4324 and 0.5135. Every convention correctly encodes the kinetic ordering immature, then oil generation, then cracking toward gas, as the ladder drains. A window position never carries the temperature or depth of its edges, which depend on heating rate, and never carries the converted fraction, which depends on the kerogen spectrum.
