# What Ro does not say

Reflectance is this tier's headline output and the industry's favourite maturity number. Its authority tempts people to read more out of it than the kinetics put in. This lesson is a disciplined list of what a reflectance does not tell you, each item with the machinery behind it.

## It does not give a temperature

Ro is a state of the drained ladder, and many histories drain a ladder equally. The graded pair proves it: 0.9871413464062039 belongs to a rock that reached 150 degC at 3 degC per Ma, and almost the same state, 1.006902942398925, belongs to a rock that reached only 144 degC three times slower. A measured Ro of 0.99 cannot distinguish them. Every "geothermometer" use of reflectance smuggles in a heating-rate assumption; module 3's crossing table is the honest version, a temperature per assumed rate, never a temperature.

The useful inversion runs the other way. Given an independently constrained history shape, from burial reconstruction, the measured Ro selects among candidate heat scenarios. Reflectance constrains histories, not instants.

## It does not give a yield

The vitrinite clock knows nothing of your source rock's chemistry. At the identical mid-window state, module 4's types span a factor of a few in conversion, and at cooler states two orders of magnitude. Ro answers "what history has this rock had", and only the kerogen clock, fed that history and a spectrum, answers "what did the history generate". The Expert tier goes further still: even conversion is not charge until mass and expulsion enter, and neither is visible from a polished vitrinite grain.

## It does not remember the schedule

Because Ro is a single number, it compresses away the path. A rock that spent 40 Ma in the kitchen and one that dashed through it hot and fast can land on similar reflectances while their generation histories, and critically the timing of expulsion relative to trap formation, differ entirely. Two numbers begin to recover the schedule, which is why real calibrations use reflectance profiles with depth, and why the Expert tier's forward model outputs full time series rather than endpoints. When you meet the reference basin's erosion signature there, it will be exactly a case of one number carrying memory the temperature profile has lost.

## It barely moves backwards

The ladder only drains; the engine enforces that Ro never decreases. Cooling, uplift and erosion leave reflectance parked at its historical maximum. That makes Ro a ratchet, which is a limitation and an instrument at once. Limitation: present-day Ro dates nothing, since the maximum may be ancient. Instrument: a reflectance that is too high for the present depth and gradient is evidence of a hotter or deeper past, and quantifying that excess is precisely how the Expert tier reads the reference basin's missing 600 metres.

## Reading the list as design

Notice that each "does not" is a separation this course built deliberately. No temperature: the state-versus-path lesson. No yield: the two-clock separation. No schedule: endpoint versus series. No reversal: monotone read-out. The scheme is not weak where the list says; it is precise about which question it answers, and every item on the list is answered by a different, named part of the pipeline. Software that blurs these boundaries, and module 1 named a real example, produces numbers that sound like all four answers and are none of them.

## Worked example

A wellsite report: "Ro measured at 1.1 percent at 3200 m. Present gradient gives 118 degC there. Conclusion: the section reached 118 degC and is generating today." Rewrite it honestly. Ro 1.1 is F = 0.4581, a state; on plausible rates it was reached at 155 to 170 degC, all above today's 118, so the rock has been hotter than it is now, or heated at rates outside the assumed family. Whether it generates today depends on its present kinetics at 118 degC, which for most spectra at that state is slow, and on its type, which the report never states. Three sentences, each now backed by a module.

## Exercise

For each of the four "does not" items, name the piece of tier machinery that supplies what Ro lacks. Then answer in one sentence: why is the ratchet property an instrument rather than only a limitation?

As a self check: temperature needs an assumed heating rate, module 3's crossing table; yield needs the kerogen spectrum and clock, module 4; schedule needs profiles or forward-model time series, the Expert tier; reversal is forbidden by the monotone read-out, so no machinery recovers cooling from a single Ro. The ratchet is an instrument because it preserves the thermal maximum through later cooling, letting a present-day measurement testify about vanished burial or heat that nothing else in the well records.
