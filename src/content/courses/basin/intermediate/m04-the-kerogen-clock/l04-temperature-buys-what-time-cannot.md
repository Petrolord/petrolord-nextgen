# Temperature buys what time cannot

The stall's mirror image completes the physics of this tier. Hold the duration fixed at a mere 10 Ma and walk the temperature up in 20 degree steps, and conversion multiplies by factors that no amount of waiting could match. This lesson tabulates that walk and closes the loop with module 3's ramps.

## The 10 Ma walk

Type II kerogen, 10 Ma at constant temperature:

| T (degC) | TR after 10 Ma |
|---|---|
| 80 | 0.0013691978345186095 |
| 100 | 0.022481215976523083 |
| 120 | 0.11074154468752906 |
| 140 | 0.2956748118719801 |
| 160 | 0.5558799719796457 |

Each 20 degree step multiplies conversion by 16.4, then 4.9, then 2.7, then 1.9. Set the comparison side by side: forty extra Ma at 100 degC bought a factor of 2.44; twenty extra degrees at the same 10 Ma bought a factor of 4.9. In this part of the ladder, twenty degrees is worth more than a factor-of-five of time, and the exchange rate gets more extreme the cooler you start.

## Why the multipliers shrink

The 16.4 at the cold end and the 1.9 at the hot end are the same ladder read at two depths. At 80 degC only the 46 kcal toe reacts at all, so warming to 100 unlocks the 48 bin almost from zero: near-multiplicative gain. By 140 degC the front is inside the spectrum's bulk, the 52 and 54 kcal bins, and each further step converts mass that is partly already spoken for: the gains become additive rather than multiplicative. On the panel this is the transformation curve changing character as you raise the temperature control: from hugging the floor, to rising steadily, to leaping toward completion.

By 160 degC and 10 Ma the kerogen is past half converted, 0.5558799719796457. The whole main phase of generation, from a few percent to a half, fits inside two of these 20 degree steps. That compactness is the kinetic basis of the classic idea of an oil kitchen: a relatively narrow temperature band, entered through burial, inside which most of a source's yield is realised.

## The ramps, revisited

Module 3's rocks experienced exactly this walk, spread along time by their heating rate. A ramp at 3 degC per Ma spends 6.67 Ma in each 20 degree band, so by 160 degC it has accumulated roughly what this table's rows accumulate, blended along the way. The two fixtures are one physics viewed in two cuts: hold time and vary temperature here, ride both together there. This is also why module 3's rate effect concentrated in the mid-ladder: the table's big multipliers are the same bins that discriminate between ramps.

The practical instrument this gives you: for any proposed burial history, ask which 20 degree band the source spent real time in. Time in the 80 to 100 band is bookkeeping. Time in the 120 to 160 band is generation. A history that dashes through the kitchen band in 2 Ma generates less than one that lingers there for 20, and now you can size both.

## Worked example

Rank three histories for total Type II conversion, then verify the ranking's logic. History A: 100 Ma at 100 degC. History B: 10 Ma at 140 degC after arriving cold. History C: 50 Ma at 120 degC. From the tables: A gives 0.07419624543388115. B gives 0.2956748118719801. C is not tabulated at 50 Ma, but 10 Ma at 120 gives 0.11074154468752906 and time multipliers at this depth of the ladder run near 2.4 per factor of 5, so C sits near 0.27. Ranking: B first, C close behind, A last despite having by far the most time. Heat beats time, and the margin is not small.

## Exercise

From the table, compute the multiplier for the 100 to 120 degC step, and compare it with the 10-to-50 Ma time multiplier from the stall lesson. Then answer in one sentence: why do the temperature multipliers shrink as T rises while remaining above 1?

As a self check: $0.11074154468752906 / 0.022481215976523083 = 4.926$, exactly double the 2.4367 that five times the duration bought at the same temperature. The multipliers shrink because each warming step moves the reacting front from unlocking untouched bins, a multiplicative gain, to finishing partly drained ones, an additive gain, but they stay above 1 because higher bins always hold potential the cooler front could not reach.
