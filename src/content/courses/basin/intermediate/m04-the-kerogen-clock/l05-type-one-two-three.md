# Type I, II and III

The last variable of the kinetics tier is the kerogen itself. Run the identical history through the three library spectra and the answers spread by two orders of magnitude. This lesson quantifies that spread, connects it to the spectra of lesson 1, and settles what kerogen type does and does not change in a basin model.

{{panel:bs-kinetics-explorer}}

## The spread at 100 degC

Fifty Ma at a constant 100 degC:

| type | TR at 50 Ma |
|---|---|
| Type I | 0.12881195813250912 |
| Type II | 0.05477927380797565 |
| Type III | 0.0009743781381545968 |

Identical thermometer readings, identical durations, and Type I has converted 132 times the fraction Type III has. Even Type I versus Type II is a factor of 2.35. Anyone who models "the source rock" without committing to its type has an answer uncertain by two orders of magnitude, which is to say, no answer.

The spectra explain the ordering with no further physics. At 100 degC the reacting front reaches the 48-to-50 kcal rungs. Type I holds real potential there and just below the front's reach at 52; Type II holds its modest toe, 0.17 at or below 50; Type III's spectrum only begins at 50 kcal, holding 0.01 there, with the bulk from 56 upward, unreachable by an order-of-magnitude margin.

## The spread narrows with heat

The same comparison at 130 degC and 50 Ma: Type I 0.4981773912481696, Type II 0.29756912914818, Type III 0.034745326540427834. The I-to-III ratio collapses from 132 to 14, and I-to-II from 2.35 to 1.67. Hotter histories push the front into every spectrum's bulk, and the types converge toward the same destination, full conversion, differing in schedule rather than in fate. Type III is not a rock that will not generate; it is a rock that generates deeper and later, which is why terrestrial kerogen is the classic gas source of deep basins.

The practical reading: type uncertainty matters most for marginal, cooler kitchens, where it decides whether generation has meaningfully begun at all, and least for deep, hot ones, where all three types are well along.

## What type does not change

Run the panel across the three types and watch what moves. The transformation curve moves enormously. The reflectance curves do not move at all: 0.9871413464062039 at 150 degC on the capstone ramp for every type, because Ro belongs to the vitrinite clock and the vitrinite clock's parameters are fixed. This is module 1's separation made observable, and it is worth performing deliberately once: it is the diagnostic you would run on unfamiliar software, and the audit precedent says the test is not hypothetical.

So a measured Ro constrains the thermal history; the kerogen type then decides what that history did to the source's potential. Two basins with identical Ro profiles and different source types have identical thermal stories and utterly different charge stories.

## Worked example

A prospect review offers this: "The source interval is Type III. Regional Ro at target depth is 0.7, so the source is early mature and should have begun expelling oil." Find both errors. First, Ro 0.7 describes the vitrinite ladder, not the Type III spectrum: at histories that produce Ro 0.7, the front sits around the 50 kcal rungs, where Type III holds almost nothing, so its TR is of order a percent, not meaningful generation. Second, even the converted fraction of a Type III kerogen is gas prone, so "expelling oil" doubles the error. The review has used maturity as if it were conversion and ignored the spectrum entirely.

## Exercise

State the I-to-III conversion ratio at 100 degC and at 130 degC, both at 50 Ma. Then answer in a sentence each: why does the ratio collapse with temperature, and what is the one model output that switching kerogen type can never move?

As a self check: the ratios are $0.12881195813250912 / 0.0009743781381545968 = 132.2$ and $0.4981773912481696 / 0.034745326540427834 = 14.3$. The collapse happens because heat drives the reacting front into the bulk of every spectrum, so the types differ mainly in when conversion happens rather than whether. The untouchable output is the vitrinite reflectance: Ro is computed from the fixed published Easy%Ro state, which no kerogen edit reaches.
