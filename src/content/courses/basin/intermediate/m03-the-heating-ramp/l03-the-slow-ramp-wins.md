# The slow ramp wins

The Associate tier promised you this lesson. Two rocks are heated to the same 150 degC, one at 3 degC per Ma and one at 1 degC per Ma, and when they are compared at that same temperature the slow one is the more mature. Here are the numbers, the mechanism, and the size of the effect, because all three are graded.

{{panel:bs-kinetics-explorer}}

## The pair of values

On the 3 degC per Ma ramp, Ro at 150 degC is 0.9871413464062039. On the 1 degC per Ma ramp it is 1.1129254516555198. Both carry tolerance 0.002 on the capstone. The slow rock reads 12.74 percent higher at the same temperature.

In reacted weight the gap is plainer: F is 0.4289345833269452 against 0.4613492136231176. The slow rock has reacted 0.0324 more of the scheme's weight, which is more than three whole bins' worth of the light end of the ladder.

## The mechanism, stated carefully

Both rocks passed through exactly the same temperatures, from 20 to 150 degC, in the same order. Neither ever had a rate advantage: at any given temperature, both rocks' bins reacted at identical Arrhenius rates. The only difference is duration. The slow rock took 130 Ma to make the climb; the fast one took 43.333 Ma. At every temperature on the way, the slow rock sat for three times as long, and reaction is rate multiplied by residence.

It is tempting to compress that into "time is a reagent", and the Associate tier did exactly that, with this pair of values promised as the proof. The compression is fine as a slogan as long as you keep the precise version underneath: what accumulates is the time integral of a temperature-dependent rate, so time spent hot counts enormously and time spent cold counts almost nothing. The slow ramp wins not because time alone matters but because its extra time was spent at the same temperatures, degree for degree.

## Why the gap is only 13 percent

Three times the residence at every temperature might suggest three times the reaction. The reflectance gap is 12.74 percent, and even the F gap is only 7.6 percent. Where did the factor of three go?

Into the ladder. Tripling time at a fixed temperature drains a bin three times as fast toward its floor, but the bins the front has passed are already empty, and for those bins extra time buys nothing. Meanwhile the bins above the front are so slow that even three times their reaction is still nearly nothing. Extra time mostly deepens the drain in the narrow band of partially reacted bins at the front. The ladder converts a factor of three in time into roughly one third of one bin-spacing of extra progress, which is the 0.0324 of F you see. This is the same mechanism module 4 will show you in its pure form as the isothermal stall.

There is a cleaner way to say it: on a ladder of staggered exponentials, maturity depends on time roughly logarithmically at fixed temperature, while it depends on temperature nearly linearly along the ramp, and the exam will offer you both true and subtly wrong versions of that sentence.

## The comparison that would flip it

Compare the rocks at the same age instead of the same temperature and everything reverses. At 43.333 Ma the fast rock is at 150 degC reading 0.9871, while the slow rock is only at 63.3 degC, barely off the floor. The fast ramp wins any same-age comparison because it is hotter, and heat beats time whenever the two compete. The graded comparison holds temperature equal precisely to isolate the pure time effect, and you should always notice which comparison a claim is making. "Slower heating means higher maturity" is true at equal temperature and false at equal time, and both halves of that sentence are exam material.

## Worked example

Predict, before computing, the direction and rough size of Ro at 150 degC on a 10 degC per Ma ramp, from the pattern of the pair. Faster again, so lower again, and each factor of about 3 in rate cost about 0.13 of reflectance going from rate 1 to 3, so expect roughly 0.86 to 0.88. The table's value is 0.8795791051334334, and the next two lessons put it to work.

## Exercise

State the two graded values and compute their ratio. Then answer in one sentence each: where did the slow rock's factor of three in time go, and in what comparison does the fast ramp win?

As a self check: $1.1129254516555198 / 0.9871413464062039 = 1.1274$, the 12.74 percent gap. The factor of three was absorbed by the ladder, where extra time only deepens the narrow reacting front, empty bins gaining nothing and inert bins nearly nothing, so time acts logarithmically. The fast ramp wins any comparison at equal age, because it has reached higher temperatures, and heat outweighs time when they compete.
