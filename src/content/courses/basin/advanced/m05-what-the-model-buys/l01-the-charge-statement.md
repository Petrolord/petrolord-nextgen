# The charge statement

A basin model is run to answer one commercial question: did this kitchen deliver enough hydrocarbon, at the right time, to charge the prospects that depend on it? This lesson assembles the fixture's complete charge statement from the numbers you now own, as the template for stating any basin's.

## The statement

The reference basin's source rock held a generative potential of 18786.405883452077 kg of hydrocarbon per square metre of basin. By present day it has converted 74.23743797385286 percent of that potential, generating 13946.54641524398 kg/m2. Of the generated mass, 10048.985378825158 kg/m2 was expelled from the source, an expulsion efficiency of 72.05357569986879 percent; 3897.561036418822 kg/m2 remains in the source's pores. Expulsion began at 80 Ma, was 77 percent complete by 60 Ma, and finished, in the sense that no further mass has left, at 11 Ma. Overall, 53.49072856813322 percent of everything this source could ever make has been delivered to carrier beds.

Every sentence traces to machinery: the potential to module 4's closed form, the conversion to the Professional tier's clock under this tier's temperatures, the expulsion to the bucket and its squeeze, the dates to the monotone diaries.

## Scale and the sanity check

Convert to exploration units to feel the size. Ten thousand kilograms per square metre is ten million tonnes per square kilometre; over a modest 20 by 30 km kitchen, six billion tonnes expelled, of order forty billion barrels of oil equivalent. Real accumulations capture single-digit percentages of expelled mass after migration losses, so this kitchen could plausibly source a multi-billion-barrel province. The order-of-magnitude habit matters more than the digits: a charge statement whose expelled mass cannot survive this conversion into field-size language is either broken upstream or describing a basin not worth drilling.

## Timing against traps

The statement's dates are its sharpest commercial content. Essentially all expulsion here predates 60 Ma, with a final increment at 11 Ma. A trap formed at 90 Ma waited through the entire charge; a trap formed at 40 Ma missed three-quarters of it; structure formed today receives nothing but what remigrates. And the 11 Ma increment, the squeeze, is charge delivered by unroofing, a mechanism worth screening for wherever inversion postdates a mature kitchen: late uplift over a stalled source is not just a preservation risk, it is a last act of charge.

The general template: state the expulsion window's start, its bulk, and its last act, then place each prospect's trap-formation date against them. The model's series make all three dates readable; the capstone grades the endpoint masses, and this lesson's discipline is remembering the endpoint is the least of it.

## What the statement does not claim

The model delivers mass expelled from the source, at the source. It says nothing about migration pathways, trap volumetrics, seal integrity or preservation, each of which multiplies its own efficiency into what a well finds. Charge is necessary, never sufficient; the statement's role in assessment is to bound the system from above and to time it. Stating more than that from a basin model is the overreach this course has spent three tiers teaching you to name.

## Worked example

Draft the two-sentence charge summary a prospect review would quote. "The Type II source at 2800 to 3200 m converted 74 percent of an 18.8 tonne/m2 potential, expelling 10.0 tonnes/m2, 53 percent of potential, with the expulsion window effectively 80 to 60 Ma and a minor squeeze-driven increment at 11 Ma. Traps older than 60 Ma saw the full charge; younger structure competes for remigrated volumes only." Every figure quotable to full precision on request, every claim's machinery one module away.

As a self check: conversion $13946.54641524398 / 18786.405883452077 = 0.7423743797385286$; expulsion efficiency $10048.985378825158 / 13946.54641524398 = 0.7205357569986879$; retained $3897.561036418822 / 13946.54641524398 = 0.2794642430013121$; delivered $10048.985378825158 / 18786.405883452077 = 0.5349072856813322$. Timing decides because mass without coincidence charges nothing: a trap receives only what is expelled after it exists, so the window's dates, not the totals, separate charged prospects from dry ones.
