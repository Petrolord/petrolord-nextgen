# The signature is two runs

The capstone's sixth field asks for one number: the difference in final reflectance between the basin as it was and the basin without its erosion event. This lesson computes it, fixes its definition exactly, and establishes why a difference of two model runs is a measurement of something real.

{{panel:bs-charge-explorer}}

## The definition

Run one: the full reference basin, erosion event included. Final source Ro: 1.6718288798752388. Run two: the identical project with the erosion list empty. Final source Ro: 1.6151780693528823. The signature is the difference,

$$\Delta R_o = 1.6718288798752388 - 1.6151780693528823 = 0.05665081052235643,$$

graded with tolerance 0.001. The Associate tier forward-quoted it rounded, 0.0567; you now own every digit and both parents.

Definition details that grading cares about. The difference is taken with-minus-without, so it is positive. Both runs read the same layer, the source, at the same date, present day. And "without" means the event removed and nothing else touched: same stratigraphy, same heat flow, same conventions. A signature is only meaningful between runs that differ in exactly one cause.

## Why a difference measures a cause

Forward models earn trust through controlled comparison, and the signature is the cleanest possible case. Every simplification the two runs share, instant deposition, elastic rebound, the centre convention, the phantom's own inferred deposition age, cancels to first order in the difference. What survives is the causal chain unique to the event: extra burial, hotter decade, deeper ladder drainage. That is why the signature is a better-behaved number than either run's absolute Ro: absolute maturity carries every convention's bias; the difference isolates the event.

This is the tier's methodological centre. A single run answers "what does this model say"; a pair of runs differing in one input answers "what does this cause do", and only the second kind of answer transfers from model to basin.

## Where the signature comes from in time

Difference the two runs' Ro histories and the signature assembles almost entirely inside the hot decade. At 20 Ma the runs differ by 1.6077037957403433 minus 1.601785643476647, about 0.0059, accumulated slowly through the shared history by tiny transient differences around the phantom's future site. By 10 Ma the gap is 1.6678310470093916 minus 1.6097038167517639, about 0.058. The last ten million years move it only slightly, both runs' ladders nearly stalled: the final 0.0566 is a ten-megayear-old fact, preserved unchanged by the ratchet. If reflectance recorded dates as well as states, this basin's would read "acquired 18 to 11 Ma".

## The inverse reading

Forward, the fixture says: 600 m removed at 10 Ma leaves 0.0566 of excess Ro. Inverted, an interpreter measuring 0.0566 of unexplained maturity at this source, in this basin, would recover the 600 m. The next lesson supplies the calibration curve, signature against amount, and its nonlinearity; what matters here is the logical form. Missing section is not observed, it is inferred, and the inference runs through a forward model differenced against itself. The phrase "measured erosion" in a basin study almost always means exactly this construction.

## Worked example

State what the signature would be if the event were moved from 10 Ma to 100 Ma, direction and rough size, using tier machinery. At 100 Ma the basin is young and hot, heat flow 70; the phantom's extra burial would land on a source whose ladder is far less drained, where the front reacts faster, and the decade would run near 180 degrees. Expect a signature several times larger. The general rule falls out: an erosion event's kinetic signature depends on when it interrupts the maturation, and late events over stalled sources leave the smallest marks. The 0.0566 is small precisely because this event came late.
