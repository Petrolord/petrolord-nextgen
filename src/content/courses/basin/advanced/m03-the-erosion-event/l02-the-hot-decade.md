# The hot decade

Between 20 and 10 Ma the reference basin ran hotter at the source than at any other time in its 150 million years. This lesson reads that decade quantitatively, because the capstone's signature field is its residue, and because it teaches how burial and heat flow negotiate a maximum.

## The competition for hottest moment

Without the erosion event, the source's temperature history peaks early: 165.60511032656453 degC at 76 Ma, just after the Upper Shale's arrival has been digested, when the heat-flow history still supplies about 67.7 mW/m2. From there to the present the basin cools 16 degrees, geometry fixed, purely because the basal supply decays to 60. The early basin is hotter than the deep basin: the Associate tier's onward lesson promised this tier would need transient thinking, and here is the payoff.

With the event, the competition has a second entrant. The phantom decade drives the source to 168.51433459340572 degC at 18 Ma, hotter than the 76 Ma peak by 2.9 degrees, at an age when the heat flow is down to about 61. Extra depth beat fading supply, but barely: 328.79 m of extra burial bought less than three degrees net, because it arrived when the basal flux was weakest. The same phantom placed at 76 Ma would have pushed past 180.

The phantom's arrival at 20 Ma lifts the source 12.85 degrees in one step, transient catch-up trailing after, the same physics as the depositional arrivals of module 2 because to the model the phantom is a deposition. The removal at 10 Ma drops 13.4 degrees in a step, module 2's predicted downward relaxation after unroofing. In between, ten steps at 167 to 168.5.

## What ten hot megayears do

The Professional tier prices this precisely. At 168 degC the reacting front sits high on the ladder; the source's vitrinite state, already drained to Ro about 1.6077037957403433 at 20 Ma, drains further to 1.6678310470093916 by 10 Ma. In the no-event run the same decade moves Ro only 1.6017856434766470 to 1.6097038167517639. The decade's differential contribution is the bulk of what the next lesson grades.

Note also what the decade does not do much of: generation. TR moves from 0.7124276686396793 at 20 Ma to 0.7402699369369322 at 11, a gain of 0.028, the stall behaving exactly as taught even at 168 degrees, because this kerogen's remaining potential sits in its highest bins. The decade's charge significance comes through a different mechanism entirely, the cap squeeze of module 4.

## Worked example

Predict what the hot decade would look like with a 300 m event instead of 600, then check the direction on the panel. Prediction: roughly half the extra burial, therefore a smaller temperature lift, a peak nearer 166 than 168.5, a weaker differential drain of the ladder, and a signature falling by more than half by the Professional tier's rate arithmetic, since the lift sits on an exponential. The panel at 300 m shows final Ro 1.6295732164506467 against the no-event 1.6151780693528823: a signature of 0.0144, about a quarter of the 600 m event's, confirming the more-than-proportional shrinkage that the sensitivity lesson formalises.
