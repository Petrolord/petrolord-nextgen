# The squeeze

The erosion event's largest effect on this basin is not the maturity signature. It is a charge effect, invisible in any reflectance, delivered through the retention cap, and it is the sharpest fact in this course: most of the event's contribution to expelled mass exists because the bucket was squeezed at the right moment.

## The identity

Final expelled mass, 10048.985378825158 kg/m2, is graded with tolerance 5. Here is where it comes from, exactly. At 11 Ma, the last step of the phantom decade, generation stands at 13907.011498614682 and the squeezed cap at 3858.026119789524. Their difference:

$$13907.011498614682 - 3858.026119789524 = 10048.985378825158$$

The capstone's fifth field is this subtraction, to the last digit, an exact float64 identity. The final expelled mass was determined at 11 Ma, and nothing after that moment changed it.

## Why nothing after changed it

At 10 Ma the phantom vanishes and elastic rebound regrows the bucket to 4662.34280519763. Generation by then is 13912.533971907958, so generated-minus-cap falls to 9250.191: less than what has already been expelled. Expelled oil does not climb back into the rock, and the engine encodes that with one function: expelled mass is the running maximum of generated-minus-cap. The rebound leaves the rock holding 3897.56 kg, below its regrown cap's brim by 764.78, and expulsion cannot resume until generation exceeds the cap plus what already left, which this basin's stalled kitchen never achieves in its remaining ten megayears.

## Splitting the event's charge contribution

Run the comparison the signature lesson taught, on expulsion. Without the event: 8790.335784168848 expelled. With it: 10048.985378825158. The event contributed 1258.64959465631 kg/m2 of charge, a 14.3 percent uplift. Now split it. Extra generation from the hot decade, the difference in generated mass between runs: 493.8678258775017. The remainder, 764.7817687788085, came not from making more oil but from the cap being 804.3 kg smaller while the making was happening. Three-fifths of the event's charge is squeeze, not cook.

That split deserves a slow read, because it inverts a habit. Erosion events enter most discussions as maturity anecdotes, the delta-Ro of module 3. In this basin the event's charge effect is twenty-two times its signature's mass-equivalent in extra generation, and the mechanism is mechanical, not kinetic: deepen a generating source and you wring its stored oil out of it, whether or not the extra temperature cooks anything further.

## The timing condition

The squeeze worked because the event interrupted a source that was generating, or had generated, more than its squeezed cap. The monotone rule then locked the gain in. Move the same event to 120 Ma, before real generation: the squeezed bucket sits empty, nothing to wring, no charge effect. Move it after a hypothetical complete stall with the cap already exceeded: the squeeze still expels the stored increment. The general condition: cap squeezes convert retained inventory into charge, so they pay exactly when the rock is holding oil, which module 5 will turn into a screening question for real basins with unroofing histories.

## Worked example

Compute the event's charge contribution per metre of eroded section, and compare the marginal rates. Total contribution: 1258.64959465631 over 600 m, 2.098 kg/m2 per metre. From the sensitivity table, the 300 m event contributes 468.263 over 300 m, 1.561 per metre, and the 900 m event 2429.343 over 900, 2.699 per metre. Convex again, and for the same ultimate reason as the signature: deeper phantoms squeeze harder, both factors of the cap falling with depth while the hot decade's extra generation also compounds.
