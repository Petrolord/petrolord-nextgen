# The petroleum system

A petroleum system is the set of things that all have to be true at once for a discovery to exist. There are six of them. Any one of the six can fail on its own and the result is the same: a dry hole, or a structure full of water, or a shale that has all the organic carbon in the world and never made anything with it.

This lesson introduces the vocabulary the rest of the ladder uses. Learn the words now, because from here on they are used precisely and not as loose synonyms for each other.

## Source

A source rock is a rock that contains enough organic matter, preserved in the right chemical state, to generate hydrocarbons when it is heated. Two properties describe it. The quantity of organic carbon says how much raw material is present. The kerogen type says what that material will produce and at what temperature, since marine and lacustrine and terrestrial organic matter break down along different paths and yield different mixtures of oil and gas.

A source rock is a claim about potential. It says nothing yet about whether the potential was ever realised.

## Maturity

Maturity is how far the source rock has actually gone down that path. It is the product of temperature and time acting together on the kerogen, and it is the single element this ladder spends the most effort on, because it is the one you cannot observe directly in the past. You can measure a maturity indicator on a sample today. You cannot measure when the rock became mature, and when is what decides whether the charge had anywhere to go.

Maturity is usually spoken of in three stages. Immature means the kerogen has barely reacted. The oil window is the temperature range in which the bulk of the liquid is generated. Beyond it, gas is generated and any oil left in place is cracked to gas.

The Professional tier of this course computes maturity properly, with a kinetic model that integrates the whole temperature history rather than reading a single present day temperature. This tier does not compute it and does not examine it. What this tier gives you is the temperature history that any maturity calculation has to be handed.

## Migration

Migration is the movement of hydrocarbons out of the source rock and into a reservoir. It has two stages that are worth keeping separate in your head. Primary migration, also called expulsion, is the departure of fluid from the source rock itself, which will not happen until enough has been generated to fill the source's own pore space and push out. Secondary migration is the journey after that, up through carrier beds, along faults, under seals, driven by buoyancy.

Migration is where a lot of good source rocks stop being interesting. Generation that never escapes the source is generation you cannot produce.

## Trap

A trap is the geometry that stops the fluid moving. Structural traps are shapes, such as anticlines and fault closures, where the carrier bed turns down in every direction from a high point. Stratigraphic traps are changes in the rock itself, such as a sand that pinches out into shale or a reef that sits inside mud. The earth modelling course in this ladder builds the container that a trap lives in.

## Seal

A seal is the rock above the trap that the hydrocarbons cannot pass through, at least not at the rate at which they arrive. Seals are usually fine grained, usually shale or evaporite, and their effectiveness is a question of capillary entry pressure rather than of a rock being literally impermeable. Every seal leaks eventually. A working seal is one that leaks more slowly than the trap is filled.

## Timing

Timing is the sixth element and it is the one that has no physical existence. The other five are rocks and shapes you could in principle point at. Timing is the requirement that the five line up on the same clock.

The trap has to exist before the charge arrives. The seal has to be in place before the trap starts to fill. The source has to reach maturity while both are still standing. A basin that produced all five elements in the wrong order produced nothing.

This is why a basin model is the workflow that runs the clock. Everything else in the ladder maps the elements. Only a basin model dates them and lays them on one timeline so that the order can be checked.

## What this tier builds

Of the six elements, this tier builds the physical foundation of exactly two, and it does so in a form the higher tiers consume directly.

Burial gives you where every layer sat at every time in the past. It is the input to everything, because temperature follows depth and generation follows temperature.

Heat gives you the temperature at every depth for a given heat flow and a given set of rock conductivities.

That is the honest boundary. Burial and heat are geometry and conduction. They are arithmetic you can do by hand and check to the last digit, which is exactly what this tier asks you to do. Maturity, generation, expulsion and migration are chemistry and flow, they need the burial and heat history as an input, and building them on top of a burial history you do not understand is how people produce confident basin models that are wrong from the first step.

## Exercise

For each of the six elements, write one sentence saying what failure of that element looks like at the well. Then say which of the six can fail even when all the physical ingredients are present and correct.

Self check: no source means nothing was ever available to generate. No maturity means the source rock is present and unreacted. No migration means the charge stayed in the source. No trap means the charge passed through and kept going. No seal means the trap filled and then leaked away. Timing is the element that can fail with every physical ingredient present and correct, since a source that matured and expelled before the trap or the seal existed leaves you a perfect structure with nothing in it.
