# The story so far

Five modules, one claim, and the evidence for it.

## The claim

A well test measures a pressure history. Everything reported from it is the result of choices, and the largest of those choices is which points go into the straight line. On the buildup you have been working with, that one choice moves the permeability by a factor of nearly four and inverts the sign of the skin.

Everything else in this tier is either the machinery that produces those numbers or the evidence that the claim is true.

## What each module established

**Module 1.** A well test disturbs the reservoir and records a pressure. The volume it interrogates is large, the quantities it reports are inferred rather than measured, and each of them feeds a decision that costs money. The rate has to be right before anything else can be.

**Module 2.** In infinite-acting radial flow, pressure is linear in the logarithm of time with a slope of 162.6 q B mu / (k h). The slope contains permeability and thickness and nothing else. What the test actually determines is the product kh; splitting it needs a net pay from a log interpretation, and that division is where a large part of the reported permeability's uncertainty enters.

**Module 3.** Skin is a residual: whatever pressure drop the ideal radial solution failed to predict, collected into one dimensionless number. It comes from where the line SITS, evaluated at a one-hour reference, so it depends on the window even more strongly than the permeability does. Converted to psi it is what an intervention decision is made on, and converted to a flow efficiency it is what gets communicated. Damage is only one of six things that produce it.

**Module 4.** A buildup is two superimposed wells. That gives the Horner ratio, which runs backwards and is bounded below by 1, and the extrapolation to that bound is p*, which is not the average reservoir pressure. Agarwal equivalent time is the transform used for derivatives and type curves, and it saturates at the producing time, which means a buildup can never see further than the production before it.

**Module 5.** Wellbore storage dominates the early data and is steeper than radial flow on a semilog plot, so any window containing it reports a permeability that is too low and a skin that is too small. Narrowing the window walks the answer up towards the truth from below, monotonically, without reaching it. r squared rises to 0.9997 while the permeability is still 9 percent wrong, so it cannot be used to justify a window.

## The four numbers to carry

- The semilog slope for this reservoir at the planted permeability: 21.520588235294117 psi per cycle. Any fit far from that is not radial flow.
- The skin pressure drop at the planted values: 121.47352941176467 psi, against a total drawdown of about 269 psi.
- The permeability from fitting everything: 23.12907021605519 mD, against a planted 85.
- The skin from fitting everything: minus 2.6837412661474804, against a planted plus 6.5.

The first two are what the well is. The last two are what a defensible-looking analysis says it is.

## What you cannot yet do

You can fit a line and you can walk a window until the answer stops moving. What you cannot do is JUSTIFY a window from the data, because the tool for that is the pressure derivative and it belongs to the Professional tier.

You also cannot tell a fractured well from a damaged one, or notice a boundary, or recognise that a reservoir has two porosities. All of those change the shape of the response in ways a semilog plot hides, and all of them are diagnosed on the log-log derivative plot.

Nor have you met a rate history more complicated than one change, a gas well, or a well analysed from production data with no shut-in at all. Those are the Expert tier.

## What this tier certifies

That you can take a buildup, choose a window and say why, fit the semilog line, and report a permeability, a skin, a skin pressure drop, an extrapolated pressure and a radius of investigation, with the arithmetic right and the caveats stated.

That is the Associate level of this subject, and it is most of what is actually done with well tests in practice.
