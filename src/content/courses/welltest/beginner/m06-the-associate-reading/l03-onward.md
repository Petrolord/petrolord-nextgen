# Onward

What the next tier gives you, and what to do with this one meanwhile.

## The tool you are missing

Every unresolved question in this tier has the same answer: the pressure derivative.

You could not justify a window. The derivative shows you where the radial plateau is, directly, as a flat stretch.

You could not tell whether a straight line was radial flow. The derivative separates the regimes, because each flow regime has its own characteristic slope on a log-log derivative plot: one for storage and for a closed boundary, zero for radial flow, a half for linear flow, a quarter for bilinear.

You could not see boundaries. The derivative doubles when a sealing fault is felt, falls when a constant-pressure boundary is felt, and turns up to a unit slope when the reservoir is closed.

The Professional tier is built on that one plot, and on a gallery of six more tests with different wells and different boundaries in them.

## What the Professional tier will show you

That the automatic regime labels the software prints are slope bands rather than diagnoses, and that on every fixture in this course at least one of them is a transition rather than a regime.

That past a sealing fault, the late semilog line reports about half the permeability, with the skin sign inverting again from an entirely different cause than the one you saw in module 5.

That a fractured well analysed as a radial one reports a reservoir five times better than it is.

That when a test reaches a closed boundary, the late Cartesian line gives a drainage area accurate to a few thousandths of a percent, which is the only place in this course where a well test measures a volume rather than a capacity.

## What the Expert tier will show you

Automatic model fitting, which recovers the planted values the straight line could not, and which will also converge confidently on a fault that does not exist and report a tight confidence interval for it.

Rate histories with several rates in them, and what analysing the last period alone costs.

Gas wells, where the pressure has to be transformed before any of this works, and where two accepted deliverability methods disagree on the same three data points.

Production data with no shut-in in it at all, which is how most wells are analysed now, and which measures oil in place rather than permeability.

## Before you go

Two habits are worth fixing now, while the material is small enough to see whole.

**Always state the window.** A permeability without the interval it was fitted over is not a result anybody can check. Put the window in the report, in hours, with the number of points.

**Always ask what would have made the answer physically sensible.** A negative skin on a well with no stimulation. A permeability an order of magnitude off the map. An extrapolated pressure above the initial pressure. Each of those is a thirty-second check and each of them catches the failure this tier is about.

## The one sentence

If you keep one thing from this tier, keep this: in well test analysis the answer is a property of the window as much as of the well, and the window is your choice, so the answer is your responsibility.
