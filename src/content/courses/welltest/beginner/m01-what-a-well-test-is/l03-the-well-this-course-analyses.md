# The well this course analyses

Ten tests, one reservoir, and every answer written down in advance.

{{panel:wt-buildup-explorer}}

## One reservoir, many wells

Everything in this course happens in the same rock: porosity 0.18, oil viscosity 0.9 cp, total compressibility 1.2e-5 per psi, net pay 45 ft, wellbore radius 0.354 ft, formation volume factor 1.25 rb/stb, initial pressure 4800 psia, and a rate of 450 stb/d wherever a rate is needed.

Holding those fixed is deliberate. When the fracture fixture reports a permeability five times too high in module 4 of the Professional tier, you can be certain the difference came from the well and not from a change of units, a change of thickness, or a change of fluid.

## The ten tests

| test | what is different about it |
|---|---|
| drawdown | the base case: 85 mD, skin +6.5, storage 0.015 bbl/psi |
| buildup | the same well shut in after 36 hours |
| sealing fault | skin +3, storage 0.01, and a no-flow boundary 800 ft away |
| fractured well | 5 mD rock with a 250 ft infinite-conductivity fracture |
| dual porosity | fissures holding 8 percent of the storage, feeding from the matrix |
| closed rectangle | no-flow on all four sides, the well off-centre in 2000 by 1400 ft |
| horizontal well | 2000 ft of lateral, vertical permeability a tenth of horizontal |
| oil decline | 80 days of production at constant flowing pressure, 2 MMstb in place |
| gas decline | 300 days of the same idea in gas, 20 MMscf in place |
| linear flow | a fractured well producing in linear flow, half-length times root permeability of 500 |

The first seven are pressure transients and are analysed by the methods of the Associate and Professional tiers. The last three are production histories with no shut-in in them at all, and they belong to the Expert tier.

## Where the numbers come from

None of these tests happened. They were generated by evaluating the analytical solutions in the engine at a set of times, with parameters chosen in advance, and the generator is an independent stdlib implementation in Python rather than the JavaScript the app runs. That cross-check is the reason the fixture can be trusted as a reference.

The consequence for you is that the data are noise-free. Real gauge data are not, and the Professional tier spends a module on what noise does to a derivative. But teaching interpretation on noiseless data is the right order: if an analysis gets the wrong answer on perfect data, noise was never the problem.

## The panel

The panel above is the buildup, plotted the way a buildup is plotted, with one control: which shut-in points are in the straight line. Everything else is fixed.

Open it and change nothing yet. Notice that the horizontal axis runs from about 3600 on the left down to about 1.45 on the right, and that shut-in time increases from left to right along it. That reversed axis is Horner time and it is the subject of module 4. Notice also that the pressure points curve at the left-hand end and straighten out towards the right.

Then change the control once and watch the permeability move. You will come back to this in module 5 and take it apart properly.

## What the well is like

It is worth having a physical picture. The rock is good: 85 mD over 45 ft is a productive interval. The well is damaged: a skin of plus 6.5 means the near-wellbore region imposes a substantial extra pressure drop, of the order of a hundred psi at this rate, which is a large fraction of the drawdown the well is being produced under.

That combination is common and it is exactly the situation a well test is run to diagnose. Is the well disappointing because the rock is poor, or because the completion is damaged? Those have different answers and very different costs, and a test that gets the skin's sign wrong sends the money to the wrong place.

## The misconception to avoid

"Synthetic data make the exercises artificial." A synthetic fixture is one whose true answer you know, which is what makes it a teaching fixture. The analyses run on it are the same analyses that would be run on a real test, and the mistakes they make are the same mistakes. The difference is that here you can see them.
