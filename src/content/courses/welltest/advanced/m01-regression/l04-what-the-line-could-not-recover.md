# What the line could not recover

Three parameters, six significant figures, and one honest caveat.

## The comparison

The same buildup, analysed two ways.

| | permeability | skin | storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| best semilog window | about 3 percent low | about 0.42 low | not available |
| regression on all 40 points | 85.00000370982579 | 6.500000554344087 | 0.015000000255902136 |
| planted | 85 | 6.5 | 0.015 |

The regression is not slightly better. It is exact to the precision the optimiser was asked for.

## Where the improvement comes from

**The storage-affected data are used rather than discarded.** The model includes storage, so those twenty-seven points are informative rather than contaminating. They are, in fact, where the storage coefficient comes from.

**There is no asymptotic bias.** The straight line converges towards the radial asymptote from above in slope and never arrives. The model has no asymptote to converge to; it evaluates the full solution at every time.

**Two quantities are matched, not one.** The pressure and the derivative both enter the residual, so the fit has to reproduce the shape as well as the level.

## The parameter the line could never give

The wellbore storage coefficient.

The straight-line method treats storage as a nuisance to be waited out. The regression treats it as a parameter and returns it, and on this fixture it returns it to eight significant figures.

That is worth more than it sounds. A storage coefficient that comes out much larger than the wellbore volume implies means the well has a liquid level or a gas cap in the annulus. One that comes out changing during the test means the superposition assumption is broken. Both are diagnoses that the straight-line method has no way to make.

## The caveat, stated once and properly

The fixture was generated by the same analytical solution the model evaluates, with no noise. The regression is recovering values that are exactly representable by the model it is fitting.

Real data are not like that. There is gauge noise, the rate is not exactly constant, the reservoir is not exactly homogeneous, and the storage coefficient is not exactly constant. Every one of those puts a floor under the achievable residual, and the parameters come out with real uncertainty attached.

What this fixture demonstrates is that the METHOD and its IMPLEMENTATION are correct. What it does not demonstrate is that a regression on a real test recovers a reservoir to six figures, and any confidence interval suggesting it does should be read with the next module in mind.

## What a good real fit looks like

Not this. A good fit on real data has visible residual scatter, a sum of squared residuals set by the noise level rather than by machine precision, and confidence intervals that are a few percent on permeability and wider on everything else.

A real fit with a residual near machine precision means the data were synthetic, the model has more parameters than the data support, or something has gone wrong in the comparison.

## Starting values, on a well-conditioned problem

Start the same fit from a permeability of 5 mD and a storage of 0.5 bbl/psi, both far from the answer, and it converges to the same place in ten iterations rather than twelve.

That insensitivity is what a well-conditioned problem looks like: the objective function has one clear minimum and the optimiser finds it from anywhere sensible. Remember what it looks like, because the next module is entirely about problems that are not like this.

## The misconception to avoid

"The regression gave the right answer, so the method is validated." The method is validated for a model that exactly describes the data, which is what a fixture is for. Validating a method against real data is a different exercise, and the honest form of it is a blind test where the answer is known from an independent measurement.
