# From straight line to model

Stop discarding the data that do not fit the equation. Use an equation that fits all of it.

## What the straight line was doing

Every analysis in the first two tiers had the same shape: identify a stretch of data obeying a particular asymptotic solution, discard everything else, and fit a line to what remains.

That works, and it has two costs.

**Most of the data are thrown away.** On the buildup, thirteen points out of forty were in the semilog fit. The other twenty-seven contain the storage coefficient, the shape of the transition, and a great deal of information about the skin, and none of it was used.

**The asymptote is never reached.** The remaining thirteen points still carry a trace of the transition, which is why the permeability converged to about three percent low from below and stopped there. The bias is inherent: an asymptotic method applied to data that have not reached the asymptote is biased in a known direction.

## What a model fit does instead

The engine's model catalog holds full analytical solutions, not asymptotes. Each one is a dimensionless pressure in Laplace space, inverted numerically by the Stehfest algorithm, and each one includes wellbore storage and skin explicitly.

So a homogeneous model with the right storage coefficient reproduces the unit slope, the hump, the transition AND the radial plateau, all from the same three parameters. There is no stretch of the data it cannot describe.

Fitting it means adjusting the parameters until the model curve lies on the data curve. All forty points participate. Nothing is discarded.

## What that buys on this buildup

Fit the homogeneous model to the whole buildup and it returns

| parameter | fitted | planted |
|---|---|---|
| permeability | 85.00000370982579 mD | 85 |
| skin | 6.500000554344087 | 6.5 |
| storage | 0.015000000255902136 bbl/psi | 0.015 |

Six significant figures on all three, in twelve iterations, with a sum of squared residuals of 3.9e-13.

The straight line, at its best, gave a permeability about three percent low and a skin about four tenths low, and could not give the storage coefficient at all.

## Why the recovery is exact

Because the data were GENERATED by the same solution the model evaluates. The fixture is the homogeneous model at k = 85, S = 6.5, C = 0.015, sampled at forty times, with no noise added.

So this is not a demonstration that regression recovers reservoirs. It is a demonstration that the optimiser works and that the model is correctly implemented, which is what a fixture is for.

Real data are noisy and no model is exactly right, and both of those degrade the recovery. What survives the move to real data is the STRUCTURAL advantage: all the data are used, storage is modelled rather than discarded, and there is no asymptotic bias.

## The three things a fit needs

**A model.** Chosen from the diagnosis, which is the Professional tier's whole subject. The fit does not choose it, and the fit cannot tell you it was wrong.

**Starting values.** The catalog supplies defaults. On well-behaved problems they barely matter; on ill-conditioned ones they decide the answer, and module 2 has an example.

**Bounds.** Every parameter has a physical range, and the catalog carries them. They stop the optimiser wandering into nonsense and, occasionally, they stop it reaching the right answer.

## The order this changes

Diagnosis still comes first. The regression is a better measurement, not a replacement for knowing what you are looking at, and every failure in the next module comes from fitting a model that the diagnosis did not justify.

## The misconception to avoid

"Automatic matching removes the interpretation." It removes the arithmetic of the interpretation. The choice of model, which is the interpretation, is still yours, and the regression's willingness to fit any model you name is the single most dangerous property it has.
