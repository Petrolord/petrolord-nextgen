# The phantom fault

A boundary that does not exist, located to plus or minus twenty feet, moved a hundred and sixteen feet by rounding.

{{panel:wt-regression-explorer}}

## The experiment

Take the buildup. It was generated by the homogeneous model: infinite acting, no boundary of any kind, anywhere.

Fit the sealing-fault model to it. That model has four parameters instead of three: permeability, skin, storage, and a distance to a fault.

It converges. It reports the permeability, skin and storage correctly. And it reports a fault, at about three thousand feet, with a 95 percent interval a few tens of feet wide.

## Why it converges

The extra parameter has nothing to explain, so the optimiser pushes the fault far enough away that its effect on the fitted time range is negligible, and stops when the residual stops improving.

Far enough away is not infinitely far away. At a finite distance the image well still contributes something, and the optimiser lands wherever that contribution best absorbs the last few parts in a trillion of misfit.

Its residual is worse than the homogeneous fit's by a factor of hundreds of thousands, and both are so small that the two fitted curves are indistinguishable on any plot.

## The part that settles it

Write the same pressure change two arithmetically equivalent ways.

The fixture stores a pressure change for each point. You can also compute it by subtracting the flowing pressure at shut-in from the recorded shut-in pressure. Those are the same quantity, and the two versions differ by at most 4.547473508864641e-13 psi, a relative difference of about 2.4e-14.

Fit the sealing-fault model to each.

| pressure change written as | fitted fault distance | 95 percent interval |
|---|---|---|
| the fixture's own dp | 3092.921381005056 ft | 3050.8420739350518 to 3135.5810747488313 |
| pws minus the flowing pressure | 3209.25285825101 ft | 3194.2329762941845 to 3224.3433665070665 |

The fault moves 116.33147724595392 ft. The two 95 percent intervals do not overlap.

The homogeneous fit on the same two data sets returns 85.0000040685739 and 85.00000370982579 mD, which is the same permeability to seven significant figures.

## What that proves

The permeability is CONSTRAINED by the data. Perturb the data at the thirteenth decimal place and it does not move.

The fault distance is NOT constrained by the data. It is decided by the last bits of the arithmetic, because the objective function is essentially flat along that direction and the optimiser is picking a point on a plateau.

And the confidence interval, computed from the curvature of that objective function, reports a tight range around whichever point it happened to stop at. It has no way to know that a different but equally valid way of writing the same data would put the answer 116 ft away.

This is why the quantity is taught here and is deliberately not a graded field. No learner could reproduce it, and that is the entire point.

## The general principle

An extra parameter always improves the fit, because it can always be set to the value that does not hurt, and it will usually find something better than that.

So a lower residual does not favour the more complex model. Comparing models with different numbers of parameters requires a penalty for the extra ones, which is what the Akaike and Bayesian information criteria do, and neither is in this engine.

What IS available, and is more useful in practice, is the test above: perturb the data slightly and refit. Parameters that are constrained do not move. Parameters that are not, move a lot. That takes two runs and it answers the question the confidence interval cannot.

## What to do about it

Do not fit a boundary model unless the diagnosis says there is a boundary. The derivative on this buildup shows a plateau and nothing else: no doubling, no plunge, no unit slope. There is no evidence for a fault and no reason to offer the fit a parameter for one.

That is the Professional tier earning its place. The diagnosis constrains the model space, and a regression let loose on an unconstrained model space will find structure in anything.

## The misconception to avoid

"If the model with the boundary fits better, there is probably a boundary." It always fits better. The question is whether the improvement is more than an extra parameter buys for free, and on this fixture the improvement is a factor of hundreds of thousands in a residual that was already at machine precision, which is nothing at all.
